👉🏾👉🏾👉🏾Explanation of the Holy Scripture:#Clarifying 1 #Timothy 4:3–5 .

👉🏾Question :-1 Timothy 4: “3 They forbid people to marry and order them to abstain from certain foods, which God created to be received with thanksgiving by those who believe and who know the truth. 4 For everything God created is good, and nothing is to be rejected if it is received with thanksgiving, 5 because it is consecrated by the word of God and prayer.”
How is this interpreted? Why then are we not to eat some foods like pork?

👉🏾Answer :- My beloved child in Christ,Your question shines with sincere longing to understand the mysteries of Holy Scripture. Such desire is precious before God, for the wise man seeks not only to read the Word but to discern its true meaning through the light of the Church. 

You have brought forth the teaching of the Apostle Paul in his letter to Timothy, a passage that has stirred the thoughts of many through the ages. To approach it rightly, we must listen not only with our minds but with the heart of the Church, which preserves the faith once delivered to the saints. So let us, with reverence and clarity, unfold its meaning together.

1. What Paul is addressing in 1 Timothy 4:3–5
Saint Paul is speaking against certain false teachers (heretics) who were making two mistakes: they taught that marriage is sinful, and they taught that all meat is unclean. Paul corrects them. He says that God created the world good, and that what we receive with thanksgiving is not to be rejected, because it is sanctified by the word of God and prayer. His point is: do not make God’s good creation into something evil by false asceticism or by saying God’s gifts are intrinsically unclean.

2. “Everything God created is good” what does that mean?

This means that creation itself is not sinful. Animals, plants, marriage, bread, and meat all come from the hand of God and are good as part of His work. But “good” does not automatically mean “to be eaten by human beings.” God, in His wisdom, gives laws and commands for our guidance. Something can be good by nature yet not appointed for food.

3. Where Leviticus 11 fits in (the dietary laws)

God gave Moses clear instructions about which animals may be eaten and which must not. Leviticus 11:3 says animals that both chew the cud and have a split hoof are clean allowed as food. Animals lacking one of these signs (for example, the pig, which has a split hoof but does not chew the cud) are called unclean and were not to be eaten by God’s command. The reason this is given is obedience to God’s revealed law, not because the creature itself is “evil.”

4. Why then are we not to eat pork?

According to the Old Testament law God gave, pork is forbidden because the pig does not meet both signs of a clean animal. For the faithful, obedience to God’s commandments is greater than our personal preference. The Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church, in receiving Scripture and holy tradition, treats these commandments with respect. Thus we refrain from eating pork because of God’s command and the Church’s teaching about holiness and obedience.

5. How Paul’s words and the Old Testament law work together
Paul warns against false teachers who reject marriage or make all foods unclean as a spiritual ideal. But Paul does not abolish God’s law by saying “everything is allowed.” Rather he teaches that what is permitted and received in faith, thanksgiving, and prayer is sanctified. In other words:

Don’t condemn God’s gifts by calling them evil.

Yet keep God’s commandments and the Church’s guidance about what is permitted and what is forbidden.

Always receive your food with thanksgiving and prayer, trusting God’s word to sanctify what He allows.

May the name of the Holy God be praised forever and ever in every tongue that He has created, today and always.

May the blessings of God's mother, the virgin, and the cross be upon us.
Amen.
