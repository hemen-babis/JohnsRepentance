---About #Material #Wealth 

👉 Question፦ When the church teachings ask us to assess where our treasure is. Does that mean we should prioritize God and have him at the center of our life. 

Or does it mean, we shouldn’t care too much about things like money. For example if a person wants to be a millionaire but puts God first and serves Him. Is that still having God as the treasure or is what God wants for us to leave wealth completely and follow him only? Like the bible story of the guy who did everything but wasn’t able to give up His wealth to follow God.

Answer፦ Beloved in Christ, your inquiry touches upon a foundational teaching of our faith, mirrored in the profound words of our Lord Jesus Christ: "For where your treasure is, there your heart will be also" (Matthew 6:21). This passage invites us to reflect deeply on the orientation of our hearts and the true nature of our desires.

The teachings of the Church, drawing from the wellspring of Holy Scripture and the wisdom of the Fathers, guide us to understand that prioritizing God and centering our lives around Him does not inherently require the abandonment of all material wealth. Rather, it calls for a transformation in how we perceive and utilize such wealth. St. John Chrysostom, a voice echoing through the ages, teaches us that wealth, in itself, is not evil, but it becomes so depending on how we use it. Wealth should be seen as a tool for doing good, for serving the needs of the Church and the poor, thus becoming a means through which we can grow closer to God.

The story of the rich young man in the Gospels (Matthew 19:16-22) serves as a poignant reminder that when material possessions or the pursuit thereof eclipses our love for God and our neighbor, it becomes a barrier to entering the Kingdom of Heaven. It is not wealth itself that is the issue, but the attachment and priority we give it over our relationship with God.

In striving to be a millionaire, one must question: "Is this pursuit drawing me closer to God? Am I seeking wealth to serve Him and others, or is it fostering attachment, pride, and avarice within my heart?" Remember, our Lord Jesus Christ calls us to seek first the Kingdom of God and His righteousness (Matthew 6:33), trusting that all things necessary for our earthly journey will be provided to us according to His divine will.

As you continue to navigate the complexities of living a Christ-centered life in a material world, ask yourself: How can I use the resources and talents God has entrusted to me for the greater glory of His Kingdom? How can I ensure that my heart remains anchored in the love of God, regardless of my material success or failure?

Now, reflecting on these thoughts, what is one step you can take today to align your treasure with the Kingdom of Heaven?
