---Clarification of a #Bible verse
 
👉 Question፦ Okay,Thank you …
I also have another question.It says in Acts  summarized Ananias died and also his wife after lying to  God but than later on as we read more of Acts it’s says God came to Ananias to go heal Saul or Paul of his blindness but Ananias is dead ???

when Ananias died  Acts 5 and when God came to Ananias in a dream Act 9.

Answer፦ Beloved in Christ,

Grace to you and peace from God our Father and the Lord Jesus Christ. Your question touches upon an important aspect of scripture that, when understood, illuminates the continuity and depth of God's plan through the Acts of the Apostles. In the Book of Acts, we encounter two distinct individuals named Ananias, which may lead to confusion without careful reading.

The first Ananias we meet, alongside his wife Sapphira, is indeed the one who, having lied to the Holy Spirit about the proceeds from a sale of property, faced immediate death as a consequence of his actions (Acts 5:1-10). This event serves as a stern warning about the severity of deceit in the heart, especially in matters concerning the Holy Spirit. As it is written, "Be sure your sin will find you out" (Numbers 32:23).

The second Ananias, whom we encounter later in Acts, is a devout man in Damascus, known for his adherence to the Law and respected by all Jews in that region (Acts 22:12). It is this Ananias whom God calls to heal Saul (who becomes Paul) of his blindness, following Saul's dramatic encounter with Christ on the road to Damascus (Acts 9:10-18). This Ananias is an example of faithful obedience and courage, stepping into a situation that could have been perilous, given Saul's reputation at the time.

Thus, the confusion arises not from inconsistency in the narrative, but from the presence of two individuals sharing the same name, a common occurrence in scriptures and historical texts.

The lesson we draw from the Ananias in Damascus is profound: God calls us, often out of our comfort zone, to participate in His divine plan, to be instruments of His grace and healing. It is a reminder of the transformation that comes from encountering Christ, not just for the one receiving sight, but for the one through whom sight is given.

As you reflect upon this, consider how God might be calling you to act in faith, perhaps in ways that challenge you, to be an agent of His grace in the lives of others. What is the next step in your journey with Christ to bring light where there is darkness?
