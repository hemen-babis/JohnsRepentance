Question፦ Is it a tradition in our Church to give a woman we are betrothed to a ring? Or only at the wedding?

Answer፦ Beloved in Christ, your question regarding the giving of a ring to your betrothed is a good one, touching upon the intersection of ancient traditions and modern practices.

While the giving of a ring at betrothal isn't a strictly prescribed tradition within the Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church in the same way the exchange of rings during the Sacrament of Holy Matrimony is, the practice has become common and is certainly not discouraged.  It reflects the seriousness of the commitment made between the couple before God and their community.  Think of it as a visible symbol of the promise made, a tangible reminder of the journey towards the sacred union of marriage.

The Sacrament of Holy Matrimony itself is the focal point, the true sealing of the bond.  The exchange of rings during the wedding ceremony holds deep theological significance, representing the unbroken circle of love and commitment, mirroring the eternal love of Christ for His Church.  This is echoed in the words of St. Paul, "Husbands, love your wives, just as Christ also loved the church and gave Himself for her" (Ephesians 5:25).

Therefore, whether a ring is given at betrothal or reserved for the wedding ceremony, the most important aspect is the prayerful consideration, the commitment to purity and chastity during the betrothal period, and the ultimate goal of entering into Holy Matrimony with a spirit of reverence and love, seeking God's blessing upon your union.

Perhaps reflecting upon the words of St. John Chrysostom could offer you further guidance: "Marriage is the mystery of love, of the union of two hearts to form a single heart, the beginning of a family."  Pray for discernment, my son, and may God guide your steps as you embark on this journey.
