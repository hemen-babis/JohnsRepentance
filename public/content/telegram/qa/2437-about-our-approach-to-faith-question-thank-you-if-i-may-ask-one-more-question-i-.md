About our approach to #Faith 

👉 Question፦ Thank you! If I may ask one more question, I want to know if Christianity is true instead of just believing it because my parents were Orthodox. However, im worried as I try to study the proof for Christianity and studying other religions, I may face times of doubt and confusion. Especially if unbelievers ask me questions that I don't know the answer too. During those time, does that mean I'm not a faithful Christian since I am experiencing time of uncertainty of whether Christianity is true? Would I be forbidden from the Eucharist or the Orthodox life since I don't yet believe everything wholeheartedly to be true? Or is it that uncertainty is part of the journey, and I would only be forbidden if I believed with conviction that they were false?

Answer፦ Beloved in Christ,

Your inquiry is a beacon of earnest seeking, and it is with great joy that I address your heartfelt concerns. The path to understanding and embracing the Christian faith, especially within the profound depths of Orthodoxy, is indeed a journey—one marked by questions, learning, and growth.

To address your concerns, I wish to remind you of the words of the Apostle Paul, who said, "Test everything; hold fast what is good" (1 Thessalonians 5:21). The endeavor to understand your faith more deeply and to seek answers is not a sign of weak faith but rather an indication of your desire to grow in the truth that the Lord has revealed. Questioning and seeking are not only natural but necessary for the maturation of your faith.

The Church Fathers, too, understood the importance of engaging with and understanding one's faith. A revered father encouraged catechumens to "not believe blindly but with justification." Therefore, encountering doubts or questions does not alienate you from the Church or the sacraments; rather, it is a part of your spiritual evolution. The Orthodox Church does not demand blind faith but an informed, living faith that grows and strengthens through experience, questioning, and seeking.

In moments of doubt or confusion, it is crucial to remain anchored in prayer, the sacraments, and the community of the Church. Engaging with a spiritual father and participating in the life of the Church can provide guidance, clarity, and the grace of God to navigate these challenges. The Church is a hospital for the soul, where we are all in the process of healing and growing toward the likeness of Christ.

Remember, faith is a relationship, and like all relationships, it goes through seasons of certainty and doubt. What is essential is your commitment to continue seeking, knocking, and asking with a sincere heart. God honors the earnest seeker and reveals Himself to those who earnestly seek Him with humility and perseverance.

As you continue this journey, ask yourself: What steps can I take today to deepen my understanding and experience of the Orthodox faith? How can I engage more fully in the life of the Church to find the answers and peace I seek?

In Christ's love.

May the name of the Holy God be praised forever and ever, today and always.
