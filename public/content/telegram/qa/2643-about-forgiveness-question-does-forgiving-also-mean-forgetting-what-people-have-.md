About #Forgiveness

👉🏾 Question፦ Does forgiving also mean forgetting what people have done or is it still considered fully forgiving if we remember and use that information to set boundaries. For example, if someone is consistently behaving in certain ways that’s hurtful. Would forgiving the person and loving the person mean we forget it and continue the relationship in the same way. Or would it still be considered forgiving and still maintaining love if i forgive the person, but create some distance and set boundaries to protect myself? (while making sure that i have love in my heart for the person). 

My second question is regarding the teaching of reconciling with others before taking Holy Communion. Does that mean forgiving and asking forgiveness or is it also restoring the relationship back to how it was previously if the level of closeness with the person changed after you set boundaries and distance to protect yourself? Are we expected to be close with the person (even though they continue to hurt us) or is the expectation more about being at peace with the person having forgiven or asked for forgiveness if i’m at fault. 


Thank you 🙏🏾

👉 Answer፦ Dear seeker of truth,

Grace to you and peace from God our Father and the Lord Jesus Christ. Your inquiry touches upon the profound virtues of forgiveness and reconciliation, essential in our walk with Christ and our dealings with one another.

Forgiveness, indeed, is a commandment from the Lord, reflecting His own infinite mercy towards us. As the Lord teaches us in the Gospel of Matthew, "For if you forgive others their trespasses, your heavenly Father will also forgive you" (Matthew 6:14). To forgive does not necessarily mean to forget, for we are not called to ignore the reality of our hurt. Rather, it is to release the bitterness and the desire for retribution against one who has wronged us. Setting boundaries is a form of wisdom, protecting oneself from recurring harm while still holding love and forgiveness in one's heart. This is well within the teachings of Orthodoxy, which always seeks the safety and well-being of its flock.

Regarding your second question about reconciliation before Holy Communion, the Church teaches us to approach the Holy Sacraments with a heart free of enmity. "Therefore, if you are offering your gift at the altar and there remember that your brother or sister has something against you, leave your gift there in front of the altar. First go and be reconciled to them; then come and offer your gift" (Matthew 5:23-24). This reconciliation involves forgiving and seeking forgiveness where necessary. It does not necessarily mandate a restoration of the relationship to its previous state, especially in cases where continual harm occurs. The Church guides us to maintain peace and love in our hearts, even if distance is necessary for personal safety and spiritual well-being.

Reflect upon these teachings, dear child of God, and consider how you might apply them in your relationships. How might you move forward in offering forgiveness and seeking reconciliation while preserving your peace and integrity?

In Christ's love,

May the blessings of God's mother, the virgin, and the cross be upon us.
