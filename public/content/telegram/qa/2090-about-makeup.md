👉 About #makeup

👉🏾 Question፦ Greetings! 

If you are a priest, bless and absolve me father. I hope this finds you well. Please don’t see below as an attack I just had a concern about something in the John’s Repentance group that I needed to address. 

I saw the answer to the makeup question in Johns repentance and was a bit confused. Everything I’ve learned from so far with teachings of the fathers and other writings have taught me that it is in fact something we are not supposed to take part in at all. I’m concerned by the answer because it allows for a small but possibly dangerous leeway for us as women and girls to think it’s ok when in fact it’s not and does no good for us whether in body or soul. Can you kindly please tell me which church fathers or which books you cite that agree with your stance that it’s ok as long as the intention is pure?

Here are some sources I’ve learned from that say otherwise.

“He commits a serious offense who adulterates the work of God. It is a serious charge to suppose that man is to be preferred to God as an artist! It is serious, indeed, when God has to say this about you: 'I do not recognize My colors or My image, not even the countenance which I have made. What is not Mine I reject. Take up your abode with him who has painted you. Seek your favors from him to whom you have given payment.' What will be your reply?” St Ambrose

Apostolic Constitutions book 1 par 8 (400 ad)
Do not paint thy face, which is God's workmanship; for there is no part of thee which wants ornament, inasmuch as all things which God has made are very good. But the lascivious additional adorning of what is already good is an affront to the bounty of the Creator.

Answer፦ Blessings and peace to you in the name of our Lord Jesus Christ,

Thank you for bringing your concerns forward with a spirit of inquiry and faithfulness. In the Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church, we hold dear the teachings of our Fathers and the sacred traditions that have been entrusted to us. Your question regarding makeup is an important one, as it relates to how we present ourselves before God and others.

As you rightly pointed out, our Church Fathers, including St. Ambrose and others, have spoken about the dangers of adorning oneself in ways that detract from the natural beauty that God has bestowed upon us. In the spirit of their teachings, the use of makeup should be approached with great caution and discernment.

The Apostolic Constitutions reminds us that our primary adornment should be the "hidden person of the heart," as noted in 1 Peter 3:3-4. The focus is on cultivating inner beauty — a beauty that reflects our devotion, humility, and love for God.

While the previous answer mentioned that makeup is not inherently sinful, it is crucial to emphasize that in our tradition, simplicity and naturalness in appearance are valued highly as expressions of humility and purity of heart. The use of makeup can easily lead to vanity and a focus on external appearances, which is contrary to the spirit of Orthodox Christian living.

Therefore, in alignment with the teachings of our Church Fathers, I would advise caution and restraint in the use of makeup, always examining one's intentions and seeking to glorify God in all things, including our appearance. However blindly labeling makeup as sin without analyzing the situation would not be fully aligned with the Church's definition of sin.  

In this regard, consider how you can cultivate the beauty of your soul through prayer, fasting, and acts of charity. How might your outward appearance reflect the inner transformation that is taking place in your heart as you draw nearer to God?

May the peace of Christ guide your heart and mind in all things. Amen.
