👉 About #Dietary restrictions in the EOTC.

👉🏾 Question፦ Thank you again for your answer. Forgive my unclarity as I meant dietary restrictions such as not eating pork. I've had a priest tell me eating any of the unclean food in Deuteronomy would be a sinful act

Answer፦ Dear Nathan,

Thank you for your patience and for guiding our conversation to deeper understanding. Your points regarding the continuity of certain practices in Ethiopian Orthodoxy illuminate how tradition and transformation are deeply woven into our spiritual life.

Indeed, the Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church has a unique historical context, having practiced Judaism before the introduction of Christianity. This historical depth contributes to certain dietary practices, like abstaining from pork, which have been maintained even after the acceptance of Christianity. This continuity is not merely a matter of cultural preference but a testament to the Church's understanding of spiritual progression—from physical to spiritual, from old to new in Christ.

The Church Fathers, in their wisdom, often retained certain practices that they believed would help the faithful grow spiritually. As you noted, these practices are seen as aids in the journey from a life dominated by the flesh to one led by the Spirit. The decision to maintain such practices is grounded in their potential to foster spiritual transformation and discipline, reflecting the Church’s role in guiding the faithful through gradual sanctification.

It's also important to recognize, as you pointed out, that these practices are not rigidly imposed on new converts from different cultural backgrounds. This flexibility underscores the Church's universal mission and the adaptability of the Orthodox faith to diverse cultural contexts, always with the aim of bringing all into a deeper communion with Christ.

Your engagement with these issues suggests a deep commitment to understanding the interplay between culture, tradition, and spiritual growth. How might this understanding continue to shape your journey in the faith, and how can it help you to live out the transformative power of the Gospel in your own life and community?
