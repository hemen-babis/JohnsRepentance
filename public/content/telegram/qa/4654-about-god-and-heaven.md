👉 About #God and #Heaven

👉🏾 Question፦ Another question that was asked. 

1. When was God born / when did He began to exist? (Not when Jesus Christ was born). 

2. How does heaven look like? 

Thank you!

Answer፦ These are profound questions, my child, reaching into the very heart of our faith and the mysteries of God.  Let us explore them with reverence and humility.

1. When was God born / when did He begin to exist?

The very notion of God being "born" is incompatible with our Orthodox understanding of His divine nature. God is eternal, without beginning or end. He is the Uncreated Creator, the Alpha and Omega, as revealed in the Holy Scriptures (Revelation 1:8, 21:6). He is existence itself, the source of all being.  Time, as we perceive it, is a creation of God, existing within His eternal embrace.  Therefore, to ask when God began is akin to asking what lies outside of existence itself - a question that transcends human comprehension.  The Church Fathers, like St. Gregory of Nyssa, explored this concept of God's timeless eternity, emphasizing His transcendence beyond the limitations of our created understanding.  We can only approach this mystery with awe and reverence, acknowledging the limitations of our finite minds in grasping the infinite nature of God.

2. How does heaven look like?

The nature of Heaven, the Kingdom of God, is another profound mystery.  The Scriptures offer glimpses, describing it as a place of unimaginable beauty, joy, and communion with God.  St. Paul speaks of things "eye has not seen, nor ear heard, nor have entered into the heart of man, the things which God has prepared for those who love Him” (1 Corinthians 2:9).  The Book of Revelation speaks of a new heaven and a new earth, a city of gold, and the presence of God illuminating all things.

However, these are symbolic representations, attempts to convey the inexpressible reality of dwelling in God's presence.  We should be cautious about interpreting these descriptions literally. The essence of Heaven lies not in its physical characteristics, but in the perfect union with God, the source of all love, joy, and peace.  It is a state of being, a transformation of our very selves into the likeness of Christ, free from the limitations of sin and death.  The Church Fathers often emphasized this transformative aspect of salvation, describing Heaven as a process of theosis, becoming partakers of the divine nature.

Therefore, rather than focusing on what Heaven looks like, we should concentrate on what it means to be in communion with God.  Through prayer, fasting, almsgiving, and participation in the sacraments of the Church, we begin to experience a foretaste of this heavenly Kingdom here on earth.  This journey of spiritual growth, guided by the wisdom of the Church and the grace of the Holy Spirit, prepares us for the ultimate joy of eternal life in the presence of God.  

May these reflections illuminate your understanding, my child. Do not hesitate to ask further questions.  May God guide you on your spiritual journey.

May the name of the Holy God be praised forever and ever in every tongue that He has created, today and always. 

May the blessings of God's mother, the virgin, and the cross be upon us.
Amen.
