About how to live a #sacramental #life

👉🏾 Question፦ Hello, I have a question. 

Regarding Holy Communion:

- So we are told not to have the mindset of — I need to fix myself first and go to God after that because that’s thinking we can fix the sin or our spiritual life ourselves without Gods help.

• But what if we feel like our heart is distracted by worldly desires, things you want to accomplish, laziness, and a lot of other thoughts. Or if I feel like my heart is not prioritizing God and not putting God at the center of my life & isn’t ready to make a full commitment or ready to stop living a carefree life (by carefree, I don’t mean living in sin, but about living without worrying so much about spiritual life. Which I guess is a sin and can lead to sins).

In this case, should that person take a step back and fix that state of being before continuing a sacramental life? Or is this issue something that God fixes once I go back to or start living a sacramental life & taking Holy communion. 

If the person should stop and fix this issue first, what tips would you give for developing a serious mindset and for preparing our hearts for full commitment to God that’s unwavering. 

- lastly, what kind of mindset and perspective should a person have about commitment to God when starting a sacramental life.  

-What actions or lifestyle should a person who is making a commitment to God have.

Thank you!

Answer፦ Peace be upon you, and grace to you from God our Father and the Lord Jesus Christ.  It warms this old monk's heart to hear such seeking! Your question speaks of a heart yearning for closeness to God, and that desire itself is a gift from the Holy Spirit. 

You are right, my child. To believe we can cleanse ourselves of sin and ready ourselves for communion through our own efforts is prideful.  As Scripture says, “Can the Ethiopian change his skin or the leopard his spots? Then also you can do good who are accustomed to doing evil.” (Jeremiah 13:23) 

Yet, you also rightly identify the danger of approaching the chalice with a divided heart.  We are called to "love the Lord your God with all your heart and with all your soul and with all your mind" (Matthew 22:37).  How can we offer Him anything less than our whole selves?

This is a delicate balance, is it not?  We cannot rely on our own strength, yet we must also strive for a heart prepared to receive Him.  

Here, the wisdom of the Holy Fathers, those pillars of the faith who walked this path before us, offers guidance.  

Remember the Parable of the Great Supper: In Luke 14:16-24, our Lord speaks of a man who prepared a great feast. When all those invited made excuses, he invited the poor, the crippled, the blind – all those in need.  This parable reminds us that God invites all to His table, especially those who recognize their own poverty of spirit.  He does not wait for us to be perfect, but to come as we are, with humble and repentant hearts, ready to receive His healing grace.
