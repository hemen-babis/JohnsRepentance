👉 About the preparation needed to partake of the #Holy #Communion 

👉🏾 Question፦ Beloved John's Repentance 
Please explain 
"If those who receive the sacrament are married, they should abstain from meat for three days before receiving communion. This is not because sexual relationship is a sin or unclean but because it is a work of the flesh. We do this out of reverence for the Eucharist. "
Is this a suggestion or requirement? Maybe the word meat is a typo? Also is this unique to the Ethiopian Church or the other sister churches as well? Meaning is this a traditional practice exclusively in the Ethiopian church or is this from the Holy Fathers....please excuse my ignorance.  Thank you

Answer፦ Peace be upon you, my child.

Your question touches upon a delicate and often misunderstood aspect of our faith—the preparation for receiving the Holy Eucharist. You are correct; the word "meat" appears to be a mistranslation or misunderstanding. The intended meaning is indeed sexual relations.

The early Church Fathers, including St. John Chrysostom and St. Clement of Alexandria, spoke of the importance of marital chastity, especially before receiving Communion. They emphasized that abstaining from sexual relations for a period before partaking was not because marital intimacy is sinful, but rather a way to elevate the soul and focus on spiritual communion with Christ. This practice invites us to set aside even the good things of this world, preparing ourselves fully to receive our Lord in the Eucharist.

This discipline is not unique to the Ethiopian Orthodox Church alone; it is shared by many Oriental Orthodox traditions and is rooted in the wisdom of the Holy Fathers. They remind us that "the flesh lusts against the Spirit, and the Spirit against the flesh" (Galatians 5:17), encouraging us to submit our bodies and desires to the work of the Spirit, in reverence and holy preparation.

Regarding whether this is an absolute requirement, it is a matter best discussed with a spiritual father who can provide personalized guidance. The Church, in her wisdom, provides this as a path to elevate our reverence, but how each of us embraces this may differ according to our journey and circumstances. Matters such as these require careful discernment and the counsel of a spiritual father, who can guide you on the most fitting path to prepare your heart and soul.

How might you approach your spiritual father to seek this guidance as you prepare for the holy Eucharist?
