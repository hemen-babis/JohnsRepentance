About the book of #Psalms 

Question፦ What is God trying to tell his disciples through this psalm. Also, what is the deep significance behind the reading psalms?

Answer፦ In the heart of the Psalms, we find a reflection of the human soul in all its aspects: joy, sorrow, hope, despair, faith, and doubt. They are prayers that Christ Himself prayed and thereby sanctified, teaching us that in these sacred hymns, we find a direct pathway to commune with God. The Psalms, therefore, are not merely readings; they are the breath of the Church's life, an intimate dialogue between the Creator and His creation.

Consider Psalm 139 (138 in the Septuagint), "O Lord, You have searched me and known me." This Psalm, like many others, reveals the omniscience and omnipresence of God, teaching His disciples about His unfathomable love and the profound knowledge He has of each one of us. It invites us into a deep trust in God, who knows our path and our lying down and is acquainted with all our ways.

The reading of Psalms is deeply significant because it provides us with the words when we have none, elevates our soul, and aligns our heart with the will of God. It teaches us patience, humility, and the profound beauty of repentance. As St. John Chrysostom beautifully articulated, "He who recites the Psalms is uttering them as his own words, and each sings them as if they were written concerning him." They are a mirror to our soul and a guide to our spiritual journey.

As you engage with the Psalms, let them transform you, guide you, and draw you closer to the heart of God. Reflect on how the Psalms resonate with your life and how they call you to live in deeper communion with God. What step will you take today to allow the Psalms to enrich your spiritual journey?
