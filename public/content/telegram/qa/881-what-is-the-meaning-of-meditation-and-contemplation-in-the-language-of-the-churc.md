---What is the meaning of #meditation and #contemplation in the language of the Church?

👉 Question: Could you teach us what meditation means in the Church? What does it mean to meditate on Scriptures or other spiritual and educational readings? How can we follow the example of King David, who meditated on God's Word day and night, as he wrote in Psalm 1:2-3: "But his delight is in the law of the Lord, and on his law he meditates day and night. He is like a tree planted by streams of water, which yields its fruit in season and whose leaf does not wither. Whatever he does prospers."

Answer: The Psalmist Saint David wrote in Psalm 1, verses 2-3: "Blessed is the man who does not walk in the counsel of the wicked or stand in the way of sinners or sit in the seat of mockers. But his delight is in the law of the Lord, and on his law he meditates day and night. He is like a tree planted by streams of water, which yields its fruit in season and whose leaf does not wither. Whatever he does prospers."

An Orthodox believer who contemplates the Word of God, studies it, and listens to it, is like a tree planted by a river. Therefore, the believer who reads and listens to the Word of God will bear the fruit of morality. This fruit is manifested in the believer's observance of the commandments of God, including fasting, praying, repenting, bowing down, and receiving the holy flesh and precious blood of Christ.

Meditation on the Word of God is essential to remember and follow its teachings. One must not forget the Word of God, even when sitting, standing, walking, sleeping, or rising. Meditating on the Word of God means following God's commandments, including not lying, not stealing, and honoring one's parents.

Since God suffered and died for us, believers should not wrong anyone whom God loves. By following God's commandments and meditating on His Word, the believer will produce the fruit of morality and be fit for the Kingdom of God.
