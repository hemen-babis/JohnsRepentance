👉🏾 About #Marriage #issues and their #resolutions. 

Answer፦ My dear sister in Christ,

You speak with pain, yet with a searching and discerning heart. Your reference to Scripture reveals a love for truth over appearances, which is good and pleasing to God. However, the Church teaches us to examine our own soul before focusing on others not because your suffering is your fault, but because salvation begins with, “Lord, have mercy on me, the sinner.”

For You

St. John Chrysostom, calls marriage “a little Church” and advises: “Correct yourself first, and then your household will be corrected.” Before addressing your husband’s hypocrisy, quietly reflect:

1. Have I allowed bitterness to take root?

2. Do I pray for his repentance with tears, or do I mostly accuse him in my heart?

3. Do I speak to him to win arguments or to heal?

4. Have I sought my father confessor’s counsel, bringing my wounds to Christ’s light?

This is not to excuse his sins but to protect your soul, ensuring his failings don’t become yours.

For Him

You’ve rightly identified questions a deacon or any Christian man must face. Scripture is clear:

1. If he stands at the altar but despises the one flesh God joined to him, his prayers are hindered (1 Peter 3:7).

2. If he offers incense while unreconciled with his wife, the Lord says, “Leave the gift and be reconciled first” (Matt. 5:23-24).

3. St. Chrysostom warns that a man who adorns the Church but neglects his wife “has overturned the very altar of his own house.”

Such service, without humility at home, risks becoming empty ritual.

A Path Forward

Spiritual Father: Bring your struggle to a trusted confessor. This ensures obedience and protects you from mere complaint.

Prayer with Tears: Pray the Jesus Prayer for yourself and your husband by name, asking Christ to soften both hearts.

Gentle Truth: In peaceful moments, speak simply, without accusation: “My heart is hurt. I need us to pray and seek forgiveness together.”
Boundaries with Love: If his words become abusive or threatening, know that the Church does not bless silence in the face of harm. Seeking help honors the image of God in you and your children.

A Final Reminder

1. You cannot save him through arguments; only Christ can pierce the heart.

2. Your salvation is not bound to his hypocrisy. Your humility, prayer, and patience free of self-condemnation are precious to God.

3. A deacon can deceive himself like a Pharisee, but you must not let his blindness draw you into darkness.

Stand before Christ, confess your sins, keep your heart free of hatred, and pray for light to reach your husband. This is the Orthodox way: repentance beginning with oneself and love that neither excuses sin nor assumes judgment, but entrusts it to God.

May the name of the Holy God be praised forever and ever in every tongue that He has created, today and always. 

May the blessings of God's mother, the virgin, and the cross be upon us. Amen.
