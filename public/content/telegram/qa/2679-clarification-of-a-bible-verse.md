👉 Clarification of a #Bible verse 

👉🏾 Question፦ Philippians 2:1-11 “do nothing from selfish ambition or conceit, but in humility regard others as better than yourself. Let each of you look not to your own interests, but to the interest of others” 

Can you explain why God would tell us to do this? Why do we need to humble ourselves for God to exalt us? What if we were better in something than someone else should we still regard them as better? Why would we need to exalt others interest above ours? Why can’t it be mutual? Why does Christ chose the lowest position (slave or poverty) when he could have chose to be a regular man with a regular income instead of poverty?

My second question, what would happen to someone if they were to go to a psychic for a reading? Some say Satan will rest on the person as soon as they enter and other say nothing will happen. Is there an immediate consequence or curse that comes on someone who chooses to go to a psychic once or twice for answers because they feel Gods answer is delayed?

Answer፦ Grace to you and peace from God our Father and the Lord Jesus Christ.

The passage from Philippians you cited beckons us to embrace humility, reflecting the very nature of Christ Himself. Saint John Chrysostom, in his homilies on Philippians, elucidates that Christ "emptied Himself" not by putting off His divine nature, but by taking on the form of a servant. This profound act of humility is not just an ethical directive but a transformative journey that aligns us with the divine will. Humbling oneself, therefore, is not about self-deprecation but about ascending to true spiritual nobility. When we consider others better than ourselves, we cultivate a heart of service, reflecting the image of Christ who "came not to be served but to serve" (Matthew 20:28).

As to the matter of talents or superiority in certain aspects, the wisdom of the Desert Fathers offers insight. Abba Dorotheus teaches that humility and the love of God perfect our talents, making us aware that every good gift comes from the Father (James 1:17). Thus, in humility, we recognize our gifts as means to serve others, not as pedestals for self-exaltation.

Regarding exalting the interests of others above our own, this is not a call to negate our own needs but to foster a spirit of generosity. It does not diminish mutual care but elevates it, promoting a community where each one looks after the other, mirroring the early Church (Acts 2:44-47).

Christ chose the lowest position to fully identify with the human condition — to redeem it from within. His choice of poverty is a radical call to spiritual richness, teaching us that true wealth is found in heavenly treasures (Matthew 6:19-21).

Turning to your second question, the Church has consistently warned against seeking guidance from psychics or engaging in any form of divination. Such practices are considered doors opened to deception by malevolent spirits, as they rely on sources apart from God. A revered father of the Church once said "whosoever does not have Christ for his head is inevitably ruled by the devil." Therefore, it's not merely a matter of immediate consequence but of spiritual alignment. When one consults a psychic, they turn away from the light of Christ, potentially inviting spiritual discord.

The right path, when feeling God's answer is delayed, is to deepen one’s faith through prayer, fasting, and communion with the Church. As the Psalmist says, "Wait for the Lord; be strong, and let your heart take courage; wait for the Lord!" (Psalm 27:14).

How might you deepen your reliance on God and His Church instead of turning to uncertain and perilous sources?

May the name of the Holy God be praised forever and ever in every tongue that He has created, today and always.
