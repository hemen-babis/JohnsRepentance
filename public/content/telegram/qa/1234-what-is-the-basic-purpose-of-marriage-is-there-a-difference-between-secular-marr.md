What is the basic purpose of #marriage? Is there a difference between secular marriage and a marriage conducted in church?

✍️Answer፦ The institution of marriage holds a deep significance in the Orthodox Church, reflecting the union of the bridegroom and the bride. It is regarded as a sacred mystery that is celebrated through a ceremony conducted in the church, accompanied by a sacrament that symbolizes the joining and oneness of the couple. In the words of Scripture, "A man shall leave his father and mother and be joined to his wife, and the two shall become one flesh" (Matthew 19:5). Thus, marriage is a profound mystery in which the couple commits to love and cherish each other exclusively, with the intention of starting a family, sharing their lives and possessions, and living together as long as God permits.

The purpose of marriage can be understood through three pillars: mutual support, procreation, and safeguarding against adultery. Firstly, marriage provides a framework for helping and supporting one another. Recognizing the words of God, "It is not good for man to be alone. Let us create a suitable helper for him" (Genesis 2:18), Orthodox teachings emphasize that marriage is a divine gift that assists individuals on their spiritual journey. At its core, marriage relies on genuine affection and love between spouses, where both partners prioritize the needs of their spouse, care for each other, and honor God through their commitment.

Secondly, marriage is seen as a means of continuing the generations and fulfilling the commandment to multiply. After blessing Adam and Eve in Paradise, God instructed them to "multiply and fill the earth and subdue it..." (Genesis 1:28). This divine blessing signifies God's approval of marriage and the union of Adam and Eve. In the Orthodox tradition, God's presence is invoked during the marriage ceremony, just as He was present during the union of Adam and Eve in Paradise. This demonstrates the significance of a legal marriage, solemnized in the holy church where Christ's body and blood are offered.

Lastly, marriage serves as a safeguard against the temptations of adultery. As the Apostle Paul advised, "Because of the temptation to sexual immorality, each man should have his own wife and each woman her own husband" (1 Corinthians 7:2). By committing to a holy marriage, individuals avoid the perils of yielding to lustful desires outside the confines of a sacred bond. It is essential for those living a religious life to steer clear of adultery, and marriage provides a path to resist such temptations.

Thus, the primary goal of marriage is to walk together in righteousness, pursuing eternal life and inheriting the Kingdom of Heaven. Married life is understood as a spiritual journey, necessitating the initiation of the marriage covenant within the Christian framework of the Church. This sacramental aspect signifies the involvement of three parties: the couple and God. Without all three, a marriage cannot be considered complete. In the sacrament of marriage, the physical union of the couple mirrors the unity of Christ's body and the Church. As stated in the Holy Scriptures, "They will become one flesh" (Mark 10:8), the great mystery of physical and spiritual unity finds its fulfillment in the Holy Eucharist.

The reason for elevating marriage to the status of a sacrament within the Church lies in allowing the Lord Jesus Christ to be the head and center of the couple's spiritual journey. In the words of St. Paul, "This mystery is profound, and I am saying that it refers to Christ and the Church" (Ephesians 5:32). The sacramental aspect signifies the union between the divine and the physical, and it is through the visible service and the reception of invisible grace that this union is made possible. The Holy Communion, in particular, transforms the marriage into a new reality, where the couple becomes one flesh.
