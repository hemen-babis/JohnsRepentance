4. The Zeal and Faith of Saint Peter

Peter was not only faithful in deed but fervent in spirit. He was bold to ask questions when the others kept silent and quick to answer when the Lord inquired. He stood at the front of the apostolic circle—not because he sought power, but because he loved deeply and earnestly.

Walking on Water

When Christ walked upon the sea, Peter still not yet filled with the Holy Spirit said, “Lord, if it is You, command me to come to You on the water.” The Lord said, “Come.” And though he began to sink from fear, the Lord stretched out His hand to save him (Matthew 14:25-34). Here, we see both Peter’s boldness and weakness his readiness to draw near and his need for grace.

The Confession of Christ’s Divinity

At Caesarea Philippi, when the Lord asked, “Who do you say that I am?”, Peter answered with divine clarity: “You are the Christ, the Son of the Living God” (Matthew 16:16). For this, the Lord blessed him, saying, “Flesh and blood has not revealed this to you, but My Father in heaven.” And He declared, “You are Peter, and on this rock I will build my Church.”

Here, the foundation of the Church was not Peter the man, but Peter’s faith the unwavering confession that Jesus is the Son of God. This became the cornerstone of Christian doctrine long before Nicaea.

The Mount of Transfiguration

Peter, with James and John, beheld the glory of the Lord on Mount Tabor. His garments shone as the sun, and Moses and Elijah appeared. Overwhelmed, Peter offered to build three tabernacles. But the voice from the cloud declared: “This is My beloved Son; listen to Him” (Matthew 17:1–5). The vision was a confirmation of Peter’s earlier confession.

The Temple Tax and the Coin in the Fish’s Mouth

When questioned about the temple tax, Peter answered without consulting Christ. Yet the Lord, knowing all, used the occasion to teach him that the Son of God is not bound by temple tribute. Still, to avoid offense, He miraculously provided the coin from the mouth of a fish (Matthew 17:25–27).

5. Moments of Weakness and Restoration

Peter’s love was deep, but his human frailty was not absent.

Washing of the Feet

When Christ began to wash the feet of His disciples, Peter, in awe, refused. But when the Lord said, “If I do not wash you, you have no part with Me,” Peter yielded entirely: “Lord, not only my feet, but my hands and head also!” (John 13:1–13). Such was the eagerness of his heart, though slow to understand.

The Denial and the Rooster's Crow

When Christ foretold that Peter would deny Him, Peter declared boldly, “Even if all fall away, I never will.” Yet, when fear seized him in the high priest’s courtyard, he denied his Master three times. But the moment the rooster crowed, he remembered, and he wept bitterly. Those tears were not of shame alone, but of repentance. And thus he was restored (Matthew 26:69–75).

6. After the Resurrection: The Strengthened Shepherd

Peter was the first of the apostles to enter the empty tomb. And when Christ appeared by the Sea of Tiberias and asked him three times, “Do you love Me?”, Peter responded, and was charged: “Feed My sheep.” The denials were undone by triple love. Thus the Lord restored him and confirmed his calling.

Filled with the Holy Spirit at Pentecost, Saint Peter became the mighty preacher of Christ. He baptized three thousand souls in a single day, discerned the secret lies of Ananias and Sapphira, and healed the sick. He traveled through Samaria, Lydda, Joppa, and beyond establishing the Church with unwavering zeal.
