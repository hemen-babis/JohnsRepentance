---About #misuse of #sacred_texts

Question፦ Hello, 

I have a question about something i’ve been hearing about but don’t really understand. What exactly is “metet” and how come there are people in the church (priests and deacons) that do these things using church prayer books? How can something evil be done with a Holy Book & church leaders? And what are people supposed to do to protect themselves from evil things like this that people do. 

Thank you!

Answer፦ Dear seeker of truth,

I am grateful for your question, which touches upon a profound and delicate aspect of our faith and practice. The term "metet" might not be familiar to many outside the Ethiopian Orthodox community; it refers to the misuse of sacred texts and prayers, twisting them for purposes not aligned with God's will. Such actions, no matter who commits them, are deeply contrary to the teachings of our Church and the very essence of Christianity.

In the Scripture, we are reminded, "Do not be overcome by evil, but overcome evil with good" (Romans 12:21). The core of our defense against any form of evil is our faith, prayer, and the grace of God. When sacred words are distorted, it is not the texts themselves that cause harm but the intent with which they are used. The Holy Scriptures and our Church's prayer books are sources of life and healing, and when used rightly, they lead us closer to God.

To protect oneself from any form of evil, one should adhere firmly to the life of the Church, participating in the sacraments, engaging in regular prayer, and seeking the guidance of a spiritual father. The sanctity of our faith lies in its proper practice and in living a life modeled after Christ's teachings. By embedding ourselves in the authentic teachings and life of the Church, we shield our spirits from the corruption of the world.

In light of this, how will you deepen your commitment to living a true Orthodox Christian life, thereby safeguarding your soul from all that seeks to detract from its purity and sanctity?

May the name of the Holy God be praised forever and ever, today and always.

May the blessings of the Holy Mother of God, the Virgin, and the Cross be upon us. Amen.
