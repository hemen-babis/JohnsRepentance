---Clarification of a #Bible verse

👉 Question፦ Hello, I was wondering why we are not allowed to eat #pork anything related to it. Like I’m reading acts right now and it says 
”“The voice spoke from heaven a second time, ‘Do not call anything impure that God has made clean.’“
Acts 11:9

Answer፦ Beloved in Christ,

The passage you refer to in Acts 11:9 is a profound testament to the transformative power of Christ's coming and the new covenant established through His sacrifice. In the Old Testament, certain foods, including pork, were considered impure or unclean for the Israelites, as part of the Mosaic Law given to guide them and set them apart from other nations. These laws were not just about diet but were deeply intertwined with spiritual and communal holiness.

The vision Peter received, as narrated in Acts 11, symbolizes the inclusion of the Gentiles into the Christian faith and signifies that what God has cleansed through Christ's sacrifice, no one should call unclean. This marks a pivotal shift from the ceremonial laws of the Old Testament to the spiritual and moral laws of the New Testament, emphasizing faith in Christ and love as the fulfillment of the law.

In the Orthodox tradition, emphasis is placed not on the adherence to the Old Testament dietary laws but on the spiritual practice of fasting and abstention, which serves as a tool for spiritual discipline, self-control, and communion with God. It is not the avoidance of specific foods like pork that purifies us, but our repentance, prayer, and the grace of the Holy Spirit working within us.

As Saint Paul teaches, “Therefore, if what I eat causes my brother or sister to fall into sin, I will never eat meat again, so that I will not cause them to fall.” (1 Corinthians 8:13) This reflects the Orthodox Christian understanding that our actions, including our dietary choices, should be guided by love, consideration for others, and the pursuit of spiritual growth.

In the Ethiopian Orthodox tradition, the abstention from pork is upheld, not merely as a continuation of the Old Testament dietary law, but as a part of our broader commitment to holiness and theosis - the process of becoming more like God in our thoughts, deeds, and life. Our Church Fathers, deeply rooted in the Judeo-Christian tradition and understanding the dietary habits and spiritual needs of our people, guided us towards practices that enhance our spiritual journey towards God.

How might you incorporate the principle of love and consideration for others in your daily life, reflecting the transformative love of Christ?
