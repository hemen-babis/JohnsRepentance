About #Pregnancy and #pennance 

Question፦ Can a pregnant women confess/repent? I ask that because it may be hard for her to do the prostrations that the priests usually ask us to do.

Answer፦ Answering from an Orthodox perspective, it is important to bear in mind the overarching emphasis our Church places on love, mercy, and the sanctity of human life. Pregnancy is a time of unique grace and responsibility, carrying and nurturing within oneself a life created in the divine image. It is a period where women should receive exceptional care, guidance, and protection. 

Now, as to the question of repentance during pregnancy, we must remember that God's forgiveness is not confined by physical gestures or actions, such as prostrations. Rather, true repentance begins in the heart and mind. When a person feels genuine remorse for their sins and seeks God's mercy, the journey towards reconciliation with the divine begins. Prostrations, kneeling, fasting, and the like are valuable aids on this journey, but they are not its essence. The essence is contrition and a sincere desire to turn away from sinful patterns. Thus, pregnant women, as well as all others who for one reason or another cannot perform certain acts of penance, can fully partake in the sacrament of confession and receive absolution for their sins.

If there exists any particular sin that the woman feels particularly burdened by during her pregnancy, it should not remain unspoken out of fear of physical restrictions. Our faith holds life and its preservation as paramount, and it does not demand that a pregnant woman risk her health or that of her unborn child to perform certain acts of penance. What is required is the sincerity of the heart and the genuine intention to rectify one's ways, rather than any physical demonstration of penitence.

Moreover, if any transgressions were committed before the woman discovered her pregnancy, she should bring them to light post-delivery. In the interim period, she should seek the counsel of wise and pious elders, stay constant in prayer, and try to lead a righteous life, nurturing the life within her with love and care. God, who is the ultimate source of wisdom and mercy, knows our intentions, our fears, and our struggles. 

While it is true that Orthodox canon permits certain exemptions for pregnant and nursing women, such as being excused from fasting or performing strenuous acts of prayer, it does not exclude them from spiritual engagement entirely. They are encouraged to maintain a strong prayer life and remain connected with their spiritual fathers for guidance and support.

Lastly, regarding a pregnant woman's participation in communal prayer, the Church recommends a balanced approach. While it's true that faith, not the quantity of prayers or their location, is the ultimate path to salvation, the Church also acknowledges the benefit of community and mutual prayer. A pregnant woman may pray at home, and if she chooses to partake in communal prayer, she should do so in a manner that does not compromise her health or wellbeing. After giving birth, the Church community should extend its arms in love and care, ensuring that the mother and child are enveloped in spiritual blessings and God's grace.

In sum, the Orthodox Church teaches us to prioritize the sanctity of life and the health of the mother and child during pregnancy. True repentance and reconciliation with God begins in the heart, and not in the physical acts associated with penance. While physical restrictions may prevent pregnant women from participating in some traditional acts of penance, they are not precluded from confessing their sins, seeking God's mercy, or participating in communal prayers in a manner that respects their physical condition.
