👉 About #Death and #Consolation

👉 Question: How can I cope with the loss of a loved one and be comforted by the hope of resurrection?

✍️ Answer: The first step in coping with death is understanding what it is. Ecclesiastes Chapter 7, Verse 1 says, "The day of death is better than the day of birth. The righteous are born to suffering and pain, but their death is rest from all this." Thus, we must understand that death is a rest from the hardships of life and hope that our loved ones are at peace.

We are comforted by the belief that death is a journey to God. St. Yared said, "The righteous do not die, but go to their Lord." Therefore, death is a path that leads to God.

However, there is a difference in death for the righteous and the wicked. The death of the righteous is glorious in the eyes of God, whereas the death of the wicked is not. Hence, it is crucial to reflect on the nature of the death we will experience.

The third type of death is separation from this world, from its pain and suffering, and entering a world without suffering.

St. Paul's Epistle to the Philippians from Chapter 1 verse 21-24 states, "For to me, to live is Christ and to die is gain. If I am to go on living in the body, this will mean fruitful labor for me. Yet what shall I choose? I do not know! I am torn between the two: I desire to depart and be with Christ, which is better by far; but it is more necessary for you that I remain in the body." It is natural to want to accompany a loved one on their journey to God, and we can find solace in knowing that they are in His presence.

Death is a means by which God takes his people. We are God's possessions, and He has the right to take away His possessions whenever He wants. It is beyond our control, and we must accept it with understanding.

Therefore, whether a person is righteous or a sinner, they will die. The difference lies in the outcome of their death. The righteous die to enter heaven, and the sinner dies to descend into hell. It is important to acknowledge this difference while accepting death as a natural part of life.

We can find comfort in the hope of resurrection, as Christ rose from the dead and showed us that it is possible. We say "we hope for the resurrection of the dead" in our daily prayers, and it is one of the five pillar mysteries of the Church. Christ's resurrection is an example for us, and just as He rose from the dead, we will rise again by God's authority. This is the hope we hold onto.
