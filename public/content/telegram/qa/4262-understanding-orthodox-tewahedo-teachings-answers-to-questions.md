👉🏽Understanding Orthodox Tewahedo Teachings: Answers to Questions

1. On the matter of anointment

The question arises from a misunderstanding of the nature of Christ in the Orthodox Tewahedo faith. We confess with one voice, as the Holy Fathers have taught, that the Son is both God and Man in one inseparable, unconfused, and undivided nature—One Incarnate Nature of God the Word (ተዋሕዶ).  

To say that the Son is Anointer, Anointed, and Anointment in a divided sense is to fall into the error of separation, which the Council of Chalcedon (451 A.D.) falsely propagated. The Oriental Orthodox Churches, including the Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church, reject this division.  

- The Father is the Anointer (the Source of Divinity).  
- The Son is the Anointed according to His humanity, for as man, He was anointed by the Holy Spirit at His baptism (Matthew 3:16).  
- The Holy Spirit is the Anointment, the sanctifying power that proceeds from the Father and rests upon the Son.  

Yet, we do not divide Christ into parts He is one Christ, fully divine and fully human, anointed in His humanity but Himself the eternal God who anoints through the Spirit. This is the mystery of the Incarnation, preserved in the Tewahedo (Unity) faith.  

2. On the Title "Tewahedo" Before 1950 E.C.  

The Ethiopian Orthodox Church has always been a Tewahedo Church, for the term "Tewahedo" (ተዋሕዶ) means "Unity," confessing the one united nature of Christ God and Man without separation, without confusion.  

However, the formal addition of "Tewahedo" to the name of the Ethiopian Orthodox Church occurred in 1959 E.C. (1967 A.D.), when the Ethiopian Church received autocephaly (independence) from the Coptic Patriarchate of Alexandria. Before this, the Church was under the Coptic Pope, and its official title was simply "The Ethiopian Orthodox Church."  

Yet, do not be deceived the faith was always Tewahedo, for Ethiopia has confessed St. Cyril’s Miaphysite formula since the time of St. Frumentius (Abba Selama) in the 4th century. The name change merely made explicit what was always believed.  

3. Why Does the Ethiopian Church Alone Add "Tewahedo" to Its Name?  

The Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church is not alone in its Christology the Coptic, Syriac, Armenian, and Malankara Churches all hold the same faith. However, only Ethiopia includes "Tewahedo" in its official title. Why?  

- Historical Identity: After autocephaly, Ethiopia emphasized its unique heritage the only ancient African Church that preserved Christianity without foreign domination.  
- Doctrinal Clarity: To distinguish itself from Chalcedonian ("two-nature") churches and even from other Oriental Orthodox churches that may not stress the term as prominently.  
- Liturgical Tradition: The Ge’ez term "Tewahedo" is deeply embedded in Ethiopian hymns, prayers, and theological discourse, making it natural to formalize.  

Other Oriental Orthodox Churches believe the same doctrine but may use different terminology (e.g., "Miaphysite" in academic circles). Ethiopia, however, boldly proclaims "Tewahedo" as a banner of its faith.  


Conclusion: Hold Fast to the Apostolic Faith  

Let no one deceive you with novel interpretations or historical half-truths. The Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church is the same yesterday, today, and forever holding fast to the Unity (Tewahedo) of Christ, the undivided Tradition of the Fathers, and the unshaken faith of the Apostles.  

As St. Paul says: "Keep the pattern of sound teaching you have heard from me, in the faith and love that are in Christ Jesus" (2 Timothy 1:13).
