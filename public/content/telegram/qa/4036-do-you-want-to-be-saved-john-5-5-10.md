👉🏽👉🏽👉🏽Do You #Want to #Be #Saved? (John 5:5-10)

Beloved brethren in Christ, let us open our minds and hearts to the depths of divine wisdom that the Gospel bestows upon us. The passage from John 5:5-10 is not merely an account of a miraculous healing but a profound revelation of God's mercy, human endurance, and the necessity of faith.

The Long-Suffering of the Paralytic: A Testament to Perseverance

Behold with the eyes of your soul the man who lay bedridden for thirty-eight years! His life was not one of comfort but of suffering, yet he endured, waiting for the mercy of God. Day and night, under the scorching sun and the biting frost, he remained at the edge of the water, hoping for healing. He had no friend, no kin, no helping hand—only the bitter companionship of affliction.

How unlike this man are we today! We tremble at the first sign of hardship, faint under the weight of the smallest burden, and abandon hope if our prayers are not answered immediately. The iron bed upon which this man lay for thirty-eight years was not just a bed but a throne of patience. As iron attracts frost, so too does suffering attract despair—but he did not yield. What a lesson for us, who are so quick to abandon hope!

The Church: The Gate of Mercy and the Test of Love

The church is the door of forgiveness and the fountain of mercy, and yet do we not see many poor souls waiting for our help, stretching out their hands in silent supplication? Our Lord Himself warns us, saying:

"I was hungry and you did not feed me, I was sick and you did not visit me…" (Matthew 25:31-46)

Where is our love for Christ? Have we not read that almsgiving is love itself? Saint John Afework teaches that to give alms is to give love. If our hands are empty of charity, our hearts are empty of Christ. For the Lord is not found in temples made of stone, but in the broken hearts of the poor, the sick, and the suffering. If we seek Christ, let us seek Him in love.

The Stirring of the Waters: The Shadow of Salvation

The man waited for the stirring of the waters, for the angel to descend and grant healing. But what is this stirring if not the foreshadowing of Christ Himself? The angel's descent prefigures the coming of the Savior, for no mere movement of water can cleanse the soul. When the man lamented that he had no one to carry him, was he not echoing the sorrow of Adam, who longed for redemption?

But now, the Good Shepherd enters through the sheep gate! The One who was promised has come! And He asks:

"Do you want to be healed?"

What a question! Would not any sufferer cry out, “Yes, Lord!”? And yet, our Lord does not force salvation upon anyone. He respects the freedom of the soul. He does not command but invites, does not coerce but offers. Had He healed without asking, the man might have later said, “I did not seek healing, I did not consent.” Thus, even in His mercy, Christ upholds our free will.

The Miracle of Obedience: Rise, Take Up Your Bed, and Walk

At the command of the Lord, the man who had lain in suffering for thirty-eight years stood up. He did not hesitate, did not question. He obeyed, and in his obedience was healed. And what of us? Are we not also sick? Not in body, perhaps, but in soul?

Are our eyes not blind to the suffering of others? Are our ears not deaf to the call of the church? Have we not grown paralyzed in sin, unable to rise and walk in the path of righteousness? The healing of the paralytic is not just a miracle of the past but a call to us today: “Do you want to be saved?”

If we answer “Yes, Lord,” then let us not delay. Let us enter the house of God, the gate of mercy, the dwelling place of forgiveness. Let us not turn away in pride, nor remain in the sickness of our sins.

The Bread of Life: The True Healing of the Soul

Christ did not merely heal bodies—He came to heal souls. And where is this healing found? In the Holy Church, where we partake of the true medicine of life: His Body and Blood.
