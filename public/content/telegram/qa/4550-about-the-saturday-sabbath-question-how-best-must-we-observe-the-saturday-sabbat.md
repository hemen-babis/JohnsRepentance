About The #Saturday #Sabbath 

Question፦ How best must we observe the Saturday sabbath? Are we to observe it in the old testament way or not? I'm not asking about the Sunday sabbath. I think this is much more clear to understand. My question is directly addressing the Saturday sabbath since there are many grey areas about what one should be doing on that day

Answer፦ Peace be upon you, my child.  Your question regarding the Sabbath is a good one, and one that has been pondered by the faithful for centuries. It reveals a heart seeking to understand God's will more fully.

The Sabbath, as established in the Old Testament (Genesis 2:2-3), is indeed a day of rest, sanctified by God Himself.  While we, as Orthodox Christians, now primarily celebrate the Resurrection of our Lord on the first day of the week (Sunday), the Saturday Sabbath remains a day worthy of reverence.  We understand this not as a rigid adherence to the Mosaic Law, but as a continuation of the spiritual principle of setting aside time for God.

The Church Fathers, enlightened by the Holy Spirit, teach us that the true Sabbath rest is not merely a cessation of physical labor, but a turning towards God. St. John Chrysostom, whose eloquence I strive to emulate, reminds us, "The Sabbath was not made for idleness, but for spiritual activity."  Thus, while abstaining from unnecessary work is commendable, the emphasis should be on prayer, reflection, and acts of charity.

Consider the words of Isaiah (Isaiah 58:13-14):  “If thou turn away thy foot from the sabbath, from doing thy pleasure on my holy day; and call the sabbath a delight, the holy of the Lord, honourable; and shalt honour him, not doing thine own ways, nor finding thine own pleasure, nor speaking thine own words: Then shalt thou delight thyself in the Lord; and I will cause thee to ride upon the high places of the earth, and feed thee with the heritage of Jacob thy father: for the mouth of the Lord hath spoken it.”

This passage illuminates the true spirit of the Sabbath – a dedication to God, not a burden of restrictions. We honor Him not simply by refraining from work, but by actively seeking His presence through prayer, study of Scripture (especially the Septuagint), attending Divine Liturgy if available, and acts of love and service.

Therefore, my child, I would encourage you not to become overly concerned with specific regulations regarding the Saturday Sabbath.  Instead, focus on dedicating this day, as much as possible, to drawing closer to God.  Engage in practices that nourish your spirit and bring you peace.  

Fasting, if your health permits, can be a powerful tool for spiritual growth, and almsgiving, even a small offering, can open your heart to God’s grace.

Would you like me to share some specific prayers or passages from the Holy Scriptures that might be helpful for your Sabbath observance?  Perhaps the Psalms of David, or selections from the Wisdom of Solomon?

Remember, my child, the journey of faith is a personal one.  While I can offer guidance, true discernment comes through prayer and communion with God.  Seek out the counsel of your spiritual father or a trusted elder within the Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church for deeper guidance.  I will check in with you again soon to see how your spiritual journey is progressing. May God bless you abundantly.
