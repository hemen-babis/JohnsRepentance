👉🏾👉🏾👉🏾How is the #Time System Defined in #Ethiopia?

 (Part 5)

Brethren, time itself is the servant of God. The sun and the moon were set for seasons, days, and years, and Ethiopia, by divine grace, has preserved a calendar that begins in light, not in darkness. Each year opens on Sunday, the first day of creation, when God said: “Let there be light.” Thus the Ethiopian system is not mere calculation, but a witness to the order of salvation.

The Order of the Months

In this sacred reckoning, the months of the year are aligned not by chance, but by a divine harmony:

Meskerem 1 is the beginning of Miyazya 1.

Tikimt 1 is the beginning of Ginbot 1.

Hidar 1 is the beginning of Sene 1.

Tahisas 1 is the beginning of Hamle 1.

Tir 1 is the beginning of Nehase 1.

“Thus, the year itself begins where creation began on a Sunday. Yet the presence of Pagume 5 prevents Sunday from always being the first day of the new year.”.

The Mystery of Pagume

At the close of the year stands the month of Pagumen, which seals the year and prepares the way for the new.

Pagumen 5 is called Mriya—“the chosen day,” the day of medicine, light, and birth. It is not used to alter the year’s order but is set apart as holy. For example, if Meskerem 1 fell on Wednesday in 2017, it fell on Thursday in 2018.

This day is bound to the mystery of the Lord’s Nativity. The 5th of Pagumen always corresponds with the day of the coming Christmas. Thus, if Pagumen 5 was on a Wednesday, then Christmas also fell on Wednesday. For this reason the day is also named the Feast of the Nativity of the Savior.


When the year is a leap year, Pagumen becomes six days, and the sixth day is called the Feast of the Nativity of John, the day of crossing over. Why is this so? Because the fifteen kal’its—fractions of time—accumulate year by year, and in four years they form one day. Hence, every four years a sixth day is added.

But what of the remaining kal’its? For one day is made of 3600 kal’its, requiring 600 years to complete. At that time, Pagumen itself becomes seven days, revealing the fullness of creation, for seven is the number of perfection.

The Harmony of the Feasts

“But in the time of John, the sixth day of Pagumen caused the Feast of the Nativity to be celebrated one day earlier, on Tahisas 28. Some may ask, why then do Baptism, Circumcision, and other feasts remain unchanged? The scholars answer: because generations have always counted the weeks in expectation of the Nativity; and since the other feasts flow from His birth, they follow their order without alteration

The Difference from the Gregorian Calendar

In the Gregorian calendar, the leap year is hidden in February, after their new year has already begun. But in Ethiopia, the extra day is revealed openly at the year’s end, before the dawning of the new year. Thus, our reckoning is not cloaked in secrecy but shines with light, testifying to God’s order.
