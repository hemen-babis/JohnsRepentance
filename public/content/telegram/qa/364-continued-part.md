Therefore, what we learn from this is that it is God who taught, placed and appointed these fathers among us to tell us the truth, teach us, and advise us. The penitent fathers are also responsible and accountable for the souls of every person under their fatherhood. God said through the prophet Ezekiel "When I say to the wicked, ‘You shall surely die,’ and you give him no warning, nor speak to warn the wicked from his wicked way, to save his life, that same wicked man shall die in his iniquity; but his blood I will require at your hand." Which is a strong reminder of this serious responsibility. This is why the Apostle St. Paul said, "Obey your superiors, be submissive; As if they were giving a reckoning, so that they would do this not with sorrow but with joy...strive for your souls" (Hebrews 13:17). These are the fathers of penitence who are said to give account and fight for your soul.

Therefore, our questioner should understand it in this regard. Also, a quote from the Bible? For those who asked, we recommend that you read and understand what we have listed below.

- St. John the Baptist was baptizing the people with a baptism of repentance that prepares them for Christ. At that time, the people would come to him and confess their sins to him before being baptized in the baptism of repentance. When the Holy Bible talks about this, it says: "At that time all Jerusalem and Judea and all the country around the Jordan would go up to him and be baptized by him in the Jordan River, confessing their sins" (Matthew 3:5-6). Therefore, just as the people used to confess their sins to John the Baptist when they repented so that they could be baptized in the baptism of repentance, it means that the faithful confess their sins to their penitent fathers before they approach the holy mysteries. Also, just as John gave them advice and rebuke, the fathers of penitence will give advice and canon that they think is appropriate based on the life and circumstances of the deceased. Matthew 3:8-10

- When we look at Achan's story, in Isaiah 7:21-23, Joshua said to Achan: My son, give glory to God, the God of Israel, confess to Him, and tell me what you have done, don't hide from me. And Achan answered Joshua and said, "I have indeed sinned against the God of Israel, I have done such and such" (7:21-23). Joshua, give Achan the glory of God, confess; Just as he says, "Tell me, don't hide from me, what a person confesses to his penitent father is part of his confession to God." When Achan confessed to Joshua, he was confessing to God. Therefore, when he confesses to his penitent father, he makes God the judge, and the penitent believes that his father will forgive his sins and be forgiven according to the authority given by God, and reconcile him to his creator.

In the New Testament, when a man with leprosy approached him and asked, "Lord, if you want to, you can cleanse me," bowing down; then the Lord stretched out his hand and touched him and said, "I want to cleanse you. Be Cleansed." And he was free from leprosy. After that, our Lord immediately ordered the leper whom he had just cleansed to say: " Be careful not to tell anyone, but go and show yourself to the priest; And as a witness to them, offer him the offering that Moses commanded." Matthew 8:2-4 The surprising thing is that, even though our Lord healed the leper himself, (although there was nothing else that was needed more in our Lord's healing), but our Lord himself  has ordered the person by saying :- "Go, and show yourself to the priest,”
