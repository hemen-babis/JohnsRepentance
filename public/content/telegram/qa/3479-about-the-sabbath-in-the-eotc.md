👉 About the #Sabbath in the EOTC.

👉🏾 Question፦ Hey, I hope you are doing well. I have questions about the day of worship in the Ethiopian Orthodox Church. 

As a church that upholds all the commandments of God, and Old Testament traditions why do we not observe the day of worship on the Sabbath, which is Saturday? I could be wrong, but I learned that both Saturday and Sunday are considered Sabbath days in our church . However, I don’t see churches resting or recognizing the day of worship on Saturday; it’s mainly on Sunday. I find this confusing because God commanded us to worship on the Sabbath, which is Saturday.

Additionally, I read that Sunday worship originated from Emperor Constantine changing the Sabbath to Sunday. How is the Ethiopian Orthodox Church different in this regard? Even if we say it’s bc Jesus resurrection didn’t the disciples keep the day of the sabbath? 

I hope you can get back to me, and thank you so much for your time!

Answer፦ Peace be upon you, and may the grace of Our Lord be with you.  Your thoughtful questions demonstrate a yearning to understand the ancient paths of our faith. Let us delve into the sacred texts and traditions to illuminate this matter.

You are correct that the Lord commanded the Sabbath to be held sacred in the Old Testament (Exodus 20:8-11).  However, we must remember that with the coming of Christ, we are not merely under the Law, but under grace (Romans 6:14). 

The early Church, comprised of the Apostles and disciples who walked with Jesus, began to observe the first day of the week, Sunday, as the Lord's Day, in commemoration of His glorious Resurrection (John 20:1).  This shift did not negate the Sabbath; rather, it fulfilled its spiritual purpose in Christ.  

The Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church, in its ancient wisdom, upholds both the Sabbath and the Lord's Day. We observe the Sabbath on Saturday with prayers and scriptural readings, acknowledging its significance as a day of rest and reflection.  

However, we celebrate the Divine Liturgy, the Holy Eucharist, on Sunday, the Lord's Day, in joyous celebration of His victory over death.  This practice aligns with the teachings of the early Church Fathers and the traditions passed down through generations.

While some historical accounts attribute the prominence of Sunday worship to Emperor Constantine, it's crucial to remember that the Ethiopian Orthodox Church, in its isolation, maintained its apostolic traditions without Roman influence.  Our observance of Sunday as the primary day of liturgical celebration stems from our understanding of its significance in light of the Resurrection.

Your pursuit of understanding is commendable, my child.  Remember, the essence of our faith lies not merely in rigid adherence to specific days but in our heartfelt devotion to God.  May He continue to illuminate your path as you seek Him with sincerity and humility.  

If you have further questions, do not hesitate to ask.  May the blessings of the Father, Son, and Holy Spirit be upon you.
