---About the feast of the #Transfiguration 

👉 Question፦ Why does God call Moses and Elijah apart from the prophets for his transfiguration on Mt. Tabor?

Answer፦ Indeed, the event on Mount Tabor, the Transfiguration of our Lord Jesus Christ, is one replete with divine mysteries and profound theological insights. The very essence of this event speaks volumes about the nature of Christ and His mission on Earth. Yet, the presence of Moses and Elijah alongside Christ is no mere happenstance. It carries with it a theological weight that must be understood in the light of both the Old and New Testaments.

Let us begin with Moses and Elijah: two towering figures in the narrative of God’s relationship with humanity. Moses, the lawgiver, conversed with the Almighty and brought forth the Ten Commandments from Sinai. His presence on Mount Tabor is an affirmation that Jesus is the fulfillment of the Law. In the book of Exodus, Moses, yearning to see the full glory of God, was told, “You cannot see my face, for no one can see me and live” (Exodus 33:20). Yet, on Mount Tabor, Moses stands beside the Incarnate God, witnessing His transfigured glory—a testament to the promises of God being fulfilled.

Elijah, on the other hand, represents the prophets. This fiery prophet, who was taken up into heaven in a whirlwind, represents the prophetic tradition that pointed towards the coming of the Messiah. His presence confirms Jesus as the long-awaited Messiah, the fulfillment of the prophetic utterances.

The presence of both Moses and Elijah beside Jesus serves as an irrefutable testimony to the Jews. They encompass the entirety of the Old Testament – the Law and the Prophets. Their presence proclaims that Jesus is neither a mere prophet nor just another wise teacher. He is the Messiah, the fulfillment of all that was promised.

Saint Peter’s exclamation, “Lord, it is good for us to be here,” is a testament to the profound realization of the divine mystery unfolding before them. The mountain, in its elevated state, is reminiscent of the Church, lifting us closer to the heavens and the divine. To reside in such a place, to dwell with God, is indeed the highest of all desires, echoing the sentiments of Prophet David in his psalm.

Moreover, when the masses stumbled at the teachings of Jesus, the apostles remained, for they recognized in Him the words of eternal life. The Transfiguration reaffirmed this belief, showing them a glimpse of the divine glory of Jesus, strengthening their faith in the face of impending trials.

To be in the presence of God, to witness His majesty and glory, is indeed the highest of all blessings. The Transfiguration serves as a beacon, a luminous event that calls us to recognize the true nature of Christ and to anchor our faith in Him. For in Him, the Law and the Prophets find their fulfillment, and in His light, we find our true purpose and calling.

May the name of the Holy God be praised forever and ever in every language that He has created, today and always.

May the blessings of the Holy Mother of God, the Virgin, and the Cross be upon us. Amen.
