👉🏾👉🏾👉🏾A Homily on #Saint #John the Baptist

(Part 2)

O beloved brethren, what shall we say of this wondrous man? Shall we call him prophet? He is more than a prophet. Shall we name him apostle? He is greater still. Shall we call him martyr? He is crowned with blood. Saint John the Baptist is the burning lamp that shone in the wilderness, the voice that shook the desert, the bridge from the shadow of the Law to the dawn of Grace.

He was the finger pointing to the Sun, the herald crying: “Prepare the way of the Lord, make straight His paths!” He sought no honor for himself, but with every word and deed directed the eyes of men to Christ, the Lamb of God who takes away the sin of the world.

I. The Ministry and Testimony of John

John was sent not to glorify himself, but to point with a burning finger toward the Christ. With humility he declared: “I baptize you with water for repentance, but He who comes after me is mightier than I, the latchet of whose sandals I am not worthy to untie. He will baptize you with the Holy Spirit and with fire.”

Here we behold his humility. Though multitudes came to him, he would not accept their reverence as though he were the Messiah. Rather, he proclaimed, “Behold, the Lamb of God who takes away the sin of the world.” This was no common word, but the greatest testimony ever uttered by human lips, for in those words John revealed both the incarnation and the divinity of our Lord Jesus Christ.

When the Pharisees questioned him “What do you say about yourself?” he confessed without hesitation: “I am not the Christ. I am the voice of one crying in the wilderness: Make straight the way of the Lord.” See his faithfulness! He refuses glory for himself, but labors only that Christ may be known.

II.His Encounter with the Lord

When the Virgin Mary came to Elizabeth, the mother of John, she exclaimed with holy awe: “How is it that the mother of my Lord should come to me?” Thus, even before his birth, John bore witness to the Lord, leaping in the womb at the sound of Mary’s voice.

When our Lord came to be baptized by him, Saint John humbly acknowledged Christ’s divinity, saying: “I need to be baptized by You, and do You come to me? How can the Lord come to His servant, while the servant goes to his Master?” (Matthew 3:14).

When Saint John said to our Lord, “I baptize others in Your name, but in whose name shall I baptize You?” the Lord answered: “You are a priest forever, according to the order of Melchizedek; You are the Lamb of God who takes away the sins of the world; You are the Light of God, the Son of the Father. Jesus Christ, have mercy on us.”

In obedience, John did as he was commanded and baptized our Lord on Tir 11.

And when the Lord consented to be baptized, the heavens opened, the Spirit descended like a dove, and the Father’s voice thundered: “This is My beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased.” In that moment the mystery of the Holy Trinity was revealed to the world through the ministry of John.

5. His Martyrdom

John, who had baptized Christ, was not spared the cup of suffering. At thirty years of age, he was called to bear witness with his own blood. Because he reproved Herod for his unlawful union with Herodias, he was cast into prison.

During a feast, Herodias’ daughter danced and pleased the king. Bound by his drunken oath, Herod commanded John’s beheading. O tragic folly! Not out of justice, not out of grief, but for the sake of a banquet, a dance, and a rash promise, the righteous man was slain. Truly, where there is drunkenness and singing, there is the snare of demons; where there is feasting without God, there the wrath of God is provoked.

Yet John did not resist. By the Spirit he knew the hour of his death. He bowed his head to the executioner, yielding it as though laying it upon the altar. Thus, he received the crown of martyrdom. The Church remembers his beheading on the second of Meskerem.
