III. The Divine Condescension: The Word Made Flesh  

Beloved, the Son of God, who is co-eternal with the Father, "emptied Himself, taking the form of a servant" (Phil. 2:7). He did not cease to be God, yet He became perfect man, uniting two natures in one person without confusion.  

St. Cyril teaches us: "The Word became flesh, not by changing His divinity into flesh, but by assuming humanity into His divine person." He who fashioned Adam from the dust now takes Adam’s nature upon Himself to redeem him.  

Why did He do this?  

- To pay the debt of sin, as St. Athanasius declares.  

- To destroy death by death, trampling down the devil’s power.  

- To open Paradise again, as He
promised the thief.

Behold the mystery! The Second Adam undoes the curse of the first. 

"Through one man, Adam, death entered the world; through one Man, Christ, life is restored!" (Rom. 5:12-21).  

IV. The Song of the Angels and the Triumph Over Hell  

When Christ was born, the angels sang, "Glory to God in the highest, and on earth peace!" (Luke 2:14). Why? Because:  

- Sin was destroyed—the devil’s tyranny was broken.  

- The debt was canceled—the handwriting of our transgressions nailed to the Cross (Col. 2:14). 

- Hell was plundered—for Christ descended and led captivity captive (Eph. 4:8).  

The devil, who once deceived Adam with a fruit, is now crushed by the wood of the Cross. The stone of the tomb could not hold the Author of Life!  

V. Let Us Give Thanks and Imitate the Theotokos  

Therefore, brethren, let us:  

1. Repent like Adam, who wept for his sins.
  
2. Embrace purity like the Virgin Mary, keeping ourselves unspotted from the world.
  
3. Rejoice in redemption, for where sin abounded, grace abounded much more (Rom. 5:20).  

O Most Holy Theotokos, pray for us, that we may not lose the grace of your Son, who freed Adam from bondage!  

May the name of the Holy God be praised forever and ever in every tongue that He has created, today and always. 

May the blessings of God's mother, the virgin, and the cross be upon us.
Amen.
