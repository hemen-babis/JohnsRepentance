👉🏾👉🏾👉🏾(#Anqetse #bitsuan) #Blessed are the #meek, for they shall #inherit the #earth"
(Matthew 5:5 )

( Part 11)


Dear followers of the Orthodox faith, we greet you with the peace and grace of God. Today, we share with you an important lesson on the virtue of meekness, based on the Holy Scriptures May you read it with understanding and apply it with humility in your lives.

✍🏾The Great Prophet Moses – The Meekest Man on Earth

One of the greatest examples of meekness in the Old Testament is the High Prophet Moses. The Holy Scripture testifies of him, saying,

“Now Moses was very meek, above all the men who were on the face of the earth.”(Numbers 12:3)

Although Moses was born among the Israelites and raised in Pharaoh’s palace, he did not boast of his royal upbringing. When God called him to deliver His people from bondage, Moses humbly replied,

“Who am I that I should go to Pharaoh and bring the children of Israel out of Egypt?”
(Exodus 3:11)

This reveals the true heart of meekness seeing oneself as a servant in the great plan of God, not seeking honor but obedience. In this spirit, Moses led Israel before the Lord with faith and humility.

When the people of Israel sinned and provoked God’s wrath by making a golden calf, Moses interceded for them with compassion, saying,

“Alas, this people has sinned a great sin and made for themselves gods of gold. Yet now, if You will forgive their sin but if not, blot me out of Your book which You have written.”
(Exodus 32:31-32)

In this, Moses showed his deep meekness and self-sacrificing love. He pleaded for their forgiveness rather than seeking his own glory. Because of his humble intercession, God’s mercy prevailed, and His grace delivered Israel from destruction and suffering.

The meekness of Moses teaches every believer that true greatness is found in humility before God. The one who bows low in spirit rises high in grace.

✍🏾Ruth – The Humble Servant of God

Among the women of the Old Testament, Ruth 1 out as a true example1 of meekness, faithfulness, and devotion. Born and raised in the land of Moab, she was known for her integrity and gentle spirit. When her mother-in-law Naomi urged her to return to her own people after the death of her husband, Ruth answered with deep humility and unwavering loyalty:

“Do not urge me to leave you or to return from following you;
for wherever you go, I will go, and where you lodge, I will lodge.
Your people shall be my people, and your God my God.
Where you die, I will die, and there I will be buried.
May the Lord do so to me, and more also, if anything but death parts you and me.”
(Ruth 1:16–17)

In her meekness, Ruth left behind her homeland, family, and comfort to follow the path of faith. God, who exalts the humble, rewarded her with Boaz, a righteous and gentle man, as her husband (Ruth 3:9). From their union came Obed, the father of Jesse, and the grandfather of King David.

Thus, by her humility and steadfast love, Ruth was honored to become part of the holy genealogy of our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ, as recorded in the Gospel of Matthew (1:5).
