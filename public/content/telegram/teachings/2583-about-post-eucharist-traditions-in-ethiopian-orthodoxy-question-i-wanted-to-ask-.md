About post #Eucharist #Traditions in Ethiopian Orthodoxy

Question፦ I wanted to ask another culture-centered question if you don't mind. How come I hear a huge emphasis on what the faithful should do after taking the Eucharist? It's so emphasized I have had a priest tell me it's a sin for me to break these rules that requires confession

Example of Rules:
1. Don't touch water
2. Don't spit
3. You cannot sweat or play
4. You cannot cry
etc.

Answer፦ In the Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church, the guidelines and traditions following the reception of the Holy Eucharist are deeply embedded in our liturgical and spiritual life, emphasizing the sanctity of the sacrament.

When an Ethiopian Orthodox Christian partakes of the Holy Eucharist, it is believed that they receive Christ Himself. Given the profound holiness of this act, the faithful are taught to remain in a state of purity post-communion as long as the Body and Blood of Christ are present within them. The guidelines you mentioned—such as not touching water, not spitting, avoiding physical exertion, and not crying—are cultural practices specific to Ethiopian Orthodoxy, designed to honor the presence of Christ within us and to avoid any act that might seem to expel or disrespect the Holy Gifts.

These practices are not ends in themselves but are disciplines that help the faithful to maintain a prayerful and contemplative state after receiving the Eucharist. They serve to cultivate a deep, ongoing awareness of God's immediate presence and to extend the sacredness of the liturgical experience into daily life. As St. John Chrysostom teaches, "Let us return from that Table like lions breathing out fire, having become terrible to the devil, thinking of our Head [Christ] and the love He has shown for us... This Blood makes the image of our King to be freshly painted in us, produces beauty unspeakable, permits not the beauty to fade, but refreshes it continually, when it is about to be destroyed."

However, it's crucial to understand that the Church's canonical law does not specifically prescribe these as absolute requirements; rather, they are traditions that have been upheld by the faithful over centuries. It is essential for those who find these practices burdensome or confusing to speak with their spiritual father. A spiritual guide can provide personalized guidance and help distinguish between essential ecclesiastical mandates and beneficial cultural traditions.

Given your interest in these practices, might I suggest further exploring how they might enhance your reverence and love for the Eucharist, guiding you to a deeper communion with our Lord Jesus Christ? What ways do you feel called to honor this sacred gift in your daily walk with Christ?
