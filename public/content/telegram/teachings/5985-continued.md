5. The Priesthood in the Holy Liturgy

✍🏾Just as the gathering of the faithful reveals the Body of Christ, the priest who presides over the Holy Liturgy reveals the Priesthood of Christ, who is the Head of the Church:

👉🏾“Christ is the Savior of the body, the Church.”(Ephesians 5:23)

✍🏾The priest serves by grace, bearing witness to the eternal priesthood of Christ. His vestments are not personal decorations; they manifest the Church as the dwelling place of Christ.

For this reason, according to the Church’s order, the priest must not wear his liturgical vestments before the faithful gather. The Liturgy is not an individual act; it belongs to the whole Body.

The crown worn by the priest testifies to the eternal priesthood of Christ:

👉🏾“You are a priest forever according to the order of Melchizedek.”(Psalm 109:4; Hebrews 7:1–22)

✍🏾In this way, the eternal priesthood of our Lord is manifested among the faithful in His eternal and heavenly sanctuary, the Holy Church. When we gather together, Christ Himself is in our midst. Therefore, our gathering in the Holy Liturgy is truly great, not because of our number or worthiness, but because our Savior, Jesus Christ, is present among us.

6. Preparation for the Holy Liturgy

If Abraham was commanded to leave his land and his father’s house in obedience to God, how much more should we leave behind worldly desires when we ascend to Mount Zion, the assembly of the firstborn?(Genesis 12:1; Hebrews 12:22–24)

✍🏾 If the people of Israel prepared for three days before the revelation of God on Mount Sinai, washing their clothes and sanctifying themselves, how much more should we prepare to meet Christ Himself, who was crucified for us?

Our preparation must go beyond clean clothes. We must cleanse our hearts, bodies, flesh, and blood through repentance.
(Exodus 19:14–15)

✍🏾Our Response During the Liturgy

✍🏾When the child Samuel heard the voice of God in the temple, he responded with readiness and obedience. Likewise, we who are gathered in the true Temple, the Holy Church, must respond attentively to God’s voice spoken through Scripture, prayers, hymns, and mysteries during the Holy Liturgy.(1 Samuel 3:1–14)
