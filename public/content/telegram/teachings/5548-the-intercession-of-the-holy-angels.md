(Part 13)

Dear followers of the Orthodox teachings ,we greet you with the peace and blessings of our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ. Today, based on the above-mentioned topic, we present to you this important spiritual lesson. We ask all of you to read carefully and learn from it with humility and faith.

✍🏾 “I will open my mouth in a parable.” (Psalm 77:2)

To fulfill this prophecy, our Lord and God Jesus Christ taught many things in parables during His earthly life. Among these parables is one that reveals the mystery of the intercession of the holy angels.

✍🏾 The Parable of the Barren Fig Tree

“A man had a fig tree planted in his vineyard, and he came seeking fruit on it and found none.
He said to the vinedresser, ‘Behold, for three years I have come seeking fruit on this fig tree and find none. Cut it down; why should it use up the ground?’

✍🏾The vinedresser answered to his master, “Let it alone this year also.” This reveals an act of intercession. The master could have demanded simple obedience, saying, “I hired you to obey, not to plead.” Yet the worker’s words were a petition, a plea made not for himself but for the fruitless fig tree. What is remarkable is that the fig tree itself did not beg for mercy; it remained silent. The worker, moved by compassion, spoke on its behalf.

If the worker interceded freely, out of his own kindness and will, how much greater would his plea have been? Even a person who is not accustomed to ask for mercy can still do so when love compels them. In the same way, this worker’s humble request shows the depth of mercy that dwells in those who serve the Lord.

The master’s response is even more revealing. He did not say, “I hired you to obey,” but rather allowed the worker’s request and accepted it. This shows that God is the Lord who welcomes supplication and rejoices in intercession. He is not a tyrant who delights in punishment, but a Father who listens when His servants plead for mercy.

✍🏾 The Interpretation of the Parable

The Lord (owner) of the fig tree represents God the Creator.

The fig tree represents first the people of Israel, and by extension, all of humanity.

The worker represents the guardian angel appointed to protect and intercede for each person.

The fruit expected from the fig tree symbolizes the good faith, repentance, and righteous works that the Creator desires from every soul.

The three years during which the owner came seeking fruit represent the three main stages of human life in which God expects spiritual fruit:

👉🏾 Childhood, 

👉🏾Youth,

👉🏾 Adulthood,

The command, “Cut it down,” signifies the divine judgment that comes upon the soul that remains fruitless and unrepentant. Yet the worker’s plea shows the intercession of angels, who beg mercy on behalf of humanity.

Our Lord Himself teaches through this parable that the angels continually pray for us, just as the vinedresser pleaded for the fig tree. Scripture confirms this truth, saying:

“Behold, a watcher, a holy one, came down from heaven.”
(Daniel 4:13, 23)

and again:

“He will command His angels concerning you, to guard you in all your ways.”(Psalm 91:11)

Each word of this parable holds deep spiritual meaning. But since our subject is the intercession of angels, let us focus on this mystery alone.
