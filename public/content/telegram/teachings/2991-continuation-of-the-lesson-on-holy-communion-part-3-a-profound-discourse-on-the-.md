Continuation of the lesson on Holy #Communion (Part 3) A Profound Discourse on the Holy #Eucharist

Beloved brethren in Christ, who adhere faithfully to the Orthodox teachings, I extend to you divine greetings in the love and grace of our Lord. As we gather to contemplate the sublime mystery of the Holy Eucharist, let us delve deeply into its divine essence and the comportment it demands from us, its humble recipients.

The Sacrament of Holy Communion: A Divine Mystery

The Holy Eucharist stands as a profound mystery, instituted by Christ Himself, to unite our frail human nature with His divine grace. This sacred act is not merely a ritual but a transformative encounter with Christ, where bread and wine are transubstantiated into His true Body and Blood. As we approach this holy sacrament, let us cleanse ourselves as the temple of God, with hearts contrite and spirits humbled, much like the Roman centurion who recognized his unworthiness before the Lord (Matthew 8:8). This profound humility must frame our approach, acknowledging our unworthiness yet receiving God’s mercy.

Preparation for Receiving the Eucharist

Our preparation for this holy communion must be thorough, engaging not just the body but also the spirit. The Church, in her wisdom, has bequeathed to us prayers that elevate the mind, detaching it from the carnal and anchoring it in the spiritual, thus preparing us to receive the sacred mysteries worthily.

In the ritualistic reception, order and reverence govern the distribution: from the highest clergy down to the lay faithful, each according to their ordained role, reflecting the cosmic order Christ Himself established. Children, men, and women each receive in their turn, ensuring that the sanctity of the sacrament is maintained, and the Body and Blood of Christ are received with due reverence.

Post-Communion Conduct

After partaking of the Holy Communion, our conduct must reflect the sanctity of the experience. The Church is likened to a bridal chamber where the mystical union between Christ (the Bridegroom) and the Church (His Bride) is consummated. Just as a bride adorns herself for her bridegroom, we too must remain in a state of spiritual purity, eschewing sin and worldly distractions. Our actions post-communion should reflect this sanctity; avoiding quarrels, excessive indulgence, and any activity that might desecrate the newly received grace.

The Church enjoins us to spend the remainder of the day in peace and contemplation, avoiding any mundane tasks that might detract from the sanctity of the sacrament. Such discipline helps in retaining the grace received and in fostering a deeper communion with Christ.

Theological Understanding and Differences

The Orthodox Tewahedo Church holds a distinct belief in the mystery of the Eucharist. Unlike other Christian traditions that may view the elements of communion symbolically or as a spiritual presence, we affirm that in the Eucharist, the bread and wine are truly and substantially transformed into the Body and Blood of Christ. This change, a divine mystery unfathomable to the human intellect, unites us physically and spiritually with Christ.

The Eucharistic Elements: Separately Consecrated

In adherence to Christ’s Last Supper, the Orthodox Church consecrates the bread and wine separately, reflecting His commands to eat His Body and drink His Blood—each element distinct yet united in the sacrament. This separation underscores the profound reality of the Eucharist and respects the specific instructions given by Christ Himself.

Memorial and Reality

Contrary to the assertions of some who reduce the Eucharist to a mere memorial, the Orthodox Church firmly holds that the Eucharist is not just a commemoration but an actual participation in the Body and Blood of Christ. This is not merely symbolic but a real and substantial presence that binds us to Christ and to one another in the most intimate union possible in this life.
