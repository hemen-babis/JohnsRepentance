4. Christ Descended Into Hell to Raise the Dead

What of the souls before the resurrection were they forgotten? No! The Gospel declares: “By Him He went and preached to the spirits in prison” (1 Pet. 3:19). The flesh of the Lord lay in the tomb, but His Spirit stormed the gates of Sheol. He did not merely speak He preached freedom to those bound in chains. Saint Peter declares: “God raised Him from the dead, having loosed the pains of death, for it was not possible that He should be held by it” (Acts 2:24).

Saint Ephraim, that fragrant harp of the Holy Spirit, teaches: “By His precious blood He saved those who were in the world, both in body and soul.” This is not mere metaphor; it is the mystery of divine compassion.

5. The Resurrection Restores Sonship

When the Risen Lord stood among His disciples despite the doors being locked He spoke peace, not judgment. He showed them His wounds, not to shame them, but to heal their disbelief. Their fear turned to joy. Their shame to confidence. Their orphanhood to sonship.

“As the Father has sent Me, I also send you… Receive the Holy Spirit” (John 20:21-22). Here the Lord breathes once more into man, just as He did to Adam in Genesis 2:7. But now it is no longer the breath of dust-life but of Spirit-life. The second Adam restores what the first had lost.

In eating the forbidden fig, Adam died. In tasting the bitter gall of the cross, Christ gave life. What was lost in the garden was regained at Golgotha. The fig tree destroyed childhood; the Tree of the Cross restored it.

6. The Thief: The First to Be Restored

Let none think that this gift of resurrection was for apostles only. The good thief, crucified beside Christ, became the firstfruits of redemption. When he cried, “Remember me…”, Christ did not delay. He replied, “Today you will be with Me in paradise” (Luke 23:43). In that hour, Christ granted what Adam lost a return to Eden, a return to sonship.

7. Scriptural Confirmation of the New Baptism by Christ’s Blood

Let no soul be deceived the power of this baptism is in the blood of Christ, not in water alone. The apostles became children of God not at the Jordan, but at Calvary and the empty tomb. Hear now the Scriptures:

“He purchased the Church with His own blood” (Acts 20:28)

“We have been sanctified through the offering of the body of Jesus Christ once for all” (Heb. 10:10)

“Redeemed… with the precious blood of Christ” (1 Pet. 1:18-19)

“He washed us from our sins in His own blood” (Rev. 1:5)

“You were slain and have redeemed us to God by Your blood” (Rev. 5:9)

These are not poetic verses but divine testimony. The blood is the seal of the covenant, the door of adoption, and the fountain of life.

Conclusion: “You Will See Me… and You Will Live Also” (John 14:19)

Let the world scoff; let unbelievers doubt the resurrection. But for those who see with eyes of faith, Christ has not left us as orphans. He has come again, and again, and again at every altar, in every baptism, in every outpouring of the Spirit.

He who once said “I will not leave you as children of the dead” has fulfilled His word in full. The apostles saw, believed, and lived. And through them, the Church lives, the saints live, and we, too, are made sons and daughters of the resurrection.

May the name of the Holy God be praised forever and ever in every tongue that He has created, today and always. 

May the blessings of God's mother, the virgin, and the cross be upon us.
