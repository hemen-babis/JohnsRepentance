👉🏽👉🏽👉🏽On the #Holy #Priesthood

Beloved brethren, peace be upon you all. In reverence and humility, let us gather in the sanctuary of our hearts to contemplate a most sacred office: the Holy Priesthood. As taught through the wisdom preserved by our Orthodox forefathers and compiled by the faithful ministry of John the Repentant, let us delve deeply into the mystery of the priest, that servant of the Most High, the guardian of the flock, the steward of divine mysteries, and the father to the faithful.

I. The Name and Nature of the Priest

The word "Priest" is rooted in the Ge’ez phrase tekhene agelegele, meaning:

Servant of God – for he ministers not to men but before the altar of the Almighty.

Father of the Faithful – for he begets sons and daughters not by the flesh but by the Spirit through the font of baptism.

Guardian – for he watches over the flock lest any stray into the thickets of heresy or the valley of sin.

Steward – entrusted with the divine mysteries, dispensing grace not by his own merit but by the will of the Holy Spirit.

II. The Priesthood in the Old Testament

Before the tabernacle was raised or the Law inscribed on tablets of stone, we see in Genesis 14:18 a9 man named Melchizedek, priest of the Most High God, offering bread and wine. A foreshadowing indeed! For he bore no genealogy, no beginning nor end recorded, as if to prepare us for the eternal priesthood of Christ.

Later, God commanded Moses to appoint Aaron, that he might minister before the Ark and make atonement for the people. From Aaron until John the Baptist, priesthood was bound to lineage, handed down only to the sons of Levi.

Yet, this priesthood was but a shadow of the true, for it could not perfect the worshiper. The sacrifices of bulls and goats could not cleanse the conscience. Therefore, the priesthood was destined to be replaced.

Why? Because Christ came not for one tribe or people, but for the whole world. The temporal was swallowed by the eternal, and the blood of animals was replaced by the precious flesh and blood of the Lamb of God (John 6:32). No longer does the priest save merely from temporal plagues, but from eternal death itself.

III. The Priesthood in the New Testament

The final priest of the Old Covenant was John the Baptist, the voice in the wilderness. With his hand, he anointed the Anointed One at the Jordan. Thus, the Lord sanctified the waters, showing us that baptism must be received through the priest (Matt. 3:13).

Our Lord affirmed the authority of the priesthood:

“Go, show yourself to the priest” (Matt. 8:4)

“I will give you the keys of the kingdom of heaven” (Matt. 16:17)

“Whatever you bind on earth will be bound in heaven” (Matt. 18:18)

“Go, baptize them…” (Matt. 28:19)

“Receive the Holy Spirit” (John 20:22)

In John 21:15, our Lord, before His ascension, commissioned Peter three times to “feed my sheep.” Here He established the apostolic priesthood, appointing the apostles as priests and Saint Peter as the first bishop. From them descends the Holy Synod, led by the Patriarch.

IV. The Ranks of the Priesthood

1. The Patriarch

Appointed as the head of the Church and leader of the Synod (Acts 20:28)

Elected by clergy and laity alike (Acts 5:2)

His authority is supreme in spiritual matters (Synod 51)

His name is pronounced during the Church’s prayers.

2. The Episcopal Bishop

Oversees a diocese as a spiritual father

Confirmed by the faithful and fellow bishops (Ft. 5, Abt. 2)

Ordains priests and deacons, blesses churches and holy items (1 Tim. 5:2, Acts 5:4)

V. The Ordination and Life of Clergy

The priesthood is not merely given but is earned through holiness, learning, and obedience.

A. Monastics

Must first dwell in a monastery and learn from the holy fathers.

After receiving monastic orders, they may ascend the clerical ranks if they are found worthy in conduct and doctrine (1 Cor. 7:32).

B. Deacons Turned Priests

Chosen by the local faithful, examined by the bishop.

Must be thirty years of age or older to be ordained.
