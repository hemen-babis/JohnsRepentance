2. Grief (Hazen)

There are two types of grief:

Undeserved grief, which arises from worldly loss—inheritance, death of relatives, or being deprived of material goods. This the Church counsels us to transcend, for our family is no longer limited to the flesh. We have as fathers the Holy Trinity, as brothers and sisters the saints and angels. “You are children of God,” said the Apostle, and “heirs of the Kingdom” (Galatians 4:7). Why should we weep for fading earthly things, when the heavens await us?

Deserved grief, the sorrow of the soul over true spiritual loss. This includes:

The death of the Son of God,

The martyrdom of the just,

The sins of our neighbors and our own.

Saint John the Evangelist, whose name means “Joy,” lived in sorrow after Good Friday, not for himself, but in memory of the Passion. The heart that sorrows for the sins of others and weeps for their salvation is the heart that beats like Christ’s.

The Verdict of Eternity

On that great and dreadful day, when the books shall be opened and each one judged, the question shall not be, “How much did you know?” but “How much did you love?”

Did you love the stranger? The hungry? The one imprisoned by sin?

If you did, you loved Christ Himself. And if you loved Christ, then your name is written not in the sand but in the Lamb’s Book of Life.

Conclusion

Let us then, dearly beloved, treasure love above all riches. Let us feed it with prayer, clothe it in mercy, shelter it with truth, and walk in it even when the path is thorny. For upon love hangs all the Law and the Prophets, and by love the Kingdom is inherited.

4:16)
