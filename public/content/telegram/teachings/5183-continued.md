7. How Should We Celebrate the New Year?

Beloved, let us not think of the New Year as mere numbers written in a book. Time is not given to us for vanity, but for repentance. Every New Year is God’s gift, that we may amend the sins of yesterday, reconcile with our enemies, clothe the naked, feed the hungry, and quench the thirst of the poor.

The Apostle Peter warns us:

“The past time of our life may suffice us to have done the will of the Gentiles, when we walked in lasciviousness, lusts, excess of wine, revelings, banqueting, and abominable idolatries” (1 Pet. 4:3).

Therefore, let us not spend the New Year in drunkenness, dancing, or vanity. Rather, let us spend it in prayer, in the Divine Liturgy, in the giving of alms, and in thanksgiving to God.

The parable of the fig tree reminds us that God grants us yet another year as a loan of time (Luke 13:6-9). Let us not squander this grace, but let us bear fruits thirty, sixty, and a hundredfold for the kingdom of heaven.

Conclusion

The Ethiopian New Year, celebrated on Meskerem 1, is more than a date it is the mystery of renewal in creation, in salvation history, and in the life of the soul. It unites the harmony of the stars, the memory of the flood, the witness of the Evangelists, the tradition of the Queen of Sheba, and the call of John the Baptist into a single proclamation: “Repent, for the Kingdom of Heaven is at hand!”

May the name of the Holy God be praised forever and ever in every tongue that He has created, today and always. 

May the blessings of God's mother, the virgin, and the cross be upon us.
Amen.
