VI. The Fourth Requirement: Forgiveness and Mercy

Hear this well, O Christian soul: he who does not forgive, shall not be forgiven. How dare we, stained with many offenses, approach the throne of grace while holding grudges in our heart?

“Blessed are the merciful, for they shall obtain mercy.” (Matthew 5:7)

To pray is to enter the presence of the One who forgave His murderers. Can we come to such a Lord with hatred in our hearts? Never!

Forgiveness opens the gate of heaven; bitterness shuts it. Let us remember always: if we refuse to hear the cry of the poor, God will refuse to hear our own (Proverbs 21:13).

VII. The Fifth Requirement: Persistence and Patience in Prayer

It is not enough to pray once and walk away. The Lord teaches us to pray without ceasing, to knock continually, and not to lose heart.

“If you abide in me, and my words abide in you, ask whatever you wish, and it will be done for you.” (John 15:7)

God delays not to frustrate, but to refine. He waits not because He is indifferent, but because He desires our persistence to purify our desires. The prayer of the impatient is like a spark in the wind—it flickers and dies.

VIII. The Sixth Requirement: Tears of Contrition

What does it mean to pray with tears? It is the prayer of the soul that has truly encountered its own poverty and God's immeasurable mercy. Tears are not weakness; they are the overflowing of divine sorrow and love. Saint Ephrem wept so much in prayer that his tears became his theology. Tears cleanse where words fail.

Let us then not be ashamed to cry before our Father for the proud heart remains dry, but the humble heart is watered with tears, and those tears ascend like incense to the throne of God.

Conclusion

Prayer is no light matter. It is the highest work of the soul. It requires faith, purity, love, forgiveness, persistence, and often, tears. These are not burdens, but graces. They are not obstacles, but wings by which prayer ascends to heaven.

Let us not rush to speak before preparing our hearts. Let us not dare to approach with unclean lips and divided souls. But let us walk the narrow way, embracing the sacred requirements, and there at the mercy seat of God we shall find not silence, but answer.

May the name of the Holy God be praised forever and ever in every tongue that He has created, today and always. 

May the blessings of God's mother, the virgin, and the cross be upon us.
Amen.
