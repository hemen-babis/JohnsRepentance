3. Mary the Intercessor: Our Advocate Before God

When the archangel Gabriel appeared to her, he did not simply greet her, but proclaimed heaven’s affirmation:

“Do not be afraid, Mary, for you have found favor with God.” (Luke 1:30)

This favor is not a passive acknowledgment it is divine empowerment, a seal of her role as intercessor for mankind. She is the dove of Noah, bringing hope to a perishing world. She is the door through which mercy enters.

The Church teaches us through the first miracle at Cana of Galilee, the wedding of Dokim (John 2):

“They have no wine,” she said.

What humility ! What profound compassion! She did not command she interceded. Her intercession did not move the Lord by force, but by love. He performed His first miracle not at the request of the apostles, but at the petition of His Mother. Thus, the power of her intercession was confirmed at the very beginning of His ministry.

The great saint Abune Gorgorios taught:

“Our Lady is a compassionate woman who quickly understands the suffering of men and presents them before her Son.”

4. The Eternal Covenant with Mary

This intercession is not momentary it is eternal. As the Psalmist declares:

“I have made a covenant with My chosen one” (Psalm 88:3)

She stands at the right hand of the King, clothed in gold (Psalm 45:9). This is not poetic flourish, but a prophetic revelation. The Church bears witness in Snaksar Zewerha (yekatit):

“She keeps her memory, calls on her name, and gives even cold water to the poor and needy  her covenant remains valid forever.”

Unlike any other saint, her memory itself is salvific. The very act of calling upon her name is a source of grace, and her prayer reaches swiftly to her Son.

5. Our Prayer and Confidence in Her Intercession

With the saints we cry:

“O pure Virgin Mary, faithful intercessor of mercy for the children of men, pray for us to your Son, Christ, that He may forgive us our sins.” (Wudasie Maryam Ze arb)

With Abba George, we offer this prayer:

“O Holy Virgin Mary! I trust in you  not in horses and chariots but in you and your Son. Deliver me from the open disasters of this world and from the gnashing of teeth in the world to come.”

We, the children of the covenant, must approach her not only with reverence but also with repentance. For her prayers are for those who weep, for the contrite, for the penitent. She stands not only as Mother but as Reconciler, pleading to her beloved Son on our behalf.

6. Conclusion: A Gift at the Foot of the Cross

Let us never forget that she was given to us at the most sacred hour  at the Cross itself:

“Behold your mother.” (John 19:27)

She was not a gift from men, but from Christ Himself. Let us cherish her. Let us imitate her humility. Let us trust in her prayers. Let us offer to her our gratitude, not with cold hearts, but with burning devotion.

O Holy Virgin, Mother of Light, pray for us! May your intercession ever protect us. May your humility be our ladder to the heights of heaven.

may the name of the Holy God be praised forever and ever in every tongue that He has created, today and always.

May the blessings of God's mother, the virgin, and the cross be upon us.
Amen.
