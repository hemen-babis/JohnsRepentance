---About #Tsomë_Tsigée 

Beloved brethren in Christ, in this sacred season known to us as Wereha Tsigée in the Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church, we commemorate the migration of the Holy Family—the Blessed Virgin Mary, our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ, and the righteous Joseph—into Egypt and Ethiopia. This event, memorialized in the fast of Tsomë Tsigée, is a journey marked by divine guidance, trials, and heavenly interventions, all culminating in the profound revelation of God’s care for His chosen vessels. Let us now embark on a spiritual reflection of this journey, rich in meaning and deep in mystery, a story that resonates through time as a testament to God’s enduring love for humanity.

The Divine Command to Flee

The Holy Family’s migration began with a divine command from an angel, saying to Joseph: "Take the child and his mother and flee to Egypt" (Matthew 2:13). This simple yet urgent directive set in motion a journey that would stretch across nations and echo through centuries. Our Lady, the Blessed Virgin Mary, carrying in her arms the hope of the world—our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ—embarked on this perilous journey with the righteous Joseph, who obediently followed the angel’s command, and Salome, Our Lady’s aunt. Their departure from their homeland was not merely a geographic movement but a spiritual exodus, carrying within them the Light of the World to lands beyond their own.

Though commanded to journey to Egypt, the Holy Family passed through many regions, encountering trials that revealed both the frailty of human nature and the overwhelming grace of divine providence. Among their first stops was the forest of Geraral, a place that would become significant as a shelter and a temporary refuge. Yet, even in these secluded places, the enemies of Christ and His Holy Mother sought them out, driven by the jealousy and hatred of King Herod.

Yosa’s Effort and His Punishment

As the Holy Family journeyed, a striking episode unfolded involving Yosa, the son of Joseph, who had remained at his father’s house. Herod, in his fury, sent soldiers to seek out the Holy Family. Yosa, learning of Herod’s murderous intent, rushed to warn his relatives. But as he traveled, Satan, disguised as a man, attempted to dissuade him from his mission, sowing doubt and confusion. Yet, like the righteous of old, Yosa remained undeterred, pushing forward to reunite with his father and the Holy Family.

When Yosa finally arrived, he brought grievous news to Joseph and the Blessed Virgin: Herod’s massacre of the innocents in Bethlehem had begun, and no child was spared. Upon hearing this news, the Holy Virgin wept bitterly, clutching the infant Jesus to her breast, her heart pierced with the knowledge of such suffering. Yosa’s words were like a sharp blow to her maternal heart, for she realized the danger that still loomed over her beloved Son.

In this moment, our Lord Jesus Christ, though still a child, demonstrated His divine authority and profound wisdom. Though Yosa’s intentions were noble, he had startled and distressed the Holy Mother. Christ, in His justice, punished Yosa’s body for this slight offense, yet in His mercy, He restored Yosa’s soul to life. This act of divine retribution was not meant as condemnation but as a reminder that those who are beloved of God must endure discipline. As Scripture says, “My son, do not despise the Lord’s discipline, and do not resent His rebuke, because the Lord disciplines those He loves, as a father the son He delights in” (Proverbs 3:11-12). Like Moses and Aaron, who were close friends of God but were chastised for their momentary lapses, Yosa too was disciplined for a small offense, yet his soul was preserved.

The Encounter with the Robbers: Titos and Dacres

The Holy Family’s journey was not without encounters with human evil, a reflection of the sinfulness that Christ came to redeem. Two robbers, known as Titos and Dacres, notorious for their extortion and violence, pursued the Holy Family through the wilderness.
