5. The Cross – The Tree of Life and Seal of Faith

The Holy Cross, upon which our Savior gave His life, is the scepter of Christian identity, the banner of the Church, and the sign of victory. During the Liturgy, priests and deacons carry it solemnly, not merely as a symbol, but as a testimony.
This act reenacts our Lord’s journey to Calvary on that most holy Friday, His death, and His glorious Resurrection. Thus, in our daily prayers, with reverence and awe, we proclaim: “I worship the cross on which Jesus Christ was crucified in order to save the whole world.” We worship not wood, but the mystery of our salvation engraved upon it.

6. The Gold Sacrifice – Offering of Praise and Fulfillment of Prophecy

The deacon, like the Magi bearing gifts, carries the gold sacrifice an offering of praise from Bethlehem to the sanctuary. It is a mystical sign, commemorating the Lord’s birth in Bethlehem and His journey to be sacrificed on Golgotha.
As the prophets foretold, the Son of God came to fulfill both cradle and cross. The gold carried in the Liturgy proclaims both His Kingship and His suffering.

7. The Plate – The Silent Witness of the Tomb

Made of clay, brass, iron, or precious metal, the plate upon which the sacred Body is placed is not merely a vessel it is the symbol of the tomb. On it rests the Lamb, slain and risen.
In the Old Covenant, offerings were made on altars. In the New, the Altar of Sacrifice has become the Table of the Lord, and the plate is the sacred bed upon which the mystery of the Eucharist is laid.

8. The Chalice – The Cup of Light and Life

This vessel of clay, wood, stone, or metal, holds the most precious Blood the Blood of the Lamb. The chalice is more than a cup; it is the vessel of salvation. It symbolizes the chalice of light received by the angels, which bore the blood shed at Calvary and scattered it across the earth for sanctification.
Each time the chalice is lifted in the Mass, the whole Church beholds the cup of blessing and drinks from the well of immortality.

9. The Shroud – The Cloth of Rest and Resurrection

This holy covering, made of chosen fabric, is used to veil the sacred mysteries. Five shrouds are used:
One laid upon the Table
Three to cover the sacrifice
One for the chalice
These coverings remind us of the Sabbath of the Lord His rest in the tomb. They are not mere cloths, but echoes of that moment when heaven was hushed, and the earth waited for the Resurrection.

10. The Sacred Candlestick – Light of the Sanctuary

From the time of Moses, the candlestick has held its place in the tabernacle of God (Ex. 25:31–40, 27:20; Heb. 9:2). It is made of carved, non-toxic wood overlaid with gold. It has a base and seven branches, filled with oil, and burns through the night as a testimony to the enduring presence of God.
Its light is not extinguished, for it represents the eternal flame the Word made flesh, the Light of the world.

Conclusion:

All these things crown, incense, books, cross, gold, plate, chalice, shroud, candlestick are not dead instruments. They are alive with meaning, breathing with the Spirit, radiant with divine glory. They draw the soul upward, revealing Christ hidden in mystery, manifest in flesh, victorious in resurrection.

May the name of the Holy God be praised forever and ever in every tongue that He has created, today and always. 

May the blessings of God's mother, the virgin, and the cross be upon us.
Amen.
