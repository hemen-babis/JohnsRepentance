👉🏽👉🏽👉🏽The #Biblical Teaching on #Friendship

Part 2

Beloved brethren, who seek the wisdom of God and the righteousness of His ways, may the peace of our Lord be upon you. Today, let us reflect on the divine teaching of friendship as illuminated by the Holy Scriptures, so that we may discern between those friendships that lead to life and those that bring destruction.

The sacred pages of the Bible present to us two striking examples of companionship: one radiant with virtue, and the other tainted by wickedness. These accounts are not mere historical recollections but divine lessons written for our edification, that we may walk in the path of righteousness and guard our souls against the snares of the enemy.

The Friendship of David and Jonathan: A Bond of Righteousness
Among the most blessed friendships in the history of mankind is that of David and Jonathan, a companionship fashioned in purity, selflessness, and divine love. Jonathan, the son of Saul, saw in David not a rival, nor a threat to his own inheritance, but a man chosen by God. Though his father, King Saul, was consumed with envy and sought to destroy David, Jonathan did not partake in his father’s malice. Instead, his soul was bound to David's in a covenant of love, so pure and unshaken that it reflected the very love with which God loves His children.

The Holy Scriptures declare:

"Jonathan's soul was bound up in David's soul, and Jonathan loved him as his own soul, and he made a covenant with David because he loved him" (1 Sam. 18:10-15; 1 Sam. 19:1-5).

Such a friendship is rare upon the earth, for it is not founded upon selfish gain, nor upon fleeting pleasures, but upon truth and virtue. Jonathan risked his own safety, defied his father’s wrath, and placed himself in peril—all for the sake of his friend, whom he loved as his own soul. He interceded for David before his father, seeking to preserve his life rather than betray him for the sake of power or advantage.

This is the essence of true friendship: to seek not our own gain but the good of the other; to love not with words alone but with deeds; to stand beside our friends in times of peril, even at great cost to ourselves. Such friendships are born not of the flesh but of the Spirit, and they reflect the love of Christ, who laid down His life for us.

The Deception of Jonadab: A Friendship of Wickedness
But, brethren, not all friendships are alike. Just as there exists companionship that leads to life, so too is there friendship that leads to destruction. The Scriptures present to us the tragic example of Amnon and Jonadab.

Amnon, a son of David, was consumed with an unlawful passion for his own sister, Tamar. Instead of resisting this sinful desire and seeking the counsel of the righteous, he turned to his friend Jonadab, a man of cunning but devoid of virtue. Rather than rebuking Amnon, warning him of the wrath of God, or guiding him towards repentance, Jonadab, with deceitful lips and a heart filled with iniquity, instructed him in evil.

By Jonadab’s wicked counsel, Amnon defiled his sister, an act that led not only to her ruin but to his own destruction at the hands of Absalom (2 Sam. 13:1-39). Here, we see the friendship of the ungodly: not one that edifies, but one that corrupts; not one that leads to life, but one that brings death. Jonadab was not a friend but a deceiver, an agent of the adversary, whispering lies and leading his companion into the pit of destruction.

Beware, therefore, of such men, for they are not friends but executioners of the soul. They speak soothing words, yet their counsel is filled with poison. They appear as allies but are, in truth, the enemies of righteousness. Let not the young and the passionate be deceived, for the fire of youthful desires, when unchecked by the Word of God, leads only to ruin.
