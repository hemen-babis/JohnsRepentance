👉🏽👉🏽👉🏽A Homily on the #Birth of the #Holy #Virgin #Mary, Celebrated on May 1 (genbot 1) Ethiopian calander

Part 2

“Many shall rejoice at her birth” (Luke 1:14)—Behold, a day of immeasurable gladness, a celebration most sublime, when heaven and earth exult together! This is not a mere commemoration of mortal flesh, nor a passing remembrance of earthly nobility, but the revelation of the Mother of God, the gate through whom salvation entered into creation. This is the feast of the Holy Virgin Mary, whose nativity we celebrate on Genbot 1, according to the tradition of our Holy Church, a day which draws forth awe from the angels and trembling praise from the tongues of men.

Woe unto the so-called Christian who, blind to this mystery, scoffs or neglects the honor due on such a day! For the wise know that the beginning of our salvation was planted in the womb of St. Anna, and from that sacred soil blossomed the Virgin, undefiled, full of grace, the new Eve, mother of all the living. Adam called his wife Eve because she was the mother of the living (Genesis 3:20), but the true Eve is Mary, from whom Life Himself took flesh.

The Divine Vision to the Righteous Joachim and Hanna

What mortal language can capture the depth of the visions granted to the barren couple, Joachim and Hanna, whom the Lord visited with fruitfulness?

Hanna, in a dream, beheld a white dove alighting upon her head, descending to her ear, and coming to rest upon her womb. A strange and wondrous vision! The white dove symbol of purity, symbol of the Spirit, symbol of our Lady did not flutter in vain. It signified that the Immaculate One would enter the world not through passion, but by divine ordination. That her very conception would be untouched by corruption.

Joachim likewise was given a vision, wherein he beheld the seven heavens opened like a curtain, and a white dove descending from the highest realm to sit upon his head. The dove, here, is the incarnate Word, Jesus Christ pure, majestic, heavenly. The descent from the heavens is the foretelling of the Incarnation. The dove sits upon the righteous Joachim as a sign that from his loins would spring the Mother of God.

These two visions, joined in their meaning, prefigure the Incarnation: the ear that hears the Word (Hanna), the head upon which divinity rests (Joachim), and the womb wherein heaven would take root upon the earth.

The Testimony of the Fathers and Prophets

Saint Yared, the melodious tongue of Ethiopia, declares: "Mary shines like a white pearl in the nature of Adam." In a world tainted by sin and weighed down by the curse of the Fall, she appeared as a radiant gem, unstained, clothed in virginity, a bridge between fallen man and the Redeemer.

He sings further, saying, "You are the vine with roots in the earth and its branches in the sky." O mystery! Her humanity touches the dust from whence we were made, yet her branches her fruit reach into the heavens. She is Jacob’s ladder, upon whom angels ascend and descend. Born of earth, but home to heaven.

Abba Hiriakos, the Archbishop of Athens, declares with boldness: "O Virgin, you were not conceived by sinful lust, but were born of the law of Hanna and Jacob." Here, the conception of Mary is sanctified by lawful union, yet set apart by divine intervention. The Spirit of God watched over her even in the womb her purity was not merely moral, but mystical, guarded and sanctified by the presence of the Lord from the moment of her conception.

The Prophet Isaiah, beholding the sorrow of a cursed world, cries out, “Unless the Lord of hosts had left us a seed, we would have been like Sodom!” (Isaiah 1:19). That seed is Mary, the holy remnant from which the Deliverer springs.

King Solomon, gifted with the spirit of prophecy, beholds her and exclaims, “You are all beautiful, my love, and there is no blemish in you. Come from Lebanon, my bride!” (Song of Songs 4:7-16).
