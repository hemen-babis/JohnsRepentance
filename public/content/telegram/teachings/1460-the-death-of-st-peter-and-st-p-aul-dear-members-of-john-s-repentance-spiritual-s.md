The Death of St. #Peter and St. P#aul

Dear members of John's Repentance spiritual service, today we comemorate the death of St. Peter and St. Paul. Accordingly, we have prepared a lesson for you to read and participate in the blessings, so we entrust you to read and apply the learnings to your life. 

St. Peter, the chief of the apostles, was a simple fisherman hailing from Bete Saida. It was on the day after His baptism that our Lord encountered him and chose him to be His disciple. Prior to that, Peter's brother, Andrew, had been the first to join Jesus. Peter displayed exceptional faith, recognizing Jesus as the Christ, the Son of the living God, when others were unsure of His identity. Jesus, in turn, praised Peter, declaring him the recipient of the keys to the Kingdom of Heaven.

Empowered by the comforting grace of the Holy Spirit, St. Peter fearlessly ventured into the world, confronting the metaphorical wolves of this world through his preaching. Alongside innumerable miracles performed by his hands, Peter penned three invaluable epistles and collaborated with St. Mark in the translation and writing of the Gospel. His journey eventually led him to the city of Rome, where a significant event unfolded.

In the great playground of Rome, where nobles congregated, St. Peter addressed the people, and astonishingly, four stone pillars present there resounded with a thunderous "Amen." This miraculous occurrence left those who witnessed it in awe and inspired great reverence. Furthermore, a man who had been tormented by a demon for seventy years experienced liberation upon hearing the word proclaimed by those stones. Stirred by this supernatural intervention, a nobleman named Kewstos and his wife, Acrosia, embraced the teachings of St. Peter regarding renunciation of worldly possessions and compassionate care for the poor.

Impressed by their example, they distributed their wealth among the needy, reserving only their daily sustenance. Subsequently, Kewstos received a summons from the king, which caused anxiety due to their depleted funds. Acrosia, however, encouraged him to proceed, assuring him of God's providence. True to her words, Kewstos embarked on the journey and encountered numerous blessings along the way. The king received him with joy and honor, proving the divine favor that accompanies faithful commitment.

Tragedy struck Kewstos and Acrosia's household upon their return, as their two children were found lifeless, having consumed poisoned water. Concealing this devastating news from her husband, Acrosia resorted to various excuses. Yet, through the intercession of St. Peter and the power of his teachings, a heavenly voice proclaimed that the children would be granted new life due to their acceptance of Peter's words. As promised, the resurrected children became fervent adherents to the teachings of St. Peter. One of them, named Clement, revealed extraordinary visions granted to St. Peter after the Lord's ascension.

In these visions, St. Peter beheld the glorious presence of the Immaculate Virgin Mary, the Mother of God. A cloud, resembling an arrow, bore a luminous tent wherein the Holy Virgin sat, surrounded by angels and archangels brandishing flaming spears and swords. St. Peter received divine revelations, understanding the Virgin Mary as the vine and her womb as the pure hall that bore the Lamb of God. The secrets and mysteries unveiled in these visions were impossible for St. Clement to have known otherwise, attesting to their divine authenticity.
