4. On the Reverence of Names: What Should Not Be Used

Some names, though biblical or divine in origin, are too holy for human use. Names such as:

Emmanuel (God with us),

El Shaddai (God Almighty),

Jesus — the name above every name,

These are reserved in sanctity. They are not fit for our bearing, lest we profane what is ineffable. Instead, let us adopt names that bear witness to God without equating ourselves to Him:

Gebre Egziabher (Servant of God)

Sahle Selassie (Grace of the Trinity)

Fekrete Christos (Love of Christ)

ZeAmmanuel (Belonging to Immanuel)

These names confess truth while preserving humility.

5. Examples of Proper Christian Names

The names of the saints may be taken directly or reflected indirectly. Examples include:

Zemariam – one who is “of Mary”

Zemikael – one who is “of Michael”

Atsede Mariam – one consecrated to Mary

Tekle Haymanot – the great Ethiopian pillar of faith


Such names root us in the communion of saints. They are bridges between our earthly pilgrimage and the heavenly homeland. When a soul is named after a saint, it walks not alone, but under the guardianship of that holy one.

Conclusion: Let the Name Be a Blessing

Let us not be careless with the names we bear or the names we give. The Christian name is a treasure  a memory of holiness, a vessel of blessing, and a testimony of our heritage in Christ. Let it be chosen wisely, remembered fervently, and borne with reverence. Let the names of the saints, the servants of God, and the confessors of the truth be ever upon our lips and in our hearts.

May God make us worthy to receive the blessings that flow from this holy tradition.

may the name of the Holy God be praised forever and ever in every tongue that He has created, today and always.

May the blessings of God's mother, the virgin, and the cross be upon us.
Amen.
