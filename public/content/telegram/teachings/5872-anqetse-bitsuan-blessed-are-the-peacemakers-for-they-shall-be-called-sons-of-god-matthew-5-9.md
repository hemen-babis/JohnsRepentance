1. Making Peace Between Man and God

The way the world pursues peace and the way Christianity teaches peace are different.

The world focuses on peace between people, achieved through:
• Building good relationships
• Resolving conflicts by scientific, cultural, or legal means
• Maintaining order and security

Christian teaching, however, emphasizes that true peace begins with a good relationship between man and God.

Peace between man and God means harmony in all aspects of this relationship:

• Creator and creature
• Father and son
• Worshipper and God
• Prayer-maker and prayer recipient
• Lawgiver and judge

This relationship was first disrupted when the Devil desired to be God, turning what should have been harmony between Creator and creature into enmity.

✍🏾Through repentance, faith, and reconciliation, peacemakers restore this divine harmony, becoming instruments of God’s peace in the world.

✍🏾The peace between man and God is founded on God’s love, but this peace was broken when man sinned. Enmity arose when man disobeyed God’s command and was expelled from Paradise (by eating the fruit of the forbidden tree), creating a barrier between humanity and God (Hebrews 9:3).

✍🏾Peace in the Old Testament (Torah System)

In the Old Testament, peace with God was symbolized and temporarily restored through sacrificial practices:

The burnt offering represented reconciliation between man and God (Leviticus 6:8–13).

The blood sacrifice was offered for God alone, symbolizing atonement for sin (Hebrews 9:22; Romans 6:23).

Although these sacrifices atoned for temporary sins, they could not fully remove the enmity between man and God (Hebrews 10:4).

The wall of enmity was ultimately broken down by our Lord and Savior, Jesus Christ, through His sacrificial death on the cross.

✍🏾As the Mediator of the New Covenant, God incarnate reconciled humanity to Himself, restoring the lost peace and bringing man back into true fellowship with God.

✍🏾The Apostle Paul speaks of the salvation that restores peace between man and God:

👉🏾“For if, when we were enemies, we were reconciled to God through the death of his Son, much more, having been reconciled, we shall be saved by his life.” (Romans 5:10)

He further explains:

👉🏾“But now in Christ Jesus you who once were far off have been brought near by the blood of Christ. For he is our peace, who has made the two one, having abolished the law of commandments contained in ordinances, and having broken down in his flesh the middle wall of hostility, that he might create in himself one new man from the two, making peace, having killed the hostility, and by him reconcile both to God in one body, through the cross.” (Ephesians 2:13–16)

👉🏾This teaches that our Lord Jesus Christ is our peace, who removes the barrier of hostility between humanity and God.

Paul also affirms:

👉🏾“God was pleased for all his fullness to dwell in him, and through him to reconcile to himself all things, whether on earth or in heaven, by making peace through the blood of his cross.” (Colossians 1:20)

✍🏾Through the sacrifice of His blood, Christ restores harmony and establishes true peace, reconciling man with God and created the foundation for peace among people and within the soul.
