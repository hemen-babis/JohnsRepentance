👉🏾👉🏾👉🏾The Glory of the Holy #Cross in #Orthodox #Teaching

(Part 5)

✍🏾Beloved in the Lord, the glory of the Cross is the glory of our faith. For through the Cross the curse was broken, death was destroyed, and the gates of Paradise flung open to the sons of Adam. The Orthodox Church, like a faithful guardian, raises the Cross high, venerates it, sings of it, and engraves it upon her children. For what the world despises, the Church embraces as her crown, and what men once feared as a sign of shame, we proclaim as the very throne of victory.

✍🏾Here lies the difference between the holy Orthodox faith and those who walk in the steps of Protestantism. For they neither trace the sign of the Cross upon themselves, nor bless their homes or garments with it. They do not wear the Cross upon their bodies, nor lift it upon their temples, nor celebrate its feasts, nor rejoice in its praises. But the Orthodox, taught by Christ and the apostles, have been entrusted with the honor due to the Cross, not as an ornament of wood or metal, but as the power and wisdom of God.

1. Christ Himself set the Cross at the center of discipleship

✍🏾Our Lord, even before He stretched out His hands upon the wood, proclaimed the mystery of the Cross. He said: “Whoever does not take up his cross and follow after Me is not worthy of Me” (Matt. 10:38). Again He declared: “If anyone would come after Me, let him deny himself and take up his cross and follow Me” (Mark 8:34). To the rich young ruler He said: “Take up your cross and follow Me” (Mark 10:21). And once more, “Whoever does not bear his cross and come after Me cannot be My disciple” (Luke 14:27).

✍🏾Behold how He speaks with insistence: not once or twice, but many times He sets the Cross as the mark of true discipleship. Therefore, When we make the sign of the cross with our hand (Mamateb), we remember and honor the teachings and commandments that our Lord has given us.

2. The Cross is proclaimed by angels and apostles

✍🏾Even the angels confessed the Lord as the Crucified One. When they announced the resurrection, they said to the women: “I know that you seek Jesus who was crucified” (Matt. 28:5–6). Though He had risen, the angel did not cast aside the name of the Cross, for the Resurrection cannot be separated from the Crucifixion.

So too did the apostles preach. Peter cried to the Jews: “Let all the house of Israel know assuredly that God has made this Jesus, whom you crucified, both Lord and Christ” (Acts 2:36). Paul declared boldly: “We preach Christ crucified” (1 Cor. 1:23). And again he said: “I determined to know nothing among you except Jesus Christ, and Him crucified” (1 Cor. 2:2).

Thus the Cross is the unshakable foundation of apostolic preaching and angelic proclamation alike.

3. The Cross was the apostles’ glory

✍🏾The apostle Paul confesses: “Far be it from me to boast except in the cross of our Lord Jesus Christ” (Gal. 6:14). See the paradox: the cross, a thing despised by men, becomes the very boast of the saints. For in it Paul beheld his true freedom: “The world has been crucified to me, and I to the world” (Gal. 6:14). The cross stripped him of earthly pride and clothed him with heavenly honor. What greater glory can a man have than to be marked with the cross?

4. The Cross reveals divine love and forgiveness

✍🏾Every time we Blessing our bodies by moving our hands in the shape of a cross, we remember the immeasurable love of God. Isaiah foresaw this mystery: “The Lord laid on Him the iniquity of us all” (Isa. 53:6). John the Baptist pointed to Him: “Behold the Lamb of God, who takes away the sin of the world” (John 1:29). The apostle John declares: “He is the propitiation for our sins” (1 John 2:2).

The cross, then, is not merely wood it is the place where mercy triumphed over judgment, where sin was blotted out, and where forgiveness gushed forth like an endless spring.
