2. They instruct the unlearned.

Look at the schools of the world: even their books are filled with pictures, so that children may learn quickly. How much more in the Church! A picture of the martyrdom of a saint speaks to the illiterate more clearly than many pages of text. Through sight, the soul is led to understanding.

3. They provide role models and examples.

Every man hangs portraits of his parents and forefathers in his house, not because he worships them, but because he honors their memory. The saints, though they bore no children of the flesh, begot many by the Spirit. Their images are placed in the holy temple, not for vain decoration, but to rouse us to follow their footsteps. They remind us that men and women of flesh and blood triumphed by God’s grace and so can we.

The Proper Placement of Holy Icons

Yet, beloved, do not handle holy things carelessly. Too many treat icons as ornaments, scattering them about their homes without reverence. But icons are sacred and should be kept in clean places. If possible, set aside a space for them; if not, gather them together in dignity, veiling them to protect them from dust.

Do not place them in profane spots, nor cram them into pockets or bags as if they were trinkets. And if an image grows old or damaged, do not cast it aside as refuse. Bring it to the Church, that it may be preserved with honor. For that which once bore the image of Christ or His saints must never be treated with contempt.

Conclusion

Therefore, dearly beloved, let no one deceive you by equating holy pictures with idols. Idols are demons in disguise, but icons are teachers of the faith, companions in prayer, windows into heaven. They gather the wandering mind, instruct the ignorant, and stir the heart to holiness. Handle them with reverence, and may they always lift your gaze from earth to heaven.
