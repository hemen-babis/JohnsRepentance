7. His Writings and Martyrdom

Saint Peter wrote two epistles to the early Christians. The first was addressed to those suffering persecution and dispersion after the martyrdom of Stephen. The second was written amid the terror of Emperor Nero and warned against false teachers.

After his travels through Asia Minor, Saint Peter arrived in Rome, where he fearlessly proclaimed the gospel. There, under Nero’s persecution, he was arrested, imprisoned, and sentenced to death by crucifixion. Remembering the Lord’s prophecy in John 21:18, he accepted death, requesting to be crucified upside down, deeming himself unworthy to die as his Master did.

Conclusion: The Perfect Servant of the Lord

Thus ends the earthly pilgrimage of Saint Peter, the great apostle. From a fisherman to a shepherd of souls, from fear to boldness, from denial to martyrdom his life is the testimony of what divine grace can make of a humble and willing heart.

Beloved, let us emulate his faith, his humility, his repentance, and his courage. Let us say with him, “Lord, to whom shall we go? You have the words of eternal life” (John 6:68). Let us also be rocks in our own generation, steadfast in faith and immovable in love.

May the name of the Holy God be praised forever and ever in every tongue that He has created, today and always. 

May the blessings of God's mother, the virgin, and the cross be upon us.
Amen.
