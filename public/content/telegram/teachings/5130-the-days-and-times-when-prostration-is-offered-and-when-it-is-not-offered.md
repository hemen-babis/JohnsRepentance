👉🏾👉🏾👉🏾The #Days and #Times When #Prostration Is #Offered and When It Is Not Offered

(Part 5)

Dear brothers and sisters, the Holy Church has given us clear teaching about prostration. She shows us the days and times when it should be offered and the days when it should not. These rules are not given by chance, but are established with wisdom, so that we may worship God in the right way and at the right time.

Days When Prostration Is Withheld

First, let us understand when it is not permitted to prostrate. For the Church, like a wise mother, teaches that not every day is the same, nor every hour alike.

Saturdays and Sundays are exempt from prostration. For these are the days of joy, the days of rest, the days in which the light of the Resurrection shines. How can one bow in mourning on the day that Christ has triumphed over death?

Likewise, on the great feasts of the Lord His conception, His birth, His baptism, His triumphal entry on Hosanna, His resurrection, His ascension, His descent of the Spirit at Pentecost, and His glory revealed upon Mount Tabor the Church forbids prostration. These are days not of lament but of jubilation, not of abasement but of exaltation, for on these days Christ revealed His power and majesty.

In addition, on the feasts of the holy ones Our Lady on the 21st, Saint Michael on the 12th, and the Fathers on the 29th we do not bow to the ground, for the joy of the saints is shared by the faithful.

Thus, let all understand: there is a difference between the reverence we show when entering the church, bowing before the cross, venerating the holy Gospel, or greeting the holy icons, and the canonical act of prostration that belongs to the ordered life of the Church. The teaching on prostration does not refer to every gesture of adoration, but specifically to the prostration established as a permanent canon of the Church.

Days and Times When Prostration Is Commanded

Yet the Church, like a skillful physician, also prescribes for us the times when prostration is necessary, when the soul must humble itself before God in body as well as in spirit.

When we are given a special canon of penance, we must prostrate.

When we hear the words of Scripture that call us to worship, we must prostrate.

During the Divine Liturgy, when the Church proclaims, “Worship God with fear,” “Pray,” “Humble yourselves in repentance,” then let every knee bend, for heaven itself bends in awe before the mysteries.

During the time of the Passion, in the holy and great week, the Church orders us to prostrate with fasting, prayer, and tears, for then we walk with Christ in His suffering.

During the holy fasts, we must sanctify the season with prayer, almsgiving, tears, and prostration, so that our fasting may be acceptable to the Lord.

In the life of penance, when the Scriptures declare, “Humble yourselves, you who are in penance,” let us fall to the ground, for the Fathers taught their children that prostration is inseparable from repentance.

Even in our daily prayer, when we say:
“Peace be upon the Holy Church, the image of the heavenly Jerusalem, the dwelling of the Godhead, the model of Him who has set us free from sin,”
the very words call us to bend low, for the soul cannot utter such words without humbling the body before the Lord.

Thus, before the cross, before the holy Scriptures, before the images of the saints, before the Gospel of life, let every Christian prostrate in faith.
