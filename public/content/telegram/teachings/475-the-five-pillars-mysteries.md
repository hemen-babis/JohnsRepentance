3. The Mystery of Baptism (Continued Part)

The water and spirit baptism of rebirth is what Jesus taught Nicodemus. St. Peter the Apostle also described this as “..having been born again, not of corruptible seed but of incorruptible seed, through the word of God, which lives and abides forever...” 1 Peter 1:23. Also, the Bible clearly states that “he who believes and is baptized will be saved.” Mark 16:16. It is also required to enter God's kingdom. Our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ stated this clearly: “Unless one is born of water and the Spirit, he cannot enter the kingdom of God.” John 3:5. There is only one baptism of rebirth (Ephesians 4:4–5). It can’t be repeated. As we are only born once from our parents, we are only born once from the Holy Spirit and water (via baptism).

Our Lord's baptism fulfills the law and saves all mankind. According to Luke 16:17, the Lord was baptized, saying, "We must fulfill all the law." (Matthew 3:1) The Orthodox Tewahedo Church, which is the Church of Christ, baptizes all those who believe in the divinity of Christ in the name of the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit. The Church teaches that baptism is the gateway to all the mysteries and the basis for salvation and forgiveness of sins.

According to the apostolic teaching of Galatians 3:27, "For as many of you as were baptized into Christ have put on Christ," the church always teaches all its children to put on Christ and to grow in wisdom and understanding. Because there is only one Lord, one religion, and one baptism, the church baptizes us as children when males are 40 days old and females 80 days old. Those older than the recommended age for baptism can be baptized after they learn and believe in the dogmas of the church. However, if an infant is sick or misses the 40 or 80 day mark due to different reasons, they should be baptized before or after the 40 or 80 day mark. However, these are exceptional cases.

For those who ask, is infant baptism for rebirth or remission of original sin? It is for rebirth. But there are differing views on this depending on one's conceptualization of original sin. Some say it’s for both remission of the original sin and rebirth. Others say it’s to "heal" the consequences of original sin and ensure their salvation through rebirth. The upshot is that original sin is avoided by Jesus, and infant baptism is for rebirth from water and the Holy Spirit only. Overall, infants should be baptized for re-birth in water and the Holy Spirit to ensure their salvation. For those who died after at 40/80 days, the parents will be accountable. Unbaptized infants who died before 40/80 days, they are free as they are not responsible for any sin. Ezk 18:20

There is only one baptism; there is no re-baptism. Just as we are born only once from our parents, we are born only once from water and the Holy Spirit. If someone was already baptized in our church but denounced it and went to another religion, he or she won’t be re-baptized if he or she decides to return. Instead, a baptism of repentance will be conducted. Unless coming from other oriental churches, anyone coming from other denominations shall be re-baptized as his or her previous baptism is not considered valid.

Yes, we believe in and confess baptism for the remission of sins.

The baptism of rebirth, in general, is one; it cannot be reputed, and it is the gateway, door, or point of entry into Christianity, as well as the foundation for salvation and sin forgiveness; one cannot be a Christian without baptism.It’s the first sacrament to be performed for all Christians. Through baptism, we become children of God and members of the apostolic Church of God. All other sacraments are performed only for those who are baptized. That is why the mystery of baptism is referred to as the gate to Christianity.
