✍🏾Sharing in Christ’s Suffering

(Matthew 5:11–12)

👉🏾Our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ said:

“Blessed are you when they revile you and persecute you and say all kinds of evil against you falsely for My sake. Rejoice and be glad, for your reward is great in heaven; for so they persecuted the prophets who were before you.”
(Matthew 5:11–12)

In these words, our Lord teaches that sharing in suffering for His sake being reviled, persecuted, and falsely accused is a true manifestation of the Christian life. Suffering endured for Christ is not a sign of abandonment, but a mark of faithfulness.

The Evangelist Luke also writes:

“Blessed are you when people hate you, and when they exclude you, and revile you, and cast out your name as evil, for the sake of the Son of Man. Rejoice in that day and leap for joy, for indeed your reward is great in heaven; for in like manner their fathers did to the prophets.”(Luke 6:22–23)

These words show clearly that hatred, exclusion, and separation are also part of the path prepared for those who follow Christ.

👉🏾In the previous verses, our Lord had been referring to “them” in the third person, speaking from a distance in time, extending to the end of the world, and in place, to the ends of the earth. But at this point, He changes His manner of speech and addresses them directly as “you,” speaking in the second person.

✍🏾By doing so, He speaks first to the Christians who were present at the Sermon on the Mount, and at the same time to all who would later be gathered into His Holy Church. He addresses them personally as “you.” In this verse, He speaks especially of His disciples those who are reviled, persecuted, hated, excluded, and falsely accused of all kinds of evil for His name’s sake, that is, for the sake of the Son of Man.

✍🏾 In this verse, our Lord teaches that the reason Christians suffer is because they believe in Him the God who became Man and because they follow Him. In short, they suffer because they are Christians. This verse is a unique and powerful teaching about the suffering that will come upon the Church, and about the glory that is revealed through enduring suffering for Christ’s sake.

✍🏾In this passage, our Lord makes special mention of the holy prophets who suffered before. By this, He teaches that just as people persecuted the prophets whom God sent in earlier times, they will also persecute His disciples. God sent the prophets, and He also sent the apostles. Both the prophets and the apostles were taught that they would endure suffering for His sake and, through that suffering, enter into eternal joy.

✍🏾 Just as persecuting the prophets who were the messengers of God is truly persecuting the God who sent them, so persecuting Christians is persecuting Christ Himself. Christians are the Body of Christ, and He identifies Himself with them.

This truth is clearly revealed in the words of our Lord to Saul:

👉🏾“And he fell to the ground and heard a voice saying to him, ‘Saul, Saul, why are you persecuting Me?’ And he said, ‘Who are You, Lord?’ Then the Lord said, ‘I am Jesus, whom you are persecuting.’”(Acts 9:4–5)

👉🏾Our Lord did not say, “Why are you persecuting Christians?”
He said, “Why are you persecuting Me?”

Therefore, persecuting Christians is persecuting Christ Himself.
