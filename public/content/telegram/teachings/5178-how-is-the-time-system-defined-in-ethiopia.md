(Part 4)

Beloved children of the Church, heirs of the ancient faith of the fathers, peace and grace be multiplied unto you. Today we turn to a matter profound and wondrous: the understanding of time itself, and how our Ethiopian tradition preserved through the wisdom of the saints and scholars defines, measures, and sanctifies it.

For time is no mere invention of men, but a gift from God, a framework within which the mystery of salvation unfolds. As the Psalmist testifies: “Of old Thou didst lay the foundation of the earth, and the heavens are the work of Thy hands. They shall perish, but Thou shalt endure; yea, all of them shall wax old like a garment, and as a vesture shalt Thou change them, and they shall be changed. But Thou art the same, and Thy years shall have no end” (Ps. 101:24–28).

Thus, while the heavens and the earth pass away, the Lord remains immutable; and man, His creature, moves through the appointed times until he returns to the Maker.

The Scholar of Time: Abushakhr Wolde Abel Herem

The Scholar of Time: Abushakhr Wolde Abel Herem

Among those who reflected on the divine order of times was Abushakhr Wolde Abel Herem, also remembered as Yohannes Fiqrum. He was a mathematician and philosopher, one who delved into the mystery of chronology and the reckoning of days. His work, published in 1962, builds upon the study of ancient systems of time.

Nine scholars,  examined and commented on his numbers and ideas:

Seid Welde Bettrik

Mahboub Menbegawi

Seidawyan  Jews

Yohannes Afewerk

Qerlos

Epiphanios

Yohannes Zedamasko

Markos Welde Qember

Giorgis Welde Amid (also called Hamid)

Though his book is mathematical, Ethiopians also count it as a poetic book, because it explains time in a way that praises God’s creation.

The Threefold Division of Time

Abushakhr explains that time, when measured by the conscience, divides itself into three great periods:

1. The time of the present – that which we see and live in.

2. The time of the past – that which has been and is remembered.

3. The time of the prophet (the future) – that which is yet to come and is foretold.

Thus, human thought, word, and action are bound in time. Our life is measured in ages, our deeds unfold in succession, and our salvation is worked out in the history God governs.

Moses himself, speaking in Deuteronomy, bears witness to this mystery: “Since the day that God created man upon the earth, and from one end of heaven unto the other, hath there been any such thing as this great thing is, or hath been heard like it?” (Deut. 4:32).

The Year of the World

The Ethiopian system teaches that from Adam to Christ there are 5,500 years. This is called the Year of Judgment (Fda). From the birth of Christ until now, we live in the Year of Mercy, the age of grace brought forth by the Incarnation.

The union of these two gives us the Year of the World (Amete Alem):

Year of the World = Year of Mercy + Year of Judgment

Thus, in the year 2018 A.D., the calculation is:

2018 + 5500 = 7518

The Evangelical Cycle

Time is also sanctified through the witness of the Four Evangelists. The Year of the World is divided by four, revealing which Gospel governs the age.

2018 + 5500 = 7518

7518 ÷ 4 = 1879 {remainder 2}

7518 - ( 4 × 1879)

         = 7518 -  7516=2

If remainder = 1 → the time of Matthew

If remainder = 2 → the time of Mark

If remainder = 3 → the time of Luke

If remainder = 0 → the time of John

Thus, since the remainder is 2, the year 2018 belongs to Mark, the Evangelist of boldness and action. This is the Time of Mark.

Even if we divide only the Year of Mercy (2018) by four, the result is the same: remainder 2 again, the time of Mark.
