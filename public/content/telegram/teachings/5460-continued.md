Continued 👇🏾

9.In Jeremiah 42:1–12, all the people, from the least to the greatest, came to Jeremiah and said, “Please let our supplication come before you, and pray to the Lord your God for us, that the Lord your God may show us the way in which we should walk and the thing we should do.” Jeremiah answered, “Behold, I will pray to the Lord your God according to your words, and whatever He answers, I will declare to you; I will not hide anything from you.”

10.In the New Testament, a Canaanite woman cried out, “Have mercy on me, O Lord, Son of David! My daughter is severely demon-possessed.” When our Lord was silent, His disciples came and pleaded, saying, “Send her away, for she cries out after us.” The Lord, seeing the woman’s faith and hearing the intercession of His apostles, healed her daughter (Matthew 15:21–28).

11.When the Jews stoned St. Stephen, he knelt and prayed, saying, “Lord, do not hold this sin against them” (Acts 7:60).


12.St. James gave apostolic instruction, saying, “Pray for one another, that you may be healed. The prayer of a righteous man avails much” (James 5:16).

✍🏾 Accordingly, the prayers of the saints for sinners are heard and accepted by God. Therefore, it is wise and beneficial to seek their intercession. The saints not only intercede while they live in the flesh, but also after their departure, for they are alive in the Spirit before God.

God said to Moses,
👉🏽 “I have made you as God to Pharaoh” (Exodus 7:1).

And our Lord said to His apostles,
👉🏽 “You who have followed Me will sit on twelve thrones, judging the twelve tribes of Israel” (Matthew 19:28–29).

St. Paul also teaches,
👉🏽 “Do you not know that the saints will judge the world?” (1 Corinthians 6:2).

Therefore, if the saints have been given the authority to judge as “gods of grace,” how much more will their prayers and intercessions bring forgiveness and mercy upon sinners through the compassion of our Lord.
