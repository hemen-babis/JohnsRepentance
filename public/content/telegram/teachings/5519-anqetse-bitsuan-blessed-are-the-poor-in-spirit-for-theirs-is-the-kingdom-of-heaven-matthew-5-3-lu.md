👉🏾👉🏾👉🏾(#Anqetse #Bitsuan)#Blessed are the #poor in #spirit, for theirs is the #kingdom of #heaven(Matthew 5:3; Luke 6:20)

(Part 2)

Dear beloved in Christ, peace and grace to you. Today, we continue our reflection on our Lord’s words: 
“Blessed are the poor in spirit, for theirs is the kingdom of heaven.”

Let us now learn from the holy examples of the Publican, the Thief on the Cross, the Canaanite Woman, and Saint Paul, who were all exalted through their humility.

To be poor in spirit means to recognize our nothingness before God, to depend entirely on His mercy, and to cast away every prideful thought. It is the root of true humility. The Scriptures and the lives of the saints give us many examples of such humility.

The Humility of the Publican (Luke 18:9–14)

Our Lord told this parable to those who trusted in their own righteousness and looked down on others:

“Two men went up to the temple to pray, one a Pharisee and the other a publican.”

The Pharisee stood and prayed proudly, saying:

“God, I thank You that I am not like other men extortioners, unjust, adulterers or even like this publican. I fast twice a week; I give tithes of all that I possess.”

His words were full of pride and self-glory. He compared himself to others, thinking his good works made him righteous. But the publican stood afar off, not daring even to lift his eyes to heaven. He beat his chest and said:

“God, be merciful to me, a sinner.”

The Lord said,

“This man went down to his house justified rather than the other. For everyone who exalts himself will be humbled, and he who humbles himself will be exalted.”

The publican’s prayer, offered from a humble and broken heart, was accepted by God. His humility opened the door of forgiveness and righteousness.

The Humility of the Thief on the Cross (Luke 23:39–43)

When our Lord was crucified, two thieves were crucified beside Him. One mocked the Lord, saying:

“If You are the Christ, save Yourself and us!”

But the other rebuked him and said:

"Do you not even fear God, seeing that you are under the same condemnation? we receive the due reward of our deeds; but this Man has done nothing wrong.”

He recognized his sins and accepted his punishment humbly. Then he turned to the Lord in faith and said:

“Lord, remember me when You come into Your kingdom.”

And Jesus answered him, saying:

“Truly, I say to you, today you will be with Me in Paradise.”

Through his humble confession, the thief was forgiven and entered eternal joy. Even on the cross, humility led him to salvation.

The Humility of the Canaanite Woman (Matthew 15:22–28)

A Canaanite woman came to our Lord, crying:

“Lord, have mercy on me, Son of David; my daughter is grievously tormented by a demon.”

At first, the Lord did not answer her. The disciples urged Him to send her away, but she continued to plead. Then Jesus said:

“I was sent only to the lost sheep of the house of Israel.”

Yet she came closer, knelt before Him, and said:

“Lord, help me.”

The Lord said,

“It is not right to take the children’s bread and throw it to the dogs.”

But she humbly replied:

“Yes, Lord, yet even the dogs eat the crumbs that fall from their masters’ table.”

Her humility was greater than her suffering. She accepted even to be called a dog if only she could receive God’s mercy. Then Jesus said to her:

“O woman, great is your faith! Let it be to you as you desire.”

And her daughter was healed at that very hour.

This woman became poor in spirit, acknowledging her unworthiness before God, and for this reason, she was exalted. As Saint James teaches:

“Humble yourselves in the sight of the Lord, and He will lift you up.” (James 4:10)
