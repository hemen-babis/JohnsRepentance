2. Mystery of Incarnation

(Part 5)

The next major pillar, among the 5 pillar mysteries is called  'Mystery of Incarnation'. Mystery of Incarnation  basically defines the religious and spiritual identity of Orthodox Christianity. Through incarnation, mysteries of the kingdom of God were revealed to human beings. It’s on the basis of this mystery that our apostolic orthodox church got the prefix in its name “Tewahedo”. Tewahedo is a Geez word which represents the inseparable unity between Divinity and humanity in the person of our Lord and Saviour Jesus Christ.

This is a teaching about the revelation of God to human beings. This is the Word, one part of the Holy Trinity who never man. “No man hath seen God at any time; the only begotten Son, which is in the bosom of the Father, he hath declared him.” (John 1:18) His coming is basically to save the world, to reveal his love, a religion where we declare that he is a perfect man. It reveals the reality of redemption of Adam's sin, about how God became Man. He loved mankind and was born of the Holy Virgin Mary to save mankind whose grace was rejected and was about to be crushed by the feet of demons. The other reason He chose to become Human is to reveal his love to mankind. He loved man to the extent of being crucified on the cross.  (Hos 14:4 John 3:16 1John 4:19 Romans 5:8 Ephe 2:4). "When the end of the age came, God sent forth his Son, born of a woman, born under the law." As Galatians 4:4 says. The other  reason of His being human is to become an ultimate role model for human beings, (Gen 3:12 4:8 13:13 Ps 61:4 Matt 11:29 2Peter 2:21)., God taught us everything we need to do to become his children.  

We believe that God the Son (Jesus Christ) has two births.  In the pre world, God the Son, has been begotten from God the Father without a mother. In the latter days, through incarnation, He was born from the Holy Virgin Mary with Virginal conception and without a human fatherhood. Then after, the incarnated God the Son, was named Jesus (savior), Christ (the anointed one or Messiah.), Emmanuel (God is with us) and there are other names too, among them Orthodox Christians usually call Him 'Our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ' (Medicine of the world). (Galatians 4:4 Isa 7:14 Gen 3:15 18:13 12:8 Ps 107:20).
 
In the mystery of the flesh, the divinity, which is invisible in its nature, was manifested by the incarnation of the flesh. The intangible was grasped, beaten, the immortal died in the flesh, the unhungry hungered and thirsted, the draft smote. The flesh was made alive by the divinity, the flesh to which the ages are counted, and the divinity to whom the ages are not counted (was before all ages), was called the first. Adam became one of us.” According to Genesis 3:22, the first deity was reckoned at the time of his incarnation, the son of God the Father became the son of the Virgin Mary. The Son of a Virgin was called the Son of God the Father.  

Based on Orthodox Tewahdo teaching, incarnation is from two Persons to one Person; and from two natures to one united nature. It means Divine Person and Human united in to one hypostasis, Jesus Christ – He is both perfectly Divine and perfectly Human. Likewise, from two natures to one nature means Divine nature and Human nature perfectly united into one united nature. We believe that this incarnation is without alteration/change, without reversion, without mingling/mixing, without dwelling, without superimposing, without separation, and without detachment.

The Mystery of Baptism
The mystery of baptism is one of the five pillars of mysteries in the Orthodox Tewahdo church. Baptism means immersion in blessed water (in the name of the Holy Trinity). Adam and Eve, by their choice, became slaves to the devil's will. The Lord appeared to save us, and at the age of 30, he was baptized in the Jordan Sea by the hands of John.

The Lord is naturally compassionate, and He wasn't baptized because He needed it. He washed away our sins by being baptized, but not for the sake of adding glory to himself. He had nothing to lack or add to himself because he was baptized or not. He prophesied that he would baptize. Because he pretended to be an example, he was baptized by the hand of John in the Jordan Sea and became an example for us. And so do we. “And now why are you waiting? Arise and be baptized, and wash away your sins, calling on the name of the Lord." According to the instructions of Acts 22:16, we are baptized in the name of the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit.

The mystery of baptism is established by our Lord and Savior, Jesus Christ. He established it in three ways: by performance, preaching, and giving commission to the church. He established baptism by being our role model and getting baptized by John the Baptist in the Jordan River. (Matthew 3) He cemented the place of baptism in Christian life by establishing baptism through His preaching, as he said, “He who believes and is baptized will be saved; but he who does not believe will be condemned.” Mark 16:16 says, “Unless one is born of water and the Spirit, he cannot enter the kingdom of God.” John 3:3-6. He also commissioned the Church to perform baptism as He ordered his disciples: “Go therefore and make disciples of all the nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit.” Matt 28:19

So, for all who believe in the mystery of the Trinity and the mystery of our incarnation, baptism is our power to erase sin, to receive childhood, and to inherit the kingdom of heaven. "He who believes and is baptized will be saved; but he who does not believe will be condemned." Mar. 16:16. Baptism is the gate, door, or point of entry to Christianity. One cannot be a Christian without baptism. It’s the first sacrament to be performed for all Christians.

We also believe in baptism for the remission of sins. St. Peter told the people to repent and be baptized: “Repent, and let every one of you be baptized in the name of Jesus Christ for the remission of sins.” Acts 2:38. This is a type of baptism for the remission of sins; there is another type of baptism in which we are baptized on a regular basis for blessing and healing from sickness; the other is sprinkling with holy water to be blessed in holy water during Epiphany to celebrate Jesus' baptism every year; all of these types of baptisms are reputable. But baptism of rebirth, to which we refer in this topic, is the most important of all these types of regular baptism; baptism of rebirth is a necessity for salvation. It’s not just a mere ceremony. It is the type of baptism that is at the heart of church teachings on the Mystery of Baptism.
