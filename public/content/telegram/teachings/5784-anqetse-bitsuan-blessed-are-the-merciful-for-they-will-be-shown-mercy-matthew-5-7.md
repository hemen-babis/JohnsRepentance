👉🏾👉🏾👉🏾 (#Anqetse #Bitsuan)“#Blessed are the #merciful, for they will be shown #mercy” (Matthew 5:7)

(Part 26)

Dear followers of the Orthodox teachings , we greet you with the peace of the Lord. Today, based on the topic mentioned above, we present to you the following important lesson. We encourage everyone to read it carefully and learn from it.

✍🏾Ruth the Moabite

Ruth showed deep mercy toward Naomi, who had lost her husband and her two sons. Though Ruth herself had nothing, she chose to care for Naomi with unwavering devotion. She left her homeland, Moab, and journeyed with her to Bethlehem.

When Naomi urged her to return to her own people, Orpah turned back, but Ruth remained faithful to the end. Her kindness and sacrifice made her worthy of the blessing spoken to her:
“May the Lord reward you according to your work, and may your reward be full from the Lord, the God of Israel, under whose wings you have come to seek refuge.” (Ruth 2:12)

✍🏾The Widow of Zarephath

During the great famine in the days of Elijah, the widow of Zarephath had only a handful of flour and a little oil left. She told the prophet,“I am gathering sticks to prepare a meal for myself and my son, that we may eat it and die.” (1 Kings 17:12)

Even in such desperate poverty, she shared what little she had with Elijah. Because of her mercy and obedience, God blessed her household throughout the famine.

Our Lord Himself confirmed her blessedness, saying that Elijah was sent not to any widow in Israel, but to the widow of Zarephath (Luke 4:26).

✍🏾The Poor Widow

While our Lord was teaching, a poor widow came and placed two small copper coins into the treasury. Christ called His disciples and said:

“Truly I tell you, this poor widow has put in more than all the others. For they gave out of their abundance, but she, out of her poverty, put in all she had to live on.”(Mark 12:42–44; Luke 21:1–4)

Her offering became a symbol of pure, heartfelt sacrifice. It is remembered in the intercessory prayer found over 260 times in the Book of Acts:

“Lord, accept the thanksgiving I offer in my time of need, remembering the thousands of angels who serve You at all times, just as You accepted the small offering of the poor woman more than those who gave from their abundance.”

✍🏾Tabitha (Dorcas)

Tabitha, also called Dorcas, lived in Joppa and was known for her abundant good works and almsgiving. Scripture says:

“Now in Joppa there was a certain Tabitha, full of good works and almsgiving. In those days she fell sick and died. And they laid her in an upper chamber. And when the disciples heard that Peter was near Joppa, they sent two men to him, begging him not to delay in coming to them. And Peter arose and went with them. And when he was come, they brought him up into the upper chamber: and all the widows stood before him weeping, and showing the coats and garments which Dorcas had made while she was with them. And Peter put them all forth, and kneeled down, and prayed; and turning to the body, he said, ‘Tabitha, arise.’ And she opened her eyes: and when she saw Peter, she sat up. And he gave her his hand, and lifted her up; and when he had called the saints and widows, he presented her alive.” (Acts 9:36–43)
