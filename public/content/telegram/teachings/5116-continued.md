5. Words of Worship

The prayers of the Church guide our bows and prostrations. When we prostrate always we need to say:-

I prostrate before the Father.

I prostrate before the Son.

I prostrate before the Holy Spirit.

And we proclaim:

Glory be to the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit.

Hallelujah to the Father, Hallelujah to the Son, Hallelujah to the Holy Spirit.

Holy God, Holy Mighty, Holy Living, Holy El Shaddai, Holy Adonai, Holy Yahweh, Holy Hosts, Holy Jesus, Holy Christ, Holy Emmanuel glory to the Son of the Virgin Mary.

6. The Penitential Adoration

In times of repentance we cry out with bowed bodies and contrite hearts:

Kyrie Eleison, Jesus.

Kyrie Eleison, Christ.

Kyrie Eleison, Emmanuel.

Lord Christ, have mercy.

Lord God, have mercy.

Lord Savior, have mercy.

Lord Son of the Virgin Mary, have mercy.

These words, joined with prostration, are the visible tears of the soul, pleading for mercy from our Savior.

Conclusion

Beloved, prostration is not an empty motion of the body, but a holy act of faith. In it we confess our weakness, honor God’s majesty, revere His saints, and repent of our sins. It is rooted in Scripture, practiced by the prophets, embraced by the apostles, and ordered by the Church.

Let us therefore bow with understanding: fully to God in worship, to His saints in reverence, and in repentance for our sins. For in every bow, we humble ourselves; in every kneeling, we confess Christ’s lordship; in every rising, we proclaim our hope in His resurrection.
