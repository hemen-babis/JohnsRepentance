5. . The Wickedness of Herod and the Martyrdom of the Innocents

✍🏾As the prophet Hosea declares, “I have called my son out of Egypt for the glory of the saints” (Hosea 11:1–2). From the very beginning, the life of our Lord Jesus Christ fulfilled the sacred prophecies. His exile to Egypt was not a mere historical event, but a divine plan ordained to glorify God and prepare the salvation of humanity. The prophets foretold that the Messiah would be delivered even from danger, demonstrating God’s providence and care for His creation.

6,Prophecy and Fulfillment: The Flight into Egypt

✍🏾When our Lord was born in Bethlehem, the kingdom trembled before His coming. Herod, in jealousy and fear of losing power, sought to kill the newborn King. Yet God’s timing is perfect, and the Messiah would not shed His blood before the appointed hour on the Cross. By the word of the angel Gabriel, Joseph was commanded: “Take the child and his mother, and flee into Egypt, for Herod seeks to kill the child.” Obediently, Joseph took Mary and the Infant at night, fleeing danger with faith and vigilance.

✍🏾During this time, the Virgin Mary’s heart was full of sorrow for the dangers surrounding her Son, yet she remained steadfast in her trust in God. The Church remembers her lamentations in hymns, offering a model of courage, obedience, and devotion. Meanwhile, the wise men, guided by the star of the East, sought the newborn King, bearing gifts of gold, frankincense, and myrrh. Even those outside Israel recognized the divinity of Christ, while Herod, blinded by envy, sought the destruction of innocents, fulfilling the prophecy of Jeremiah: “A voice was heard in Ramah, weeping and great mourning…”

✍🏾God, in His providence, preserved the Child. After three years of exile, Mary and Joseph returned safely to Nazareth. This return symbolizes the triumph of divine protection, the fulfillment of prophecy, and the establishment of God’s plan for redemption. It reminds us that even in trials and danger, God’s will is perfect and His care is unwavering.

7. The Commemoration and Fasting of the Faithful

✍🏾The Ethiopian Orthodox Church commemorate Mary’s journey from Bethlehem to Egypt to protect the Infant Jesus from the wrath of Herod, and their subsequent return to Nazareth after the death of the tyrant. 

✍🏾During these forty days, believers offer praise and worship, recounting the story of Mary’s flight and return. Many also observe voluntary fasting, honoring our Lady, seeking closeness to God, and cultivating humility and spiritual discipline. As Saint John Chrysostom would exhort, the fast is meaningful not when it is outward only, but when it transforms the heart, drawing the faithful nearer to Christ and His holy Mother.
