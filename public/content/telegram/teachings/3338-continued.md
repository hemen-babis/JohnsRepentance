5. The Supreme Goal of Love

Love, dear brethren, is the crown of Christian virtue. If we have not love, we have nothing, for it is love that brought God from His throne to the cross. “Greater love has no one than this, that someone lay down his life for his friends” (John 15:13). As Christians, we are called to love deeply, not merely in word but in deed. Love is the true manifestation of our faith, for it reflects the very nature of God. And whom do we love?

5.1 Loving God by Obeying His Commandments

Our love for God is shown not merely through emotion but through obedience. “He who has My commandments and keeps them is the one who loves Me” (John 14:21). How can we claim to love God if we do not follow His commands? Therefore, our goal must be to live in such a way that we fulfill His laws, for they are not burdensome but the path to true freedom.

5.2 Loving All That is God’s

To love God is to love all that belongs to Him. This includes honoring His saints, cherishing our nation, upholding the family, and loving all humanity.

5.2.1 Loving and Honoring the Saints

The saints are those whom God has set apart, honored, and glorified. “Those whom He predestined, He also called, justified, and glorified” (Romans 8:29). We honor the saints because they are reflections of God’s grace. Their lives encourage us to pursue holiness, and their intercessions uplift us in our struggles. Let our hearts be ever inclined to honor them, not with mere words, but with the humility that comes from knowing we are part of a great communion of faith.

5.2.2 Loving One’s Country

Our nation, too, is a gift from God. In it, we find the peace and order necessary to worship and serve Him. The Psalmist cried, “If I forget you, O Jerusalem, let my right hand forget its skill” (Psalm 137:5). So, too, must we love our nation, for through it we can offer our thanks to God in peace and joy. A Christian must be a faithful citizen, upholding justice and seeking the welfare of the land, for the peace of the country enables the peace of the Church.

5.2.3 Loving the Family

The family is the first school of faith, where we learn the commandments of God and the virtues of love, patience, and humility. “If anyone does not provide for his relatives, and especially for members of his household, he has denied the faith and is worse than an unbeliever” (1 Timothy 5:8). Our love for God is mirrored in our care for our families, for through them we learn the love that binds us to Christ.

5.2.4 Loving All Humanity

Finally, the Christian heart is one that loves all people, even enemies. “Love your enemies and pray for those who persecute you” (Matthew 5:44). This is the height of Christian love, to embrace those who hate us and to return curses with blessings. In this way, we reflect the love of Christ, who died for us while we were still sinners.

6. Christian Life as a Public Witness

It is not enough to cultivate these virtues in private; they must be evident in every aspect of our lives. From the way we dress to the way we speak, a Christian’s life should be a clear testimony of faith. We must live in such a way that our Christianity is visible to all. “Let your light shine before men” (Matthew 5:16). In this, we fulfill the call to be a light to the world, that through us, others may come to know Christ.

7. Examining Ourselves in Personal and Social Life

In both our personal and social lives, we must constantly examine ourselves in light of these goals. In our personal lives, we are to repent, fast, pray, and partake of the sacraments, ever striving toward the heavenly kingdom. In our social lives, we must comfort the sorrowful, rejoice with those who rejoice, and offer help to those in need. For in doing these things, we carry within us the death and resurrection of Christ, revealing His life in our mortal bodies (2 Corinthians 4:10).

Beloved, let these goals guide your every step, for they are the marks of a true Christian, one who lives not for this world but for the glory of the world to come. Amen.
