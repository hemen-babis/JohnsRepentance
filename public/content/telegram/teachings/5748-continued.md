5. He Who Does Not Show Mercy Will Not Receive Mercy

The wise Solomon said, “Whoever shuts his ears to the cry of the poor will also cry himself and not be heard” (Proverbs 21:13). One who refuses mercy will not find mercy.

The rich man was condemned for not showing mercy to Lazarus (Luke 16:19–31). A person who denies mercy not only loses the mercy due to them but also risks having the mercy previously given taken away. God shows mercy so that we may imitate Him toward our neighbor.


Our Lord taught this clearly through a parable:
“The kingdom of heaven is like a king who wished to settle accounts with his servants. As he began the reckoning, one was brought to him who owed ten thousand talents. Since he could not pay, the master commanded that he, his wife, his children, and all he had be sold so that payment might be made.

The servant fell before him and pleaded, ‘Lord, have patience with me, and I will repay you everything.’ The master was moved with compassion, released him, and forgave the entire debt.

But that same servant went out and found a fellow servant who owed him one hundred denarii. He seized him and began to choke him, saying, ‘Pay me what you owe.’ His fellow servant fell at his feet and begged him, ‘Have patience with me, and I will repay you.’ Yet he refused and threw him into prison until the debt was paid.

When the other servants saw what had happened, they were deeply grieved, and they reported everything to their master. The master summoned him and said, ‘You wicked servant! I forgave you all that debt because you pleaded with me. Should you not also have had mercy on your fellow servant, just as I had mercy on you?’ In anger, the master delivered him to the tormentors until he should repay all that was owed.

Our Lord concludes: ‘So will My heavenly Father do to each of you, if you do not forgive your brother from your heart.’”
(Matthew 18:23–35)
