Dear followers of the Orthodox teaching we greet you with the peace and blessing of God. Today, based on the topic mentioned above, we present to you the following important teaching, and we encourage you all to read, reflect, and learn from it.

✍🏾Blessed Are Those Who Mourn

“Blessed are those who mourn, for they will be comforted.”
(Matthew 5:4; Luke 6:21)

Is the Christian life a life of sorrow and mourning? Is joy a sin? Absolutely not. Joy is not a sin. In fact, the Holy Bible considers joy a fruit of the Spirit, as written in Galatians 5:22.

Our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ taught His disciples about spiritual joy, saying:

“I will see you again, and your heart will rejoice, and no one will take your joy from you.”
(John 16:22)

Likewise, the Apostle Saint Paul said of eternal joy:

“Rejoice in the Lord always; again I will say, rejoice.”(Philippians 4:4)


Our Lord also spoke of the heavenly joy that comes from repentance:

“There will be more joy in heaven over one sinner who repents than over ninety-nine righteous persons who need no repentance.”
(Luke 15:7)

Another spiritual joy is found in seeing others grow in their spiritual lives. Thus, the Apostle John said:

“I rejoiced greatly when the brothers came and testified of your truth, that you walk in the truth. I have no greater joy than to hear that my children walk in the truth.” (3 John 2–4)

Spiritual and Physical Sorrow

When our Lord said, “Blessed are those who mourn,” He was not speaking of physical sorrow, but of spiritual sorrow.

Physical sorrow is the sadness we feel when a parent, child, relative, or friend dies, or when we lose something we hoped for. But spiritual sorrow is much deeper. It is the sorrow a person feels for his own sins, for the sins of others, and for the suffering of the Son of God. This sorrow is not despair but repentance that leads to salvation.

Saint Paul teaches:

“Godly sorrow produces repentance leading to salvation, not to be regretted; but worldly sorrow produces death.”(2 Corinthians 7:10)

✍🏾Mourning That Leads to Repentance

While we live on this earth, we must come to repentance through mourning and tears for our sins. When our Lord carried the Cross to Calvary, He said to the women who were weeping for Him:

“Daughters of Jerusalem, do not weep for Me, but weep for yourselves and for your children. For the days are coming when they will say, ‘Blessed are the barren, the wombs that never bore, and the breasts that never nursed.’”
(Luke 23:28–29)

The Apostle Paul also teaches us to share in the sorrows of others:

“Weep with those who weep.”
(Romans 12:15)


And Saint James exhorts us to weep over our sins:

“Cleanse your hands, you sinners; purify your hearts, you double-minded. Be afflicted and mourn and weep; let your laughter be turned to mourning and your joy to gloom. Humble yourselves before the Lord, and He will lift you up.”(James 4:8–10)

He further warns the rich, saying:

“Come now, you rich, weep and howl for the miseries that are coming upon you.”(James 5:1)
