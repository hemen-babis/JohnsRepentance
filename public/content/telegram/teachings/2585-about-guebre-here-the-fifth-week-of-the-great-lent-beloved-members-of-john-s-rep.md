About #Guebre_Hère; the fifth week of the #Great #Lent 

Beloved members of John's Repentance spiritual service, as we dwell upon the teachings of the fifth week of Lent, known reverently as Guebre Hère, let us immerse ourselves in the profound insights of the Parable of the Talents, as rendered in the Gospel according to Saint Matthew, chapter 25, verses 14 through 30. This parable, bestowed upon us by our Lord Jesus Christ, is a beacon of spiritual guidance, particularly during this penitential season.

In this sacred parable, a man, about to journey afar, entrusts his property to his servants. To one he grants five talents, to another two, and to another one, each according to his unique ability. Upon his return, he assesses what has been achieved with his assets. The servants who received five and two talents respectively, engage diligently in their tasks, each doubling what was entrusted to them, and are thus richly praised and rewarded: "Well done, good and faithful servant. You have been faithful over a little; I will set you over much. Enter into the joy of your master." The servant who received one talent, however, out of fear, conceals his talent in the earth and is severely reproached and expelled into the darkness.

From this narrative, essential truths unfold:

1. Divine Entrustment of Capacities: The master distributes talents in alignment with the servant's abilities, mirroring how our Heavenly Father endows us with spiritual gifts tailored to our capacities. Each of us is given specific talents, meticulously aligned with divine intent and purpose, as Saint Paul articulates in his letter to the Corinthians.

2. The Imperative of Stewardship: The expectation of the master that the talents be not merely preserved but augmented underscores our duty to enhance and amplify the gifts bestowed upon us. These gifts, both spiritual and temporal, are granted not for idleness but for active and fruitful multiplication in God’s service.

3. Accountability before God: The master’s return and settlement of accounts with his servants highlight the unavoidable truth of divine judgment. Each of us must live with the awareness that we shall stand before God to account for our stewardship, urging us to live each day with zeal and commitment to His divine will.

4. Reward for the Diligent: The master’s generous commendation of the faithful servants exemplifies God's boundless grace towards those who labor earnestly in His vineyard. The joy promised by the master symbolizes the eternal happiness that awaits the righteous, a reward infinitely surpassing earthly pleasures.

5. The Consequence of Neglect: The chastisement of the servant who hid his talent is a stern admonition against negligence and fear. We are called to act boldly, employing our talents in the propagation of faith and spiritual advancement, rather than succumbing to fear or complacency.

Let us, therefore, brothers and sisters, draw inspiration from the essence of Guebre Hère, the exemplar who maximized the potential of what was entrusted to him. Let us introspect on our own talents, whether they be of spiritual insight, material means, intellectual acumen, or relational capacities, and consider how we might employ these gifts for the greater glory of God and the betterment of humanity.

May this contemplation on the Parable of the Talents embolden us to be like Guebre Hère, wisely and vigorously investing in the gifts we have received. Let us shun the path of the fearful servant and aspire to hear those blessed words from our Lord, "Well done, good and faithful servant... Enter into the joy of your master."

In this sacred season of reflection, may our hearts be stirred to fuller dedication and purpose, ever mindful of the watchful gaze of our Savior, who measures our faithfulness in the stewardship of His gifts. For it is in these faithful endeavors that we sow the seeds of our eternal felicity in the kingdom of our Lord and Savior, Jesus Christ. Amen.
