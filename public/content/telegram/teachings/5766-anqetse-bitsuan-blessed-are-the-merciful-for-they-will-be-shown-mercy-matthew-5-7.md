👉🏾👉🏾👉🏾 (#Anqetse #Bitsuan)“#Blessed are the #merciful, for they will be shown #mercy” (Matthew 5:7)

(Part 25)

Dear followers of the Orthodox teachings , we greet you with the peace of the Lord. Today, based on the topic mentioned above, we present to you the following important lesson. We encourage everyone to read it carefully and learn from it.

6. Showing Mercy Made the Samaritan Stand Out From the Priest and the Levite

Our Lord teaches that mercy toward those in need reveals the true servant of God. In the Parable of the Good Samaritan (Luke 10:30–37), He said:

✍🏾A man went down from Jerusalem to Jericho, and fell among robbers who stripped him, beat him, and left him half dead. A priest happened to come down that way, saw him, and passed by on the other side. Likewise, a Levite came, saw him, and passed by. But a Samaritan, as he traveled, came to where the man was; and when he saw him, he had compassion. He approached him, bound his wounds, pouring on oil and wine. He placed him on his own donkey, brought him to an inn, and cared for him. The next day he took out two denarii and gave them to the innkeeper, saying, “Take care of him; and whatever more you spend, when I return, I will repay you.”

After telling the parable, the Lord said:

“Which of these three was a neighbor to the man who fell among the robbers?”The reply was, “He who showed mercy.”Then the Lord said, “Go and do likewise.”

In this, Christ teaches that what made the Samaritan more favored than the priest and the Levite was his kindness and compassion. Mercy, not position or title, makes one pleasing to God.

Saint Paul gives the same instruction when he says:
“Remember the prisoners as if you were bound with them, and those who suffer, as if you yourselves were in the body.” (Hebrews 13:3)
We are called to stand with the suffering as though their pain were our own.

7. A Heart Filled With Mercy Shows Compassion Even to Sinners

A merciful heart does not measure a person by their failures. Instead, it sees the image of God in every soul and desires their restoration.

When the scribes and Pharisees brought to Christ the woman caught in adultery, they demanded judgment. Christ instead revealed the truth of all humanity: “Let the one who is without sin be the first to cast a stone.” When they left one by one, acknowledging their own sin, Christ told the woman, “Neither do I condemn you; go, and sin no more.” (John 8:11)

He teaches us not to stand as judges like the Pharisees, but to offer mercy as He did.
