IV. New Testament Thursday – The Fulfillment of the Law

On this sacred day, the old was fulfilled, and the new was born. The blood of bulls and goats was silenced forever, and the Lamb of God stepped forward.

“This cup is the new covenant in My blood, which is shed for you.” (Luke 22:20)

The Torah, with its shadows and sacrifices, bowed before the reality of Christ’s self-offering. No longer would men look to altars of stone, but to the Cross. No longer would they seek animal blood, but the pure, eternal blood of the Son of God.

This day binds heaven and earth in covenant. The blood of Christ sealed the eternal promise to Adam and all his descendants: “I am yours, and you are Mine.” Christians are not mere beneficiaries of this covenant we are its guardians. Woe to us if we forget!

V. Liberation Thursday – The Day of Freedom

Here lies the true Passover. Not from Egypt to Canaan, but from slavery to sin into freedom in Christ. The Master did not call us servants but friends.

“I no longer call you servants… but I have called you friends.” (John 15:15)

The whip of Pharaoh is broken. The chains of Satan are shattered. Sin no longer has dominion. Let no Christian return to slavery! Let no soul dwell in the Egypt of old habits!

“Every Christian should live his life in holiness before God, avoiding the slavery of sin, for He made us His friends when we were His enemies.” (Matthew 26:17-19)

This is a day of liberation. Yet liberation is not lawlessness, but holiness. We were freed not to wander, but to worship.

VI. Maundy Thursday – The Day of Sacred Prayer

Let it be remembered: our Lord prayed. He who spoke galaxies into being knelt in prayer. He who needed nothing prayed with urgency. He who could command angels chose instead to commune with the Father.

Prayer is no minor act. It is the heartbeat of the Christian life. On Maundy Thursday, He taught us that no trial can be faced without prayer. Let every Christian take refuge in Gethsemane and say with Christ, “Not My will, but Yours be done.”

A Final Exhortation

Beloved brethren, let us not treat this holy day lightly. Let us not approach it with routine or distraction. For on this Thursday, eternity bent low, and the heavens trembled. Let us approach it with fasting, prayer, repentance, and reverence.

Be washed. Be fed. Be humbled. Be liberated. Be united with Christ.

For the Lord who washed feet also washes hearts. The Lord who wept in the garden now rejoices at our return. The Lord who offered Himself calls us to the same table.

May this Semune Hemamat not pass us by as another commemoration, but be a living flame in our souls.

May the name of the Holy God be praised forever and ever in every tongue that He has created, today and always. 

May the blessings of God's mother, the virgin, and the cross be upon us.
Amen.
