3. The Third Persecution: For the Sake of Righteousness The Pursuit and Service of Holiness

The third persecution is for the sake of righteousness the suffering borne by those who seek to obtain, teach, or learn righteousness:

To seek righteousness to strive after holiness through repentance, fasting, and prayer, resisting temptation and renouncing sin.

To preach righteousness to proclaim truth to a world that prefers lies, facing misunderstanding,  and rejection for teaching what is just.

To learn righteousness to grow in divine wisdom and knowledge, persevering through trials, fatigue, and spiritual opposition.

Many saints endured persecution in these ways they suffered in their quest to serve Christ faithfully, to instruct others, and to remain steadfast in repentance.
As our Lord promised: “Everyone who has left houses or brothers or sisters or father or mother or wife or children or lands for My name’s sake will receive a hundredfold and will inherit eternal life.” (Matthew 19:29)

4. The Fourth Persecution: From Righteousness  The Fall from Light to Darkness

The fourth is the persecution from righteousness the soul’s exile from holiness into sin and death. This is not the persecution endured by the faithful, but the suffering of those who turn away from God after having known His truth.
It is the torment of Judas, who betrayed his Lord for silver, and of Demas. Their anguish was not for righteousness but from it the bitter harvest of apostasy, where the soul finds no peace because it has abandoned the source of life.

The First Exiles from Righteousness

“The first two exoduses in the history of creation were exoduses from righteousness.”


1. The First Exile: The Fall of Satan

The first exile was the casting down of Satan from the world of angels into the abyss. Once radiant among the hosts of heaven, he grew proud and rebelled against his Creator. 

2. The Second Exile: The Fall of Man

The second exile was the expulsion of mankind from paradise to the earth, caused by the deceit of that same fallen spirit. Adam and Eve, clothed in innocence, broke the divine command and were driven from Eden, exiled to a land of labor and death. Yet even in judgment, God wove mercy.

The great Abba Hiriakos, in the Liturgy of Mary, spoke of this tender mystery:

“You are Adam’s hope when he was exiled from paradise.”

These words honor the Holy Virgin Mary as the comfort and redemption of fallen humanity. For through her obedience, the hope that Adam lost was restored; the gate of paradise, once shut, began to open again through her Son.

Abraham: The Pilgrim of Righteousness

Our father Abraham, too, knew exile not as punishment, but as obedience. God called him from his homeland in Ur of the Chaldeans, saying, “Go from your country to the land that I will show you” (Genesis 12:1). In faith, he left all behind and journeyed to Canaan, and later to Gerar (Genesis 20:1). His wandering was holy; it was the pilgrimage of faith seeking righteousness.

And Scripture bears witness: “Abraham believed God, and it was counted to him for righteousness” (Genesis 15:6). He who left his homeland received a greater inheritance not of soil and stone, but of promise and grace.

The Exiles of Israel

The people of Israel, Abraham’s descendants, also tasted the bitterness of exile. Driven by famine, they went down to Egypt and were enslaved (Genesis 46). Centuries later, again to Babylon, as recorded in 2 Kings 24–25. .
