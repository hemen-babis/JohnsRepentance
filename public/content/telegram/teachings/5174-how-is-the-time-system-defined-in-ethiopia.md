(Part 3)

Brethren beloved in Christ, let us behold the mystery of time, not as a mere counting of hours, but as the wisdom of God written upon creation. For He who set the sun to rule the day and the moon the night has also appointed the months and the years, that man might learn to order his life in holiness. The Ethiopian reckoning of time is not invention of men alone, but a heritage sanctified by labor, by memory, and by faith.

1. The Months and Their Origins in Ge’ez

The year of Ethiopia, preserved in the tongue of the fathers, Ge’ez, is not a mere calendar of convenience, but a mirror of life, labor, and the mysteries of God’s providence. Each month bears a name not chosen at random, but springing from the rhythms of creation and the deeds of man.

Meskerem (1st month) — From Mes’e and Kerem, meaning “the night has passed, the rainy season is over.” Just as Noah beheld the dry land after the flood, so too Meskerem proclaims new beginnings after the darkness of rain.

Tikimt (2nd month) — From "Tekeme", meaning “work, useful time.” This month summons us to diligence, reminding us that man was placed in the garden to labor and keep it.

Hidar (3rd month) — From "Hadere," “to spend the night in the harvest field.” It recalls the toil and vigilance of the farmer, who guards his fruits even through the night.

Tahsas (4th month) — From "Hasese", meaning “searched.” In this month crops are inspected, and the fathers teach that it also signifies the searching of the wise men who sought the newborn Christ. Thus, it binds earthly labor to divine revelation.

Tir (5th month) — From Netere, Teyere, Atreye, meaning “flash, heat, buying season.” The sun burns, and commerce is busy, cattle and grain exchanged. Creation teaches man both need and provision.

Yekatit (6th month) — From Ketete, Ketit, “to nest, to store in the barn.” 

Megabit (7th month) — From "Megebe", “to feed, to eat standing.” A month of strength and nourishment, calling man to rise and be sustained.

Miyazya (8th month) — From "Mehaze", “to be brave, to be strong,” and also remembered as the season of weddings. It binds together courage and joy, the household and the covenant.

Ginbot (9th month) — From "Genebe", “to build, to dig, to prepare the land.” Here we see the farmer clearing fields, just as the soul must prepare its heart for the seed of the Word.

Sene (10th month) — From "Seneye", “beautiful.” Nature itself bursts into glory, creation testifying to the majesty of God.

Hamle (11th month) — From "Hamele", “to harvest, to pick.” The barns are filled, but let us not forget the greater harvest, when the Lord of the vineyard shall return for His fruit.

Nehase (12th month) — From Anahsye, Nehase, “to lighten, to ease.” The heavy rains depart, and the earth breathes relief. 

Pagume (13th month) — From the Greek Epagumenos, “to add.” It is an addition, a reminder that God’s times are not bound by man, but that He adds as He wills.

Thus each month is a sermon in itself, and a chapter of creation.
