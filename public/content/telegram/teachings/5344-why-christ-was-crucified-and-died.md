👉🏾👉🏾👉🏾Why #Christ Was #Crucified and Died

(Part 9)

Beloved children of the Holy Orthodox Church, let your hearts be lifted to the mystery of the Cross the tree of life planted anew in the place where Adam fell. Through it, death was slain by death, and corruption was trampled by incorruptibility. Let us behold with trembling joy the purpose of our Lord’s crucifixion and death, for every wound of His flesh became the healing of the nations.

2. The Cross Restores the Food of Life

Sin entered the world and brought death because of food.
In Genesis 3:1–24, Adam and Eve ate the fruit of the fig tree and brought death upon humankind. Likewise, in 1 Kings 13:20–25, the man of Judah who was sent to rebuke Jeroboam lost his life because he disobeyed and ate what was forbidden.

The manna that the Israelites ate in the wilderness did not save them from death (John 6:49). Therefore, Jesus gave us His own flesh as the Bread of Life on the cross, so that whoever eats of it may not die. He confirmed this when He said:

“Truly, truly, I say to you, unless you eat the flesh of the Son of Man and drink His blood, you have no life in yourselves. Whoever eats My flesh and drinks My blood has eternal life.” (John 6:53–54)

Thus, the Holy Eucharist, which is offered daily in the Church and received in faith, is the true Body and true Blood of Jesus Christ. The bread is changed and becomes His flesh, and the wine is changed and becomes His blood (Matthew 26:27–29).

That is why the Apostle Paul warned:

“Whoever eats this bread or drinks the cup of the Lord in an unworthy manner will be guilty of the body and blood of the Lord. But let a person examine himself, and so eat of the bread and drink of the cup. For whoever eats and drinks in an unworthy manner eats and drinks judgment on himself, not discerning the body of the Lord.” (1 Corinthians 11:27–30)

3. The Cross Grants Us Peace

When the Lord stretched out His hands upon the wood, He bridged heaven and earth. His arms became the measure of reconciliation. “Peace I leave with you; My peace I give to you—not as the world gives” (John 14:27). This peace was not written on parchment or decreed by kings; it was written in blood upon the wood of the Cross.

The Apostle Paul reveals this mystery: “Through Him, God reconciled all things to Himself, making peace through the blood of His Cross.” (Col. 1:19–20) The blood that flowed down Calvary is the river that quenched the fire of enmity. Once we were far off wandering in the shadow of wrath but now we have been brought near by the blood of Christ, who Himself is our peace (Eph. 2:13–14).

When the risen Lord appeared to His disciples, His first words were : “Peace be with you.” (John 20:19) Thus, every time the faithful bow before the Cross, they receive not terror, but tranquility the peace purchased at so great a price.

4.We Have Become a New Person

Through the redemption accomplished on the cross, our bodies once burdened by sin and curse have been made new. The Apostle Paul declares,

“We know that our old self was crucified with Him, so that the body of sin might be done away with, that we should no longer be slaves to sin.” (Romans 6:6)

Again, he writes,

“Therefore, if anyone is in Christ, he is a new creation; old things have passed away; behold, all things have become new.” (2 Corinthians 5:17)

As St. Paul teaches, our Lord has renewed us through His redemption, given us a new covenant through the Gospel, promised us a new heaven and a new earth, and prepared for us the eternal kingdom of heaven (Mark 1:15; 2 Peter 3:13; Revelation 21:1).
