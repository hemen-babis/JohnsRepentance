IV. The Law Fulfilled, Not Abolished

Let not the ignorant say that the Law is dead. For Christ our Lord declared: “I did not come to abolish the Law or the Prophets, but to fulfill them” (Matt. 5:17). If this were not so, why would Peter and John continue to ascend to the Temple at the hour of prayer? (Acts 3:1)

Saint Paul too affirms this: “What agreement has the temple of God with idols?” (2 Cor. 6:16). Shall we now turn the ark into idolatry by forsaking it altogether? Nay. The temple still stands. The ark still speaks. The Law is fulfilled not discarded.

And if the Old Covenant were only a shadow, then the words of the Lord in Matthew 21:13 “My house shall be called a house of prayer” would have no place in the New. But He spoke them in the Temple, in Jerusalem, under the Gospel. Thus, the continuity is evident. The Old is not abolished but transfigured in the light of the New (2 Cor. 3:7–11; Col. 2:17; Heb. 10:1).

V. Temple and Ark: Inseparable Mysteries

Let us mark carefully: the Ark is never separated from the Temple, whether in the Law, in the Prophets, or in the Revelation.

The heavens themselves proclaim this:

“The temple of God was opened in heaven, and the Ark of His covenant was seen in His temple” (Rev. 11:19).

How then shall we separate them on earth?

Those who scoff at the Ark, who reject its necessity, are not only irreverent they are ignorant. They betray a shallow understanding of Scripture, for they fail to see the thread that binds worship from Moses to Christ, from David to the apostles.

VI. Continuity of Ritual: Incense, Praise, and Garments

Let us not despise the rituals ordained by God:

The rising of incense,

The psalms sung with drums and cymbals,

The choir vested in Gothic garments,

These are not distractions but divine links between the two covenants. As it is written in Ezekiel 3:10 and 2 Chronicles 20:21–22, praise and worship are the liturgical chain that unites the Torah and the Gospel.

And this unity is affirmed in the apostolic teaching:

“You are built upon the foundation of the prophets and apostles” (Eph. 2:20).

Our religion is not built upon empty rites or mystical philosophies. It is built upon revelation a divine disclosure, not man’s imagination.

VII. A Holy Building: The Human Temple

Let none think we speak only of wood and stone. The human body is the building of God, and the conscience is the sanctuary within. Religion is not true unless it sanctifies the soul and brings the heart to moral order.

And yet, just as the heart requires the Word to live, the Church requires the Ark to breathe. The foundation is Christ laid by apostles, preserved by bishops, handed down to us.

As Paul writes:

“Like a wise master builder, I laid a foundation... No one can lay any foundation other than the one already laid, which is Jesus Christ” (1 Cor. 3:10–11).

So beware, O man of pride, who builds a “new church” on carnal wisdom, separate from the Ark, the apostles, and the Temple. Such a structure is not a sanctuary but a tomb.

Conclusion: Glory to the Ark of Christ

Let us rejoice in the Ark of the New Covenant, which bears the Name of Christ and is enthroned in the temple of His Body, the Church. Let us honor the Ark, not as a relic of the past, but as a living testament of God’s presence among us. Let us glorify:

The eternal Father,

The Virgin Mother,

The victorious Cross,

and the Ark that carries the Word of the Covenant.

May the name of the Holy God be praised forever and ever in every tongue that He has created, today and always. 

May the blessings of God's mother, the virgin, and the cross be upon us.
Amen.
