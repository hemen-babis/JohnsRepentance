6. On the 12th, 21st, and 29th Days of Each Month

These days have been set aside by ancient custom.

The 12th is the feast of Saint Michael the Archangel, celebrated monthly in joy, for the Archangel is the defender of God’s people.

The 21st is dedicated to the Theotokos.

The 29th is consecrated to the memory of the Lord’s feasts.
On these days, as on the greater feasts, prostration is forbidden.

In addition to the nine major feasts of our Lord, there are also nine minor feasts. These include the Sermon, the Light, the Shepherd , the feast of Circumcision , Feast of the Presentation of the Lord, the Wedding at Cana of Galilee, the Mount of Olives, Gena, and the Feast of the Cross.

Although the King of Justice did not give a detailed explanation about these minor feasts, they are still counted as feasts of the Lord. Since prostration is forbidden on the feasts of the Lord, our forefathers treated these days in the same way. In particular, the 29th of the month was set apart as a feast of those 9 Minor feast , and on that day prostration is not performed.

In addition to the nine major and nine minor feasts, the Passion and the Resurrection are also counted among the feasts of the Lord.

The Church commands that the Passion be observed with prayer, fasting, and sorrow. The Resurrection, however, is celebrated differently. It begins on the day of Christ’s rising and continues throughout the week, until the feast known as the Second Resurrection.

Just as the Passion is remembered with mourning, fasting, and prayer, so also the Resurrection must be celebrated with fullness of joy. In the Passion we recall His crucifixion with devotion and sorrow, but in the Resurrection we rejoice in His victory over death.

Conclusion

See then, beloved, how wisely the Church instructs her children. She teaches us that there is a time to kneel and a time to stand, a time to weep and a time to rejoice. To prostrate on days of triumph would be to mingle grief with joy, but to stand upright on feast days is to confess the victory of Christ over death.

Therefore, let us honor the holy canons, and let our bodies be trained in obedience, as our souls are trained in faith. For discipline in outward worship shapes discipline in the inward man.
