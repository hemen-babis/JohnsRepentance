Reflection on the Holy #Apostles: 

The sacred designation of "apostle," deriving from the ancient term meaning "sent," encapsulates the profound mission and essence of those chosen by Christ. As the divine herald of His Father, our Lord Jesus Christ embodied this role, and thus called upon His disciples to partake in this holy vocation, transforming them into His Apostles. The Scriptures, especially Hebrews 3:1, illuminate this divine commission, "As the Father has sent me, I am sending you."

The Chosen Witnesses

The Apostles, chosen not from the learned or the elite, but from the humble and often overlooked populace, were ordained to proclaim the fulfillment of ancient prophecies. Their selection, as noted in the poignant words of John 2:5, underscores a divine preference for the "poor of this world," who are rich in faith and heirs to the kingdom of God. This choice reveals a divine inversion of worldly values, where the meek are exalted as bearers of the kingdom's mysteries.

The Commission of Simplicity and Trust

Christ’s command to His Apostles, as they ventured to proclaim the nearness of the kingdom of heaven, was marked by a call to simplicity and reliance on divine providence. Instructed to travel without the common comforts of shoes, staff, or money, they were to embody the total dependence on God, a theme recurrently stressed in Matthew 10:1-11. This radical trust was not only a practical directive but a profound theological statement about the nature of their mission and the source of their sustenance.

The Enduring Presence and the Priestly Authority

The promise of Christ's perpetual presence with His Apostles, as they journeyed to spread the Gospel, assured them of an unbreakable connection with His divine love (Matthew 28:19-20). Moreover, the Apostolic authority extended beyond mere teaching to encompass binding and loosing—terms deeply rooted in rabbinic authority, granting them the power to govern the spiritual well-being of the community (Matthew 16:19, 18:18, and John 20:23). The pastoral charge "Feed my lambs, feed my sheep," further entrusted them with the spiritual nourishment and guidance of the faithful (John 21:15).

The Apostolic Identity and Mission

The apostolic identity, richly layered with the notions of being "sent," "selected," and "guardians," also embodies the role of a messenger—an envoy who represents Christ Himself. This role was not merely administrative but profoundly incarnational, as they were to be Christ's presence in the world. Their mission was characterized by hardship and opposition, yet they were fortified by the assurance that nothing could sever them from the love of Christ.

The Legacy of Apostolic Suffering and Victory

The ultimate testament to their faith and mission was the manner of their martyrdom, celebrated within the Orthodox Tewahedo Church with great veneration. These solemn commemorations not only honor their sacrifices but serve as a continual reminder of the cost of discipleship and the victory of faith over worldly powers.

Reflections from the Life of St. Peter and St. Paul

St. Peter and St. Paul, as paragons of apostolic faith, exemplify the transformative power of Christ’s call. From humble beginnings, Peter, a fisherman, and Paul, a former persecutor of Christians, were elevated to the pillars of the Church. Their lives and ministries reflect the profound personal conversion and the universal scope of the Gospel message, reaching both Jew and Gentile alike.

The Continuing Call

This apostolic heritage beckons all believers to emulate their faith, courage, and unwavering commitment to the Gospel. As the modern faithful stand upon the foundation laid by the apostles, they are called to be witnesses of Christ's love and power, striving to live out the Gospel in a world that is often hostile to its message.
