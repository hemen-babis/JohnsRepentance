7. Enkutatash – The Pearl of the New Year

This feast is also called Enkutatash, meaning “the gift of jewels.” Two ancient traditions explain this:

In the time of Noah, When Noah first divided the continents among his three sons, Africa was given to Cam.he landed in Ethiopia during Meskerem. Seeing the land blooming with beauty, he cried and said “You have given me a pearl of a pearl!”(Enku Eta wetashlgn  ) Hence, this season was named Enkutatash.

In the time of the Queen of Sheba, when she visited Solomon, she brought rich gifts of gold and spices. Solomon, showing her the treasures of his palace, placed a jewel upon her finger, saying: “Let the pearl be an ornament for you.” This also took place in Meskerem, and from it the name Enkutatash entered our heritage.

Thus, Enkutatash signifies joy, blessing, and the flowering of new life.

8. The Duty of the Present Generation

For more than two thousand years, the Ethiopian Church has preserved the good works and borne the trials of the generations. Now it is our turn. We must strengthen monasteries, promote the spread of the Gospel, build good administration, and preserve unity with sister churches. Faith is not a heritage to be wasted, but a treasure to be safeguarded and handed down.

9. Conclusion – A Call to Repentance and Fruitfulness

Beloved, the feast of Saint John is not a mere tradition. It is the voice of repentance at the gateway of every year. It is the trumpet calling us to turn away from sin and to begin anew with God. May the Lord grant us in this year to return with repentance, to bear fruit thirty, sixty, and a hundredfold, and to inherit the Kingdom prepared for His saints.
