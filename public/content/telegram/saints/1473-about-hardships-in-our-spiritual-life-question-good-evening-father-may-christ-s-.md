---About #hardships in our #spiritual life

Question፦ Good evening father, may Christ’s peace be with you!
I have one question that I would like you to cover. 
I live around many non-Orthodox or atheist people. I also have witnessed my closest friends convert to other religions . I have always felt sad and broken about that part of my life. 
My friends mostly throw questions at me or invite me to their gatherings which I respectfully decline most times and sometimes address/attend out of respect. 
I am concerned because I find it very hard to defend my religion and practices when they raise questions because I feel that can be arrogant and hypocritical. As time goes by, I fear I am losing my faith and connection with the saints (even with our Holy Mother) slowly

Answer፦ Peace to you in the name of our Lord Jesus Christ, dear member. Your struggles are genuine and shared by many in a world that increasingly drifts away from the teachings of the Church. Your humility in these circumstances is admirable. 

You are right, it is indeed disheartening to see our loved ones adopt a worldview contrary to our own, even more so when we see them turn away from the orthodox faith. And yet, we should remember that our faith is not dependent on the beliefs of those around us. We are called to "stand firm in the faith, be courageous, be strong" (1 Corinthians 16:13). This might seem challenging when we find ourselves to be the sole believers in our surroundings, but remember that Christ promised, "I am with you always, even to the end of the age" (Matthew 28:20).

There will always be questions about our faith that we cannot answer, and that's okay. It does not imply that our faith is wrong or lacking. This is why we have the Church Fathers, the saints, and our teachers, who dedicated their lives to preserving and interpreting the teachings of the Church. They are our guides in navigating these questions. They tackled numerous heresies and philosophical challenges to Orthodoxy. Thus, when a question puzzles you, seek guidance from the wealth of wisdom left by our Fathers. Record the questions and consult with knowledgeable priests or theologians who can guide you. We are not expected to know everything; our faith is a journey of learning and growing closer to God.

About your living situation, St. Paul teaches us, "If it is possible, as far as it depends on you, live at peace with everyone" (Romans 12:18). Living peacefully does not mean abandoning your faith but preserving it, while also respecting the beliefs of others. In harmony, we can live our Christian life, exhibit love, humility, and patience as Christ commanded, which may even draw others to Christ. 

However, if you find your faith is in danger, consider seeking an environment more conducive to spiritual growth. There is no shame in this. It's the same principle as a person leaving a place of disease for the sake of their physical health. Our faith, our spiritual health, is more critical, for it concerns our eternal souls. 

If you find that your interactions are leading you away from God or our Holy Mother, this is indeed a serious concern. It's crucial to evaluate these relationships and decide what boundaries might need to be set. 

Lastly, reach out to your local Orthodox community for support. Engage with them, participate in the sacraments, join the fellowship and Bible studies. This can help strengthen your faith and provide the support you need to stand firm amidst the challenges you are facing. 

Remember, our faith is not founded on our ability to defend it but rather on the love and grace of Christ. Do not let unanswered questions shake your faith. 

Our prayers are with you in this challenging journey. May God bless and guide you, and may the prayers of St. Mary and all the saints be with us.

May the name of the Holy God be praised forever and ever in every tongue that He has created, today and always.
