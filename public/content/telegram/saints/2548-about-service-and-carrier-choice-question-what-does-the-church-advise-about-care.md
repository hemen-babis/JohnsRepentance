---About #Service and #carrier choice 

👉Question፦ What does the church advise about careers that consume a lot of your time & potentially limit the amount of time you can spend in church service & activities ? 

For example if i want to become a doctor, the education process will keep me busy and limit the time i can spend in service. In addition, when i start working as a doctor it will be the same. Would this be considered okay as long as i strive to live a sacramental life and serve God with the time I have. Or would the church recommend carefully considering the careers we choose with respect to how that affects our service life. 

Thank you 🙏🏾

Answer፦ Dear beloved in Christ,

Your inquiry touches upon a fundamental aspect of our journey with the Lord, where the balance between worldly duties and spiritual commitments often presents a challenge. The Orthodox Church, deeply rooted in the wisdom of the Holy Scriptures and the teachings of the Fathers, offers a perspective that transcends the apparent dichotomy between secular occupations and spiritual life.

Saint Paul reminds us in Colossians 3:23, "Whatever you do, work heartily, as for the Lord and not for men." This verse encapsulates the essence of the Christian approach to work and vocation. The Church does not discourage us from pursuing careers, such as in medicine, that demand a significant portion of our time. Rather, it encourages us to see our professions as arenas for serving Christ and manifesting His love. Being a doctor, you are provided with unique opportunities to serve Christ in the sick and the suffering, embodying the healing ministry of our Lord Jesus.

The key is to ensure that your career does not lead you away from your ultimate goal—the salvation of your soul and union with Christ. It is indeed possible to lead a sacramental life and serve God with the time you have, integrating your faith into your daily work. The challenge is to maintain a prayerful life and participate in the Church's sacraments as much as your circumstances allow.

Consider the words of St. John Chrysostom, who taught that every aspect of our lives could be an offering to God if done in a spirit of love and dedication to His glory. Your career as a doctor, fueled by compassion and the desire to alleviate suffering, can be a powerful ministry and a path to sanctity.

Reflecting on how to balance your career and spiritual life, ask yourself: How can I make my work an offering to God? What steps can I take to ensure that my profession strengthens rather than diminishes my relationship with Christ?

In serving others through your profession, you serve Christ Himself. What will be your first step to integrating your faith with your work, making your life a living testimony of God’s love and mercy?

May the name of the Holy God be praised forever and ever in every tongue that He has created, today and always. 

May the blessings of God's mother, the virgin, and the cross be upon us. Amen.
