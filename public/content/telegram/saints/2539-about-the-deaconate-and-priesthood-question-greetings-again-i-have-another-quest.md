---About the #deaconate and #priesthood

Question፦ Greetings again, I have another question. For me, being a Deacon is amazing. I feel like the Holy Spirit is dwelling with in me so much. I’m always intrigued to read the Holy Bible and to learn the Kidase. All though, I don’t know what a great blessing and significance being a Deacon truly is. Please explain to me the full history of Deaconhood and the history of the first Deacon who was known as St. Steven. Blessings, Goodnight.

Answer፦ Greetings in the peace of Christ,

The Diaconate is a sacred order, rich in history and spiritual significance, embodying service and dedication to the Church and to God’s people.

The role of a Deacon traces back to the early Church, as outlined in the Acts of the Apostles, chapter 6. The Apostles, overwhelmed by their responsibilities, sought out seven men "of good repute, full of the Spirit and of wisdom," (Acts 6:3) to assist in serving the widows and the needy. This marked the beginning of the Diaconate. Among these seven was Stephen, a man full of faith and the Holy Spirit (Acts 6:5), who became the first martyr for Christ, bearing witness to the truth of the Gospel even unto death. His martyrdom highlights the depth of commitment and sacrifice inherent in serving God and His Church.

Being a Deacon, therefore, is not merely a role or a function; it is a vocation to embody the love and service of Christ, to minister to the faithful, and to be a living witness to the Gospel. It is a call to humility, service, and sometimes, sacrifice, as exemplified by St. Stephen. 

Your attraction to the Holy Scriptures and the Kidase (Divine Liturgy) is a foundation upon which your service is built, for through them, you encounter Christ and convey His presence to others. The significance of your role lies in this very act of service and witness, in the footsteps of Christ and His saints.

"Whoever wishes to become great among you shall be your servant, and whoever wishes to be first among you shall be slave of all." (Mark 10:43-44)

Reflecting on the life and martyrdom of St. Stephen, how can you see your service as a Deacon being a reflection of Christ's service to the world?
