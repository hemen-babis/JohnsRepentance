About St. #George 

Dear members of John's Repentance page, today is the monthly feast of St. George in the EOTC and so we send you this short writing for you to learn about the life of this great saint and martyr. 

St. George is a Christian saint who is widely known for his courage, steadfast faith, and unwavering devotion to God. He was born in 280 A.D. in the city of Capadocia, which was part of the Eastern Empire in Asia Minor. He came from a wealthy and noble Christian family and followed the usual career path of young nobleman by joining the Roman army.

St. George's ability and charm quickly brought him promotion within the army, and eventually, he was made a tribune or an officer in the Imperial Guard by the Emperor. According to one story, he was a friend of Constantine, another officer who later became the first Christian emperor. The legend says that St. George accompanied Constantine to Britain and visited some holy places, including Glastonbury and Caerleon. When he returned to Nicomedia, the capital of the Eastern Empire, he encountered the fierce storm of persecution against Christians.

Diocletian, the emperor at the time, issued a formal edict against Christians on February 23, 303 A.D., which was the feast of Termhlalia. The edict mandated that all churches should be leveled to the ground, all sacred books burned, all Christians who held any honorable rank were to be degraded and deprived of civil rights, and all Christians who were not officials were to be reduced to slavery.

Despite the persecution, St. George boldly stood up for his faith. He pushed his way through the market to read the proclamation in front of the awe-stricken crowd and then deliberately tore down the Imperial edict and threw it away. This act of defiance resulted in his arrest and subsequent encounter with Diocletian.

The Emperor tried to seduce St. George by promising him a higher position in the empire, but St. George remained steadfast in his faith. Diocletian then began to threaten him with unbearable tortures that he would inflict on him. St. George refused to renounce his faith, even in the face of torture, and eventually was imprisoned.

St. George's chastity and suffering are two prominent aspects of his story. Diocletian arranged for a very beautiful woman to spend the night with him in prison to try to debauch him. St. George, who could only look forward to heavenly joy, began to pray and within a short while managed to swing her heart to the Christian faith. She seriously began to inquire about salvation, and by morning she declared that she found her true Bridegroom, Jesus Christ. With great courage, she announced her Christianity to the Emperor and his men and joined the other saintly martyrs.
