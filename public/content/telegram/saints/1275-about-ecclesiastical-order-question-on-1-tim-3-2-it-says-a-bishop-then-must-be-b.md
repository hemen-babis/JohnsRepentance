About #Ecclesiastical Order

Question፦ On 1 Tim 3:2 it says :"A bishop then must be blameless, the husband of one wife, vigilant, sober, of good behavior, given to hospitality, apt to teach" who are metropolitan bishops in Orthodox Church? Can they get married or can a bishop be a married person?

Answer፦ The nature of the ecclesiastical order as outlined in the Pauline epistle to Timothy gives us insight into the role of bishops within the Orthodox Church. The bishops, serving as successors to the apostles, are regarded as the principal teachers, administrators, and ceremonial leaders of the Church. In Orthodox tradition, bishops have always been seen as leading lives of celibacy, dedicating their lives entirely to the service of the Church and the will of God. This is manifested in their choice to live as monks, separated from worldly affairs from a young age.

From a historical perspective, we see that the apostles themselves had different backgrounds, including marital status and occupations. The focus, however, has always been on their role as disseminators of God's teachings. Similarly, the roles within the Church, be it bishops, priests, or deacons, have expanded and adapted with the growth of the Church, a point that is well illustrated in the example of the appointment of the first seven deacons, led by St. Stephen.

Now, it's important to understand that the evolution of roles within the Church does not imply a shift away from the principles established by St. Paul. Rather, it signifies the Church's efforts to faithfully fulfill its spiritual obligations as its reach expands. Hence, it is indeed the monks who have embraced a life of celibacy and full devotion to God that take up the mantle of the bishopric. This is consistent with the canons of the Orthodox Church.

While the Orthodox Church encourages celibacy for bishops, it also acknowledges the apostolic guidance for bishops to be 'the husband of one wife.' This underlines the significance of monogamy, but it should not be misconstrued as an injunction for bishops to be married. In the practical and historical context of the Church, the role of the bishop has been fulfilled by those who have embraced a monastic life of celibacy.

Yet, it's important to mention that the authenticity of the ecclesiastical office cannot be guaranteed by monastic life alone. It also requires a deep and genuine spiritual life and knowledge, the absence of which has unfortunately led to errors and aberrations. The Church, in its wisdom, acknowledges that all humans, including its leaders, are prone to mistakes and temptations, as seen in the account of Simon the Magician in the book of Acts. It is essential, then, to avoid appointing those lacking in spiritual knowledge and life to leadership positions in the Church.

And so our dear member, we find that the Church continues to adapt to the needs of its faithful while remaining anchored to its apostolic foundations. It understands the challenges of living a life of peace and equality but insists on this aspiration, urging its flock to transcend the earthly trials and tribulations in pursuit of God's will. Let us, therefore, remember to bear our responsibilities faithfully, both within the Church and in the wider world, and to uphold the principles of peace, equality, and righteousness as taught by the apostles and preserved by the saints. Amen.
