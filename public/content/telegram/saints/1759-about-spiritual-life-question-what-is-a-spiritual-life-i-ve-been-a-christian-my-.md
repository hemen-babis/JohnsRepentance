About #spiritual life 

👉 Question፦ What is a spiritual life?
I’ve been a Christian my whole life and I’m lost I don’t know what path to take I feel like I don’t hear god calling me

Answer፦  Beloved in Christ, a spiritual life is a journey of deep intimacy and communion with our Lord Jesus Christ, marked by prayer, fasting, almsgiving, and participation in the sacramental life of the Church. It is a life where the soul seeks to grow in virtue and humility, turning away from the passions and temptations of this world, and focusing on the eternal Kingdom. Just as our physical body needs nourishment and care, so too does our soul. Saint Seraphim of Sarov once said, "Acquire the Spirit of Peace and a thousand souls around you will be saved."

In essence, a spiritual life is the constant pursuit of holiness and unity with God. As the Scriptures teach us: "But as He who called you is holy, you also be holy in all your conduct, because it is written, 'Be holy, for I am holy.'" (1 Peter 1:15-16).

Beloved one, your heartfelt cry resonates with many souls, even those who walked this Earth long before us. In moments of spiritual desolation, it's important to remember the words of St. John of the Cross, a mystic, albeit from a different Christian tradition, who spoke of the 'Dark Night of the Soul' – a period where one feels distant from God, yet it is often in these times that our souls are deepened and purified.

Remember the Psalmist's lament: "Why are you cast down, O my soul, and why are you in turmoil within me?" (Psalm 42:11). Yet, he also reminds himself: "Hope in God; for I shall again praise him, my salvation and my God." Also note that the call might be subtle, a whisper rather than a shout. As it is written in 1 Kings 19:12, God sometimes speaks in a "still small voice."

In the Orthodox tradition, we believe that sometimes, God allows us to experience such feelings to draw us closer to Him in humility, dependence, and trust. It's crucial not to rely solely on feelings, for our relationship with God transcends mere emotion. In these moments of desolation, the practices of the Church, like attending the Divine Liturgy, confession, prayer, and reading Scriptures, can be a guiding light.
