👉🏽👉🏽👉🏽What Does #Apostle ( #Apostles ) Mean? A Sacred Teaching of the Church (Parts 1 )

“How beautiful are the feet of those who bring good news of good things!” (Isaiah 52:7, Romans 10:15)

Brethren beloved in Christ, peace and divine blessing be multiplied unto you. Today we delve into a sacred mystery: the meaning and ministry of the Holy Apostles, those glorious witnesses who stood as pillars of the nascent Church, illuminated by divine light, and went forth like torches set ablaze by the fire of the Holy Spirit. May their memory be a fortress to us and their intercession a shield over our journey!

The Meaning of the Word “Apostle”

The word “Apostle” is the English rendering of the Ge’ez term “Horo-Hede,” which means a sent one, a messenger, or one commissioned to declare. Thus, the Apostles are not self-sent, nor do they speak of their own will, but are sent by the Lord Himself, bearers of the Gospel of the Kingdom and heralds of salvation to the whole world.

Their divine commission was to proclaim the Gospel, to reveal the mystery of salvation, and to harvest the fruits of that Gospel among the nations. The apostolic era, in Orthodox tradition, begins not with their calling, but with their empowerment on the Day of Pentecost, when the Holy Spirit descended upon the 120 gathered souls. That day was the sowing of heavenly seed, and they were the laborers in the field of mankind.

Their Calling and Formation

Our Lord, after His baptism by John and His forty-day fast, began His public ministry by choosing His disciples. These men were not philosophers, nor scribes, nor priests of the Temple they were fishermen, tax collectors, zealots, and commoners. He called the humble to shame the proud, the unlearned to rebuke the wise, and the meek to overturn the mighty.

As Saint Mark reveals in his Gospel (Mark 3:13), the Lord:

First called them to live with Him—to walk, eat, and pray with the Son of Man.

Showed them the miracles of God in the flesh.

Made them eyewitnesses of His salvific work.

Chose them to proclaim the Gospel of the Kingdom to every creature.

Commissioned them to be heralds of salvation across the earth.

This divine calling was no respecter of class:

Saint Peter, a poor fisherman, was chosen just as Saint Matthew, a tax collector and man of wealth.

Saints John and James, sons of Zebedee, were unlearned, yet beloved.

Simeon the Zealot, once a political rebel, was brought into the haven of divine peace.

The Gospel reveals this truth: “All are equal in the sight of God.” All left behind worldly possessions and followed Him.

The Apostles and the Church

As the Old Covenant was established upon the twelve patriarchs born of Jacob, so now the New Covenant the Society of the Spirit of Israel is built upon the twelve apostles, chosen by Christ.

Their unwavering testimony sprang from:

1. Seeing Christ with their eyes (1 John 1:1).

2. Touching Him with their hands.

3. Hearing His voice with their ears.

4. Being sent by His authority (Matt. 10:1-4).

5. Witnessing His resurrection (John 21).

6. Performing miracles in His name (1 Cor. 12:12).

They received the New Testament priesthood, supplanting the Levitical order, when the Lord breathed upon them and said, “Receive the Holy Spirit” (John 20:22).

In humility and obedience, they passed this authority to their successors not with pride, but with solemn grace. By word, by life, and by writings, they preserved the Church of God, which was redeemed by the blood of Christ.

The Spread of the Gospel

The first church was founded in Jerusalem in 34 A.D., when 3000 souls were baptized after the sermon of Saint Peter on Pentecost. Thereafter, the Apostles cast lots to divide the world, traveling in every direction:

Tearing down idolatry.

Casting out demons.

Plowing the barren soil of nations with the Cross.

Sowing the Gospel as a seed, often watering it with their own blood.
