👉🏽👉🏽👉🏽#Hosanna – The Feast of the King of Glory (Eighth Week of Lent)

Part 1

Grace and peace be multiplied unto you, beloved brethren, who hearken to the Orthodox teachings proclaimed through the website of John the Repentant. On this day, rich in mystery and overflowing with divine symbolism, we are summoned to meditate with awe upon the holy feast of Hosanna, celebrated on the eighth week of Great Lent. This feast is not merely a remembrance, but a living entrance into the heavenly mysteries revealed on that blessed day when our Lord Jesus Christ entered Jerusalem in glory.

What is Hosanna? Who is the King of Praise?

The name Hosanna springs from the Hebrew “Hosheanah”, a fervent cry meaning “Save us, we beseech Thee!” or “Deliver us!” It is a petition raised with hope, and at once a song of acclamation to the One who alone has power to save. On this feast, also known as Palm Sunday, the Holy Church becomes a vessel of jubilant worship, as clergy and faithful chant praises from dawn until the setting of the sun.

At the heart of the divine liturgy, we behold a sacred dialogue between heaven and earth. As the deacon, adorned in vestments, ascends to the western doors, he proclaims with solemn voice:

“Open the doors, O nobles!”

From within, the priest responds with a question that pierces the heavens:

“Who is this King of Glory?”

The deacon then declares:

“The Lord, strong and mighty, the Lord mighty in battle  this is the King of Glory!”

The priest then welcomes Him, saying:

“Come, come, O King of Glory! Come, O King of Mercy!”

This sacred reenactment is not a mere dramatization, but a living proclamation of the Messiah’s entrance not into Jerusalem only but into the sanctuary of our hearts, into the temple of the Church, into the history of salvation.

The Scriptural Fulfillment and Its Deep Symbolism

The Gospel according to Zechariah foresaw this majestic entry:

> “Rejoice greatly, O daughter of Zion; shout, O daughter of Jerusalem: behold, thy King cometh unto thee: He is just, and having salvation; lowly, and riding upon a donkey, and upon a colt, the foal of a donkey.” (Zechariah 9:9)

This image was not arbitrary. In the days of the prophets, kings rode horses in times of war, bearing spears and judgments. But in times of peace and reconciliation, they mounted donkeys, humble and slow, bearing no threat but rather mercy. Thus, Christ, the true King, comes not in wrath but in meekness. He who once thundered from Sinai now rides gently upon the back of a beast of burden.

The Donkeys and Their Spiritual Meaning

Christ’s entrance into Jerusalem upon a donkey and a colt is not a mere fulfillment of prophecy—it is a mystery pregnant with deep significance.

These animals, as interpreted by our Holy Church, represent mankind itself. They were tied, just as humanity was bound by sin and deception, held captive by the deceit of the devil since the fall of Adam. Just as these animals were not owned by those who tied them, so too the human race, fashioned by God, fell under the dominion of a deceiver who was never its rightful lord.

But behold the mercy of God! The Lord sends His apostles, saying:

Go into the village before you, and immediately you will find a donkey tied, and a colt with her. Untie them and bring them to Me. And if anyone says anything to you, say: "The Lord has need of them."
