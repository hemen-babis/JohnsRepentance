2.The Recipient of Intercession

The word “Temalaj” refers to the recipient of the prayers offered by intercessors. In our faith, the sole recipient of all prayers and intercessions is God.

While the saints and angels serve as intercessors, they are not divine. They pray to God, not as God. All intercession ultimately rises to the one God, who exists as the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit three persons in one divine essence.

This divine unity is not divided by the three persons; rather, the Father, Son, and Holy Spirit share one lordship, one power, one glory, one divinity, one kingdom, and one will. Together, they receive the thanksgiving and supplications of all creation those in heaven, on earth, and under the earth and carry out every act of divine mercy and goodness.

There is no hierarchy among the three persons of the Trinity; none is superior or inferior of each other. All are equal in essence, majesty, and authority.

Scripture affirms this truth:

“And I heard every creature in heaven and on earth and under the earth and in the sea, and all that is in them, saying,
‘To Him who sits on the throne and to the Lamb be blessing and honor and glory and power forever and ever.’
And the four living creatures said, ‘Amen,’ and the elders fell down and worshiped.”
(Revelation 5:13–14)

Thus, all intercession whether through saints, angels, or Our Lady finds its fulfillment in God alone, who alone is worthy to hear and answer the prayers of His creation.

3.Those Who Are Interceded For(the sinner who receives the prayer)

Those who are prayed for or interceded for also have a part in the work of intercession. Intercessors offer their prayers to God for the salvation of sinners, and though evil spirits exist in opposition, the grace and blessing of intercession are given by God solely for the benefit of sinners.

Those who receive the prayers of intercessors also share in the work of intercession. This participation comes through faith in the intercessor’s prayer, trust in the power of intercession, and a sincere heart that turns away from sin.

However, if a person continues to sin while claiming to rely on intercessors, such faith becomes false. It would make the saints those holy intercessors partners in sin, which is impossible, for they are united with God, the righteous Judge, who cannot take part in wrongdoing.

There are times when God does not accept the intercession of the saints. This happens when a sinner refuses to repent and persists in evil. Scripture provides several clear examples of this truth:

“How long will you mourn for Saul, since I have rejected him from reigning over Israel?”
(1 Samuel 16:1)

“Therefore do not pray for this people, nor lift up a cry or prayer for them, nor make intercession to Me; for I will not hear you. Do you not see what they are doing in the cities of Judah and in the streets of Jerusalem? The children gather wood, the fathers kindle the fire, and the women knead dough to make cakes for the queen of heaven and to pour out drink offerings to other gods, to provoke Me to anger.”
(Jeremiah 7:16–18)

“Then the Lord said to me, ‘Even if Moses and Samuel stood before Me, My heart would not turn toward this people. Send them out of My sight and let them go.’”
(Jeremiah 15:1)

These passages remind us that intercession is not a substitute for repentance. The mercy of God flows freely through intercessors, but only to those who turn from sin with a contrite heart. Without repentance, even the prayers of the greatest saints cannot avail before the throne of God.
