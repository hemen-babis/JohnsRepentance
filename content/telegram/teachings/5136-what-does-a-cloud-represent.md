👉🏾👉🏾👉🏾What does a #cloud #represent?”

(Part 2)

Beloved in Christ, peace and grace be multiplied to you. Today let us reflect on the mystery of the cloud in Holy Scripture. What does the cloud signify? What does it reveal to us about God, His saints, and His holy Mother, the Virgin Mary? The Church, following the words of the prophets, apostles, and evangelists, teaches us that the cloud is not an only mere natural wonder, but a holy sign of God’s presence, His protection, and His glory.

1. The Cloud and the Virgin Mary

St. John the Theologian writes: “And the woman fled into the wilderness, to a place prepared by God, that they should feed her there for one thousand two hundred and sixty days” (Rev. 12:6). This woman is none other than the Virgin Mary, who fled into exile, carrying her Son, the Creator of the worlds. She wandered as a refugee, holding Him who Himself appointed the bounds of creation, yet who, in His humility, allowed Himself to be borne in her arms.

When the dragon was cast down, he pursued the woman, but she was given two wings of a great eagle to fly into the wilderness (Rev. 12:13–15). The Fathers call her a swift cloud, bearing within her the Sun of Righteousness. Isaiah had spoken long before: “Heaven is My throne, and the earth is My footstool” (Isa. 66:1). She carried Him who fills all things, once in her arms, once upon her back, as she journeyed through the deserts of Egypt and into Ethiopia.

Blessed was Ethiopia, for she received the Virgin and her Child. Habakkuk foresaw this, saying: “I saw the tents of Ethiopia in affliction, and the curtains of Midian trembled” (Hab. 3:7). Thus Ethiopia rejoiced, offering love and humility in welcoming the Mother and her Son.

2. The Cloud as the Glory of God

The cloud in Scripture is a revelation of God’s majesty. In the days of Moses, God appeared in the tabernacle of meeting as a pillar of cloud. “When Moses entered the tabernacle, the pillar of cloud descended and stood at the door, and God spoke with him” (Exod. 33:8–9).

When Solomon completed the great Temple, again the cloud descended: “The house of the Lord was filled with the cloud, so that the priests could not stand to minister, for the glory of the Lord filled the house” (1 Kings 8:10).

In the New Testament, on Mount Tabor, the Lord revealed His divine light to Peter, James, and John. A bright cloud overshadowed them, and from it the voice of the Father was heard (Matt. 17:1–5).

And on the fortieth day after His Resurrection, when Christ ascended from the Mount of Olives, “a cloud received Him out of their sight” (Acts 1:9). The same cloud shall appear again at His Second Coming: “Behold, He is coming with clouds, and every eye shall see Him, even those who pierced Him” (Rev. 1:7).

3. The Cloud as the Saints

The cloud is also an image of the saints, in whom the glory of God is revealed across the ages. St. Paul writes: “Since we are surrounded by so great a cloud of witnesses, let us lay aside every weight and the sin which so easily ensnares us” (Heb. 12:1).

Why are the saints likened to clouds? Just as the cloud shields us from the burning heat of the sun, so the saints, through their prayers, shield us from the wrath of God. Where there are clouds, there is rain; where there are saints, there is mercy. Their intercession brings refreshment to the Church as rain waters the earth.

4. The Virgin Mary as the Highest Cloud

Above all, the Virgin Mary is the cloud most glorious. In her, the Creator revealed His glory more profoundly than in any other creature. The prophets foretold her, Moses proclaimed her, and through her God made His dwelling among men. Her womb became His throne for nine months; from her flesh He took flesh, from her soul He took a soul, and at her breast He received nourishment. In her the glory of God was most perfectly revealed, hidden yet shining forth for the salvation of the world.
