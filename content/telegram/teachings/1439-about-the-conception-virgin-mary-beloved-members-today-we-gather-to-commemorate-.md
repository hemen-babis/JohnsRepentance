---About the #conception Virgin #Mary 

Beloved members, today we gather to commemorate the monthly feast of the conception of the blessed Virgin Mary. Let us delve into the profound significance of this event and may our hearts be enlightened by the wisdom and grace of the Holy Spirit.

In the year 5485 since the creation of the world, fifteen years prior to the dawn of the Year of Mercy, the conception of our Lady took place. It was a time when the world was engulfed in the darkness of sin, and the consequences of human transgression weighed heavily upon all creation. The floodwaters of Noah's time serve as a vivid testimony, for they rose fifteen cubits above the highest mountains, covering the earth and cleansing it from the defilement of wickedness (Genesis 7:19).

Reflect upon the significance of those waters, dear members, for they were a symbol of the pervasive sin that had plagued humanity from the time of our progenitor Adam until the advent of Christ. Just as the floodwaters harmed not only humanity but also every living creature on earth, the sin of Adam affected not only humankind but also the animals and even the celestial beings of heaven.

The holy angels, who dwell in glory and serve as intermediaries between God and man, were exempt from the burden of this sin. However, they refrained from entering into the service of humanity, recognizing that it was through man's agency that this sin had entered the world. It was in this context that the angelic proclamation of peace and salvation to the shepherds on that holy night came to pass (Luke 2:8-12).

Our Lady, the chosen vessel, descended from priestly lineage through her mother and royal lineage through her father. She was a descendant of King David and Solomon, as well as of Aaron the High Priest. Yet, her ancestors, Hannah and Joachim, faced great trials and reproach due to their barrenness. Barrenness was erroneously believed to be a consequence of sin, and thus, they were scorned, reproached, and even prevented from entering the House of God for worship.

However, let us be mindful, dear members, that God's ways are not like those of mortals. In their distress, Hannah and Joachim wept before the Lord, beseeching Him to remove the shame that clung to them. They offered fervent prayers and supplications, joining the chorus of many prophets and saints who had longed for the advent of the Virgin Mary. The culmination of these cries for divine intervention found their fulfillment in the birth of our Lady.

The barrenness that had afflicted Hannah and Joachim, perceived as a consequence of sin, transformed into a vessel for God's divine plan. It was a vessel through which the Virgin Mary, the bearer of heavenly secrets, would enter the world. She appeared in a vision to her mother Hannah, and even before her conception, the fruit of life, the mother of Jesus Christ, the embodiment of righteousness, was foretold by the angel of the good news, Gabriel. 

While still in her mother's womb, our Lady, the fruit of the holy union between Hannah and Joachim, performed extraordinary deeds that testified to her greatness. She bestowed light upon the blind and strength upon weak hands, even before her birth. From the moment of her conception, she exuded sanctity, and her mere presence caused the dead to rise.

Witnessing these events, Satan could not remain silent. Though unaware of the precise timing, he understood that a moment of great significance was upon him. If such miracles occurred even before her birth, what extraordinary wonders would unfold when she came into the world? In his relentless enmity, Satan stirred up the Jews against the parents of our Lady, seeking to torment her even while she resided within the sacred confines of her mother's womb. But the Virgin Mary had already confounded him, even before her birth, by opening the eyes of the blind and causing the mute to speak. Who among God's creation had ever achieved such wonders from the confines of the womb?
