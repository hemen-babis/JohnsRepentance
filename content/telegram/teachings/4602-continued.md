IV. The Bell of the lord - Mourning at the Foot of the Cross

During the sacred moment of the Liturgy, when the priest and people cry out “Lord, have mercy on us” forty-one times, the Bell of the Lord is rung twelve times.

Why twelve? It is the number of the apostles, the foundation stones of the heavenly Jerusalem.

But listen deeper this bell is a cry of sorrow, echoing the weeping of Our Lady, the Holy Virgin Mary, and Saint John the Beloved, who stood beneath the Cross (John 19:25).

As this bell tolls, we are reminded of the Passion, of the grief that filled the heavens and earth when the Son of God was crucified. It beckons us to join in their holy sorrow, to remember that the Cross was borne not only by Christ but also by those who loved Him.

Let the bell pierce your heart, O faithful one, and awaken in you the sacred mourning that leads to eternal joy.

V. The Bell of the Cross – The Descent of the Divine Gifts

When the priests depart from the sanctuary bearing the Holy Body and Precious Blood, the Bell of the Cross is rung.

This bell is a symbol of the holy acts of Saint Joseph of Arimathea and Saint Nicodemus, who took down the Lord’s Body from the Cross and laid it in the tomb with reverence and sorrow.

It also represents the teaching of the apostles and the descent of the Holy Spirit. For just as Christ gave His Body and Blood for the life of the world, so now, through the apostles’ successors, He gives it still.

At this bell, bow deeply. For the Lamb once slain is now offered to you. The Cross, once lifted high on Golgotha, is lifted once more upon the altar of grace.

Final Blessing

O brethren, every bell in the Church is a voice of heaven. Let us not treat it lightly nor hear it with deaf hearts. It is the trumpet of the Spirit, the cry of the prophets, the call of the apostles, the lament of the Virgin, the voice of John in the wilderness.

Each ring is a reminder that time is sacred, that we are called, gathered, instructed, and fed by the Lord who desires not the death of sinners but their return to Him.

Let us thank God for these sacred instruments. Let us not grow weary of their sound, but let them lead us from sleep to prayer, from sorrow to hope, from sin to grace, from earth to heaven.

may the name of the Holy God be praised forever and ever in every tongue that He has created, today and always.

May the blessings of God's mother, the virgin, and the cross be upon us.
Amen.
