IV. The Spiritual Power of This Fast

This fast is not barren. It bears fruit in abundance. When undertaken with a pure heart, it blesses us with peace, love, and health. It is a time when the servants of God bishops, priests, and deacons pray fervently for the Church, for their ministry, and for the faithful. And the people, seeing the labor of their shepherds, join with them in repentance and intercession. Together, clergy and laity ascend the holy mountain of prayer and fasting.

We fast:

To receive the blessings of the apostles.

To overcome the world as they overcame it.

To be reconciled with God in love and humility.

To work out our own salvation in fear and trembling.

Let every soul now remember: the apostles were not only teachers of doctrine they were icons of obedience, examples of sacrificial living, and faithful stewards of the mysteries of God. Their fasting was not optional, and neither is ours. Let us therefore strive, like them, to “work out our salvation through love and fear of obedience.”

V. An Admonition for the Present Day

In our times, many hide behind false distinctions. They say, “This is for the priests; that is for the monks; we are but ordinary people.” But Christ did not shed His blood for monks alone. The Spirit did not descend only upon the Twelve. The Gospel was not preached to a chosen caste but to all nations. And the grace of fasting is given to all who seek it with.

May the name of the Holy God be praised forever and ever in every tongue that He has created, today and always. 

May the blessings of God's mother, the virgin, and the cross be upon us.
Amen.
