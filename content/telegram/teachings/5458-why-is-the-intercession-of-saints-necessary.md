👉🏽👉🏽👉🏽 Why is the #Intercession of #Saints Necessary?

(Part 5)

Beloved brothers and sisters in Christ,
we greet you in the name of our Lord and Savior, who has called us to holiness and to the fellowship of His saints. Today, with hearts full of faith, we continue our reflection on the sacred mystery of the intercession of the saints, learning why God reveals His divine will to His chosen friends and how their prayers become a light for the world.

3.In Genesis 20:1–18, we read that God was angry with Abimelech, the king of Gerar, because he took Sarah, the wife of Abraham, into his house. The Lord said to Abimelech,
“I also kept you from sinning against Me; therefore, I did not allow you to touch her. Now then, return the man’s wife, for he is a prophet, and he will pray for you, and you shall live. But if you do not return her, know for certain that you shall surely die, you and all who belong to you.”

Abimelech, trembling at the word of the Lord, obeyed His command and returned Sarah to her husband. Then Abraham prayed to God, and the Lord heard his prayer. God healed Abimelech, his wife, and his servants, for the Lord had closed all the wombs in Abimelech’s household because of Sarah, the wife of Abraham.

✍🏾 From this, we understand that the prayer of a righteous man has great power before God. Even though Abimelech was a king, his deliverance and the healing of his house depended upon the prayer of Abraham, the friend of God. Thus, Scripture teaches us that God Himself honors the intercession of His saints, for through their prayers, mercy triumphs over judgment.

4.In Exodus 32:7–14, when the people of Israel turned away from God and worshipped the golden calf, the Lord said to Moses,
“I have seen this people, and behold, it is a stiff-necked people. Now therefore let Me alone, that My wrath may burn hot against them, and I may consume them; and I will make of you a great nation.”

Yet God, who needs no counsel from man, could have destroyed them without speaking to Moses. The phrase “Let Me alone” The phrase ‘Let Me alone, that I may destroy them’ essentially means ‘Do not intercede for them”but Moses, moved by compassion and faith, stood before the Creator and pleaded for mercy. Because of his intercession, Scripture says,
“the mercy of the Lord was stirred; God turned aside the destruction He had prepared..”

King David later described this moment of holy mediation in the Psalms:

“They made a calf in Horeb and worshipped a molten image. They exchanged their glory for the likeness of an ox that eats grass. They forgot God their Savior, who had done great things in Egypt, wondrous works in the land of Ham, and terrible things by the Red Sea. Therefore He said that He would destroy them, had not Moses, His chosen, stood before Him in the breach, to turn away His wrath.” lest He destroy them.” (Psalm 106:19–23)
