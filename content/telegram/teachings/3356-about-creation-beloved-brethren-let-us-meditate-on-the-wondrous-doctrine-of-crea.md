About #Creation 

Beloved brethren, let us meditate on the wondrous doctrine of creation, a doctrine that reveals to us not only the beginning of all things but also the wisdom, power, and goodness of our Almighty God. Creation, as taught by the holy fathers, is the very act by which God, in His infinite authority, brought into existence all things seen and unseen, tangible and intangible. It is a testimony not only to His power but to His love and purpose for His creatures. Let us, therefore, consider deeply what it means for God to create, and how this truth calls us to contemplate His majesty and our own place within His creation.

Creation from Nothing: The Supreme Power of God

In the beginning, as the sacred Scriptures teach us, “God created the heavens and the earth” (Genesis 1:1). This act of creation is unparalleled and incomprehensible to human reason, for it was not the work of a craftsman who fashioned something out of pre-existing materials. No, beloved, it was the act of God’s omnipotent will, who, with the simple command, "Let it be," brought forth from nothing all that exists (Isaiah 44:24). This is the very essence of creation: to bring into existence that which did not exist before. The grass that covers the fields, the rain that falls from the sky, the animals that move upon the earth, all these things owe their existence not to chance or to necessity but to the will of the Creator.

The creation of the universe reveals that God was under no compulsion, neither internal nor external. He was not driven by any lack within Himself, nor was He constrained by anything outside of Himself. His creation was an act of pure freedom, flowing from His wisdom and goodness. This, brethren, is the profound mystery of God’s omnipotence: He speaks, and out of the void, there arises life, order, and beauty.

The Existence of the Creator: Witnessed in the World

As we gaze upon the world around us, it becomes clear that creation itself bears witness to the Creator. The heavens are stretched out, the earth is spread wide, day and night succeed one another, seasons come and go, the winds blow, and the celestial bodies move in their appointed courses. How can these wonders exist without a Creator? They proclaim that there is a hand behind them, a hand that guides, sustains, and governs all things. "In the beginning, God created the heavens and the earth," and from that moment forward, the whole cosmos declares His glory and His power.

The very name of this Creator is God, the Almighty One, who made all things good and beautiful. Creation itself testifies not only to His power but also to His wisdom. It is as if the order of creation is a book, and each creature is a letter written by God, revealing to us something of His divine nature. Every sunrise, every change of season, every living thing proclaims the artistry of the One who made it.

The Purpose of Creation: God’s Glory and Our Good

Why did God create the heavens and the earth? What was His purpose? It was not for His own benefit, for God is complete and perfect in Himself. He neither gains nor loses by His creation. Rather, the purpose of creation was twofold: first, to manifest His glory, and second, to prepare for us an inheritance in His eternal kingdom. As the Apostle teaches us, “We are His workmanship, created in Christ Jesus for good works” (Ephesians 2:10).

God created the world so that His name might be praised and His glory might be known. All creation, in its beauty and order, reflects the glory of God, and it is our duty as His creatures to acknowledge this glory and give Him thanks. Creation also serves as food for both body and soul. Just as God has provided for our physical nourishment through the fruits of the earth—meat, vegetables, and fruits—He has also prepared spiritual food for us. The wheat that becomes bread, the wine that becomes His sacred blood, these are but signs of the greater sustenance He offers to our souls.
