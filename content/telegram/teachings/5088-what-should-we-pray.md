👉🏾👉🏾👉🏾What #Should We #pray?

(Part 8)

Beloved children of the holy Orthodox faith, I greet you with the peace of our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ. Today we consider an essential matter for the life of the soul: What should we pray? This question is not small, for prayer is the very breath of the Christian. As the body dies when it ceases to breathe, so the soul grows faint and falls when it ceases to pray. Therefore, let us open the Scriptures, the wisdom of the Fathers, and the tradition of our Church, and learn with zeal what our Lord requires of us in prayer.

1. Short Prayers – When They Are Right, and When They Are Wrong

Sometimes, short prayers are necessary:

When we are in deep trouble, like Peter crying, “Lord, save me!”

When time does not allow us to pray long.

These are good and acceptable. New Christians may also begin with short prayers, like infants who first drink milk. but As we mature in faith, we must also grow stronger in prayer.

If we always give God only a few hurried words, and then excuse ourselves saying, “He is merciful, He will understand”, we are misusing His kindness. That is not true devotion but laziness.

The Church gives us prayers to strengthen us:

The Psalms of David,

The Hail Mary,

The liturgical prayers in the services.

These are not only for monks or priests but for all: youth, deacons, clergy, evangelists, singers, and every believer. Whoever neglects them becomes weak and easily falls into temptation.

2. The Foundation of Prayer – “Our Father”

The center of all prayer is the one Christ Himself taught us: “Our Father who art in heaven” (Abune Zebe-Semayat).

Whenever we pray, we should begin with this. If we are interrupted in the middle of prayer, we should seal that pause with “Our Father,” handle the matter, and then return to prayer, again beginning with “Our Father.”

This way, our prayers are never left unfinished or empty, but always held together by the words of Christ.

3. The Power of Prayer in the Church

Do not limit prayer to your home. The Church is the house of God, and prayers offered there are greater and more powerful than any other.

God Himself said:

“My eyes will be open and My ears attentive to the prayer made in this place. I have chosen this house; My eyes and My heart will always be there.” (2 Chronicles 7:15–16)

When the faithful gather together, led by priests, their prayer has great strength because it is united. Therefore, we should be present in church, lifting up our voices with the assembly.

4. The Sabbath and the Liturgy 

Our greatest duty is to honor the holy Sabbath and to celebrate the Liturgy .

The ancient Fathers Abba Michael and Abba Gabriel warned against those who would celebrate the Liturgy carelessly or without reverence.

We must not only come for the Eucharist itself, but also take part in the hours of prayer, psalms, and hymns before it. These are the spiritual banquet of the Church. To neglect them is to treat ourselves as strangers in our own Mother’s house.

Those who join in these prayers grow in love for the faith of the Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church. Those who neglect them risk becoming outsiders to their own inheritance.
