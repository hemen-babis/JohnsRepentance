part 1

Brethren beloved in the Lord, I write not with idle words but with the incense of truth drawn from the bosom of Holy Scripture and the unbroken tradition of the Apostles, to illuminate your minds concerning faith and religion, and to call your hearts to unity in the Spirit, as once the prophets proclaimed and the apostles upheld.

On the Nature of Faith

What, then, is faith? It is not the conclusion of a philosopher nor the observation of the scientist, but—as the apostle Paul hath written—“the assurance of things hoped for, the conviction of things not seen” (Hebrews 11:1). Faith is the eye of the soul, the organ by which we perceive what lies beyond the veil of the visible world. The visible is temporal, but the invisible is eternal.

Behold the kingdom of heaven, which no eye hath seen and no ear hath heard, yet we press toward it with hope. What confirms this hope? Faith alone. Faith is not subject to the inquiry of microscopes nor does it rest upon the theories of men. It transcends. It rests in absolute truth, not relative or fluctuating like the truth sought by science. Science alters with age, but faith stands unmoved.

Religion: The Manifestation of Faith

Faith, while inward and spiritual, does not remain idle or formless. Religion is the outward expression, the sacred form that binds faith with worship. Just as the soul is clothed in the body, faith is clothed in religion. Religion is that holy order by which we believe rightly in God and worship Him as He has ordained. This includes what we believe of Him, how we adore Him, and how we perform the sacred rites in His name.

Therefore, religion is the manifestation of faith—the flower which springs from the root of belief, and it is not fashioned by human opinion, but revealed and preserved by divine ordinance.

One Faith, One Lord, One Baptism

Hearken to the voice of the Apostle Paul: “One Lord, one faith, one baptism.” (Ephesians 4:5). Not many lords, not divided faiths, not manifold baptisms, but one—a sacred unity that befits the majesty of the one God. The purpose of this divine teaching is to preserve the unity of the Spirit in the bond of peace, that schisms and heresies may not defile the holy body of Christ.

This oneness has many aspects, each like a jewel reflecting the light of the one True Faith:


1. One God

The God of Adam is the God of Abraham, and the God of Abraham is the God of Moses, of David, of Peter, and of us all. He changeth not. His essence is eternal, His character unaltered. To believe in Him as Peter did, and to worship Him as Peter worshipped, is to be one in faith with the Apostles.

This unbroken belief links us across time, generation to generation. When our children worship the Lord as the prophets and apostles did, then is one religion preserved. Christianity is not a new religion, but the fulfillment and continuation of the divine covenant from the beginning.


2. One Baptism

Baptism is no empty ritual, but the sacred gate through which one enters into the life of Christ. By it, we are born of water and the Spirit, and joined to the family of God. The baptism we receive is not repeated, for there is but one spiritual birth—just as there is one natural birth.

Through baptism, we are made sons and daughters of God, co-heirs with Christ, partakers in His death and resurrection. This sonship is not partial or divided; it is one grace, one inheritance. Therefore, the true religion is that which guards this one baptism and teaches rightly its meaning and power.
