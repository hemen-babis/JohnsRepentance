👉🏾👉🏾👉🏾Can We Deliver #Prayer to Our #Home?

(Part 5)

Beloved friends in Christ who follow the Orthodox teachings, we greet you with the peace of God. Today, on this blessed subject, we present a teaching for your benefit, that you may read, meditate, and grow in the life of prayer.

The Example of the Hermits

The hermits who dwell in deserts and caves, separating themselves from the noise of the world, continually lift up prayers for the salvation of mankind. Though they live apart, they return to the monasteries to receive the Body and Blood of our Lord, for without the Eucharist no soul can live. Their life teaches us that prayer can be offered in every place, yet the Holy Mysteries are essential for eternal life.

Prayer in the Home

Christians who live among the gentile , where there is no church nearby, should not neglect their worship of God. They must offer prayer and thanksgiving in their homes. In this regard, the ancient Egyptian Christians gave us a shining example: in each of their houses they prepared a place for prayer. When they went to the church for the Divine Liturgy, they never returned without partaking of the Body and Blood of the Lord except for some special reason.

Likewise, they strove to keep the seven hours of prayer wherever they were. In this discipline, even those outside the Church put many of us to shame, they have better experience than us . For we see that infidels and heretics will not fail to perform their appointed hours of worship whether at home, at school, at work, in the market, or even on the road. How much of us, who worship the true God, faithful to the rule of prayer handed down by our Fathers?

A Call to Examine Ourselves

Let each of us ask with sincerity:

Have I prepared a place of prayer in my home?

Do I gather my family for prayer when I cannot attend the church?

Do I strive to keep the seven prayer hours where I am?

Do I make prayer a daily habit, not only in the church but in every place and time?

For prayer is not bound by walls; it is the incense of the soul that rises to heaven from every faithful heart.

The Command to Pray Seven Times a Day

Our Holy Church has ordained that we should pray seven times daily, following the testimony of Scripture:

“Seven times a day I will praise You for Your righteous judgments.” (Psalm 118:164)

Just as the holy Prophet David glorified God seven times a day, so also we, walking in the footsteps of our Fathers and according to the canons of the Church, must offer prayer at the appointed hours.

The Seven Times of Prayer

1. Morning Prayer – As David said, “My God, my God, early will I seek You” (Psalm 62:1). When the night ends and the light of day dawns, we rise from our beds and give thanks to God who preserved us through the night.

It was in the morning that Adam was created (Gen. 1:26).

It was in the morning that our Lord Jesus Christ was examined before Pilate (Matt. 27:1).

It is the hour when the angel of the night departs and the angel of the day takes his place.

2. The Third Hour (3:00 am )– At this time:

Eve was created as a helper for Adam (Gen. 2:21–24).

Daniel prayed with his face toward Jerusalem (Dan. 6:10).

The Archangel Gabriel announced the good news to the Virgin Mary (Luke 1:26).

Christ was scourged before Pilate (Matt. 27:26).

The Holy Spirit descended upon the Apostles (Acts 2:1–4).
Thus, at this hour we give thanks for the gift of creation, salvation, and the indwelling of the Holy Spirit.
