2. Righteous Joseph

Joseph, sold unjustly into Egypt by his own brothers, did not allow anger or resentment to cloud his trust in God. He proclaimed, “Now it was not you who sent me here, but God sent me; and he made me a father to Pharaoh, and lord of all his house, and ruler over all the land of Egypt” (Genesis 45:8). 

3. King David and the Prophets

King David suffered persecution at the hands of Saul, though he had done no wrong. He lamented to Jonathan, “What have I done? What is my trespass and my sin before your father, that he seeks my life?” (1 Samuel 20:1). Likewise, prophets of old endured immense trials for proclaiming the truth. Jeremiah was cast into a cistern and imprisoned for speaking God’s word (Jeremiah 38:6), and the prophet Micah was fed with the bread of affliction and the water of distress (1 Kings 22:27). These examples reveal that those who speak and live by righteousness often endure hardship, yet their faith remains steadfast.

4. Naboth the Jezreelite

Naboth was a righteous man martyred for upholding God’s law and protecting his inheritance. When King Ahab desired Naboth’s vineyard, Naboth replied, “God forbid that I should give thee the inheritance of my fathers” (1 Kings 21:3). Naboth’s refusal angered Ahab and Jezebel, and false witnesses were used to condemn him. Naboth was slain, yet God did not forget his blood. 

5. Saint John the Baptist

John the Baptist, the Preacher of the Way, suffered for boldly proclaiming the truth. He rebuked King Herod for taking his brother Philip’s wife, saying, “It is not lawful for you to have her.” For this, Herod imprisoned him. During Herod’s birthday feast, the daughter of Herodias danced before the guests, and Herod, pleased, swore to give her whatever she desired. Urged by her mother, she demanded the head of John the Baptist on a platter. Though grieved, Herod fulfilled the request for the sake of his oath, and John was beheaded in prison (Matthew 14:1–10). John’s martyrdom stands as a powerful witness that truth must be spoken, even in the face of death.

6. Saint Stephen, the First Martyr

When Stephen testified boldly about Christ, the Jewish leaders were enraged. Filled with the Holy Spirit, he looked up to heaven and declared, “Behold, I see the heavens opened and the Son of Man standing at the right hand of God.” They rushed upon him, dragged him outside the city, and stoned him. As he was dying, Stephen prayed, “Lord Jesus, receive my spirit... Lord, do not hold this sin against them.” (Acts 7:54–60). 


 👉🏾Saint Peter’s Deliverance:

During the reign of King Herod, great persecution arose against the early Church. Herod executed James, the brother of John, by the sword, and when he saw that this pleased the Jews, he proceeded to arrest Peter as well. It was during the Feast of Unleavened Bread. Peter was imprisoned and placed under heavy guard four squads of soldiers were assigned to watch over him. Yet the Church unceasingly prayed to God for his deliverance.

On the very night before Herod intended to bring him out for trial, Peter was sleeping between two soldiers, bound with chains, while others stood guard at the door. Suddenly, an angel of the Lord appeared, and a light shone in the cell. The angel struck Peter on the side and said, “Get up quickly.” The chains fell from his hands. The angel told him, “Dress yourself, put on your sandals, and follow me.” Peter obeyed, thinking it was a vision. They passed the guards and came to the iron gate that led to the city; it opened for them by itself. After they went out and walked a little way, the angel departed. Then Peter came to himself and said, “Now I know for certain that the Lord has sent His angel and rescued me from the hand of Herod and from all that the Jewish people were expecting.” (Acts 12:1–11)God protects His servants even in the darkest prison.
