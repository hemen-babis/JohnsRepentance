👉🏾👉🏾👉🏾The Mystery of #Tears in #Prayer:

A Holy Offering of the Penitent Heart

(Part 3)

Beloved children of the Most High,
Grace and peace be multiplied unto you, who seek the face of the Lord in truth and in brokenness of heart. Today, we are summoned to contemplate a holy mystery the offering of tears in prayer. This mystery is not new, nor is it foreign to the soul that longs for God. It is ancient, eternal, and full of heavenly fire. Let us incline our ears and hearts to the wisdom of our fathers, the testimony of Scripture, and the life of saints, that we may understand what it means to pray with tears.

I. The Divine Gift of Tears

Saint John the Theologian illumined with the fire of divine lovetaught that tears are both the mother and daughter of prayer. They are the fruit of love and the root of repentance, given by God Himself as a sacred gift to fallen man. For what was man’s first response after his fall? Tears. When Adam and Eve lost paradise, they wept not because of the thorns of the earth, but because they were banished from the face of the Lord.

Thus, tears became the first ladder by which man sought to return to God. The saints teach that the soul’s road home begins not with argument, but with weeping.

II. David: The Prophet of Tears

King David, a man after God’s own heart, did not weep once and move on. No. He drenched his bed with tears night after night. His sorrow was not momentary, but lifelong. He cried with groans that shook his bones. He wept in psalms that have become the hymns of every penitent heart.

“Weeping may last for the night,” says the Psalmist,

“but joy comes in the morning.” (Psalm 30:5)

This is not the joy of this world, but the joy of the Holy Spirit the joy that comes after true mourning, the joy born from sincere repentance.

III. Peter: Tears of Return

Who can forget the bitter weeping of Saint Peter?

“He went out and wept bitterly.” (Luke 22:62)

He had denied the Master. But in his tears, he returned. He did not merely mourn what he had done his tears carried him back to the feet of Christ. It was after this weeping that Christ entrusted him with the care of His flock. This teaches us a great mystery: through tears, the fallen are restored, the weak are entrusted, and mysteries are revealed.

IV. Tears: The Path of Repentance and the Fruit of Love

Tears are not just a reaction; they are a revelation.

They are the visible sign of the soul’s invisible fire.

They are the outward manifestation of perfect love for God a love that groans over sin, mourns over distance, and clings to God with all its strength.

Saint John Afework boldly proclaims:

“The fire of sin is fierce, but a few tears extinguish its flame and purify the soul.”

O, what a divine exchange! A river of tears for an ocean of mercy!

And Saint John Zeswesaw confirms:

“We shall not be judged for failing to work miracles, but for not weeping over our sins.”

Weep, therefore, not as those who despair, but as those who long to be made whole.

V. Tears in This World vs. Tears in the Next

Mark this with holy trembling:

“If we do not shed tears in this world, we will groan in the next.”

Tears in this world are like the dew of dawn they nourish, soften, and bring life. But in the world to come, tears are tears of shame, of torment, of final regret. Blessed, therefore, are those who mourn now, for they shall be comforted (Matt. 5:4). There is no greater wisdom than to weep now, that we may rejoice forever.
