IV. The Indivisible Union of the Divine Nature

Let no one imagine that while His body lay in the grave and His soul descended to Hades, His divinity was absent. God forbid!

The body was not separated from the Godhead.

The soul was not separated from the Godhead.

For Christ is one Person in two natures fully God and fully man and what God has joined in the Incarnation, no grave nor Sheol can separate. Thus, even in death, Christ was triumphant and whole.

V. The Prophetic Witness to the Third Day

Let us recall the prophets, for they, too, speak with one voice on this matter.

The Prophet Hosea declares:

"After two days He will revive us; on the third day He will raise us up, that we may live before Him." (Hosea 6:2)

The Lord Himself taught plainly:

“The Son of Man must suffer many things... be killed, and on the third day be raised.” (Matthew 16:21)
“On the third day He will rise again.” (Matthew 17:23)

And when the unbelieving scribes and Pharisees demanded a sign, He answered them with a veiled but mighty prophecy:

“As Jonah was three days and three nights in the belly of the great fish, so will the Son of Man be three days and three nights in the heart of the earth.” (Matthew 12:40)

This was not allegory, but a divine parallel: just as Jonah emerged on the third day, so did Christ rise triumphant over death.

VI. The Fruit of the Mystery: Our Life and Resurrection

Why did Christ descend into the grave for three days and three nights? Was it mere symbolism? No it was our redemption.

He entered the grave, that He might sanctify it for us.

He died, that death might die.

He rested in the tomb, that our own rest in the grave would be transformed into hope.

He arose on the third day, that we might rise with Him.

As the Psalmist says:

"My flesh also shall rest in hope." (Psalm 15:9)

Not in despair. Not in decay. But in the blessed hope of resurrection.

Let us then marvel at the wisdom of God, who from death drew forth life, and from the tomb brought resurrection. Let us worship Him, not merely with lips, but with lives offered in holiness. Let us rejoice, for Christ is risen, and by His three days and three nights in the tomb, He has trampled down death by death.

To Him be glory with the Father and the Holy Spirit, now and ever, and unto ages of ages. Amen.

May the name of the Holy God be praised forever and ever in every tongue that He has created, today and always. 

May the blessings of God's mother, the virgin, and the cross be upon us.
Amen.
