3. When Should We Pray?

The angels in heaven never cease crying: “Holy, holy, holy.” The saints, despising worldly honors, forsaking earthly pleasures, dwelling in deserts and caves, spend their lives in unceasing prayer, striking their body and offering their whole being to God.

But as for us, who live amidst the cares of the world, the Church in her mercy teaches us at least this: let us pray in the morning and in the evening. If there is a church near, let us hasten to it at those hours. If not, let us imitate the prophet Daniel, who prayed in his house facing East.

Whenever we stand to pray, whether in church or at home, let us believe that we stand in spirit before the throne of the heavenly King. We begin with a bow in the name of the Holy Trinity. If even earthly kings are greeted with uplifted hands, how much more should we bow before the Eternal King?

During prayer, let us not rush. Rather, let us fall in prostration as often as the Spirit stirs us, giving thanks, marveling at His works, testifying to His glory, and offering Him our whole heart.

Even outside the appointed times of prayer, let us not pass the house of God as though it were common. For the Lord has said: “My eyes and My heart will be there forever.”

Therefore, when we pass by the Church, let us greet her, saying: “Peace be to you, O Jerusalem, heavenly city of God.” Let every opportunity become a moment of bowing, and every bowing an ascent of the soul to heaven.
