5. The Guide of the Way (Meraë Fênot)

When young Tobias journeyed to Media, Raphael went with him in the form of a companion, guiding and protecting him from dangers (cf. Ex. 23:20). At the river Tigris, when a great fish sought to swallow him, Raphael delivered him and showed him how its gall and liver would become medicine and weapon against evil.

Thus is Raphael revealed as guide to travelers, protector of pilgrims, and helper to those who face trials along the road. Even now, the Church invokes him as guardian of the way.

6. The Angel of Marriage"Melake Kebkab"

Raphael is also called the Angel of the Bridegroom, for he blessed the union of Tobias and Sarah. Though Sarah’s seven husbands had perished by the demon’s hand, Raphael taught Tobias how to overcome the evil one: by prayer, by faith, and by the burning of the fish’s liver.

That night, the demon fled, and Tobias and Sarah prayed together, committing their marriage to God. As Christ blessed the wedding at Cana, so Raphael sanctified theirs, showing that true marriage is preserved not by fleshly desire, but by the fear of God and the intercession of His saints.

Even now, Raphael blesses the marriages of the faithful, making their homes secure in peace.

7. The Enlightener of the Blind "Kesate Eweran"

Finally, Raphael restored the sight of Tobit, father of Tobias, by means of the fish’s gall. Just as Christ opened the eyes of the blind with clay and spittle, so Raphael gave light to Tobit’s darkness.

Thus he is the angel who enlightens not only the eyes of the body, but the eyes of the heart. He opens for us the vision of faith, that we may behold the mercy of God and walk in His light.

The Feast of Pagume 3

Beloved, this feast falls in the month of Pagume, which is the bridge between the old year and the new. The Fathers teach us that Pagume signifies the Day of Resurrection: the cleansing of the old age, and the preparation for the Kingdom of God to come.

Thus the feast of Raphael, celebrated on Pagume 3, is the crown of the passing year and the threshold of the new. The waters of this time are believed to be blessed, and the faithful cry out in faith: “Saint Raphael, help us! Protect us!”

Conclusion

Beloved brothers and sisters, let us remember that Saint Raphael is not only a figure of the past, but a living servant of God for us today.

When we are sick, we can call on him, for he is the healer.

When dangers surround us, we can ask for his protection.

When demons tempt us, we can cry out to him with faith.

When our prayers feel weak, he carries them to God’s throne.

When we walk in confusion, he guides our steps.

When families begin in marriage, he blesses them.

When blindness, either of body or soul, covers us, he brings light.

If we honor him with faith and purity of heart, his intercession will guard us, his blessing will guide us, and his prayers will help lead us to the kingdom of God.

Through Saint Raphael’s prayers, may our lives be healed, our journeys safe, and our eyes opened to the glory of the Lord.

May the name of the Holy God be praised forever and ever in every tongue that He has created, today and always. 

May the blessings of God's mother, the virgin, and the cross be upon us.
Amen.
