Dear followers of the Orthodox teachings , we greet you with the peace of the Lord. Today, based on the topic mentioned above, we present to you the following important lesson. We encourage everyone to read it carefully and learn from it.

4. He Who Shows Mercy Will Receive Mercy

Showing mercy is not only a virtue but a living proclamation of God’s own mercy. Our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ taught, “For with the judgment you judge, you will be judged, and with the measure you use, it will be measured to you” (Matthew 7:2). Those who practice mercy will themselves receive mercy.

He also taught, “Whatever you want men to do to you, do also to them, for this is the Law and the Prophets” (Matthew 7:12). Anyone who desires to be shown mercy must extend mercy to others.

👉🏾King David, the one after God’s own heart, said: “Blessed is he who considers the poor; the Lord will deliver him in the day of trouble. The Lord will preserve him and keep him alive; he will be blessed on the earth; and You will not deliver him to the will of his enemies” (Psalm 41:1). Mercy opens the door for mercy.

✍🏾In teaching us how to pray, Our Lord said, “Forgive us our debts, as we forgive our debtors” (Matthew 6:12). He added, “For if you forgive men their trespasses, your heavenly Father will also forgive you; but if you do not forgive men, neither will your Father forgive your trespasses” (Matthew 6:14–15). Forgiveness is the path to receiving forgiveness.

Furthermore, He taught: “If you bring your gift to the altar and there remember that your brother has something against you, leave your gift there before the altar, and go first be reconciled to your brother, and then come and offer your gift” (Matthew 5:23–24). Mercy and reconciliation come before sacrifice.

✍🏾Also, “Whenever you stand praying, if you have anything against anyone, forgive him, that your Father in heaven may also forgive you your trespasses” (Mark 11:25–26).

Saint Peter once asked our Lord, “Lord, how often shall my brother sin against me and I forgive him? Up to seven times?” Jesus responded, “Not seven times, but seventy times seven” (Matthew 18:21–22). Forgiveness must not be limited, but continual.

And He said to the Pharisees: “Those who are well have no need of a physician, but those who are sick. Go and learn what this means: ‘I desire mercy, not sacrifice.’ For I did not come to call the righteous, but sinners to repentance” (Matthew 9:12–13). This teaches that mercy comes before sacrifice.

Saint James also teaches, “For judgment is without mercy to the one who has shown no mercy; mercy triumphs over judgment” (James 2:13).

Saint Augustine taught: “Even if you think you possess wealth, you still need eternal life. You hear the voice of the poor who ask from you, but you also ask before God. As you treat the one who asks you, so God will treat you.”

“My fellow,” he explains, means “a poor person like me.” Even those who have a little wealth on earth remain poor before God.

Saint Yohannes Afewerk teaches: “Blessed are the merciful, for they shall learn.” The mercy a person shows cannot be compared to the mercy God shows. Human mercy is small; God’s mercy is beyond measure. When a person offers a little mercy, God grants abundant mercy in return.
