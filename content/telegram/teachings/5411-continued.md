4.7 The Flower Among Thorns

✍🏾O Mary, the unfading sunflower neither from highlands nor from lawlands among the thorny Jews, deliver me, who trusts in you, from the evil of my condemnation, so that my trust in you may not be in vain. For you are the custom of mercy.

👉🏾 In the Song of Solomon, it is written: “Like a sunflower of flax among thorns, so are you” (Song of Songs 2:2).

✍🏾 Even though the sunflower is surrounded by thorns, it continues to bloom and bear fruit. Similarly, Our Lady, though living among the wicked Jews who, despite seeing many miracles, did not believe and constantly sought signs, did not allow this to hinder her faith. She continued to flourish spiritually and bore the greatest fruit—Christ.

✍🏾 Abba Tsige Dingle explains that Solomon's prophecy was fulfilled through Our Lady’s bringing forth Christ among the Jews. Reflecting on this, he prays: “ I believe in your motherhood and covenant, save me from evil judgment, for my trust and hope in you are not in vain.”

✍🏾 Mahlete Tsige is a work filled with mystery, power, and a unique beauty. Its full depth can only be appreciated when read in Ge’ez. Even a poem loses some of its spirit when translated, as the nuances, rhythm, and spiritual essence are difficult to convey fully in another language.

Conclusion 

✍🏾Fasting without humility is an empty shadow. The true fast is not of the lips or stomach alone, but of the heart. The believer must fast with compassion, give alms to the poor, restrain anger, forgive offenses, and keep the tongue from deceit. Let fasting be united with mercy, prayer, and repentance, that it may ascend as sweet incense before the throne of God.

As the fathers teach: “When you fast, do not fast for vain praise, but that your fasting may be a fasting of mercy, wherein you find help, blessing, and the grace of God.”

May the name of the Holy God be praised forever and ever in every tongue that He has created, today and always.

May the blessings of God's mother, the virgin, and the cross be upon us.
Amen.
