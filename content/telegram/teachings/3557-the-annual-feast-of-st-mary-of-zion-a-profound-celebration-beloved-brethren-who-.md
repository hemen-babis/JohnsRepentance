The Annual Feast of St. #Mary of Zion: A Profound Celebration

Beloved brethren who adhere to the Orthodox faith, let us delve into the deep spiritual significance of the annual Feast of St. Mary of Zion, celebrated on Hedär 21 (November 30 this year). This sacred observance is rooted in profound theological symbolism, historical events, and the enduring veneration of the Holy Virgin Mary, whose life and role as the Ark of Zion embody the divine mystery of our salvation.

The Ark of Zion as a Symbol of the Holy Virgin Mary

The Holy Virgin Mary is intimately linked to the Ark of Zion in both theology and devotion. As the Ark of the Covenant carried the divine presence, so too did the Virgin Mary bear the incarnate Word of God, our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ. The term Zion, meaning "refuge," signifies the spiritual shelter offered to humanity through the Virgin Mary, who is described as the "refuge of God" and the "basis of our salvation." Psalm 86:5 declares, "And of Zion it will be said, 'This one and that one were born in her;
And the Most High Himself shall establish her.'” reflecting how Christ, the new Adam, entered the world through her womb to free humanity from the bondage of sin and death.

For nine months and five days, the Holy Virgin carried Christ within her, becoming the living throne of the Almighty. She is exalted by the Orthodox faithful as pure and holy, both in spirit and body, covered in "fine gold inside and outside," signifying her unblemished virginity and purity.

The Biblical Foreshadowing of the Ark of the Covenant

The Ark of Zion, crafted by Moses according to God’s command, held the two stone tablets inscribed with the Law (Deuteronomy 10:1-4). This Ark prefigured the Virgin Mary, whose womb carried the eternal Word of God. Just as the Ark was made of incorruptible wood, signifying purity, the Virgin Mary’s sinless nature made her worthy to bear the Incarnate Word. The gold that adorned the Ark symbolizes her inner and outer sanctity.

The Israelites carried the Ark into battle, and it brought them victory over their enemies. However, when they sinned, God allowed the Ark to be captured by the Philistines, who placed it in the temple of their god Dagon. The Ark's power was manifest when Dagon fell prostrate before it, his head and hands severed. Furthermore, plagues afflicted the Philistine cities until they returned the Ark. This narrative (1 Samuel 4:1-11) illustrates the Ark's sanctity and its divine power, foreshadowing the Virgin Mary's role as the bearer of God's presence.

Historical Significance and Ethiopian Tradition

The Ark of Zion is central to Ethiopian Orthodox Christianity. According to tradition, Menelik I, the son of King Solomon and the Queen of Sheba, brought the Ark to Ethiopia. It was housed in Axum, a city revered as a spiritual center. Over centuries, the Ark became a symbol of Ethiopian identity and faith, celebrated in song, procession, and liturgy.

The Feast of St. Mary of Zion on Hedär 21 commemorates pivotal events:

1. The Vision of Prophet Zechariah: He saw Zion as a source of divine seals, pointing to the sanctity of the Virgin Mary and her role in salvation.

2. Menelik I’s Entry into Axum: The Ark of Zion’s arrival in Ethiopia marked the divine favor bestowed upon the land.

3. The Role of Yodit Gudit: Even during times of turmoil, the Ark remained a symbol of continuity and peace.

4. The Temple of Abraha and Atsba: In the early 4th century, a grand temple was constructed in Axum, signifying the Ark’s centrality to worship. The consecration of this temple and the celebration of the Mass in Axum further solidified Hedär 21 as a holy day.

Theological Reflection and Spiritual Devotion

This feast reminds us of the Virgin Mary’s unparalleled role in God’s plan for humanity. She is the true Ark, the dwelling place of the divine. Her intercession continues to protect and guide the faithful. The Ark’s journey, its capture, and ultimate return symbolize the triumph of divine power over human sinfulness.
