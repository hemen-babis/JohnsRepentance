---Living in #Optimism

Beloved faithful, followers of the true and ancient teachings of our Holy Orthodox Tewahido Church, I greet you in the name of our Lord and Savior, Jesus Christ, with the grace of His everlasting peace.

Rejoice in Hope, Above All

The foundation of our joy and optimism must be grounded in hope. Our hope is not of this world, for it lies in the eternal Kingdom of Heaven. Let us reflect on this truth: whatever sufferings or trials befall us in this fleeting life, they are but stepping stones on the narrow path that leads to eternal life. Indeed, rejoice in the midst of your sufferings, for the world may trouble us, but our hearts can rest in the certainty that Christ has overcome it.

Fix your heart and mind on the Kingdom of Heaven. In Christ, we have the promise of eternal life. Rejoice in God’s promises to the saints, for He is ever faithful. Even now, let your heart be uplifted as you meditate on the heavenly association of angels and the assembly of the righteous. Such thoughts alone should stir our souls to deep joy.

Rejoice in Prayer

When you pray, consider the profound privilege of conversing with your Heavenly Father. Has He not cared for you from your youth? Has He not preserved you through every trial and challenge? How can you not be joyful when you speak with the One who has been your guide, your protector, and your peace? He, who has given you the spirit of sonship, allowing you to cry out, "Abba, Father!"

When you pray, let your heart ascend to the company of angels, and the saints who stand before the throne of God. This spiritual reality should fill you with gladness. Let every word you offer in prayer be a reminder that God hears you, that He aids you in your daily tasks, and that His providence goes before you.

In your prayers, bring before Him your needs and desires, trusting fully that He will answer according to His perfect will. Cast your burdens upon Him, for He cares for you. In this trust, find joy.

Rejoice in Thanksgiving

Thankfulness is the gateway to joy. Even when you seem to lack much in this life, remember that God’s provision has never failed you. Take a moment to thank Him for every good thing—your health, your family, your work. Even if these things are imperfect, recognize that there are many who do not enjoy them at all. Let your heart overflow with gratitude for the countless blessings God has poured out upon you, both seen and unseen.

Do not overlook the many miracles He has worked for you. From the moment of your creation to this very day, God’s hand has been upon your life. Recall how many dangers you have escaped, how many illnesses you have survived, even when there was no remedy in sight. Did He not sustain you in childhood, when you were vulnerable to disease and disaster? How many of us have narrowly escaped car accidents, war, or other calamities? 

Indeed, cry out in thanksgiving: “O Lord, my healer, what can I give You in return? I will sing of Your mercy!”

Think on Good Things

The mind is the battlefield of the soul, and what we dwell on shapes who we become. Just as gravity pulls everything to the ground, so too do our thoughts shape our character. Think of yourself and others with charity and grace. Do not burden yourself with thoughts of inadequacy, but strive to improve and grow in holiness. Look to St. Paul as your role model—one who pressed forward, forgetting what lay behind, striving toward the high calling of God in Christ Jesus.

Do not compare yourself to others in bitterness. Instead, follow their virtues while acknowledging that every person’s journey is unique. God has given you a path, and it is enough.

Find Joy in Sacred Practices

Rejoice in the simple yet profound acts of worship that fill our Orthodox faith. Listening to our sacred hymns will lift your heart toward God. Practicing the use of musical instruments in worship is a source of great delight, one that draws you deeper into the mysteries of our faith.

About the #Archangel St. #Rufael 

The Feast of Archangel Raphael, celebrated annually on Pagume 3 according to the Ethiopian calendar, is one of the most significant celebrations in the Church, filled with profound spiritual meaning. As with the archangels Michael and Gabriel, St. Raphael is exalted as a divine messenger and protector, entrusted with a unique mission toward humanity by the Almighty God. His name, composed of two Hebrew words—Rufa meaning "healing" and El meaning "God"—literally translates to "God heals," encapsulating his divinely appointed role as a healer and guide to the faithful.

1. Saint Raphael: The Healer of the Sick

One of the most prominent and cherished titles of St. Raphael is "Fewase Duyan," meaning "he who heals the sick" He is celebrated as an angel who carries out the will of God in healing both physical and spiritual ailments. The scriptures bear testimony to his healing ministry, beginning with his intervention in the Book of Tobit. In that sacred text, Raphael is revealed as a healer of human infirmities, a task he carries out on behalf of God, who is the ultimate source of all healing. Raphael’s healing touch not only cures physical sickness but also heals the soul. The prophet Enoch writes that Raphael is the angel appointed by God over all diseases and wounds of mankind (Enoch 10:13). This healing power is manifested in both the Old and New Testaments, where the angel's prayers and intercession allow for the restoration of the faithful to health.

Just as our Lord healed the sick during His earthly ministry—raising the paralytic man and restoring the sight of the blind—so Raphael, as an emissary of God, brings healing and renewal to the faithful, curing them of both bodily afflictions and the deeper wounds of sin. This power to heal is a grace bestowed upon the Church, wherein the faithful can seek comfort through prayer to the archangel, trusting in the divine mercy he mediates on their behalf.

2. "Fetahe Mahtsen": The Opener of the Barren Womb

In both the Old and New Testaments, we encounter numerous women who were barren but, by the grace of God, were blessed with children. These miraculous conceptions were often heralded by the intercession of angels. The scripture recounts the stories of Sarah, the mother of Isaac; Hannah, the mother of Samuel; and Elizabeth, the mother of John the Baptist. To this day, many faithful call upon St. Raphael during times of infertility or difficulty in childbirth, trusting in his role as the Fetahe Mahtsen, the one who "opens the womb." Raphael is known to care for both mothers and children from conception to birth, interceding for the health and well-being of those who are pregnant or seeking to conceive. His presence is invoked in the moments of labor, where women, overwhelmed by the pains of childbirth, call upon him for relief and swift delivery. Through his intercession, the barren womb is opened, and the hand of God is made manifest in the joy of motherhood.

3. "Sadade Aganant": The Chaser of Demons

In the spiritual struggle that each believer faces, Saint Raphael stands as a guardian and protector against the forces of darkness. Just as Christ Himself cast out demons during His ministry, St. Raphael too is renowned for his power to expel evil spirits. The story of Sarah, from the story of Tobit, is a prime example, where Raphael delivers her from the demon who had caused the death of her previous seven husbands. This story, from the Book of Tobit, illustrates how Raphael intervenes in moments of great spiritual peril, driving out demons and restoring peace to those who suffer under the weight of evil.

Raphael's role as a protector is not limited to ancient texts. Even today, he is invoked in prayers for protection against demonic forces, malicious spirits, and all forms of temptation and sin. The faithful call upon him when they face spiritual battles, trusting in his God-given power to chase away the enemy and restore divine peace to their hearts and homes.
