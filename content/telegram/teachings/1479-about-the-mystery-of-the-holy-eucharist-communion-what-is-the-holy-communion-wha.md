---About the Mystery of the #Holy #Eucharist (#Communion)

What is the Holy Communion? 
What is the origin and system of the Holy Communion? 
Why is the sacrament called a mystery? 
Who is worthy of the Holy Communion? 
What preparation do we do before and after receiving the Holy Communion?

The sacrament of Holy Communion is one of the seven sacraments of the Orthodox Church. The term "Qurban" is an Arabic word meaning "blessing," "gift of money, knowledge, labor and time." In the Old Testament, the blood of animals offered by the Israelites for their sins and for the glory of God was called a sacrifice.

In the New Testament, communion or sacrifice means the faithful (true) sacrifice that God offered as an offering to reconcile Himself with mankind. This offering is Jesus Christ, the Lamb of God. Thus, the sacrament means the perfect Body and Blood of our God, Jesus Christ, our Savior.

It was Jesus Christ who founded the sacrament of Holy Communion on the eve of the crucifixion on Thursday. Only his twelve disciples were with him when he established this great sacrement and mystery. He did not reveal it to all the people who were following him like he did with other sacrements. This is why the Holy Church shroudes it in veil and honor as she offers it to the faithful. 

The Holy Communion is called a mystery because of the transformation of bread into the flesh of God and wine into the blood of God, which is visible only to believers with the eyes of faith. The priests prepare the bread only from pure wheat and prepare the wine only from pure grapes or raisins. When the priest blesses the bread and wine by saying, "Aækote Barëkë Wokëdësë," the authority of the priest, the word of God, and the action of the Holy Spirit transform the bread and wine into the flesh and blood of God, making it a mystery that is hidden from the unbeliever.

Before receiving the Holy Communion, one must believe without doubt in the change of the bread and wine into the flesh and blood of God, which is necessary for the healing of the stain and wound of sin and the source of eternal life. One must also repent from past sins, confess, return, and guard against future sins. There is no age limit for receiving Holy Communion, and one should not stay away from the Holy Communion until the end of one's life.

After receiving the Holy Communion, one should take precautions, such as refraining from eating or drinking for a certain period, and contemplating on the spiritual benefit received from the sacrament. The Holy Communion is a great mystery and is the sacrament that is central to the Orthodox Church's teachings.

Overcoming #Lust: An Extended #Lesson

Dear members of John's Repentance spiritual service, today we send you different lessons from different stories about how to overcome the temptation of lust. Hence we entrust you to read it and use it in your daily lives. 

1. A man held captive by lust wrestles with a ravenous flame in his heart, a fire that burns unceasingly. In his struggle against this humiliating proposition, he stumbles upon the power of prayer and fasting. As he adopts these practices, the once irresistible flame begins to ebb, its light diminishes, and his heart is relieved. Through fasting and prayer, he refocuses his energy away from his carnal desires and towards his spiritual growth.

2. Another individual, burdened by lust, seeks counsel from an elder. His transparency with the elder lifts the weight of his struggle, replacing it with encouragement and support. The elder advises him, “Fear not, yield not, but whenever the spirit combats you, remember the sanctity of the body and its higher purpose." By illuminating the sin, it loses power, and the individual regains control over his desires. It's essential to remember that opening up about our struggles lessens their burden and can allow for spiritual guidance and support.

5. Two brothers plagued by lust abandon their monastic lives for the pleasures of the world. However, they soon realize the emptiness of their actions, the looming dread of divine judgment, and decide to return to their monastic duties. They seek forgiveness, performing acts of penance, gradually releasing the grips of their earthly desires. Their transformation serves as a testament to the transformative power of repentance.

6. A brother, troubled by lustful temptations, receives an offer of divine intervention from an elder. However, he realizes that his struggle is refining his spirit, and so asks for strength instead of removal. This request enlightens the elder to a new understanding of struggle and spiritual growth.

7. Another brother is tormented by the image of a beautiful woman. The image is so overpowering that he finds himself engulfed in a fierce battle. He exposes himself to the reality of mortal decay, which helps him resist the tempting vision. Understanding the transient nature of physical beauty aids him in his fight against lust.

8. A son struggles against lustful desires and considers renouncing his monastic life. His father sends him to live in the desert for forty days, during which the true repulsive nature of lust is revealed. This experience strengthens his resolve to lead a spiritual life.

9. A monk beseeches an elder for advice on managing lustful thoughts. The elder advises him to contemplate death and eternal punishment—bitter truths that act like a powerful antidote to earthly temptations. The fearful consideration of these realities can help quiet the allure of lustful desires.

10. A brother endures forty days of intense battle against the spirit of lust, appearing in the form of a beautiful woman. His perseverance is recognized by God, who liberates him from the clutch of this temptation. His struggle highlights the value of persistence in spiritual battles.
