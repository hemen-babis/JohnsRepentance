3. One Hope

If our God is one and our baptism one, then our hope is one: the kingdom of heaven. Eternal life is not promised differently to Jew and Gentile, nor to one people over another. Peter preached it to the circumcised, and Paul to the Gentiles, but the substance of their message was the same. Their hope was one, and their faith was one.Thus, we must not divide what God has joined. We must not seek other paths, for salvation lies in the one true hope held forth by the apostolic Church.

4. One Body

The Church is not a collection of independent factions, but one mystical body, with Christ as the Head and all believers as members. Paul, inspired by the Spirit, likens us to a body with many members—hands, feet, eyes—yet all forming a single organism.

We are branches of the one Vine, which is Christ (John 15:5). Though we differ in gifts, in functions, and even in language, we are united in essence. To divide into separate religions is to sever the body, and to do violence to the unity which Christ Himself ordained.

5. One Spirit

Finally, the Church is led not by many spirits, but by One—the Holy Spirit, who descended at Pentecost (Acts 2:1), and who continues to guide the Church through the Holy Synod and the apostolic succession.

As Paul wrote, “We have all been given one Spirit” (1 Cor. 12:27). If we follow different spirits, we cannot remain in the one Church. If we listen to conflicting voices, we fall into confusion. But where the Holy Spirit speaks, there is harmony, truth, and peace.

Therefore, we confess with boldness and humility: There is only one true religion, led by one Holy Spirit, preaching one hope, grounded in one baptism, centered on one Lord, and rooted in the faith once delivered to the saints.


Final Exhortation

Let us not be deceived by the multitude of opinions that blow like the winds. Let us stand firm upon the rock of apostolic truth. Let us hand down to our children not novelty, but the ancient faith, and let us worship with the saints as they worshipped: in spirit and in truth, in unity and in holiness.

For what greater inheritance can we pass down, than to say: “I have kept the faith,” the one faith that saves, the one religion that sanctifies, and the one Spirit that seals us for the day of redemption?

May the name of the Holy God be praised forever and ever in every tongue that He has created, today and always. 

May the blessings of God's mother, the virgin, and the cross be upon us.
Amen.
