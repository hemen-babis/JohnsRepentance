Part 3_ A Contemplative Exposition 

Beloved children of the Orthodox faith, Peace and grace be multiplied unto you through the knowledge of our Lord Jesus Christ. With reverence, we greet you in the name of the Almighty, whose commandments are life to those who walk in them. In the sacred teaching of our Lord, preserved in the Gospel according to St. Matthew, it is written: “On these two commandments hang all the Law and the Prophets” (Matthew 22:40). Let us then meditate upon this sacred utterance—not with idle curiosity, but with brokenness of heart and trembling of soul, that we may behold the depth of God’s love and the weight of His commandments.

What is Love?

The second of these commandments is like unto the first: “You shall love your neighbor as yourself” (Matthew 22:39). Not less than the commandment to love God, this holy precept binds the soul to its fellow man, and in so doing, fulfills the will of the Father who made us all in His own image.

This love is not an emotion, fleeting as the wind, nor a sentiment confined to words. It is the manifestation of God’s love within us—proved and perfected in our relationship with those around us.

The holy Apostle John, the beloved of the Lord, he who leaned upon His breast and stood by Him even at the cross, bears witness to this truth when he writes:
“If anyone says, ‘I love God,’ yet hates his brother, he is a liar. For he who does not love his brother whom he has seen, how can he love God whom he has not seen?” (1 John 4:20).

Herein lies the heart of the matter: love for God is not in isolation. It must overflow to our neighbor. Otherwise, it is mere illusion. The Apostle, inspired by the Spirit, makes no separation between love of God and love of man—for the latter is the fruit and evidence of the former.

If you would love God, begin by loving your brother. If you would draw near to the Creator, begin by honoring His image in the creature. For how shall a man claim to revere the King, and yet trample upon the royal image?

Our True Identity Revealed Through Love

What greater test is there of our Christianity than how we love our neighbor?
It is not in the knowledge of doctrine alone, nor in ritual observance, but in whether we embrace the other, the suffering, the poor, and the lowly. In the crucible of hardship and injustice, we are measured. In the furnace of trials, our hearts are revealed. Shall we love only when it is convenient? Shall we do good only to those who please us?

Nay, brethren. We are called to love with a heart broken and contrite, and a spirit pure and undefiled. Not as the lawyer who, seeking to justify himself, asked, “Who is my neighbor?” (Luke 10:29). For he wished to limit love with legal precision. But love knows no boundaries, no calculations. It flows freely from the heart that has known the mercy of God.

If our desire is genuine—to do good with sincerity—then neighbors shall appear to us at every step. The poor, the weary, the sorrowful—they will be ever present, as our Lord Himself declared: “The poor you will always have with you” (Matthew 26:11). It is not their presence that is lacking, but our willingness to love.

How Do We Live in Love?

Let us not live as those who give merely out of abundance, or with grudging hands, but as those who understand the depth of God’s love toward us. There are two wellsprings from which love must pour:

1. Understanding God’s Love for Us

From the beginning, He formed man not like the beasts, but in His image and likeness. He breathed into us the breath of life. He clothed us with glory and made us rulers over creation. When we fell through sin, He did not cast us away. Rather, He stooped down to lift us up.
