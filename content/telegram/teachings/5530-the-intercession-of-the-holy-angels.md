👉🏽👉🏽👉🏽 The #Intercession of the Holy #Angels

(Part 12)

Dear beloved in Christ, peace and grace to you.Today we continue our spiritual reflection on one of the beautiful mysteries of our faith the intercession of the holy angels.
Let us therefore open our hearts and learn how the angels stand before God as intercessors.

✍🏾The Meaning of “Standing” Angels as Intercessors

If an angel were to say, “I stand in the presence of God to intercede for all people,” would this be difficult to believe?

The Archangel Gabriel himself said, “I am Gabriel, who stands in the presence of God” (Luke 1:19). This clearly shows that to “stand before God” means to serve and to intercede.

Likewise, the prophet Daniel speaks about Saint Michael the Archangel:

“And at that time shall Michael stand up, the great prince who stands for the children of your people.”(Daniel 12:1)

In this verse, Saint Michael is described as the one who stands for the people of God. For what purpose does he stand? It is not to defend God who needs no defender but to plead and intercede for God’s people.

If we interpret the phrase “he stands for the children of the people” without understanding its spiritual meaning, we may fall into confusion. The proper understanding is that Saint Michael stands before the Creator as an intercessor and protector for humanity.

Daniel further confirms the presence of countless angels standing before God in heavenly service:

“A thousand thousands ministered unto Him, and ten thousand times ten thousand stood before Him.”(Daniel 7:10)


Here, “standing” clearly represents the act of ministering, serving, and interceding before God.

✍🏾The Meaning of “Seeing” Angels as Witnesses and Intercessors

Just as the word “stand” carries deep spiritual meaning, the word “see” also bears multiple sacred meanings in the Bible. It can signify not only physical sight but also understanding, compassion, sorrow, hope, fear, and repentance.

The Psalmist says:

👉🏾 “The eyes of all wait upon You, O Lord” (Psalm 144:15).

Here, the act of “seeing” expresses trust, expectation, and prayer. In many places, the Holy Scriptures use the word “see” to mean to listen, to examine, to have mercy, to forgive, or even to become angry.

When it is said, “God looked upon me with favor,” it means that He remembered me, visited me, had mercy on me, heard my cry, and forgave me. The same word can also express divine displeasure, for when God “looks upon” sin, it can be a gaze of judgment.

Let us observe a few examples:

“And the Lord looked with favor upon Abel and his offering” (Genesis 4:4).

“Behold, the eyes of the Lord are upon those who fear Him” (Psalm 32:18).

“The eyes of the Lord are upon the righteous, and His ears are open to their cry” (Psalm 33:15).

From these verses, we understand that when God “sees,” He acts — He listens, accepts, blesses, or corrects.

Similarly, when man or creation “sees,” the meaning changes according to context. The Psalmist prays, “Look upon me, O Lord my God, and hear me” (Psalm 12:3), which means, “Have mercy on me.”

In the Gospel, our Lord says, “Show yourself to the priest” (Matthew 8:4). The command “Show yourself” here means to be examined or to be made pure again through repentance. Even in common speech, we find this reflected when people say, “Let me see my doctor,” meaning to be examined or healed.

By understanding these deeper meanings of “seeing,” we can also understand how the angels intercede for us.
