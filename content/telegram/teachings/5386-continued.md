4. Reverence and Responsibility in Using Holy Icons

The size of holy icons can be increased or decreased as needed. In prayer books, it depends on They size of the book, and in a church building, it can be adjusted according to the size of the building.

There are things we should keep in mind when using holy icons for prayer and various services. It is important to keep them in a dignified manner so that they do not become damaged or faded. Since the icons represent the one depicted God, a saint, or a martyr it is necessary to show them proper respect and reverence.

5. The Caution Against Confusion and Imitation

Artists should paint holy icons correctly so that the faithful can recognize the true subjects of the icons and pray to them. However, this practice is not always followed in some places of worship.

Two or three different images are sometimes painted to imitate the likeness of a saint. An example of this is the image of St. Arsema. This became a controversial topic among believers, and sources emerged claiming that it was not her true image. Eventually, it was concluded that the painting widely accepted as that of Saint Arsema was actually of Saint Barbara.

6. The Eternal Message of Ezekiel’s Brick

Just as Ezekiel’s brick carried a prophetic message to Jerusalem, so too do our sacred images bear divine lessons to every generation. The brick was not a mere object it was a teaching tool through which God revealed His judgment and mercy. Likewise, the Church’s icons are silent preachers of faith, teaching through color what words cannot contain.

May the name of the Holy God be praised forever and ever in every tongue that He has created, today and always.

May the blessings of God's mother, the virgin, and the cross be upon us.
