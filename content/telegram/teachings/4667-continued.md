6. The Analogy of the Heart, the Word, and the Breath

Let us employ a humble image, though all images fall short of divine mystery.

The Father is like the heart.

The Son is like the Word spoken from the heart.
The Holy Spirit is like the breath that proceeds from the heart and carries the Word.

This analogy does not explain the Trinity, but gives a simple entry into understanding unity in distinction. Just as the heart gives rise to the word and breath without division, so God is one in essence, three in persons.

7. The Incarnate Son Ascends

When the Lord says, “I ascend to my Father and your Father, and to my God and your God,” He speaks as the one mediator between God and man Jesus Christ, who took on flesh and glorified it.

He brings our humanity into the heavens. He ascends not as a disembodied spirit, but as the crucified and risen Lord, bearing in His glorified body the marks of His Passion. And He declares that the One who is His Father by nature is our Father by grace. The One who is His God according to His humanity is our God both in spirit and body.

8. Adopted by Grace

We are not children of God by nature. We are dust, clay molded by the hand of the Creator.

“O Lord, you are our Father; we are the clay, and you are our potter” (Isaiah 64:8).

But now, by the will of the Father and the blood of the Son, we are called children. “See what love the Father has given us, that we should be called children of God and so we are” (1 John 3:1). The apostles are children of grace, not by natural begetting, but by the gift of adoption.

9. A Careful Distinction for Right Faith

Note well: our Lord did not say “we ascend to our Father and our God.” That would have placed Him among the creatures. Rather, He carefully separates Himself, saying “My Father and your Father… My God and your God.” Thus He preserves the truth of His divine sonship and our adoptive grace.

He, the natural Son, speaks of His flesh: “My God”. He, the divine Word, speaks of His eternal origin: “My Father”. We, who are redeemed, call God our Father not by nature but by mercy.

Conclusion: The Glory of the Ascension and the Grace of Sonship

Let us rejoice, O people of God, that our Lord who ascended did not ascend alone. He lifted humanity with Him, and through Him we cry, “Abba, Father!”

He is our shepherd, our keeper, our helper. He is the eternal Son who made us sons by grace.

Let us honor His divine humility and glorify His saving Ascension. Let us, like Mary Magdalene, hear His voice and cling to Him not in the flesh, but in spirit and truth.

May the name of the Holy God be praised forever and ever in every tongue that He has created, today and always. 

May the blessings of God's mother, the virgin, and the cross be upon us.
Amen.
