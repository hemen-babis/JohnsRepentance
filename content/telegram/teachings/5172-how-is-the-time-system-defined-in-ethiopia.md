👉🏾👉🏾👉🏾How is the #Time System Defined in #Ethiopia? 

(Part 2)

Beloved in Christ, sons and daughters of the holy Orthodox faith, we greet you with the peace of God. Today we turn our attention to the mystery of time as it is ordered in Ethiopia a land sanctified by the Gospel, whose very measure of days bears witness to the wisdom of the Creator. Let us therefore behold with reverence how the year is divided, how the seasons are appointed, and how the order of months gives glory to God, who is the Lord of times and ages.

1. The Ethiopian Year and Its Seasons

The Ethiopian reckoning of time is wondrous and full of meaning. Unlike the systems of other nations, Ethiopia counts a year as consisting of thirteen months, and it is divided into four great seasons. These are not arbitrary, but carefully set in order by divine providence and witnessed to in the writings of Saint Yared.

The four seasons are:

1. Keremt – from Sene 26 to Meskerem 25.

2. Metsew (Tbi) – from Meskerem 26 to Tahisas 25.

3. Bega (Hagay) – from Tahisas 26 to Megabit 25.

4. Tsedey – from Megabit 26 to Sene 25.

Each season has 99 days, except Keremt, which has 95 days.

Saint Yared, the holy hymnographer, spoke of these seasons and showed how each one gives glory to God.

2. The Four Seasons in Detail

2.1 The Season of Metsew (Tbi)

This season, also called Tbi, begins in the fourth week after Keremt. It runs from Meskerem 26 to Tahisas 25, making 90 days in total.

Saint Yared gives it a noble description: “You made the seasons so that we may boast of your wonderful power. You gave the Keremt for rain and the Metsew for flowers” (Digwa Zetsge Kufale 2 Jo.2÷23; cf. Jer. 5:24).

This is the season of harvest. The rains of Keremt water the earth, and in Metsew the earth brings forth fruit in abundance. Flowers bloom across Ethiopia on fields, hills, and mountains alike. Its chief months are Tikimt, Hidar, and Tahisas. It is truly the time of nature’s adornment, when the earth rejoices in her Creator.

2.2. The Season of Bega (Haggay)

Following Metsew comes Bega, also called Hagay, which stretches from Tahisas 26 to Megabit 25, encompassing the months of Tir, Yekatit, and Megabit.

The word Hagay is derived from the Geʿez tongue, meaning “summer” or “time of the sun.” One fourth of the year it occupies, and thus it is called the hand of the seasons. Scripture itself acknowledges this order: “You made Keremt and Bega” (Ps. 75:17; Zech. 14:10; Gen. 10:22).

Bega is the season of brightness, of harvest, of spring and dryness. The grains, ripened by the sun, are gathered into barns. In this time, Ethiopia celebrates the fruit of labor. Yekatit, in particular, is a month when the gathered harvest is safely stored. Because the barns are full, the people turn to feasting, celebrating baptisms, marriages, and annual festivals. This period is known as the Merawi, the season of rejoicing.
