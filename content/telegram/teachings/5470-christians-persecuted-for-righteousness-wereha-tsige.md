👉🏽👉🏾👉🏾#Christians #Persecuted #For #Righteousness (#wereha #Tsige)

(Blessed are those who are persecuted for #righteousness, for theirs is the kingdom of heaven, #Matthew 5:10)

(Part 5)

Beloved in Christ,Peace be with you. Blessed are those who are persecuted for righteousness’ sake, for theirs is the kingdom of heaven (Matthew 5:10). Christ Himself faced rejection and suffering, and His apostles were persecuted for proclaiming the truth. Through trials and hardships, we imitate Christ, strengthen our faith, and receive the reward He has promised.

“Imitating Christ Through Trials and Persecution"

✍🏾To endure trials and tribulations is to imitate Christ. Our Lord Jesus Christ, who came to save humanity as both an example and a ransom, was persecuted by the Jews who sought to kill Him. They did not persecute Him because He had committed any crime, but because His good deeds exposed their wickedness, because He honored those whom they despised, and because He healed the sick on the Sabbath. The Evangelist John writes of this: “Therefore the Jews persecuted Jesus and sought to kill Him, because He had done these things on the Sabbath” (John 5:16).

✍🏾When Jesus, the Savior of the world, journeyed toward Jerusalem, the people did not receive Him. The Evangelist Luke recounts: “When the days drew near for Him to go up to Jerusalem, He set His face to go up, and sent messengers before Him. They entered a village of the Samaritans to prepare for Him, but the people did not receive Him, because His face was set toward Jerusalem” (Luke 9:51–53).

The Apostles and the Reality of Persecution

✍🏾The true apostles were hated and persecuted by the world. This was as our Lord had foretold: “Ye shall be hated of all men for my name’s sake: but he that endureth to the end, the same shall be saved. And when they persecute you in this city, flee ye into another: for verily I say unto you, Ye shall not have gone over the cities of Israel, till the Son of man be come” (Matthew 10:22–23). Likewise, “Therefore also said the wisdom of God, Behold, I send unto you prophets and apostles; and some of them shall they kill and persecute” (Luke 11:49).

✍🏾Observe that the Lord addressed both the persecutors and His disciples. To the scribes and Pharisees, He said: “Therefore, behold, I send unto you prophets and wise men and scribes; and some of them ye shall kill and crucify, and some of them ye shall scourge in your synagogues, and persecute from city to city” (Matthew 23:34). This warning reveals that those who oppose God’s truth will always act against the faithful.

✍🏾To His disciples, however, He spoke words of preparation and encouragement: “Before all this, they will lay their hands on you and persecute you, delivering you up to synagogues and prisons, bringing you before kings and governors for My name’s sake” (Luke 21:12). In this, the Lord teaches that persecution is not a mark of abandonment but a part of the faithful’s calling. It prepares the servants of God for ministry and strengthens their reliance on Him

Mercy, Truth, and the Persecution of the Righteous

✍🏾the words of the wise Solomon: “Those who do evil will err, but mercy and truth will be with those who do good” (Proverbs 14:22). This teaches us that those who think, plan, and act against Christians, the Church, and the work of God are walking in error. Their schemes will not succeed, for the Lord’s justice prevails.

✍🏾 It is our duty to pray for those who persecute the faithful, asking God to grant them repentance and salvation, so that they may turn away from sin and be spared from eternal punishment.

✍🏾Mercy and truth, however, belong to those who do good and endure suffering for righteousness. Those who persecute Christians are truly pitiable, for in their opposition, they attack the righteous yet gain nothing for themselves. Ironically, in persecuting the innocent and upright, they add righteousness to those they oppose.
