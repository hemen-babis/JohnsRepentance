4. The Saints Lead Us to Christ They Do Not Replace Him

The intercession of the saints is no competition to Christ’s unique role as Mediator. Far from replacing Him, they bring us nearer to Him, as friends of the Bridegroom who rejoice at His voice. God Himself established this pattern, calling Abraham to intercede (Gen. 20:7), Job to pray for his friends (Job 42:8), and urging us to pray for one another (James 5:16).

Intercession is the fruit of love. When saints pray for us, they reveal their union with Christ and love for His Church. Their prayers are not a detour from Christ, but the work of Christ’s own body the Church united in heaven and on earth.

“God is not the God of the dead, but of the living” (Matt. 22:32).

Indeed, death does not silence the prayers of the saints. Their love endures; their prayer continues. And if we ask our brethren on earth to pray for us, how much more shall we ask those who behold the face of God?

5. The Church’s Unity: In Heaven and On Earth

The Church is one body in Christ those on earth and those in heaven. Hebrews speaks of our being “surrounded by so great a cloud of witnesses” (Heb. 12:1). Revelation shows the martyrs crying out to God on behalf of the suffering (Rev. 6:10). And even Abraham, seated in heaven, receives the poor Lazarus into his bosom (Luke 16:22).

Thus, the Church does not invent this mystery but receives it from the living witness of Scripture. Those who have departed in the flesh are not severed from us. Their works follow them (Rev. 14:13), their prayers ascend, and their love remains. The Church in heaven prays with and for the Church on earth.

6. Sacred Tradition Upholds What Scripture Declares

From the earliest centuries, the faithful honored the saints. In 155 A.D., the Church of Samaria already showed special love for the martyrs. The bones of saints were regarded with reverence, their lives preserved in memory, and their prayers sought by believers. Tradition is not the invention of man it is the living memory of the Spirit moving through the generations of the faithful.

The Church builds altars in their name, celebrates feast days, and instructs the faithful in their virtues, not as an addition to the gospel but as a mirror of it. The saints are the gospel lived, the Word made fruitful in the soil of men and women like us.

7. Conclusion: Let Us Imitate the Saints, Not Revile Them

O soul who questions the saints, do not tear down what God has built. Rather, ask yourself: Do I walk in their footsteps? Do I love Christ as they did? Do I pray, fast, forgive, and serve with such zeal? Let us then strive not to be critics but imitators.

As St. Paul said: “Be imitators of me, as I am of Christ” (1 Cor. 11:1). The saints are our elder siblings in the family of God. They do not block the light of Christ they reflect it. And the Church, in her wisdom, teaches us to walk in their light until we, too, become bright lamps in the kingdom of the Lamb.

May the name of the Holy God be praised forever and ever in every tongue that He has created, today and always.

May the blessings of God's mother, the virgin, and the cross be upon us.
Amen.
