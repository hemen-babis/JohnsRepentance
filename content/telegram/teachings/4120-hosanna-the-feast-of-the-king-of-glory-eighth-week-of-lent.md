👉🏽👉🏽👉🏽#Hosanna – The Feast of the King of Glory (Eighth Week of Lent)

Part 2

“Blessed is He who comes in the name of the Lord.”

1. The Stripping of His Garments – Glory and Humility Revealed

Clothing, brethren, is not mere fabric; it is the veil of dignity, the symbol of honor. When our Lord was stripped of His garments, it was not only His body that was exposed, but also the deep mystery of divine humility unveiled.

They stripped Him not only to wound His flesh, but to proclaim:
“You are our covering our body beneath; our glory is in You.”
By laying down their garments, the people declared: “We give You our honor, for all honor belongs to You.” Thus, He who is the brightness of the Father’s glory was lowered in the eyes of men, that He might lift up the despised and lowly.

The donkey once a creature of scorn was elevated that day. Why? Because the Lord of Glory chose to sit upon it. As the prophet Isaiah declared, “He was despised and rejected by men,” yet He chose the despised to bear His glory. So it is in all generations: when the Lord visits a soul, He lifts up what the world tramples down.

So too, the saints, while they lived on earth, were beaten, mocked, driven into caves, counted as the scum of the world. But their zeal in suffering, their perseverance in faith, raised them to immortal honor. God glorified them, and even their persecutors bowed at their tombs.

2. Palm Branches – Signs of Joy and Triumph

Behold the palm branches! What are these green boughs lifted high in the hands of the people?

They are the ancient symbol of joy and victory. When Abraham embraced the miracle of Isaac, when Israel crossed the Red Sea, when Judith slew Holofernes, palm branches were cut in celebration, and thanksgiving filled the land.

So now, with these same branches, the children of Jerusalem greet the Son of David, not as a mere teacher, but as the victorious King, who had come to vanquish the dominion of death, shatter the bonds of corruption, and cast down the tyranny of the grave.

They sang,

"Hosanna to the Son of David! Blessed is He who comes in the name of the Lord!”

And long before this, the Psalmist David, in the spirit, foresaw this day and cried aloud:

“This is the day the Lord has made; let us rejoice and be glad in it. Now save us, O Lord; now restore us, O Lord; blessed is He who comes in the name of the Lord.” (Psalm 117:24–26)

This day, this Hosanna, was foretold and fulfilled. But what did the Lord find when He reached the Temple?

3. The House of Prayer Turned into a Market

He who had just received the praise of children and the adoration of the multitudes now approached His own house the Temple, the holy dwelling prepared for the worship of the Most High.

But instead of prayer, He found merchants.
Instead of incense, coin scales.
Instead of broken hearts, bargaining tongues.
Instead of reverence, robbery.

And the gentle Lamb was angered.

Yes, the humble Lord, the silent sufferer, was provoked not by insults against Himself, but by the profaning of His Father’s house. He overturned the tables, drove out the sellers, rebuked the blasphemers, and restored holiness to His temple.
