Continued 👆🏽

D. Natural Disasters and Global Suffering

"For nation shall rise against nation, and kingdom against kingdom: and there shall be famines, and pestilences, and earthquakes, in divers places." (Matthew 24:7)

The world will experience widespread famine, disease, and earthquakes.

These afflictions are not signs of divine abandonment but reminders of the fragility of earthly existence.

Instead of despairing, believers must persevere in faith and righteous deeds.

E. Persecution of the Faithful

"Then shall they deliver you up to be afflicted, and shall kill you: and ye shall be hated of all nations for my name’s sake." (Matthew 24:9)

True followers of Christ will face persecution, betrayal, and hatred.

Many will abandon the faith due to hardship.

The faithful must endure to the end, knowing that tribulation is a mark of true discipleship.

F. The Increase of Lawlessness and the Cooling of Love

"And because iniquity shall abound, the love of many shall wax cold. But he that shall endure unto the end, the same shall be saved." (Matthew 24:12-13)

The world will be plagued by moral decay.

Love will grow cold as selfishness, hatred, and sin increase.

Only those who endure with unwavering faith will find salvation.

G. The Universal Proclamation of the Gospel

"And this gospel of the kingdom shall be preached in all the world for a witness unto all nations; and then shall the end come." (Matthew 24:14)

Before the end, the Good News will reach every nation.

The spread of the Gospel is both a sign and a call to repentance.

Believers must actively participate in this mission.

4. The Call to Watchfulness and Preparedness

Christ did not reveal these signs to incite fear but to awaken spiritual vigilance. He repeatedly emphasized:

"Watch therefore: for ye know not what hour your Lord doth come." (Matthew 24:42)

"Be ye also ready: for in such an hour as ye think not, the Son of man cometh." (Matthew 24:44)

This call to watchfulness is not passive but active perseverance—prayer, repentance, charity, and unwavering devotion.

Conclusion: The Mount of Olives as a Lesson for All Generations

The Fifth Sunday of Lent, Debre Zeit, is more than a commemoration; it is a solemn reminder that we are living in the age of fulfillment. Christ’s words are not distant prophecies but present realities unfolding before our eyes.

What then must we do?

Remain firm in faith, despite deception.

Be unshaken by worldly turmoil, knowing that all things are in God’s hands.

Live a life of righteousness, preparing for the return of our Lord.

Spread the Gospel, fulfilling the divine mandate.

Endure to the end, for “he that endureth to the end shall be saved” (Matthew 24:13).

Let us heed the teachings given on the Mount of Olives, using them not as cause for fear but as motivation for repentance and godly living. For as surely as He ascended from that mountain, so too shall He return in glory.

"Even so, come, Lord Jesus." (Revelation 22:20)

May the name of the Holy God be praised forever and ever in every tongue that He has created, today and always. 

May the blessings of God's mother, the virgin, and the cross be upon us.
Amen.
