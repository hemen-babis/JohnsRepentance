Moreover, the number forty has other symbolic meanings in the Bible. For instance, the Great Flood in the time of Noah lasted for forty days and forty nights, representing a time of purification and renewal of the earth. Moses also wandered in the wilderness with the Israelites for forty years before entering the Promised Land, signifying a time of testing and preparation. Similarly, Jesus spent forty days in the desert before beginning his public ministry, demonstrating his obedience to the Father and his preparation for the work ahead.

In summary, the 40-day fast during Lent is significant in the Orthodox Church for various reasons. It is based on the 40-day fast of our Lord, which is commemorated by the Church apart from Saturdays and Sundays. The first and last weeks of the fast are added to the main fasting period of 40 days to make up the total of 55 days, but the focus is on the central 40 days. The number 40 has symbolic meaning in the Bible, representing a time of purification, testing, and preparation. Fasting during Lent is a reminder to live out our faith in action, not just in words, and to follow the example of Christ and the prophets who fasted before him.
