Dear faithful followers of the Orthodox teachings , we greet you in the peace and grace of our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ. Today, by the help of God, we present this important teaching for your reflection and spiritual growth.

✍🏾The Value of Knowing and Living the Truth

Knowing the truth is a precious gift. But what benefit is it to know the truth if one does not live according to it? For those who live by the truth, it brings true freedom and peace of heart (Jeremiah 6:16; Matthew 11:28).

Our Lord Jesus Christ said to the Jews who believed in Him:

“If you abide in My word, you are truly My disciples; and you shall know the truth, and the truth shall make you free.”(John 8:31–32)

Therefore, if we believe that truth frees us from the slavery of sin, we must desire to hear and understand the truth directly from its divine source. Those who love the truth and seek it from God Himself are guided not by emotion, but by knowledge and faith. When the pure truth is revealed, it removes shame, ignorance, and sin, leading the soul to salvation and eternal life (Proverbs 11:9).

Relying on one’s own limited understanding or false teachings from others keeps a person enslaved to error. Indeed, knowledge and identity are closely connected what one believes shapes who one becomes.

True knowledge is only useful when it is put into practice. Therefore, when God reveals the truth, the wise make the right decision and act upon it. Those who accept the truth sincerely also recognize and correct their past mistakes without hesitation.

When the path of righteousness leading to eternal life is made clear, the heart must remain firm and unwaveringvfor the truth concerns eternal salvation.

This teaching reveals the divine truth about the Intercessor and the mystery of intercession for all who desire to walk toward eternal life—those who seek to know the truth from the Holy Bible alone, the one and living Word of God that has no alternative. Whoever learns and lives by this truth is freed from the bondage of sin and walks in the light of salvation.

✍🏾 In the first part of our lesson, we discussed in detail the meaning of the term “intercessor.” To refresh our memory, let us now recall its definition and see how “intercession” is divided according to the Word of God.

✍🏾What Does “intercessor” Mean?

The word intercessor (amalaj)comes from the verb to intercede(mamaled), which means “to plead” or “to pray on behalf of another.” The act itself is called intercession. Thus, an intercessor is one who prays, pleads, or reconciles on behalf of others.

However, the word intercession (ማለደ)also carries another meaning “to rise early” or “to get up before dawn.” As it is written:

“And Abimelech rose early in the morning and called all his servants.” (Genesis 20:8)
“And Samuel rose early in the morning to meet Saul.” (1 Samuel 15:12)

Therefore, when the Scripture says, ( እየማለድሁ) in Jeremiah 7:25, it does not refer to supplication, but to the idea of rising early that is, acting before it is too late. Likewise, in Psalm 119 where it says, “My eyes are open to pray, that I may meditate on Your word,” the meaning is to rise early to seek the Word of God, not merely to make a petition.

✍🏾The Three Forms of Intercession

Although “intercession” generally means to make supplication, not all forms of intercession are the same. According to the Holy Scriptures, intercession is revealed in three primary forms:

1. The Intercession of Christ – Isaiah 53:1–12

2. The Intercession of People in the Flesh – 2 Corinthians 9:14

3. The Intercession of Bribery or False Appeal – Deuteronomy 10:16

The Holy Bible repeatedly and clearly speaks of our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ as “He intercedes”. But it is fitting to ask: “Since Christ is God, to whom does He intercede?”

Accepting the Scriptural answer with humility and faith shows spiritual understanding.
