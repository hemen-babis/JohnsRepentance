Part 1

Beloved children of the Holy Orthodox Church, peace be upon you in the name of our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ. As we tread the narrow and blessed path laid before us by the saints and the Apostles, let us today incline our hearts to receive the divine instruction on Nwaye Kedsat that is, the Holy Treasures of the Church so that our worship may be adorned with understanding, our reverence crowned with knowledge, and our souls illumined by the light of truth.

Let us not merely look, but behold. Let us not simply learn, but awaken. For what is holier than the instruments by which the mysteries of heaven are unveiled to us?

I. The Meaning of “Nwaye” and “kedsat or Holy”

The word “Nwaye,” as interpreted by the distinguished scholar of our Church, Kidane Wold Kifle whose name is recorded with honor in the annals of ecclesiastical literature means “money, wealth, heritage, gold, silver, and cattle.” These represent not merely earthly possessions, but symbolically, the riches of God bestowed upon His people. When placed in the service of the sanctuary, they cease to be common and are elevated into the realm of the sacred.

The word Holy how majestic and awe-striking a name! signifies “holiness, honor, purity, and grace.” Therefore, when we speak of Nwaye Kedsat, we are not speaking of temporal riches but of consecrated treasures, vessels and instruments that have been sanctified by the Most High, used in the mystical service of our salvation.

II. The Holy Relics of the Church: Instruments of Divine Worship

These holy relics, my beloved, are not lifeless artifacts but vibrant vessels through which the grace of God descends. They are as follows:

1. The Ark – The Dwelling Place of the Most High

The Ark, as the Scripture reveals, was built according to the precise command of the Lord to His prophet Moses (Exodus 25:1–22; 40:20). Constructed from acacia wood and overlaid with gold, it was not a product of man's imagination, but a heavenly blueprint manifested on earth.

This Ark held within it the sacred Tablets the Law written by the very finger of God. And oh, what reverence was due unto it! So sacred was its presence that it struck down those who dared approach irreverently.

But what of us? O, Ethiopia, how blessed art thou! For the Ark of Zion the very one that bore the divine commandments was brought into your bosom, into the ancient city of Axum, where it rests in the Church of St. Mary, shielded from all human sight, yet revered by all.

Each year, on the 21st of November, multitudes gather to honor the Ark’s arrival, lifting their voices in worship, giving thanks to the God of Glory. And behold the love of our Church! In every region, she raises up lesser Arks, modeled after the original, and places them in temples not of golden measure, perhaps, but endowed with honor. They are celebrated during the holy feasts of the Lord, the Theotokos, the angels, the martyrs, and the saints.

2. The Chair (Throne) – A Seat for the Ark and Altar

This sacred chair, fashioned from gold, silver, wood, or iron, is not for rest but for exaltation. Upon it rests the Ark. Upon it prayers are offered, and from it, sacrifices ascend to the heavens.

Only the consecrated hands of clergy may approach it. Deacons may stand about it, but none may presume to touch it without grace and ordination. For this chair represents not only the throne of the Ark but also the symbol of Christ, of our Lady the Virgin, of the angelic host.

Thereupon are laid sacred items: the Ark (Tablet), icons of the Virgin, altar implements, chalice, cross, and incense all of which participate in the Eucharistic mystery, wherein the Body and Blood of Christ are offered for the life of the world.
