Beloved in Christ,
Grace and peace be multiplied unto you, who love the Mother of God and her Son, our Lord Jesus Christ.
Let us enter with reverence into the mystery of Zemene Tsige  the Season of Flowers  and the sacred hymn called Mahlete Tsige, which the Fathers of our Church have handed down to us as a treasure of thanksgiving, contemplation, and spiritual renewal.

Mahlete Tsige (The Hymn of the Season of Flowers)

👉🏾Mahlete Tsige:- although it can be prayed at any time, is especially used in church services during the period that commemorates the Flight into Egypt and the return to Nazareth of the Blessed Virgin Mary , which lasts from Meskerem 26 to Hidar 5. During this time, it serves as a thanksgiving and prayer recited every Sunday night, remembering the wandering and journey of our Lady with her Son into Egypt.

✍🏾In our country, the months of Meskerem and Tikmt are known as the months of flowering (Zemene Tsige). This is a time when the mountains are adorned with blossoms and filled with beauty that delights the eye.

At this time, our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ said:

👉🏾“Why do you worry about clothing? Consider the lilies of the field, how they grow. They do not toil or spin; yet I tell you that even Solomon in all his glory was not arrayed like one of these. If God so clothes the grass of the field, which is here today and tomorrow is thrown into the oven, how much more will He clothe you, O you of little faith? Do not worry, saying, ‘What shall we eat?’ or ‘What shall we drink?’ or ‘What shall we wear?’”
(Matthew 6:28–31)

✍🏾Therefore, Mahlete Tsige reminds us that we should not be anxious about worldly needs but rather focus on seeking the Kingdom of Heaven. It teaches us to trust in God, who created the mountains and the flowers, knowing that He will never fail to provide the food, clothing, and all that we need for our lives.

The Fleeting Nature of Life

✍🏾And the noble David said, "Remember, O God, that we are dust; and man's days are like grass. Like a flower of the field, so he flourishes; for the wind passes over it, and it is gone." (Psalm 102:14–16) As it is written, human life is like grass it grows, blooms, then withers, and the wind blows it away until its place is no longer known.

✍🏾After a person grows up, he withers like the setting sun and is carried away by the wind of death. When he descends to the grave, his place is forgotten. Therefore, the time of Tsige is a season for each person to remember that his days are like flowers that quickly fade away and to awaken himself to do good deeds before this time passes.
