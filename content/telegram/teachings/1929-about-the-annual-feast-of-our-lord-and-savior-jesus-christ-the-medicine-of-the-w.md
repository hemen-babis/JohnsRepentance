About the annual #feast of Our Lord and Savior #Jesus #Christ; the medicine of the world. 

Brethren in Christ, as we commemorate the annual feast of our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ (November 7), let us take a moment and learn more about Him and the Devine gifts He has given us. Jesus Christ is both fully man fully God. He is perfect in His divinity and perfect in His humanity. This is no mere titular union, nor a confounding of natures as the heretics have vainly asserted, but a true and indissoluble union, one Person of two natures, unaltered, unconfused, undivided, and inseparable. He is of two natures, which undergo no confusion, no change, no division, no separation; at no point was the difference between the natures taken away through the union, but rather the property of each nature is preserved and comes together to form one person and subsistence.

Consider, O faithful, that the Word became flesh not by a mere appearance or diminution of His divine nature; rather, He remained what He was and assumed what He was not, for our sake and for our salvation. In the unfathomable abyss of His mercy, He who is beyond eternity entered time, He whom the heavens cannot contain was contained in a virgin’s womb, He who upholds the universe was held in the arms of a mother.

Behold the Eucharist, that venerable and most divine Sacrament of His Body and Blood. As the Incarnation unites divinity with humanity, so does the Holy Eucharist unite Christ with us, and us with Christ. This is not a mere symbol, as some have erroneously taught, but the true Body and the true Blood, offered for the life of the world and for its salvation. As St. John Damascene declares, "The bread and wine are not a foreshadowing of the body and blood of Christ—by no means!—but the deified body of the Lord, because the Lord has said, 'This is my body', not, 'This is a symbol of my body', and 'My blood', not, 'A symbol of my blood'."

And what, dear brethren, is the Church but the Body of Christ, the fullness of Him who fills all in all? Within the embrace of the Church lies the fulness of truth and the treasures of grace, dispensed through the sacraments. In her, heaven and earth are joined, and the eschatological reality of the Kingdom is made present. The Church, both visible and invisible, temporal and eternal, is the context in which the mysteries of God are made manifest and efficacious.

The incarnate Logos, Jesus Christ our Lord, is the center and culmination of all history, the Alpha and Omega, the One in whom all things hold together. By His Incarnation, life, death, resurrection, and ascension, He has recapitulated all things, offering us a path to deification, that we may become by grace what He is by nature.

In this salvific narrative, we must not overlook the kenotic movement of the Incarnation: He who was in the form of God took the form of a servant, being born in the likeness of men, and being found in human form, He humbled Himself and became obedient unto death, even death on a cross. This self-emptying is not a forsaking of His divine nature, but the expression of His divine love, a love which calls us to communion, to theosis, to partake of the divine nature.

Therefore, let us approach this great mystery with awe and with humility, with repentance and with love. Let us embrace the risen Christ with a faith not blind but seeing beyond sight, discerning the Word made flesh for our sake. As we partake of the Holy Mysteries, let us be transformed, becoming what we receive, the body of Christ, to the praise of His glorious grace.
