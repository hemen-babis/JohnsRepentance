👉🏽👉🏽👉🏽You Are #Witnesses (Luke 24:48)

Beloved brethren in Christ, heirs of the Apostolic faith handed down by the Fathers, today we are called to contemplate the sacred words spoken by our Lord after His resurrection:
"You are witnesses of these things" (Luke 24:48).

Behold the wonder! After trampling down death by death, the victorious King, the Word made flesh, the Son of the Living God, appeared to His disciples who sat trembling in fear. Not with judgment did He approach them, but with the commission of life: to bear witness to the great and fearful mystery they had seen and touched.

This witness this testimony is not mere retelling. It is martyrdom, a word whose very root (martus) means "witness." It demands the giving of one's life to affirm what has been seen, heard, and known in the spirit.

As Saint John the Theologian declares in the opening of his first epistle:

"That which was from the beginning, which we have heard, which we have seen with our eyes, which we have looked upon and our hands have handled, concerning the Word of life...we declare to you." (1 John 1:1)

Thus, a martyr is not only one who dies for Christ but is first and foremost a truth-teller, a confessor of the Resurrection and the Life, a herald of that which cannot be denied.

The Rejection of the Jews

In the days of the Apostles, the great tragedy of the Jewish people was not their denial of Christ's birth or baptism these they could not deny, having witnessed them. Rather, their stumbling block was the Lord’s resurrection and His promised Second Coming. They could not endure the fullness of His divine revelation.

They accepted His humanity but denied His divinity. Yet, it is precisely the Death and Resurrection of Christ that breathe meaning into all of human existence, as the Apostle proclaims:

"If Christ is not risen, then our preaching is empty and your faith is also empty." (1 Corinthians 15:14)

Thus, we in the holy liturgy proclaim without ceasing: "We proclaim Your death, O Lord, and profess Your resurrection until You come again."

The Apostolic Mission

When the Lord sent out His disciples, He did not shield them from the reality of suffering. He spoke plainly:

"Behold, I send you out as sheep in the midst of wolves; be therefore wise as serpents and innocent as doves." (Matthew 10:16)

Their duty was to confess Christ boldly before men, even unto death, for as the Master promised, He Himself would confess them before His heavenly Father.

Saint Stephen, full of zeal, was the first to seal his witness with blood. His martyrdom became the seed of the Church's expansion. A great persecution arose, and Christianity, like a mighty flame, leapt from Jerusalem to the ends of the earth. The number of believers multiplied swiftly, reaching eight thousand through the powerful preaching of Saint Peter (Acts 8:1).

Saint Peter — The Rock of Witness

Saint Peter — known as Petra in Greek, Cephas in Aramaic, both meaning "rock" or "foundation" — stood as the bulwark of faith. To him, Christ declared:

"You are Peter, and upon this rock I will build my Church, and the gates of hell shall not prevail against it." (Matthew 16:18)

Peter, the son of Jonah, a simple fisherman of Bethsaida near Galilee, was chosen not for worldly wisdom but for the humility and readiness of heart to leave all and follow Christ. His occupation as a fisherman became a prophetic symbol, for he was called to fish for the souls of men.

In Capernaum, where he lived with his wife Concordia, he toiled not only with nets but now with the Word of Life. His virtues were manifold: he was quick to speak, quick to act, yet humble to acknowledge his faults a rare gem, for only the hypocrite denies his errors.
