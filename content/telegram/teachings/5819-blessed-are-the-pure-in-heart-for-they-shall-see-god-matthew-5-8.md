7. Participating in the Holy Mysteries

Participating in the Holy Mysteries with purity of heart is essential, for it is through them that we receive complete forgiveness of sins and the sealing of holiness. Before approaching this divine mystery, every believer must purify the heart through sincere repentance.

The Apostle Paul teaches:
“This cup is the new covenant in my blood. Do this, whenever you drink it, in remembrance of me. For as often as you eat this bread and drink this cup, you proclaim the Lord’s death until he comes. Whoever eats the bread or drinks the cup of the Lord in an unworthy manner will be guilty of the body and blood of the Lord. Let each person examine himself, and then eat of the bread and drink of the cup. For whoever eats and drinks in an unworthy manner eats and drinks judgment to himself, not discerning the body of the Lord.”
(1 Corinthians 11:25–29)

Through this teaching, the Church calls us to approach the Holy Mysteries with a purified heart and a reverent spirit.

Paul also warns:
“I do not want you to be participants with demons. You cannot drink the cup of the Lord and the cup of demons. You cannot be partakers of the Lord’s table and the table of demons.”
(1 Corinthians 10:19–21)
This reminds us that purity and spiritual discipline are required when drawing near to the Holy Sacrament.

During the Divine Liturgy, the priest proclaims:
“Let the clean receive the sacrifice; the unclean shall not approach.”

By partaking worthily in this Holy Sacrament, we receive perfect cleansing and the blessing of a heart made pure forever.

✍🏾For They See God: How Do We See God?

The apostle Saint Paul teaches, “He alone is immortal, dwelling in unapproachable light, whom no man has seen or can see” (1 Timothy 6:16). No one sees God in His divine essence with physical eyes. Our eyes cannot even look at the full glory of the sun, let alone the infinite majesty of God. We also cannot see spiritual beings with the senses of the body.

✍🏾Our Lord explained in the Sermon on the Mount, “Blessed are the pure in heart, for they shall see God,” revealing that God is not seen with the eyes of the body but with the purified heart. In the Gospel of John He said, “I came into this world for judgment, that those who do not see might see, and those who see might become blind” (John 9:39). This shows that spiritual sight comes from faith, not from physical vision.

✍🏾Although many saw Christ with their physical eyes and heard His voice, the Pharisees, who claimed to understand the law, remained blind because they refused to believe. The apostles, however, opened their hearts in faith, received the light of truth, and became the light of the world.

✍🏾As Saint John Afework taught, just as our bodily eyes are needed to see earthly things, the eyes of the heart (the mind and inner life) are needed to see God. Just as the eye must be healthy to see clearly, the heart must be purified from sin to perceive the presence of God.

A blemish on the eye obscures vision. In the same way, sin darkens the heart and blocks the light of God. Those who cleanse their hearts by righteous living make their inner life a dwelling place for God. They sanctify their hearts, converse with Him in prayer, and see His works within them.
