👉🏾👉🏾👉🏾A Lesson on the Icon of Our Lady with the Crown" Misle Fkur Welda"( ምስለ ፍቁር ወልዳ ሥዕል )

(Part 5)

Beloved in Christ, heirs of the apostolic faith, let us speak of the holy icon of Our Lady, the Virgin Mother of God, as she appears in the paintings treasured by the Orthodox Church, and especially in the tradition of Fiqur Wolda. These images are not idle ornaments, nor are they vain embellishments. They are sermons in color and form, sacraments in paint, which instruct the faithful and remind the soul of divine mysteries.

The Crown of Our Lady

In many sacred depictions, Our Lady wears a crown. This crown is rich in meaning, encompassing both earthly imagery and heavenly truth:

1. The Crown of Martyrdom and Suffering

The crown upon her head signifies the suffering she willingly bore for Christ. From christ conception until the end of her earthly life, Mary embraced the trials of this world, enduring them with perfect love and grace. For this reason, she is rightly called Mother of Martyrs, a model of steadfastness and sacrificial devotion for all who suffer for the sake of the Lord.

2. The Crown of Queenship

Beyond this, the crown testifies that she is the Mother of the King of Kings, the Savior of the world. Chosen by God, she is appointed Queen, as prophesied by Saint David: “The queen shall stand at your right hand” (Psalm 44:4). Yet, let it be noted that this queenship is not an earthly or political glory, but the recognition of her holiness, humility, and service in God’s plan.

The Symbolism of Color and Garments

The garments painted upon the Even the types of clothing we see in her paintings are symbolic, not true representations of what Our Lady actually wore during her life."
Among them, the blue robe is most radiant:

It signifies her humility, the most precious jewel of her soul.

Humility: Blue reflects the serene and humble disposition of Our Lady.

Ark of the Covenant: Just as the Ark carried God’s presence, Mary became the dwelling of God Incarnate (Numbers 4:6–12, Exodus 28:31).

Heavenly Sky: Blue recalls the firmament and the rising sun, symbolic of Christ’s glory appearing in her (Malachi 4:2, Matthew 17:2).

This blue, once a precious and rare dye, became known as Mary’s dress in our tradition not to indicate literal clothing, but to signify her sacred and exalted role.

Crowns of the Saints and Our Lord

It is important to distinguish between earthly and divine crowns. Our Lord Jesus Christ, eternal King, is not crowned like human kings. The crown of thorns He bore, however, reminds us of His suffering for humankind.

Similarly, the crowns of saints signify their crowns of righteousness and life, granted by God in reward for their struggles and steadfastness in Christ (James 1:12, Revelation 2:10). These crowns, therefore, are spiritual symbols of victory, not earthly adornments.
