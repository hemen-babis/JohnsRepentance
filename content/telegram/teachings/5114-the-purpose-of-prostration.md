👉🏾👉🏾👉🏾The #Purpose of #Prostration

(Part 2)

Beloved in Christ, children of the holy Orthodox Church, grace and peace be unto you. Today let us meditate upon the sacred mystery of prostration, that ancient gesture of worship which the Holy Scriptures commend, and which the Fathers of the Church have faithfully preserved. For the body bows in humility, yet the soul soars in exaltation; the knees bend low, yet the heart rises to heaven. What, then, is the purpose of prostration?

1. Prostration of Worship — to God Alone

The first type is the prostration of worship. This is given only to God, because only He is divine by nature.

When we bow to God, we bow to the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit.

This is why our prayer says: “I prostrate for the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit, one prostrate .”

The word “one worship,” we do not count the number of prostrations, but rather affirm that the act is directed to the one God ( for The Father, The Son, The Holy Sprite ), and to no other. Neither angel nor man, nor any created thing, may receive such adoration. To offer it elsewhere would be idolatry, a theft of the glory due to God.

This kind of prostration must never be given to any creature not to people, angels, or things made by human hands. God warned His people:

“You shall not worship any other gods before me” (Exodus 20:3).

“For I, the Lord your God, am a jealous God” (Exodus 20:5).

To give worship to anyone else is to provoke God’s anger. Worship belongs to Him alone, for He is our Creator, Redeemer, and the source of all life.

2. Prostration of Grace

The second type is the prostration of honor,or the prostration of grace. This is not prostration of God’s divine nature, but honor shown to the saints, to the Virgin Mary, and even to the Holy Cross.

After we confess “I worship the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit,” our prayers continue with words of honor:

“I bow to our Lady, the Virgin Mary, who gave birth to God, and to the Cross on which Christ was crucified for the life of the world.”

This shows the difference:

Worship is only for God.

Honor is given to those whom God has sanctified and filled with His grace.

When we bow before Mary, we do not worship her as God. We honor her because she bore God in the flesh.When we bow before the Cross, we do not worship the wood, but honor it because it carried Christ and became the instrument of our salvation.

The Bible itself supports this kind of honor. Isaac blessed Jacob with these words:

“Let nations serve you, and princes bow down to you; be lord over your brothers, and let your father’s sons bow down to you. Cursed be he who curses you, and blessed be he who blesses you” (Genesis 27:29).

Though Esau was the firstborn, God gave Jacob the blessing. From that moment, God was called the God of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob. The act of bowing before Jacob was not worship of him as God, but honor for the grace God placed upon him.

3,What Prostration Teaches Us

Prostration has a deep spiritual meaning.

Bowing down shows humility, that we are dust before God.

Touching the ground reminds us of our weakness and mortality.

Rising again points to our hope of resurrection in Christ.

Thus, every prostration becomes a silent sermon. It teaches us humility, reminds us of God’s holiness, and strengthens our faith in eternal life.

Conclusion

So then, we must keep these two truths in our hearts:

1. Prostration of Worship — belongs to God alone: Father, Son, and Holy Spirit.

2. Prostration of Grace — belongs to the saints, to the Virgin Mary, and to the Cross, because God’s grace shines through them.

When we worship God, we confess His majesty.When we honor His saints, we glorify the God who sanctified them.
