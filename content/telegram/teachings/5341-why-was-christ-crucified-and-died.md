👉🏾👉🏾👉🏾Why Was #Christ #Crucified and Died?

(Part 8)

✍🏾Beloved in Christ, let us approach the mystery of the Cross with trembling hearts, for in it is revealed both the depth of human sin and the unfathomable mercy of God.

✍🏾Before our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ, the Cross was only an instrument of cruelty and shame. History tells us that the practice of crucifixion began in Persia, where they worshipped the earth-god Ormuzd. Believing their god would be defiled if a criminal were executed on the ground, they lifted the condemned above the earth, fastening him to wood. From there, this torment spread until it became the dreaded law of Rome. Furthermore, in the Torah, those who were executed on a cross were considered accursed. “If anyone commits a sin deserving death, and you hang him on a tree, his body shall not remain all night upon the tree, for he who is hanged is accursed of God” (Deut. 21:22–23).

Then a question rises in our hearts: if those hanged upon wood are cursed, why did our Lord die on the Cross?

✍🏾Our Lord was crucified because we were under the curse. All humanity had fallen into corruption of body and soul, enslaved to the grave, to sin, and to death. We were condemned, lost, and unable to rise again. Yet our Creator, who leaves the ninety-nine to seek the one that has gone astray (Matt. 18:12–14), descended from heaven to find us.

👉🏾He was conceived by the Holy Spirit in the womb of the Virgin Mary (Luke 1:35).

👉🏾He took flesh from her, our true Mother, and was born in Bethlehem (Luke 2:7).

👉🏾In His incarnation, God became man (Matt. 16:13; 1 John 1:1–2; 5:20).

👉🏾He grew as a child among us (Luke 2:40).

👉🏾At the age of thirty, He was baptized in the Jordan, fasted forty days and nights, chose His apostles, elders, and women disciples (Matt. 3:13–17; Luke 3:21–23; Acts 1:15).

👉🏾For three years and three months, He taught the Gospel of the Kingdom.

👉🏾At last, He gave His body to be broken, His blood to be shed, His soul to be offered, and willingly endured the Cross for our salvation (Mark 15:23–37; John 10:11; 19:30).

✍🏾Thus, He took upon Himself the curse that was ours, so that the curse might be lifted from us. As Isaiah prophesied: “Surely He has borne our griefs and carried our sorrows; He was wounded for our transgressions, bruised for our iniquities; the chastisement of our peace was upon Him, and with His stripes we are healed” (Is. 53:4–5).

✍🏾St. Paul declared: “Cursed is everyone who is hung on a tree” (Gal. 3:13). Yet Christ bore that curse to destroy it. Again he said, “He made Him who knew no sin to be sin for us, that we might become the righteousness of God” (2 Cor. 5:21). And in Philippians, “He emptied Himself, taking the form of a servant, and humbled Himself, becoming obedient unto death—even death on a cross” (Phil. 2:7–8).

✍🏾St. Peter also proclaimed: “He committed no sin, nor was deceit found in His mouth. When reviled, He did not revile in return; when He suffered, He did not threaten, but committed Himself to Him who judges righteously. He Himself bore our sins in His body on the tree, that we, having died to sins, might live for righteousness by whose stripes you were healed” (1 Pet. 2:22–24).
