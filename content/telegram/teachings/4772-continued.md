VI. Prostration and Worship

To bend the knee is to confess the majesty of God. To fall on our face is to declare, with our body, the smallness of man. Prostration is not only outward but inward a bowing of the soul.

“You shall worship the Lord your God, and Him only shall you serve.” (Matt. 4:10)

“God is Spirit, and those who worship Him must worship in spirit and in truth.” (John 4:24)

It is fitting that the faithful prostrate once or thrice before prayer, and many of the fathers prescribe 36 prostrations daily except on the Sabbath, Pentecost, and the major feasts, when joy eclipses mourning.

VII. The Lifting of Hands

“Ethiopia has stretched out her hands to God.” (Ps. 67:31)

The uplifted hands are more than gesture they are testimony. They are the sign of power (Ex. 9:29), of supplication (1 Kings 8:22), of justice (Isa. 25:11), of humility (Jer. 4:31), and of creation itself (Isa. 45:12).

To lift the hand is to say, "I have nothing, Lord, but Thee. I surrender. I wait. I hunger."

VIII. Duties Required for Prayer

Let the prayerful soul:

Give glory to God in the highest,

Honor the Ever-Virgin who bore Him,

Revere the life-giving Cross on which our salvation was accomplished.

Prayer must be mingled with repentance, dressed in humility, offered in faith, and guarded with perseverance.

Conclusion

O brethren, what dignity is ours, that we are permitted to speak to the One before whom angels veil their faces! Let us not squander such grace. Let us not approach casually what prophets and patriarchs longed for with tears.

Let your homes be altars. Let your tongues be censors. Let your hearts burn continually like incense before the Lord.

As it is written:

“Pray without ceasing.” (1 Thess. 5:17)

For he who prays never perishes. He who prays draws near to the fire and is not consumed but is made flame.

May the name of the Holy God be praised forever and ever in every tongue that He has created, today and always. 

May the blessings of God's mother, the virgin, and the cross be upon us.
Amen.
