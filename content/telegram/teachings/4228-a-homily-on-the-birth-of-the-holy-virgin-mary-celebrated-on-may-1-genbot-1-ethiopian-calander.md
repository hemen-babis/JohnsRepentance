👉🏽👉🏽👉🏽A Homily on the #Birth of the #Holy #Virgin #Mary, Celebrated on May 1 Genbot 1) Ethiopian calendar

Part 1

Brethren beloved in Christ,
Let us lift up our minds and open our hearts as we contemplate the marvelous and holy mystery of the Nativity of the Ever-Virgin, the Theotokos, the Mother of our Lord and God, Jesus Christ. This sacred event, celebrated by the Holy Orthodox Tewahedo Church on Ginbot 1, the first day of May, is not a mere historical remembrance, but a divine turning point in the salvation of mankind. It is the day when hope took breath, when purity was born into the world, when the crown of creation entered time, and when the vessel of divine incarnation made her first cry.

1. The Day of Divine Birth and Fulfillment of Prophecy
This day is sanctified by prophecy and adorned by heavenly visions. It is not a day plucked randomly from the pages of time, but a divinely prepared moment, echoed in the utterances of prophets and saints from the foundation of the world. It is the birth of her whom Saint Ephrem rightly calls “the crown of our hope and the foundation of our purity.” And Isaiah the prophet, in the voice of the Spirit, foresaw this maiden, crying: “Behold, a virgin shall conceive and bear a son” (Isaiah 7:14). The virgin foretold in Isaiah's prophecy this sacred vessel, this chosen maiden was born on this very day.

And what do we hear from the Psalmist, the noble David, whose heart was fashioned by divine fire? “The queen shall stand at Your right hand arrayed in gold” (Psalm 44:9). That queen is none other than Mary, the Mother of our Lord, radiant in divine grace, enthroned beside the King of Kings. Her glory was seen before she ever entered the world, and her arrival was announced to the waiting saints as the dawn before the Sun.

Saint John the Theologian, transported into heavenly vision, beheld her, clothed in the sun, the moon beneath her feet, and a crown of twelve stars upon her brow (Revelation 12:1). Could such a woman be born as any common child? No. Her birth was long awaited and profoundly prepared in mystery.

2. The Birth Foretold in Prophecy, Genealogy, and Symbol
The prophetic voice did not fall silent after Isaiah, for the psalmist sang, “Her foundations are in the holy mountains” (Psalm 87:1). What mountains are these? Not of mere geography, but of priestly and kingly lineage. For the Blessed Virgin sprang forth from a royal root, descended from King David on her father Joachim’s side, and from the priestly line of Aaron through her mother Hannah. The convergence of royal authority and priestly sanctity culminated in Mary her very blood bearing the symbols of governance and intercession.

Isaiah speaks again, in heightened vision, “There shall come forth a rod out of the root of Jesse, and a flower shall bloom from it” (Isaiah 11:1). Jesse, the father of David, whose root gives rise to a rod that rod is Mary and the flower is Christ, born of her virgin womb. From the stump of fallen humanity, this rod blossoms with divine life.

Saint Ephrem, intoxicated with divine praise, exclaims: “Rejoice, O beautiful dove, Mary, you are a beautiful flower; your fragrance is from the root of Jesse.” She is the flower of divine fragrance, whose aroma overcomes the stench of death and corruption.

And again, the Psalmist declares: “Children are born to you instead of your fathers” (Psalm 131:13). What does this mean? That she who springs from fathers and forefathers becomes herself the mother of Him who is before all ages. She is daughter of David, yet mother of David’s Lord. She is the culmination of prophecy and the beginning of fulfillment.

3. The Place of Her Birth—Lebanon, the Mountain of Purity
Where did this miracle take place? The Scriptures and the Fathers point us to Mount Lebanon.
