2. The Balance of Day and Night

The year is not only divided by months, but by the measure of day and night. See how the Creato orders the light:

Meskerem: Day length 12 hours Night length 12 hours

👉Tikmt: Day length 11 hours Night length 13 hours

👉Hidar: Day length 10 hours Night length 14 hours

👉Tahsas: Day length 09 hours Night length 15 hours

👉Tir: Day length 10 hours Night length 14 hours

👉Yekatit: Day length 11 hours Night length 13 hours

👉Megabit: Day length 12 hours Night length 12 hours

👉Miyazya: Day length 13 hours Night length 11 hours

👉Ginbot: Day length 14 hours Night length 10 hours

👉Sene: Day length 15 hours Night length 09 hours

👉Hamle: Day length 14 hours Night length 10 hours

👉Nehase: Day length 13 hours, the length of the night is 11 hours.

So the cycle of day and night preaches to us the mysteries of life: there is a time of joy and a time of trial, a time when light seems hidden and a time when it shines in glory. Yet all is measured by the wisdom of Him who “made the greater light to rule the day, and the lesser light to rule the night” (Genesis 1:16).

3. The Week and the Days

Time is further bound in the cycle of the week, called Samint, from the Ge’ez word Semene meaning “eight.” For though the week has seven days, the cycle leads us from Sunday back to Sunday, from first to first, thus creating an eighth day that foreshadows eternity — the day beyond time, the Day of the Lord.

Each day bears its meaning from creation itself:

Sunday (Ehud) — From Ahade, “to make one.” It is the first day, the day when God began His creation.

Monday (Segno) — From Saneye, “to make two.” It marks the second day, when God divided the waters above and below.

Tuesday (Maksegno / Selus) — From Selese, “to make three.” The third day, when the earth brought forth vegetation.

Wednesday (E'rob) — From Rebi’a, “to make four.” The day when the heavenly lights were set in their place.

Thursday (Hamus) — From Hamese, “to make five.” The day of the sea creatures and birds, filling the waters and skies.

Friday (Arb )"Arebe" — From Arebe, “to include.” The sixth day, when man himself was created, and all things brought to completion.

Saturday (Kidame )" Kedamit"— Meaning “first,” for it is the first Sabbath, the day of holy rest, when God sanctified the seventh day.

Thus, the very names of the days are a living catechism of creation, teaching us each week to remember the mighty works of God.

Conclusion

Behold then, dearly beloved, how the Ethiopian reckoning of time is no idle system, but a treasury of wisdom. Each month, each day, each hour whispers the voice of God: “Redeem the time, for the days are evil” (Ephesians 5:16). Time passes, but it does not vanish; it carries us as a river carries a boat, either toward salvation if we steer with vigilance, or toward ruin if we drift in neglect.

Therefore, let us not despise the hours, nor treat the days as common. Rather, let us sanctify them with prayer, labor, and thanksgiving. For just as the sun rises and sets according to God’s command, so too our lives are measured by Him who is the Lord of time, the Alpha and the Omega, the Beginning and the End. 

May the name of the Holy God be praised forever and ever in every tongue that He has created, today and always. 

May the blessings of God's mother, the virgin, and the cross be upon us.
