👉🏾👉🏾👉🏾The Fast of #Nineveh
Who Is #Nineveh (Tsome Nenewe)? 

(Part 1)

Dear followers of the Orthodox teachings on John's Repentance website .we greet you with the peace of the Lord.Today , based on the topic mentioned above, we present to you the following important lesson.We encourage everyone to read it carefully and learn from it.

✍🏾According to the canon of the Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church, there are seven major fasts observed by the faithful.

✍🏾The Fast of Nineveh is one of the seven fasts whose start date is calculated using the Mebaja Hamer—the combined result of the Tewosak and the yearly Metqi.

✍🏾Nineveh was a great and ancient city, founded by Nimrod, a mighty ruler, on the banks of the Tigris River, as recorded in Genesis 10:11–12.

Nineveh was known for its beauty, size, and strength.

The length of its city wall was approximately 12 kilometers, making it one of the largest cities of the ancient world.

👉🏾It served as the capital city of Assyria, just as Addis Ababa is the capital of Ethiopia or other modern capitals serve their nations.

✍🏾Assyria traces its origin to Asshur, one of the sons of Shem, the son of Noah (Genesis 10:22).

👉🏾Asshur became the forefather of the Assyrian people, whose land was located in Upper Mesopotamia.

✍🏾Nineveh became the political and military center of the Assyrian Empire.

Nineveh served as the capital of the powerful Assyrian Empire. During its peak, it frequently challenged and attacked the Kingdom of Israel. 

✍🏾 This occurred after the reign of Solomon's son, Rehoboam, when the nation split into two weakened states: the Northern Kingdom (Israel) and the Southern Kingdom (Judah).

✍🏾 ​In 720 B.C., the Assyrian Empire—with Nineveh as its heart—conquered Samaria, the capital of the ten Northern tribes. They deported the population and scattered them across five unfamiliar foreign lands, where they eventually integrated and became known as the "Lost Tribes." In their place, the Assyrians resettled people from other regions in Samaria.

✍🏾 ​The Assyrian Empire also oppressed the Southern Kingdom of Judah, at one point even taking King Manasseh captive to Babylon. However, they were unable to destroy the capital city, Jerusalem. During the reign of King Hezekiah, the Assyrians insolently challenged God. As recorded in the Holy Scriptures:

✍🏾​"That night the angel of the Lord went out and put to death a hundred and eighty-five thousand in the Assyrian camp. When the people got up the next morning—there were all the dead bodies! So Sennacherib king of Assyria broke camp and withdrew. He returned to Nineveh and stayed there." (2 Kings 19:35-36)

​Until its fall to the Babylonian Empire in 612 B.C., Nineveh was renowned as "The Great City." It officially became the capital of Assyria around 700 B.C. during the reign of King Sennacherib.
