5. Short Prayers

When time is short and we cannot read long prayers, the Fathers taught us to pray short and powerful prayers such as:

Abune Zebesemayat
(The Our Father who art in heaven) 

The Prayer of Faith(tselote haymanot)

Psalm 150 (the great hymn of David)

The Hymn of Mary

Even if the prayer is brief, it seals the day with God’s blessing.

The Fathers also taught that when we pray Psalm 150, it is as though we have prayed the entire Psalter, and we receive the blessing of David. At the very least, we should never leave our homes without saying the Our Father, just as the Lord Jesus Christ commanded.

In monasteries short prayers are often used ande Short prayer may vary from place to place : nine times ( selam leki ) and twelve times (Abune zebesemayat)

6. The Order of Short Prayers

A common order of short prayers is as follows:

1. The Our Father (Abune Zebesemayat)

2. One Psalm of David

3. The Praises of Mary (Wudase Maryam) and other hymns

4. Again the (Abune Zebesemayat), followed by BeEnte Egzi’etne Maryam, the Creed (Tselote Haymanot), and the Abune Zebesemayat with Kyrie Eleison (Lord have mercy) said forty-one times.

But the Fathers also warn: if we use only short prayers every day and neglect the longer prayers, our soul may become weak and fall into temptation.

Conclusion

Prayer is not measured by length alone but by constancy and devotion. The Psalms lift our souls to heaven; the Hail Mary( selam leki) unites us to the Virgin’s intercession;the Gospel fills us with Christ’s voice; and the short prayers keep our hearts awake to God in every place and time.

Let us, therefore, not allow a single day to pass without lifting our voices in prayer. For he who prays stands in the company of angels, partakes of the blessings of the saints, and draws down upon himself the mercy of the Holy Trinity.
