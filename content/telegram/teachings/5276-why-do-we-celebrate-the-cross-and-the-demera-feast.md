👉🏾👉🏾👉🏾Why Do We #Celebrate the #Cross and the #Demera Feast?


(Part 1)

Beloved in Christ, children of the one holy and apostolic Church, let us turn our hearts to the mystery of the Cross and to the great feast which has been delivered to us by the fathers: the Feast of the Finding of the Cross and the Demera celebration. It is not an empty tradition nor a mere spectacle of fire and incense, but a remembrance of the very power by which death was slain and life was given to the world.

The Feast of the Cross

Among the nine great feasts of the Lord, the Feast of the Cross shines with peculiar brightness. Every year, on the seventeenth of Meskerem and in the tenth of Megabit , the faithful in the Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church gather to magnify the triumph of the life-giving cross. This is not a feast limited to the Church alone, but one that has overflowed into the life of nations. From ancient times until today, the Feast of the Cross has been honored by Christians, respected by those of other faiths, and even recognized by governments. In our day, the Demera Festival itself has been inscribed in the annals of UNESCO’s world heritage, so that what the Church has long revered is now declared by the world as a treasure of humanity.

The History and Mystery of the Cross

The origins of this feast go back to the days of Saint Eleni, mother of the great Emperor Constantine. Though born of Jewish descent and once joined to a pagan king, Eleni received the call of God and became the instrument chosen to restore to the Church the most holy relic of our salvation: the Cross of the Lord.

For that Cross, upon which the Lord of Glory was hung in shame, became the wellspring of miracles. It healed the sick, opened the eyes of the blind, cleansed the lepers, strengthened the lame, and cast out the demons. Through it, Christ displayed His power, so that both Jew and Gentile might behold His mercy. Yet envy darkened the hearts of those who crucified Him, and they sought to bury the Cross in obscurity. They cast it into a place of refuse, heaping upon it filth and earth, disguising its location with the crosses of the two thieves, so that men might not distinguish it. Over years it became covered, hidden beneath a great mountain.

Although no one had the authority or ability to recover the Cross, many Christians and devout believers in the region knew the exact place where it was buried. However, after the birth of our Lord, in the year 70 AD, when King Titos invaded, Jerusalem and the surrounding lands were destroyed, and a great massacre was carried out against the people. As a result, it became impossible to determine the location of the Cross.

For this reason, though many faithful servants of God were grieved over the burial of the Cross, they were powerless to recover it, and it remained hidden in the earth for more than thirty years.

The Mission of Saint Eleni

In the year 327, Queen Eleni, aflame with faith and prayer, resolved to recover the Cross. She begged the Lord for the conversion of her son, Constantine, who had been born to a pagan mother, and she prayed  to uncover the Lord’s instrument of salvation.

After Queen Eleni arrived in Jerusalem, she sought to uncover the hill or mountain where she believed the Holy Cross was buried, according to the history she had learned beforehand. Yet her efforts bore no fruit, for she could not find it. She also questioned the Jews living in the area, but none would reveal the truth to her. Therefore, with steadfast faith, she turned continually to God in fervent and special prayer. 

In the end, because the revelation of the Cross was God’s will, an old man named Kirakos, who lived in a cave, saw the queen’s weariness and spoke to her with wisdom: “Do not grow weary in vain. Gather much wood, place incense upon it, and set it aflame. When the smoke rises to heaven and bends down to earth, it will show the place of the Cross.” The queen obeyed his counsel, and by her faith and prayer the mystery was revealed.
