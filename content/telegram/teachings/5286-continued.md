4. The Star and the prophecy Story

The star recalls the prophecy fulfilled when the wise came from the East. Their father, the philosopher Jeredshet, had once seen an icon of the Virgin with her Child while studying near the Nek-a may( ነቅዓ ማይ ). He carved it, and at his death instructed them to seek the heavenly King when a star rose. So it was that they found the Child with His Mother, as written in Matthew 2:11, and offered gifts. Thus the Virgin becomes the sign of the New Covenant, given to us as Mother.

5.  The Mitre of the Virgin

Why is she shown holding a mitre? It is the symbol of her royalty and honor. It recalls her blessing at Debre Mittimaq and her witness before Thomas and the fathers who bore the cross. Though artists vary the size and form of the mitre, its meaning endures: majesty and blessing.

6. The Veil and the Artist’s Hand

The mehareb or veil has its place in the icon, though its full meaning is not always disclosed. In such matters the artist is free to shape the image according to the tradition received, without forcing interpretations where none were intended. The image is a symbol.

Conclusion 

Whether with crown or without, the Virgin is shown as Queen beside her Son. She is His throne, His Mother, His intercessor. The icon declares her steadfast glory, her maternal love, and her royal dignity. No detail diminishes the greater truth: that she bore God in her womb, and through her the New Covenant was given to mankind.
