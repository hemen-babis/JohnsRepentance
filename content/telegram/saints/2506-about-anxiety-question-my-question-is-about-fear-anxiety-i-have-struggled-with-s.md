About #Anxiety

Question፦ My question is about fear & anxiety. I have struggled with social anxiety for a long time now, and it affects different areas of my life, like work, church, and social events. It affects how I function and interact with others in these different situations. I want to be free and not a prisoner of my mind. I really wanna find the confidence and courage to be able to walk over to someone at work and strike up a conversation, to join in on conversations with my team, to ask questions when I feel stuck or need help, to approach others at church and build that community, to speak up in meetings/groups..etc. I do pray during these situations for God to help me but sometimes I just feel stuck like there is no way for me to become a better version of myself because I can't break out of this cycle. Please help.

Answer፦ Beloved in Christ, the struggle with fear and anxiety, especially in the form of social anxiety, is a heavy burden, but it is one that can be lightened through the grace and peace of our Lord. You are not alone in this battle, and it's a path many faithful have walked before, finding solace and strength in God's presence and promises.

In the Scriptures, we are repeatedly encouraged to cast our anxieties on the Lord, for He cares for us (1 Peter 5:7). The Psalmist also speaks to us in our fears, "When I am afraid, I put my trust in you" (Psalm 56:3). These are not mere words but powerful truths to live by, offering us a foundation of trust in God's providential care.

To address social anxiety and grow in confidence and courage, consider the following steps, deeply rooted in the Orthodox tradition and the teachings of the Church:

1. Prayer and Scripture: Continue to make prayer your first resort in times of anxiety. Incorporate specific prayers for courage and peace into your daily routine. Engaging with Scripture can also be immensely comforting. Passages like Joshua 1:9, where God commands Joshua to be strong and courageous, can be particularly uplifting.

2. Participation in the Sacraments: The Sacraments, especially Confession and Communion, are sources of grace and healing. Through Confession, we can lay down our burdens and receive counsel, and in Communion, we are united with Christ, who strengthens us.

3. Seek Guidance from a Spiritual Father: Discussing your struggles with a spiritual father can provide not only spiritual guidance but also practical advice tailored to your situation.

4. Gradual Exposure and Small Goals: With prayer and trust in God, set small, achievable goals for yourself. For example, you might decide to speak to one person at church this Sunday or ask one question during a meeting at work. Celebrate these small victories, and gradually increase your goals as you become more comfortable.

5. Practice Mindfulness and Presence in God’s Creation: Sometimes, anxiety can stem from being too future-focused or self-centered in our thoughts. Practicing mindfulness—being present in the moment and aware of God’s creation around us—can help shift our perspective and reduce anxiety.

6. Christian Fellowship and Support: Engage in church groups or Christian fellowship where you can share your struggles in a safe environment. These settings can also provide opportunities to practice social interactions in a less pressured environment.

7. Reflect on the Lives of Saints: Many saints experienced profound fear and anxiety, yet they found strength in God. Reading their stories can be inspiring and offer practical insights into overcoming fear with faith.

Remember, your worth and identity are rooted in Christ, not in your social performance or perceptions. God loves you infinitely and knows your struggles. With each step you take, no matter how small, you are moving toward the freedom and peace He desires for you.

Would it be possible for you to choose one small goal to achieve this week, as a step toward overcoming social anxiety, trusting that God walks with you in this journey?
