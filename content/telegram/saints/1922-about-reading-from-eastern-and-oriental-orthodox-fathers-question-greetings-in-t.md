About reading from #Eastern and #Oriental #Orthodox fathers

👉 Question፦ Greetings in the Lord, I have a question about something that has been on my mind lately! I read books written by Eastern Orthodox authors and their saints. They have been a great source of spiritual support in my journey, however I wonder if this will cause issues for me as Oriental Orthodox! Does our church have any sort of rules about reading or using resources from churches we aren't in Communion with? Are their saints, specifically modern saints that lived after the schism considered saints in our church?

Answer፦ Greetings to you in the peace and love of our Lord Jesus Christ,

It is a joy to hear of your diligence in seeking spiritual nourishment through the writings of the holy ones who have walked the path of faith before us. The quest for understanding and the enrichment of one’s faith through the writings of saints and learned authors is indeed commendable.

In the embrace of the Orthodox tradition, both Eastern and Oriental, we find common ground in the veneration of saints who lived before the Chalcedonian schism. These holy men and women belong to both our families and provide us with a shared heritage of faith and piety.

When considering modern saints recognized after the schism, there is a space for nuanced appreciation. The Oriental Orthodox Church may not formally recognize the canonization of saints from the Eastern Orthodox tradition, yet there is a personal freedom to honor any who lead us nearer to the heart of Christ. If their teachings resonate with truth and guide you toward a life of sanctity, then they are indeed beneficial.

However, discernment is paramount. Do they bring peace, growth in virtues, and a clearer vision of Christ? It is wise to engage with these texts under the guidance of a spiritual father who can offer insights and direction because some might seem Orthodox but are not. 

The wisdom of the saints of the Eastern Orthodox Church, much like the flowers in a neighbor's garden, can be admired and even benefit from their fragrance. Their holy lives and ascetic teachings are akin to rainfalls that water the whole earth; they do not discriminate where they provide nourishment. Indeed, there is a shared veneration for those saints who shone with the light of Christ before the historical separation of our Churches.

However, for the faithful of the Oriental Orthodox communion, it is to our own garden that we must tend primarily. The saints and teachers of the Oriental Orthodox Church are the plants rooted in the soil of our specific ecclesiastical landscape. They are the ones whom our Church has officially recognized, whose teachings have been meticulously scrutinized and whose intercessions we invoke during our divine services.

To ground oneself in the Oriental Orthodox tradition is to drink deeply from the wells of our Fathers—Saints Cyril, Athanasius, Severus, and Dioscorus, to name but a few. Their understanding of Christology and theology, their pastoral care, their liturgical hymns and prayers, all these have been tailored to the fabric of our Church's life and experience through the centuries.

However, let this not discourage you from seeking wisdom and learning from fathers of the Eastern Orthodox Church. The important thing is to first look to our garden before looking at our neighbor's. 

May I ask, have you found a spiritual guide within the Oriental Orthodox Church to help you navigate these waters of wisdom, both familiar and foreign? How might you continue to seek the holy teachings within our tradition to deepen your spiritual journey?

May the name of the Holy God be praised forever and ever in every language that He has created, today and always.

May the blessings of the Holy Mother of God, the Virgin, and the Cross be upon us. Amen.
