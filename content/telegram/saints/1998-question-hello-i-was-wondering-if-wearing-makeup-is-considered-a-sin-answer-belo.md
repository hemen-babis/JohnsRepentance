Question፦ Hello, I was wondering if wearing #makeup is considered a sin?

Answer፦ Beloved in Christ,

In the eyes of the Orthodox Church, the focus is always on the state of the heart and the intent behind our actions, rather than the actions themselves in isolation. When it comes to wearing makeup, it is not inherently sinful. It is the intention and the manner in which it is used that matters. As Saint John Chrysostom wisely remarked, "For indeed both wine and intercourse are allowed, but both of these become a sin when they are used improperly and out of season." The same can be applied to wearing makeup.

In 1 Peter 3:3-4, we read: "Do not let your adornment be merely outward—arranging the hair, wearing gold, or putting on fine apparel—rather let it be the hidden person of the heart, with the incorruptible beauty of a gentle and quiet spirit, which is very precious in the sight of God." This scripture invites us to reflect on our intentions. Is the makeup worn to enhance the God-given beauty and to present oneself respectfully, or is it used to draw undue attention or to cultivate vanity? It is the latter that the Church cautions against.

Remember, the Orthodox Church encourages modesty and humility in all aspects of life. Makeup, when used in moderation and with pure intentions, is not considered sinful. It is important, however, to always guard our hearts against vanity and to seek beauty that is "incorruptible" — the beauty of a gentle and quiet spirit, rooted in our relationship with God.

As you reflect on this, ask yourself: How can I ensure that my outward appearance is a reflection of the inner beauty of my soul, cultivated through a life of prayer, humility, and love for God?

May the name of the Holy God be praised forever and ever in every tongue that He has created, today and always. 

May the blessings of The Virgin, and the cross be upon us. Amen.
