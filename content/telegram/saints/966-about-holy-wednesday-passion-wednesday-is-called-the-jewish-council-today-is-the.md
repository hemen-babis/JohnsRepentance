About Holy #Wednesday 

Passion Wednesday is called the Jewish Council.

Today is the day when the Jews and the high priests gave advice on what to do with our Savior Jesus Christ. David said in his psalms, "They have taken crafty counsel against thy people, and consulted against thy hidden ones" (Psalm 83:3). In Psalm 2:2, he said, "The kings of the earth set themselves, and the rulers take counsel together, against the LORD, and against His anointed."

In the past, the people of Israel were motivated by zeal to kill their brother Joseph. Saint Moses, the chief of the prophets, said, "And when they saw him afar off, even before he came near unto them, they conspired against him to slay him. And they said one to another, Behold, this dreamer cometh. Come now therefore, and let us slay him, and cast him into some pit, and we will say, Some evil beast hath devoured him" (Genesis 37:18-19).

There was also a time when they rose up to kill Moses and Aaron, who were leading them on the road the promise land. "But all the congregation bade stone them with stones" (Torah Numbers 14:10). 

The points agreed upon by the council for the prosecution to sue our Savior today are:

He made himself the Son of God (John 19:7).
He claimed to be the king (Matthew 2:1).
He said, "Before Abraham was, I am" (John 8:58).
He said, "I and my Father are one" (John 10:30).
He claimed, "He that hath seen me hath seen the Father" (John 14:9).
He broke the Sabbath day (John 5:18).
He said, "Destroy this temple, and in three days I will raise it up" (John 2:19).

For these reasons, they decided to kill him and make him die a bitter death on a cross. They agreed together that according to their law, a person who is crucified is not whipped, but a person who is whipped is not crucified. They whipped him 6666 times; they hung him on the cross and nailed him with 5 iron nails. They were mocking him with a rod saying, "Hail, King of the Jews!" They hung the innocent God in the midst of the crooked bandits. But the Lord is humble and merciful. There is no falsehood found in his mouth. Because of the gentleness of his speech, he does not quench a smoldering wick.

This day is also called called the day of good ointment. When our Lord Jesus Christ was sitting in Simon's house, and Mary of Bethany took expensive ointment and anointed his head. Luke 7:37-39 says, "And, behold, a woman in the city, which was a sinner, when she knew that Jesus sat at meat in the Pharisee's house, brought an alabaster box of ointment, And stood at his feet behind him weeping, and began to wash his feet with tears, and did wipe them with the hairs of her head, and kissed his feet, and anointed them with the ointment." This woman who ended her life in shame and sin but heard and understood God's divinity and received His mercy was forgiven for her sins because her tears and humility. She is an example to all of us that even the greatest of sinners can be saved through faith and repentance.

Today, we remember her tears of repentance and reflect on our own sins and shortcomings. We ask for God's mercy and forgiveness, just as the woman did, and strive to live a life that is pleasing to Him.

On this day, we also remember Judas, who betrayed our Lord for thirty pieces of silver. His actions serve as a reminder that even those who have walked with Jesus can fall into sin and turn away from Him.
