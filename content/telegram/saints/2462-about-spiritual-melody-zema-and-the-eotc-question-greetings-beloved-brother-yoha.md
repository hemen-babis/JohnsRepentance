About Spiritual #Melody, #Zema and the #EOTC

👉 Question፦ Greetings beloved brother Yohannes Neseha. I am Dcn. Zefre Birhan and I serve at Holy Trinity located in Bronx Ny. I have a question.

My question is now speaking of St. Yared. I am well aware that God took St. Yared into the heavens and that the Holy Spirit filled his mind with the holy Mezmur and Zema.

All though what alarms my mind is how were the melodies of the zema and mezmur of our church. The Ethiopian Orthodox Church created. Please get back to me when you get the chance to.

Sincerely,
Dcn. Zefre Birhan

Answer፦ Beloved Deacon Zefre Birhan,

Before the divine intervention bestowed upon Saint Yared, the worship within the Ethiopian Orthodox Church, like in other early Christian communities, likely utilized simpler chants and hymns. These early melodies and verses would have been more rudimentary, focusing on the Psalms and scriptural canticles, reflecting the Jewish temple worship traditions from which Christian liturgy originated.

In the providence of God, the liturgical music before Saint Yared served its purpose in the divine worship, yet it was through Saint Yared's visionary experience that the Church was endowed with a richer, more profound liturgical expression. This transformation symbolizes a deepening of the Church's liturgical life, a transition from simplicity to a more elaborate, heaven-inspired worship, which has profoundly shaped the spiritual identity of our Church.

Just as King David played his harp and sang the Psalms, enriching the worship of the Old Testament, Saint Yared, through divine inspiration, enhanced the worship of the Ethiopian Orthodox Church, embedding within it a heavenly dimension and a deeper mystical resonance.

The tradition of our church teaches us that the melodies and hymns, as received by Saint Yared, were indeed divinely inspired during his rapturous ascent into the heavens. It was in this mystical communion with the Divine that the Holy Spirit imbued him with the celestial melodies and hymns that form the bedrock of our liturgical worship.

In your service, Deacon Zefre, I encourage you to delve deeply into the mystical and spiritual essence of the Zema and Mezmur, allowing their divine melodies to elevate your heart and those of your congregation closer to God. As you guide your flock in the sacred liturgy, remember that you are echoing the angelic choruses of heaven, bridging earth to the divine, and partaking in the heavenly liturgy.

Now, dear brother in Christ, how will you further immerse yourself in the spiritual legacy of Saint Yared to enrich your ministry and deepen your congregation's worship experience?

May the name of the Holy God be praised forever and ever, today and always.

May the blessings of the Holy Mother of God, the Virgin, and the Cross be upon us. Amen.
