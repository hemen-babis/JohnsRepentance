4. Mystery of Eucharist

Holy Eucharist, also called Holy Communion, is the fourth mystery, which is God's gift of life to the world. The mystery of the Holy Communion tells us the secret of the flesh and blood of our Lord Jesus Christ, who was crucified on the cross at Calvary Square, to be life for the whole world.

"And as they were eating, Jesus took bread, and blessed it, and broke it, and gave it to the disciples, and said, Take, eat; this is my body. And he took the cup, and gave thanks, and gave it to them, saying, Drink ye all of it; For this is my blood of the new testament, which is shed for many for the remission of sins." Matthew 26:28

According to this divine instruction, it is a mystery where we can approach God, eat the lamb that was sacrificed for the atonement of sins, and live with God. For we are told, "Whoever eats my flesh and drinks my blood, I will live in him and he will live in me." The foundation of this holy order is Jesus Christ. This holy mystery is the conclusion and seal of our worship, the center of our Christianity. By accepting the separated soul, the divinely united holy body and the noble blood, we are called the children of the heavenly citizens. The flesh and blood of the Son of God, which we receive while testifying to Christ's death and resurrection, is holiness for our souls. Heb 10:10.

As our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ taught us in the John’s Gospel 6:56, the Holy Eucharist is a real testament of the Lord’s presence in the life of the Christian person. Hence, by receiving the Holy Body and Precious Blood of Jesus Christ, that Christian person ensures the sanctity of any other sacrament that he/she has partaken from. 

This mystery is not an example or a memory, but a real one. Even today, the blood of Abel speaks, because the blood of Christ is alive and faithful, and frees us from all sin. 1 John 1:7 "And as they were eating, Jesus took bread, and blessed it, and broke it, and gave it to the disciples, and said, Take, eat; this is my body." Matthew 26:26

According to the teachings of the Church, anyone who believes and is baptized can receive his holy body and precious blood. Just as the people of Israel ate manna and inherited the promise land, all Christians eat the holy flesh, drink the precious blood and inherit the freedom and the promised land of the kingdom of heaven.

Saint Peter said, "For you have become partakers of the glory of God." Saint Paul taught us, "Let every man examine himself and purify himself, eat of this bread, and drink of this cup." Therefore, we attend church in prayer (Mass) and unite our God with love. 1st Cor 11:28

It is called a mystery is because it cannot be seen by the physical eye. It is called a mystery because it gives an invisible grace through a visible service. We believe that the bread and wine are changed and become the Body and the Blood of Christ. We believe in this definitive/absolute change, but we don’t attempt to explain the mode of the change (“Mystery”). The change is to Body, Blood and Divine (but without Soul). The mechanism of that change is beyond human imagination and we describe it as a Mystery. We believe the Holy Eucharist is the body and blood of Christ with divinity, but without soul.

About #Humility

The Link Between Repentance and Humility

In an age when "self-focus" is the rule of the day, and where the ego seems to reign, it is hard for people to see the value of humility. Many people, from politicians to rock stars, seem to climb the ladder of success by being anything but humble. In our darkened state, with sin dominating our lives, and bad habits seemingly insurmountable, holiness seems to be about saints, but unattainable for ourselves. Stuck in our habitual patterns of behavior, we seem to have surrendered all hope for real change.

The antidote to this inertia can be found in one simple act, that of repenting. We fall down before our God, confessing our failure at having kept the commandments, and asking for God's help in turning our lives around. We commit ourselves to crushing down the ego, and acquiring a humble heart. We take every opportunity to accept correction or criticism, without becoming defensive, for we know the truth in the words of Saint John Climacus, "As with the appearance of light, darkness retreats; so, at the fragrance of humility, all anger and bitterness vanishes."

We refrain from judging anyone, but only examine our own conscience, accusing only ourselves. Saint Macarius the Great wrote, “Christians therefore ought to strive continually, and never to pass judgment on anyone—no, not upon the harlot on the street, or upon open sinners and disorderly persons—but to regard all men with singleness of intention and purity of eye, so that it may become like a fixed law of nature to despise no one, to abhor no one, to make no distinctions between them….This is purity of heart, when you see  sinners or sick people, to have compassion on them and be tenderhearted towards them.”

Source: Abbot Tryphon from the Russian Orthodox Church
