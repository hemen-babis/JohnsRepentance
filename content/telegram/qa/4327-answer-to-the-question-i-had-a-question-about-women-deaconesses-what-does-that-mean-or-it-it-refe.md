👉🏽Answer to the question: I had a question about women deaconesses? What does that mean? Or it it referring to what’s expected of a wife that marries a deacon?

In the Orthodox Christian context especially in the early Church and still in some traditions today a deaconess is not simply the wife of a deacon. Rather, a deaconess was (and can be) a woman who held a specific, ordained ministry in the Church, distinct from any marital relationship.

Here’s the breakdown:

1. Who is a Deaconess?

A deaconess was a woman officially ordained to serve the Church in various capacities, especially focusing on women's ministry.

Their roles included:

Assisting in the baptism of women (when full immersion was practiced naked, for modesty).

Caring for sick women or visiting women in prison.

Teaching and mentoring women catechumens (those preparing for baptism).

Sometimes helping the bishop or priest pastorally in situations involving women.

2. Is a Deaconess the Wife of a Deacon?

No, not necessarily.

While the wife of a deacon may be called to support her husband’s ministry, this is not what “deaconess” refers to in Church tradition.

The role of a deaconess was a calling of its own, and some deaconesses were virgins or widows, not married.

3. Is This Still Practiced?

The ancient Church (especially in the East) ordained many holy women as deaconesses—St. Phoebe (Romans 16:1) is one example.

In modern times:

The Eastern Orthodox Church generally does not have active deaconesses today, though there is discussion of restoring the order.

The Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church did have a form of deaconesses, often elderly widows, serving in monasteries and churches.

4. Biblical and Patristic Support

Romans 16:1 – St. Paul refers to Phoebe as a "diakonon" of the Church in Cenchreae.

Early Church Fathers like St. John Chrysostom and St. Epiphanius discuss deaconesses as a legitimate office.

The Apostolic Constitutions (a 4th-century Church order) contain ordination prayers for deaconesses.
