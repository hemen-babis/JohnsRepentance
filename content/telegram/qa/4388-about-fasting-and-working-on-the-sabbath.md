👉 About #Fasting and working on the #Sabbath

👉🏾 Question: Regarding fasting time of the apostles fast, the fetha negest orders that we should fast till nine o'clock , but my body's ability isn't fit enough to obey the laws of fasting  I fast till six,   besides my mind don't think good things rather something unpleasant to the God's words , totally I am out spiritual life, I don't know how to live the commandments of God since I am not fit for it, unless we partake holy communion we can't get salvation, to partake holy communion I am not fulfilling the requirements that says during liturgy, what should I do? Besides in our surrounding like in factory there's work need to be done even on Holiday (Saturday and Sunday) but if we miss attending that day in work place they will fire us,  what should I do to keep the commandments like the 3rd commandment, brethren??

Answer: Peace be upon you, my child. I hear your struggles with fasting, prayer, and keeping the Sabbath, and I commend you for seeking guidance.  It is a sign of humility to recognize our weaknesses and a sign of faith to seek strength in God.  Let us explore these matters together.

Regarding the Apostles' Fast, the Fetha Nagast indeed guides us, reflecting the ancient traditions of our Church.  While the ideal is to fast until the ninth hour, remember the words of St. Isaac the Syrian:  "A measured labor that is continuous is more profitable than a greater labor that quickly flags."  You fast until the sixth hour, and this is a good beginning. Do not despair if you cannot yet reach the full measure.  Focus on the quality of your fast, not just the duration.  Offer your struggle to God as a sacrifice, asking for His strength to endure longer periods in the future.  Perhaps gradually extend the fast by a half-hour each day, until, by His grace, you reach the ninth hour.

You speak of unpleasant thoughts during prayer.  This is a common struggle, my child.  The Evil One seeks to distract us from communion with God.  Do not be discouraged.  A revered father once said, “The remembrance of God fights against impurity, and the thought of Gehenna conquers gluttony.”  When these thoughts arise, gently turn your mind back to prayer.  Focus on the words, the rhythm, and the meaning.  You can also use the Jesus Prayer – "Lord Jesus Christ, Son of God, have mercy on me, a sinner" – as a shield against these distractions.  Remember, even a short, sincere prayer offered with a contrite heart is pleasing to God.

Regarding the Sabbath, the third commandment is indeed paramount.  However, our Church also recognizes the need to provide for ourselves and our families.  If your livelihood depends on working on the Sabbath, pray for God's guidance and understanding.  Offer up the work as a sacrifice, and dedicate another day, or a portion of your earnings, to God's service.  Speak to your employer with humility and respect about your desire to honor the Sabbath.  Perhaps a compromise can be reached.  If not, know that God sees your heart and your struggle.  He does not abandon those who strive to follow Him in difficult circumstances.

Regarding Holy Communion, it is the medicine of immortality, a source of grace and strength.  Yet, we must approach it with reverence and preparation.  Confession is key.  Unburden your heart to a priest, confess your struggles, and seek his guidance.  He will offer you absolution and counsel, preparing you to receive the Holy Mysteries worthily.  Remember the words of St. Basil the Great: "The reception of the Eucharist, though frequent, does not diminish the reverence due to it, but augments the grace in those who partake of it worthily."

My child, do not lose heart.  The spiritual life is a journey, not a destination.  There will be struggles and setbacks.  But with God's grace, and the support of the Church, you can persevere.  I urge you to seek out a spiritual father, a priest or elder in your community, for ongoing guidance and support.  They can offer personalized counsel and help you navigate these challenges.
