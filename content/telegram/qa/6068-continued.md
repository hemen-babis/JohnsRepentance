Then our Lord said to John, “We must complete the work of righteousness that has begun; allow it for a time.” And John consented and baptized Him willingly, according to the will of God.

👉🏾John the Baptist said, “The Father is in You through the Holy Spirit, and You are in the Father through the Holy Spirit. The Holy Spirit is in You and in the Father. I baptize others in Your name, but in whose name shall I baptize You?”

👉🏾Our Lord answered him, saying, “The Son is blessed, and you are a priest to the world, according to the order of Melchizedek.”

✍🏾After this, they went down together into the water. As the Gospel says, “When our Lord Jesus Christ was baptized, He came up from the water” (Matthew 3:16), and “the heavens were opened to Him.” This opening of the heavens does not mean that heaven was physically closed and then opened. Rather, just as when a sealed vessel is opened its contents become visible, so also at the baptism of our Lord a hidden mystery was revealed. That mystery is the manifestation of the unity of the Holy Trinity.

👉🏾From this arises the question: Why did the Lord go from Galilee to the Jordan to be baptized? Does a servant go to his master, or does the Lord go to His servant? Does the servant receive baptism from his master, or does the Lord receive baptism from His servant?

Our Lord’s coming was not for His own glory, nor merely for humility. 

👉🏾Rather, it was to set an example. If our Lord had said to John, “Come and baptize Me,” then kings, nobles, and rulers would later say, “Come to our houses and baptize us.” Instead, our Lord Himself went to John and was baptized, teaching us by example to go to the Church, where the priests are, and to receive baptism according to the order established by God.

✍🏾 The baptism of our Lord was not to add glory to Him, for He is already full of glory. Rather, it was to make the Jordan River a radiant womb, so that we, the believers, might be reborn of water and the Holy Spirit and enter the Kingdom of Heaven. 

Through His baptism, the waters were sanctified, and the way of spiritual rebirth was opened for humanity.

Likewise, the sacrament of baptism was established to teach us and to serve as an example. As our Lord said:

“Unless one is born again of water and the Spirit, he cannot enter the kingdom of God.” (John 3:5)

And again:
“He who believes and is baptized will be saved; but he who does not believe will be condemned.” (Mark 16:16)

✍🏾 If it is asked, why then was He baptized in the Jordan River? The answer is that this was done to fulfill prophecy and to reveal the divine mystery. The prophet says:

👉🏾“The sea saw You and fled; the Jordan turned back.”

👉🏾“The waters saw You, O God; the waters saw You and were afraid.”(Psalm 114:3; Psalm 77:16)

👉🏾Thus, through the presence of Christ, the waters recognized their Creator, and the prophecy spoken long ago was fulfilled in the baptism of our Lord.

✍🏾Our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ was baptized at the age of thirty, as the Gospel testifies:

👉🏾“Jesus Himself began His ministry at about thirty years of age.” (Luke 3:23)

The mystery of His baptism at the age of thirty is connected to Adam and Eve. Adam was thirty years old and Eve fifteen years old when they lost the sonship they had received from the Holy Trinity through disobedience. According to the tradition of the Church, Adam was created in maturity and later clothed at forty days, and Eve at eighty days. Through transgression, they lost the grace of divine sonship.

👉🏾Therefore, our Lord was baptized at the age of thirty in order to restore that lost sonship. By entering the waters, He returned to humanity what Adam and Eve had lost. For this reason, the Church baptizes male children at forty days and female children at eighty days, following the ancient apostolic and patristic tradition.
