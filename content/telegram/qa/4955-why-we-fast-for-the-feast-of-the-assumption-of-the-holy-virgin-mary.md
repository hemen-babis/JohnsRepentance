👉🏾👉🏾👉🏾#Why We #Fast for the #Feast of the #Assumption of the #Holy #Virgin #Mary?

Beloved children of the Holy Orthodox Church, grace and peace be multiplied unto you from the Lord Jesus Christ, Who was born of the Ever-Virgin Mary, and Who has given to His Church treasures both seen and unseen. Today we speak of the holy Fast that precedes the great Feast of the Assumption of our Lady, the Theotokos, a Fast observed from the first to the fourteenth day of Nehase. Let us hear, not as those who seek mere custom, but as those who long to enter the mystery hidden from ages yet revealed to the saints.

The Apostolic Origin of the Fast

This Fast is not a tradition born of men’s imagination, nor a mere ordinance added in later times; rather, it is apostolic in its very root. In the days when the holy apostles still walked upon the earth, they themselves set this Fast as a sacred preparation for the remembrance of the Dormition and glorious Assumption of the Mother of God.

When the time came for the all-pure Mother of our Lord to depart this earthly life, the apostles, by divine summons, gathered from the corners of the world to be present at her repose. For two weeks from the first day of the month of Nehase they prepared themselves in prayer, fasting, and thanksgiving, awaiting that solemn hour when they would commit her most holy body to the tomb.

Yet one of them, Thomas, was absent, being at that time far away in apostolic labor. Thus it was that when the Virgin was laid in the tomb, Thomas was not among those who carried her in honor to her rest.

The Mystery of Thomas and the Resurrection of the Virgin

On the third day after the burial, a wondrous event occurred: the prophecy was fulfilled, and the Mother of Life rose again, even as her Son had triumphed over death. As Thomas journeyed to Jerusalem, a divine cloud bore her toward him. Seeing this, the apostles marveled, yet Thomas’s heart was pierced with sorrow.

Once before, he had missed the manifestation of the Lord’s resurrection, and now, it seemed, he was again denied the vision of so great a mystery. He lamented, saying:

"I did not behold my Lord’s rising with my own eyes, and now I have not seen His Mother’s resurrection. Am I forever to be the last and least in belief?"

But the holy Virgin, with maternal gentleness, comforted him, saying that this vision was granted to him alone. As a testimony, she placed in his hands the burial cloth that had wrapped her body.

The Witness to the Apostles

Thomas came to the apostles bearing the sacred cloth. They questioned him, saying, "What has befallen our Lady?" Thomas answered with holy boldness, declaring that she had risen and that he had seen her in glory. Yet they, remembering his earlier doubt in the case of the Lord, hesitated to believe.

Thomas led them to her tomb. They opened it and beheld no body within, only the fragrance of paradise filling the air. Then the apostles, struck with awe, confessed: "Truly, she has been taken up into the glory of her Son."

The Yearly Remembrance

From that time forth, the apostles themselves established a yearly remembrance of these events. They fasted from the first day of Nehase until the fourteenth, and on that day they received again her most pure body, preserved incorrupt, and laid it once more in the tomb. On the third day thereafter the sixteenth of Nehase she was taken up into the heavenly realm, accompanied by her Son, the King of Glory, the angels, and the saints.

This is why the Church, following the example of the apostles, keeps the Fast with reverence: that we may share in the blessing they received, and that the intercession of the Theotokos may never depart from us.
