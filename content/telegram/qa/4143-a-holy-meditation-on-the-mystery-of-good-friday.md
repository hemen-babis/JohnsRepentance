👉🏽👉🏽👉🏽A Holy Meditation on the Mystery of #Good #Friday

Faithful beloved of the Most High, keep silence in reverence and open the eyes of your soul, for today we behold a mystery that shakes both heaven and earth. It is not a day like others, for this Friday, crowned with pain and soaked in the innocent blood of the Lamb, is called Good—very good for it is the day of the victory of God, the defeat of Satan, the tearing of the veil, and the redemption of mankind.

Let us kneel and listen, not with ears of flesh but with the trembling ears of the spirit, to the drama of salvation, played out not upon a stage of men, but upon the rugged wood of the Cross.

Why is it called Good Friday?

Threefold, we call it “Good”:

1. Because on this day our debt was canceled.
What Adam sold for a bite of forbidden fruit, Christ bought back with His very blood. The ransom for our souls was paid in full by the Lamb without blemish.

2. Because we were freed from the dominion of demons.
The tyrant devil, who ruled men through fear of death and the curse of sin, was conquered. He who descended into the pit in innocence broke its gates in majesty.

3. Because the Innocent was slain for the guilty, and mercy triumphed over judgment.
On this day, the righteous was condemned so the condemned could be made righteous.

The Trial of the Lord of Glory

Behold how the Son of God was shuffled like a criminal from house to house! From Annas to Caiaphas, then to Pilate, and from Pilate to Herod, then again back to Pilate—the Just One in the hands of the unjust. What irony, O soul! He who judges the nations stood trial before men, and those men could not even agree on a charge!

They questioned Him, but He answered not. For the silence of Truth is more piercing than the noise of lies. Pilate, perplexed, asked, “What crime has this man committed?” but received no answer from the mob intoxicated by rage.

The Mockery, the Pain, the Passion

The pain He endured cannot be measured by human reckoning. For He, the Bread of Life, tasted gall; He, the Prince of Peace, was bruised without cause.

Hear now the sacred count of His sufferings:

1. He was tied to a rod of iron until His back was raw.

2. He received 25 blows with fists, until His blood flowed like a river.

3. He endured 65 beatings with a stake.

4. He was struck in the face with stones 120 times.

5. He was whipped 365 times—one for every day, as if to sanctify the calendar of suffering.

6. He was scourged again with 80 more lashes.

7. He was tormented with 6666 whippings symbolic of the fullness of man's sin placed upon Him.

8. A crown of thorns was forced upon His divine brow.

9. He was thrown down 136 times.

10. He was nailed upon five sacred crosses—Sador, Alador, Edera, Danat, and Rodas.

11. He drank bitter gall, the chalice of our sin.

12. He bore the heavy wood of the Cross, the burden of our iniquity.

13. And finally, He was crucified—lifted up between heaven and earth, rejected by both.

The Field of Blood and the Betrayer's End

Judas, the betrayer, now stung by remorse, returned the silver and cried, “I have sinned by betraying innocent blood.” But the priests, as blind as he was greedy, coldly replied, “What is that to us?” And so the betrayer cast the blood-price into the temple and hanged himself. The priests, in hypocrisy, refused to put the money in the treasury, and instead bought a burial ground for strangers a Field of Blood it was named, as if the very earth remembered the wickedness.

The Seven Divine Sayings from the Cross

Mark now, O soul, the Seven Words spoken by Him whose tongue created the world, now parched and bloodied, yet pouring forth divine truths:

1. “My God, My God, why have You forsaken Me?”
These words are not a cry of despair, but a reference to Adam—who for 5,500 years longed for redemption. Christ speaks for all humanity here, standing as the second Adam, crying out for the fallen race.
