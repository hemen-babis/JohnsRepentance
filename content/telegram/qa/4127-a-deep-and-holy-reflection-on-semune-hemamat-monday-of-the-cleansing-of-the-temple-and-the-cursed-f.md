Part 1

Beloved in Christ, children of light and heirs of grace, I greet you with peace from the throne of God. As we begin Semune Hemamat, let us humble our hearts and open our souls to the holy mysteries of this sacred week.

This is no ordinary time it is the Week of Redemption and Mourning. The Church enters the Passion, fasting, praying, and watching in reverence. Each day unveils a mystery; each moment offers a taste of divine grace.

The Days of Semune Hemamat and Their Mystical Names

1. Monday – Cleansing of the Temple & Cursed Fig Tree
Christ cleansed the temple of corruption and cursed the barren fig tree.A call to purify our hearts and bear spiritual fruit.

2. Tuesday – Day of Questions, Day of Learning
Christ answered the traps of the Pharisees with divine wisdom.
A day to open our hearts to His truth and teachings.

3. Wednesday – Day of Counsel, Fragrance & Tears
A woman anointed Jesus with perfume; Judas plotted betrayal.
A call to choose love and repentance over deceit.

4. Thursday – Prayer Thursday, Mystery Day, New Covenant
Christ washed His disciples' feet and gave us the Holy Eucharist.
A day of humility, sacrificial love, and divine mystery.

5. Friday – Friday of the Cross, Good Friday
Christ was crucified for our sins, giving up His life willingly.
The darkest day becomes our light of salvation.

6. Saturday – Holy Saturday
Christ rested in the tomb while His Spirit freed souls in Hades.
A day of silence, hope, and the victory over death.

Monday: The Cleansing of the Temple and the Cursed Fig Tree

Why is this first day of the Passion Week set apart and called by these names? Because two mighty signs two sermons without words were given by the Lord of Glory to His creation: one within the Temple made by hands, and one from the tree planted by the hand of God Himself. One rebuked the corruption of religion; the other warned the soul against fruitlessness. Let us not pass them by with a careless eye.

I. The Cleansing of the Temple

The Lord entered the Temple, His Father’s house, and found it not a house of prayer, but a den of robbers. O what a shame to religion when its holy places are defiled by the greed of men! What sadness must have filled the Lord’s soul when He beheld merchants trafficking in that which was to be holy, when the sound of coins drowned out the psalms of praise!

He did not remain silent. With a whip fashioned not out of malice but out of righteous zeal, He drove them out. He overturned tables. He restored sanctity. This was not violence  this was purification. The Lord, gentle to sinners, becomes a lion to hypocrites.

And let us not think this act ended two thousand years ago. You are the temple of God. Your soul is His house. If Christ comes to your heart and finds there not prayer, not worship, not purity but the clamor of worldly cares and the buying and selling of vain ambitions will He not also cast these things out? Cleanse the inner court, beloved. Let Monday’s cleansing be the beginning of your own purification.

II. The Cursed Fig Tree

As the morning sun rose, the Lord, the very Bread of Life, hungered — not only for physical food, but for righteousness among His people. Approaching a fig tree clothed in leaves, He searched for fruit. Finding none, He said: "May no fruit ever grow on you again." And immediately it withered.

Here, the Word made flesh speaks to creation, and creation obeys. But why such a harsh judgment for a tree? Why did our gentle Lord curse it?

The Gospel of Mark tells us, “It was not the season for figs.” Why then did Christ expect fruit? The Church Fathers do not take this lightly. They tell us: the fig tree was a symbol  not merely a plant, but a parable of Israel and of every soul who bears the name of God yet produces no fruit of repentance.
