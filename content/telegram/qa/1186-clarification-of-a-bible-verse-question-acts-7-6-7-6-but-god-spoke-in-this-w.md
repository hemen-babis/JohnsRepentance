👉 Question:" [Acts 7 : 6 - 7]

6. But God spoke in this way: that his descendants would dwell in a foreign land,  and that they would bring them into bondage and oppress them four hundred years.

7. ‘And the nation to whom they will be in bondage I will judge,’ said God, ‘and after that they shall come out and serve Me in this place.’

 Genesis 15 : 12 - 14
12. Now when the sun was going down, a deep sleep fell upon Abram; and behold, horror and great darkness fell upon him.

13. Then He said to Abram: “Know certainly that your descendants will be strangers in a land that is not theirs, and will serve them, and they will afflict them four hundred years.

14. And also the nation whom they serve I will judge; afterward they shall come out with great possessions.

Bless me my father ,..
Why 400 years and which nation was to be judged?"

Answer: Dear brethren, in addressing the question posed, let us delve into the profound teachings of the Bible, drawing from the Acts of the Apostles and the book of Genesis. These sacred scriptures contain the word of God and provide insights into His divine plan, as revealed to our revered forefather Abraham.

To fully grasp the significance of this passage, we must first recognize the extraordinary relationship between God and Abraham. The Old Testament attests to the multitude of times God communicated with the Prophet Moses, estimating around five hundred and seventy occasions. Yet, it is Abraham who holds a unique position as God's cherished friend, for the Lord Himself proclaimed, "I do not have a friend like Abraham on earth."

Even today, in the New Testament, we invoke the covenant established with Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, beseeching their intercession for reconciliation with God and seeking His forgiveness. Abraham stands as the cornerstone, for without him, neither Isaac nor Jacob would have existed. They are referred to as "the fathers," but it is Abraham who lays the foundation for their lineage and their significance in God's plan.

Now, turning to the specific passage in question, we find a direct address to Abraham regarding his descendants. God speaks of their future plight, foretelling their sojourn in a foreign land and their subjugation and oppression for a period of four hundred years. This divine pronouncement is explicitly recorded in both the Acts of the Apostles and the book of Genesis.

During those four centuries, the children of Jacob, later known as the Israelites, endured the hardships of slavery and lived under the yoke of tyranny. God's promise to Abraham encompasses not only their earthly sufferings but also holds the promise of hope for all nations and peoples. Through Jesus Christ, born of Abraham's seed, salvation would be offered to all, unifying diverse peoples and redeeming their lives.

The Egyptians, as revealed in the Acts of the Apostles, would exercise dominion over the Israelites for four hundred years. God's words foretold that He would execute judgment upon the nation that oppressed His chosen people. Consequently, the Israelites would be liberated from their bondage and return to Canaan to worship and serve God in the land of their inheritance.

Turning our attention to Genesis 15 in the Torah, we encounter further details concerning the fulfillment of God's promises. At the time, Joseph held a position of high esteem as an esteemed counselor in the court of the Egyptian Pharaoh. The Pharaoh revered Joseph and granted him privileges, resulting in favorable treatment for the Israelites during this period.

However, when a new Pharaoh ascended the throne, ignorant of Joseph's legacy, the Israelites found themselves subjected to the burdensome weight of oppression. Their existence in Egypt, spanning four hundred and thirty years, was marked by hardship and servitude.
