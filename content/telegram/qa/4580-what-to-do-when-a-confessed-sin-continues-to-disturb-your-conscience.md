👉🏾👉🏾👉🏾What to Do When a #Confessed #Sin Continues to #Disturb Your #Conscience

Beloved in Christ, peace be unto you. Let us lift our minds from the earth to the heights of heaven, from the torment of memory to the balm of divine grace. Today, we reflect upon a painful, yet salvific question: What shall a soul do when a sin already confessed and absolved continues to trouble the conscience?

This matter is not of idle curiosity, but of deep pastoral concern. It is the lament of many a sorrowful Christian those who have fled to the sacrament of confession and yet feel as though the weight of their past clings to their soul like a shadow. Let us open the gates of understanding with the keys of the Holy Fathers, and shed light upon this anguish through divine truth.

Sin: A Thorn Among Flowers

The Holy Fathers, those physicians of the soul, liken sin to a weed among flowers. The garden of the soul, once planted by God in beauty, is marred by the thorny growth of vice. As no gardener leaves weeds to overrun his vineyard, so must we not permit sin to take root. Through constant repentance, we prune the garden of the heart. Without this pruning, even the most blooming soul becomes overgrown and choked.

This is why the Fathers also liken the repentant soul to a fresh, flowing spring, clear and life-giving. But the unrepentant soul  they call it stagnant water, foul and lifeless, filled with disease and decay. Which are we, beloved? A spring of life or a cesspool of regret?

Grave Sins and God's Forgiveness

Let us speak frankly of some sins that pierce the soul like arrows:

Fornication

Abortion

Attempted suicide

Sexual assault

These are not sins lightly spoken of, nor lightly borne. Yet, even these grievous wounds can be washed away through sincere repentance and the absolution of a faithful priest. Do not doubt it! The blood of Christ does not stain it cleanses. The one who confesses these sins, having wept before the Cross and received absolution, must not return to the same sin again unless he falls anew. Repetition of confession for that which was already repented is not humility, but unbelief in the mercy of God.

But What of Persistent Thoughts?

Here, a distinction must be made. There is a difference between committing sin and being tormented by desires and thoughts of sin. One may not commit adultery, and yet the fire of lust may rage within the heart, tempting and seducing.

If this root of evil remains alive even unacted upon it must be confessed. Why? Because it is better to crush the seed than let it bear poisonous fruit. The Holy Fathers teach that the desire for sin, even if not fulfilled, is a spark that can become a flame. And it is in the sacred confessional, through repentance, that this spark is drowned before it burns.

“Blessed is He Who Dashes the Infants Against the Rock” (Psalm 136:9)

O reader, do not stumble at this verse. These are no mere historical cries of vengeance; they are spiritual instructions for those waging war against the passions. The daughter of Babylon is the lust within us the worldly, self-seeking desires that lead us away from God. Her infants are the young thoughts, the temptations not yet matured into action.

These must be dashed not coddled, not entertained, but dashed against the Rock. What is this Rock? It is Christ Himself, the cornerstone rejected by men but chosen by God.

When temptation arises:

Flee to the Rock.

Throw your desires upon Him in prayer and weeping.

Confess the desire, even if the deed was not done.

Fill your mind with the Word of God.

Beg the Lord to extinguish the fire before it consumes you.

He who comes to Christ with sincerity will never be turned away. Our Lord crushes not the bruised reed nor quenches the smoking flax but He will utterly destroy the passions that war against the soul when we offer them to Him.
