👉 About the virgin #Mary, #Icons and the Holy #Trinity 

👉🏾 Question፦ Mr. Yohannes, what is the history behind St. Mary meaning what did she do in her life that pleased God? To add on to this why do we have so many icons in our church, ecspecially the Icon of our Lord Jesus Christ and St. Mary? Why is there the Trinity 3?

Answer፦ Beloved in Christ, peace be unto you. It gladdens my heart to address these soul-searching inquiries.

The Blessed Virgin Mary, ever revered as Theotokos (Mother of God), embodies humility and obedience which found favor with God. Her acquiescence to God's will—"Behold the handmaid of the Lord; be it unto me according to thy word" (Luke 1:38)—exemplifies her deep faith and submission. Her life, from the Annunciation to her presence at the Crucifixion and beyond, marks her as a pivotal figure in our salvation history, continually interceding for us as a spiritual mother.

Concerning the icons, they are not mere decorations but windows to the divine, aiding our contemplation and veneration. Each icon is a sacred invitation to enter into a spiritual dialogue with the Holy. As a revered father proclaimed, "The beauty of the images moves me to contemplation, as a meadow delights the eyes and subtly infuses the soul with the glory of God." Hence, icons like those of Christ and St. Mary help focus our worship and reflect the incarnate reality of the Word made flesh.

The Holy Trinity—Father, Son, and Holy Spirit—represents the Christian doctrine of one God in three persons, a profound mystery of our faith that emphasizes the perfect unity and distinctiveness of each Person of the Godhead. This foundational belief shapes all aspects of Orthodox theology and worship.

Now, dear soul, may I ask you to reflect on how the humility of the Theotokos might inspire your own walk with Christ? How can the presence of sacred icons in your daily environment draw you closer to the divine mystery of love that surrounds us?

May the name of the Holy God be praised forever and ever in every tongue that He has created, today and always.

May the blessings of God's mother, the virgin, and the cross be upon us.
Amen.
