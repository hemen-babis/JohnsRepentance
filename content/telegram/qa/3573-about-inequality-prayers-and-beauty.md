👉👉👉 About #inequality, #prayers and #beauty 

👉🏾 Question፦ Hello, 

A few questions that came up in conversation with some people that I wasn’t able to explain well if you can explain please 🙏🏾 

1. Why does suffering exist in this world and why are some people’s quality of life better than others. There are those that are homeless and struggling to eat and others living in luxury. 

2. Why do some people get what they want or pray for while others don’t. Two people can be praying about the same thing, for example their future partner, and one person gets what they wanted while the other person settles. 

3. Why didn’t God create everyone “attractive”. In the world, people perceive some as very attractive and others as average. Why couldn’t have God just created everyone “attractive” or is this a limitation in human perception.

Answer፦ Peace be upon you, child.  It warms this old monk's heart to hear from a soul grappling with such profound questions. Know that you are not alone in these ponderings. They have echoed through the chambers of human hearts since time immemorial. 

Let us delve into the wisdom of the Scriptures and the Holy Fathers to illuminate these mysteries.

1. The Mystery of Suffering and Inequality

Ah, the age-old question of suffering! Why does the good God, the loving Father, allow His children to experience pain, poverty, and want?  

Remember the words of our Lord, "In the world you will have tribulation; but be of good cheer, I have overcome the world." (John 16:33)

Suffering, my child, is a consequence of the Fall. When Adam and Eve partook of the forbidden fruit, they ushered sin and its companion, suffering, into God's perfect creation. Yet, we must not see suffering as solely punishment. The Holy Fathers, in their wisdom, teach us that suffering can also be a crucible, a fire that refines our souls and draws us closer to God.

Saint Isaac the Syrian writes, "Those who are smitten with the affliction of bodily illness or poverty, or persecution or slander, or any other temptation, and who endure it patiently, are crowned without any effort of their own."

As for the disparity between the rich and the poor, remember the parable of Lazarus and the Rich Man (Luke 16:19-31). Material wealth does not equate to spiritual richness. God sees not as man sees, for man looks at the outward appearance, but the Lord looks at the heart." (1 Samuel 16:7)

2. The Enigma of Unanswered Prayers

The ways of God are higher than our ways, His thoughts than our thoughts. (Isaiah 55:9)  We pray for what we desire, yet sometimes those desires are not in alignment with God's perfect will for us. 

St. John Chrysostom reminds us, "You ask why that man is wealthy, and your neighbor poor. Why you are healthy while another is sick? I will tell you. It is so that by caring for each other you may be drawn together and show your love."

Perhaps, in His infinite wisdom, God withholds what we ask for to grant us something even greater -  humility, patience, or an even deeper understanding of His love. 

3. The Beauty of God's Creation

You touch upon a fascinating point about beauty.  Remember that God, in His infinite wisdom, created everything "very good." (Genesis 1:31)  True beauty, my child, emanates from within, from the soul illuminated by the Divine Light. 

The standards of physical attractiveness that the world imposes are fleeting and subjective. What one culture deems beautiful, another may not. Our focus should be on cultivating the beauty of the soul through prayer, fasting, and acts of love. As St. Gregory of Nyssa wisely stated, "The true beauty is the beauty of the soul, the beauty of a holy and virtuous life."

Remember, my child, these are just starting points on your spiritual journey. Continue to seek, to pray, and to delve into the Scriptures and the writings of the Holy Fathers.
