---About #marriage and the #Holy #Eucharist

Question፦ To begin, if a person is married, must they constantly attend Holy Communion with their spouse?  Because my mother really wants to partake in holy communion and has already confessed her sins when we went to Ethiopia and she did timket, but my father doesn't go to church and only follows church services from home, is it possible for my mother to take Kurban without my father or do they have to do it together because she really wants to partake in holy communion (they are married)?  

Answer፦ The question is regarding a husband and wife who are living together but are not united in their spiritual life. One of them wants to partake of Holy Communion but the other is not attending church regularly.

The first issue is that the penance fathers have not provided adequate guidance and discipline to the couple to help them walk together in their spiritual journey. This is a problem that affects many faithful believers and is a failure on the part of the penance fathers. They should show the right path and teach the way so that people can live by the teachings of the Church.  

Secondly, couples must start their marriage with a focus on their spiritual life rather than solely on material wealth and property. They should not be driven by materialistic desires, but rather by their faith. Even if they give tithes or attend church, it should not be out of obligation but rather out of love for God. It is important to seek God's kingdom and righteousness first, and all other things will be added.

Regarding the question of whether the wife can receive Holy Communion alone, the answer is no. As long as they are living together and sharing a bed, one cannot receive Holy Communion alone. If the man is not able to adjust his position and come back to the faith, and if they have exhausted all efforts to reconcile, the woman may choose to separate and receive Holy Communion alone. However, it is important to try to keep the couple together and give them advice so that they can be together until the end.

The couple should seek guidance from elders and penance fathers to avoid any unwanted issues in their relationship that may lead to separation. The father is not going to church because he lacks a Christian education but not because he hates salvation, so it is important to involve responsible fathers in this process so they can pull him into the church using the wisdom that has been given to them. 

In conclusion, it is important to prioritize one's spiritual life and seek guidance from penance fathers to walk together with one's spouse. Holy Communion entails unity in flesh and should only be received together as a couple.
