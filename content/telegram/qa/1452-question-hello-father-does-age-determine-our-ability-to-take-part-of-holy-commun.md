---Question፦ Hello father, does #age determine our ability to take part of Holy #communion? I wanted to take part..

Answer፦ Age does not determine our ability to partake in the Holy Communion. Rather, it is our spiritual readiness and understanding of the sacrament that are of utmost importance. From the moment we are baptized and become children of God, we are invited to partake in the Holy Communion.

It is customary to present infants for Holy Communion forty days after birth for boys and eighty days for girls. This sacrement serves to confirm their status as children of God and members of the Church. From that point forward, there is no separation, departure, or cessation of their life as individuals who belong to God. The continuous participation in Holy Communion sustains our spiritual journey.

When considering age in relation to sin, some refer to the age of Adam for men and the age of Eve for women. This metaphorical understanding implies that individuals reach a level of maturity and responsibility where they are accountable for their actions. However, it is essential to recognize that sin does not discriminate based on age. Sin can be committed at any stage of life, and it is our duty to strive to avoid it.

Rather than focusing solely on age, the primary concern should be cutting off the chain of sin in our lives. It is akin to interrupting a power line or severing a water line. We must make a decisive choice to break the cycle of sin, no matter our age. We should not postpone this decision, waiting until we are older or assuming that age exempts us from sin. Our goal is to sanctify our lives through the sacrament and live in communion with God, thus inheriting eternal life.

Therefore, rather than determining our readiness for Holy Communion based on age, we should take a momentous step to cease habitual sin. There is no fixed system or law that permits sin based on age. The notion that one can indulge in sin while young or wait until old age due to physical limitations is a grave misunderstanding. Such thinking suggests weakness in our commitment to Christianity. God desires the hearts of the youth and children, free from sin. Repentance and forgiveness are available to all, regardless of age.

Considering this perspective, dear member, age alone does not determine when you can approach Holy Communion. We implore you not to allow any sins to hinder your approach to Holy Communion. Remember that no sin is permissible based on age. God commands us to refrain from sin in every stage of life—during childhood, youth, adulthood, and old age. If we happen to stumble and fall into sin, we must approach with repentance, without shame or fear, and seek forgiveness.
