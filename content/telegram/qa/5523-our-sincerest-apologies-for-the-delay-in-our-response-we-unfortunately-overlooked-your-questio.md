👉🏾👉🏾👉🏾The Meaning and #Diversity of #Crosses in the #Ethiopian #Orthodox Tewahedo Church

Question :- Selam, blessings and congratulations on completing the fast if you partook! I have a question about this cross. Why is there so many different type of Twehado crosses being used? Especially in church and hand held crosses priests carry? As well as the wooden necklaces worn by those baptized? Do they all bare different meanings? Again, I thank you kindly.

👉🏾Answer :- Peace be upon you, I am here to listen, to offer what solace I can from the wisdom of our Holy Fathers and the Scriptures, and to gently guide you on your spiritual journey. I assist you on this blessed day, my child?

You have asked about the mystery of the Cross, and why in our holy Tewahedo tradition we see many different types those held in the hands of priests, those lifted in the church, and those worn upon the necks of the baptized. This is a good and blessed question, for the Cross is the very seal of our salvation, the tree upon which our Lord Jesus Christ gave Himself as a ransom for the world.

My child, know this: the Cross is one in meaning but many in form. All the shapes you see whether large processional crosses, hand crosses for blessing, or the small wooden ones placed upon the baptized carry the same truth: Christ has conquered death by the Cross. But our forefathers, in their great devotion, carved and fashioned the Cross in many artistic designs, not to change its meaning, but to magnify its beauty and mystery.

The processional crosses lifted high in the church symbolize Christ exalted, drawing all people to Himself, as He said: “When I am lifted up from the earth, I will draw all men unto Me” (John 12:32).

The hand crosses of the priests are used to bless the faithful, reminding us that every blessing flows from the life-giving Cross of Christ. When the priest touches your head with it, it is as though the victory of Golgotha rests upon you.

The neck crosses given at baptism, especially the simple wooden ones, are signs of protection and belonging. They remind the newly baptized that they have put on Christ, that they must “take up their cross daily” (Luke 9:23), and that the Lord Himself guards them through His Cross.

The variations in design some like flowers, some like suns, some with intricate knots are like the many languages of praise. all point to the same mystery. Just as there are four Gospels but one Truth, so there are many crosses but one faith.

So, my child, do not be troubled by the differences. They are not contradictions, but rather treasures of devotion passed down from generation to generation. What matters is not the shape, but the heart that honors the Cross, for as the Apostle says, “God forbid that  should glory, save in the cross of our Lord Jesus Christ” (Galatians 6:14).

May the life-giving Cross guard you always, and may its light shine upon your path.
