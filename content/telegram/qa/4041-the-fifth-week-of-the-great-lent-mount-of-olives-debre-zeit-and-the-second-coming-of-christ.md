Part 1

The Fifth Sunday of Lent, known as Debre Zeit, holds deep theological and eschatological significance within the Orthodox Church. "Debre Zeit" translates to "Mount of Olives," a sacred place east of Jerusalem adorned with olive trees. This mountain serves as a monumental site in biblical history, where our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ imparted one of His most profound teachings—concerning the end of the world and His second coming.

1. The Significance of Debre Zeit in the Church

The Orthodox Church dedicates the Fifth Sunday of Lent to contemplating the final days of the world, the Second Coming of Christ, and the Day of Judgment. The choice of the Mount of Olives for this theme is not incidental but deeply rooted in biblical prophecy and the words of Christ Himself.

A Place of Divine Teaching: On this mountain, Christ responded to the inquiry of His disciples:
"What shall be the sign of thy coming, and of the end of the world?" (Matthew 24:3).
Here, He revealed the mysteries of the future—warnings, signs, and admonitions—so that the faithful may be vigilant and prepared.

A Place of Ascension: After completing His earthly ministry, Christ ascended to heaven from this very mountain (Acts 1:12), marking it as a bridge between earth and heaven.

A Place of Prophetic Fulfillment: The return of Christ is prophesied to be revealed at this location (Zechariah 14:3-5), reinforcing its connection to both the past and future of divine revelation.

2. The Inquiry of the Disciples and Christ’s Response

One morning, as the disciples beheld the grandeur of the temple, they marveled at its architectural splendor. Expecting Christ to join in their admiration, they were instead met with a shocking revelation: the temple would be destroyed. This unexpected pronouncement led them to ask the crucial question:

"When shall these things be? And what shall be the sign of thy coming, and of the end of the world?" (Matthew 24:3).

This twofold question—“When?” and “What?”—was met with a carefully measured response by Christ:

Concerning the timing, He withheld the precise day and hour, stating:
"But of that day and hour knoweth no man, no, not the angels of heaven, but my Father only." (Matthew 24:36).

Instead of specifying a date, He provided a series of signs that would precede His coming, cautioning His disciples to remain steadfast in faith.

3. The Signs Preceding the Second Coming

Although Christ did not disclose the exact time, He laid out clear warnings and prophecies, urging believers to discern the times and remain unwavering. These include:

A. The Rise of False Christs and False Prophets

"Take heed that no man deceive you. For many shall come in my name, saying, I am Christ; and shall deceive many." (Matthew 24:4-5)

Deception will increase as false teachers, claiming divine authority, arise.

These imposters will perform great signs and wonders, leading many astray.

Their deception will be subtle, sometimes even targeting the elect.

The faithful must test every spirit against the Word of God.

B. The Spread of False Teachings and Fearmongering

"Then if any man shall say unto you, Lo, here is Christ, or there; believe it not." (Matthew 24:23)

False rumors about Christ’s secret return will spread.

Some will claim to have exclusive knowledge of His presence in remote places.

The faithful must hold fast to Scripture and not be led astray by sensational claims.

C. Wars, Conflicts, and Global Turmoil

"And ye shall hear of wars and rumors of wars: see that ye be not troubled: for all these things must come to pass, but the end is not yet." (Matthew 24:6)

Conflicts between nations will escalate.

Rumors of impending disasters will incite panic.

Believers must not succumb to fear, understanding that these are merely signs, not the end itself.
