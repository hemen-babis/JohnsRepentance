✍🏾The Compassion of the Master

If someone were to question this, asking, “Does the worker have more mercy than the master?” the answer is clear: No. The mercy of the worker is born from the mercy of the master. The worker’s compassion is a reflection of the divine compassion that already exists in the Creator’s heart.

It was the master who gave the worker permission to plead. It was the master who created the opportunity for mercy. Even before the worker spoke, the master knew he would intercede and He had already decided to accept that intercession.

Thus, the parable is symbolic, revealing both the power and the mercy of God. The Creator shows His full authority over creation, yet also His deep desire that His servants His angels participate in His merciful work.

The Lord who said, “Cut it down,” is the same Lord who knew from eternity that the tree would be spared through the plea of His servant. In this, we see the wisdom, sovereignty, and compassion of God, and the sacred ministry of His holy angels, who intercede continually for the salvation of mankind.

Conclusion

The parable teaches us that:

God, the Creator, is just and merciful.

The holy angels are His servants who intercede for humanity.

Intercession is not an act that opposes God’s will but a part of His divine plan to lead us to repentance and salvation.

Let us therefore give thanks to God, who appoints holy angels to watch over us, pray for us, and protect us in every trial.

May we, through their intercession and by the mercy of our Lord Jesus Christ, be found fruitful in faith and righteousness on the day of judgment.
