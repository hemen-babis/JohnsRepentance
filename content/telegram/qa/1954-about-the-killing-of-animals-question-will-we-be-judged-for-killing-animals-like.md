About the #killing of #animals 

👉 Question፦ Will we be judged for killing animals like Rodents?

Answer፦ Beloved in Christ,

In our contemplation of life and its sacredness, we are reminded of the profound words in Genesis 1:26, where God says, "Let us make man in our image, after our likeness: and let them have dominion over the fish of the sea, and over the fowl of the air, and over the cattle, and over all the earth, and over every creeping thing that creepeth upon the earth." This dominion is not of tyranny but of stewardship, a call to care for God's creation with love and respect.

In the Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church, as well as in the broader Orthodox Christian tradition, the relationship between humans and animals is viewed through the lens of stewardship and compassion. All creatures are part of God's creation, and as such, they are to be treated with respect and care. While the Church does not explicitly condemn the killing of animals for necessary purposes, such as for food, it does encourage mindfulness and respect for all life.

The act of killing rodents, or any other animal should always be approached with a sense of responsibility and, when necessary, with a spirit of regret for the loss of life. We are reminded to always seek the most humane and compassionate solutions in our interactions with all of God's creatures.

Reflecting on our actions and their impact on the world around us is an essential part of our spiritual journey. In doing so, we align ourselves more closely with God's will, fostering a spirit of humility and love. As you consider this aspect of your life, ask yourself: How can I exercise compassionate stewardship in all my interactions with God's creation?

May the name of the Holy God be praised forever and ever in every language that He has created, today and always.

May the blessings of the Holy Mother of God, the Virgin, and the Cross be upon us. Amen.
