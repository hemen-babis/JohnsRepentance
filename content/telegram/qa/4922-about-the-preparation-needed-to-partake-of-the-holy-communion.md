👉 About the preparation needed to partake of the #Holy #Communion 

👉🏾 Question፦ Hello, thank you for the response. In the kidase, they also say verbally if someone has “leyu hasab” they shouldn’t partake in Holy Communion. What does “Leyu Hasab” mean? 

Is there anything else they say  besides the other ones already mentioned in the previous answer?

Thank you!

Answer፦ You ask a good and important question regarding the Kidase, the Divine Liturgy of our Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church.  "Leyu Hasab" literally translates to "different idea" or "different intent" or "difference in purpose" It refers to a state of being unprepared or unworthy to receive the Holy Mysteries of Christ's Body and Blood.

This unworthiness can stem from various reasons, including:

Unconfessed sin:  If one harbors unconfessed sin in their heart, they approach the Holy Eucharist without having reconciled with God and His Church. This is akin to the Apostle Paul's warning in 1 Corinthians 11:27-29, where he speaks of partaking in an unworthy manner, bringing judgment upon oneself.

Disturbed conscience: If one is in a state of anger, resentment, or unforgiveness towards another,  their heart is not properly disposed to receive the Eucharist, which is a sacrament of unity and love. Christ himself teaches us to reconcile with our brother before offering our gift at the altar (Matthew 5:23-24).

Lack of preparation: This could include neglecting prayer, fasting, and spiritual reflection in the days leading up to Communion. The Eucharist is not a casual meal, but a holy mystery demanding reverence and preparation.

Distraction and lack of focus: Receiving Holy Communion requires complete attention and devotion.  If one's mind is wandering or their heart is preoccupied with worldly matters, they are not fully present to receive the Lord.

The priest's admonition regarding "leyu hasab" serves as a reminder for self-examination and a call to approach the Holy Eucharist with reverence and fear of God.

Beyond the specific mention of "leyu hasab," the priest also exhorts those who are:

Excommunicated: Those who have been formally separated from the Church through disciplinary measures are not permitted to receive Communion until they have been reconciled.

Those in a state of impurity: This generally refers to women during menstruation, as well as individuals who have recently experienced bodily emissions. This is a traditional practice rooted in Old Testament purity laws, emphasizing the sanctity and reverence due to the Holy Eucharist.

Those who have committed serious sins and haven’t confessed: This echoes the point about unconfessed sin.

These pronouncements serve to protect the sanctity of the Holy Mysteries and to guide the faithful toward worthy participation. Remember, my child,  receiving Holy Communion is a profound spiritual act, requiring preparation and a contrite heart.  If you are unsure about your readiness, speak with your priest or Abba. They can offer guidance and ensure you approach the Eucharist with the reverence and devotion it deserves.
