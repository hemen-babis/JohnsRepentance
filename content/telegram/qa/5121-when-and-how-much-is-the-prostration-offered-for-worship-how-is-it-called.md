👉🏾👉🏾👉🏾When and How Much is the #Prostration #Offered for #Worship? How is it Called?

(Part 4)

Beloved brethren in Christ, peace and grace be multiplied unto you. Today we turn to a sacred question: When is prostration offered? How much is required of us? And what does the Church call this holy act? Let us attend carefully, for in these mysteries we are taught how to humble the body so that the soul may ascend to God.

1. Where Should We Pray?

God, who is infinite and uncontained, is not bound by any place. He is higher than the heavens, deeper than the abyss, and His presence stretches from horizon to horizon. Yet we, being finite creatures bound by space and time, are in need of holy places to direct our prayer.

Therefore, we gather in the temple of God the Church. Church means both the dwelling of Christ in His people, and the gathering place of the faithful. When we call the Church the dwelling of Christ, we mean His presence in the souls of the righteous, in the individual believer, and in the community. When we call the Church the house of Christ, we speak of the consecrated building where His people assemble.

Thus, the Church is the house of God Himself. It is the place where His name is called, His glory proclaimed, and the sacrifice of thanksgiving is continually offered. Wherever the faithful gather, there is the Church; for He has promised: “Where two or three are gathered in My name, there am I in their midst” (Matthew 18:20).

The first reason we worship in the Church is because it is His house, and where the King is, there His servants the holy angels and the righteous saints also dwell. We bow with reverence before God and honor those who serve Him.

2. How Do We Prostrate ?

 We bend our hips, fall upon our knees, and touch the ground with our hands and forehead. Why? Because though God is everywhere present, He Himself declares: “Heaven is My throne, and the earth is My footstool” (Matthew 5:35). When therefore we touch the earth with our heads, we humble ourselves at His feet, acknowledging His majesty.

In this way, both the worship that belongs to God alone (the prostration of His nature) and the honor shown to His saints (the prostration of grace) are performed outwardly in the same act yet the heart distinguishes their meaning.

When prostration is made in church, we turn to the East, for the Sun of Righteousness rose from the East. If we are not in a church, we set before us a holy sign: a cross, the Gospel, or an icon. Having bowed, we seal our prayer with the words: “O God, have mercy on us, and through the Virgin Mary, have mercy on us.”

In prayers of repentance, our spiritual father appoints how many prostrate and prayers we should perform, guiding us as a physician prescribes medicine. 

Whoever is moved by zeal may increase their prayers; whoever is weak may offer as they can but always remembering the sufferings of Christ. for the prostrations that we offer no strict limit is set.
