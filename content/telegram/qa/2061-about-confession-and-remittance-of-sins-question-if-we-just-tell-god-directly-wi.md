About #confession and remittance of sins 

👉🏾 Question፦ If we just tell God directly without confessing our sins to the priest, won't he forgive us? 

Answer፦ The query at hand asks if direct confession to God, without the mediation of a priest, suffices for forgiveness. To address this, we must first understand the nature of repentance as a foundational aspect of our spiritual journey towards God. Repentance is not merely an act of acknowledging one's sins, but an invitation to a transformative relationship with God. It involves a deep realization of our fallibility, a heartfelt sorrow for our transgressions, and a commitment to change.

In this sacred process, the role of the priest is not to be underestimated. When Christ bestowed upon His apostles the authority to forgive sins (John 20:23), He established a divine mechanism through which the Church, as His body, continues His redemptive work. This apostolic succession, passed down through the priesthood, is not a mere human tradition but a divine ordinance.

Confessing to a priest, therefore, is an act of humility and obedience. It acknowledges the Church's role as the custodian of grace and the conduit of divine forgiveness. The priest acts not in his own authority but as a representative of Christ, guiding the penitent through the sacrament of reconciliation.

Consider the scriptural basis for this practice. In James 5:16, we are exhorted to confess our sins to one another. This mutual confession is a testament to the communal nature of the Church. Moreover, in Matthew 16:19, Christ's words to Peter about binding and loosing underscore the authority given to the Church in matters of sin and forgiveness.

Furthermore, the sacrament of confession brings with it the grace of spiritual guidance. A regular confessor becomes a spiritual mentor, understanding the nuances of one's struggles and providing tailored guidance. This relationship mirrors the biblical instances of spiritual mentorship, such as Nathan with David or Paul with Timothy.

In the act of confession, the penitent is not merely recounting sins to another human but standing before God in a space sanctified by His grace, mediated through the priest. This sacramental encounter ensures that forgiveness is not an abstract concept but a tangible, transformative experience.

However, this does not diminish the importance of direct prayer and confession to God. Personal prayer is crucial for cultivating a direct relationship with God. It is where we pour out our hearts, express our deepest regrets, and seek His guidance. The sacramental confession complements this personal dialogue with God, providing a formal, communal acknowledgment of our repentance.

In conclusion, while God's forgiveness is boundless and not constrained by any human intermediary, the Church, through the sacrament of confession, provides a structured, communal, and sacramental means of receiving that forgiveness. It is in this sacred space that the penitent encounters not just a priest but Christ Himself, offering healing, restoration, and a renewed path towards holiness.
