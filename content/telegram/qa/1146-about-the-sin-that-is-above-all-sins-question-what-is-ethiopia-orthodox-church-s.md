Question፦ What is Ethiopia Orthodox church stand about suicide?

Answer፦ The Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church holds a firm stance against suicide, considering it a grave sin and strongly condemning the act. The Church does not provide a dignified funeral or orderly burial for individuals who have taken their own lives, as a means to discourage and set an example for others.

When a child is born into the Church, it is believed that they will grow up to replace their parents and contribute positively to the world. Therefore, when a child tragically succumbs to suicide, it is seen as deviating from their purpose and succumbing to the hidden and abstract influence of evil spirits. The Church views suicide as a spiritual issue, often associated with struggles such as addictions, disharmony within families, marital problems, and difficulties in social relationships.

Suicide is regarded as an act of sin and is considered evil and cursed according to the religious laws and canons of the Church. The prevalence of sin in today's society has made such acts more common, with individuals resorting to various methods such as poisoning, using weapons, drowning, or engaging in car accidents. The Church firmly condemns these actions and deems them as sinful.

To distance ourselves from the influence of the world and satanic forces, we must strive to live a righteous and virtuous life. Even in the face of disasters or circumstances that may bring shame to the soul and body, forsaking eternal life and perishing in this world without a peaceful death signifies the peak of our own injustice and self-destruction. The Church acknowledges that vulnerable individuals, including children, women, and innocents, may succumb to suicide due to pressure and temptation from the same enemy—Satan, who takes pleasure in destroying people and leading them towards self-annihilation.

While there may be instances where individuals use weapons to end their lives, perhaps driven by a misguided sense of bravery or refusing to surrender their country's honor or personal dignity on the battlefield, the Ethiopian Orthodox Church strictly condemns and prohibits suicide. The Church recognizes that people can fall victim to such disasters, but it emphasizes that embracing suicide as an option and collaborating with the power of Satan or his destructive ideas is contrary to its teachings.

The Church is ready to provide spiritual services and support to its children who are confronted with the tragedy of suicide. However, it vehemently rejects any actions that would further lead its children to perish through negative examples.
