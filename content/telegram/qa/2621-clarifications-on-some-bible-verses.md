👉 #Clarifications on some #Bible verses 

👉🏾 Question፦ Can you explain how to apply these Bible teachings in daily life:

1. What does being "the salt of the earth" mean in practical terms?
2. Does removing the log from our own eye before judging others mean we should first address our own faults?
3. Does "the measure you give will be the measure you get" suggest that our judgments will be reciprocated?
4. What does striving for God's Kingdom and His righteousness involve, and what should we expect from it?
5. In what way should we ask, knock, and seek in our relationship with God?
6. How can we identify false prophets or insincere people by their "fruits"? 

Could you help clarify these concepts?

Answer፦ Dear seeker of truth,

Your inquiry into these profound scriptures is a commendable pursuit toward deepening your understanding and spiritual growth. Let us explore these verses in light of the Church's teachings.

1. Salt of the Earth: In Matthew 5:13, Christ calls His followers to be "the salt of the earth." This metaphor signifies the indispensable Christian duty to enhance and preserve the moral integrity of the world. If the salt loses its flavor, it symbolizes losing one's spiritual zeal and virtue, becoming ineffective in our Christian witness. Saint John Chrysostom explains this as a dire warning to maintain the strength of our faith and virtue, lest we fail our purpose and are "thrown out and trampled."

2. Removing the Log from Your Own Eye: The verse from Matthew 7:5 indeed suggests that we must first address our own sins and failings before correcting others. This is more than just a practical self-improvement; it is a profound spiritual principle of humility and self-awareness. It reminds us to approach others with compassion rather than judgment, recognizing our own imperfections.

3. Measure of Judgment: In Matthew 7:2, the principle that "with the judgment you pronounce you will be judged" reflects the divine justice that mirrors our own attitudes and actions back to us. It is not akin to karma in its traditional sense but rather a biblical principle that God's judgment upon us corresponds to how we judge others.

4. Seeking the Kingdom of God: Matthew 6:33's call to "strive first for the kingdom of God and His righteousness" is indeed about aligning our lives with God's commandments and embracing a life of virtue. Practically, this means living out the virtues you mentioned—honesty, purity, and faithfulness—trusting that God will provide for our needs according to His will and in His time.

5. Ask, Knock, Seek: These actions in Matthew 7:7 highlight the importance of persistent and active faith. We must not only ask but also seek and knock, showing our commitment and effort in pursuing the things of God. It is a progressive deepening of engagement with God and His promises.

6. By Their Fruits: Finally, in Matthew 7:15-20, discerning "by their fruits" is crucial in evaluating the true character of others, particularly those who claim spiritual authority. True spiritual life manifests in observable virtues—love, joy, peace, and the like. Those consistently exhibiting negative behaviors ("bad fruits") reveal a heart not aligned with God’s ways.

In all these, the overarching theme is that our faith must be lived actively and consciously, reflecting inwardly in our actions and outwardly through our interactions with others. In Orthodoxy, this life is best supported through a relationship with a spiritual father or guide who can help nurture these virtues in us.

What step will you take today to align more closely with these teachings of Christ? May the peace of the Lord guide you on this path.
