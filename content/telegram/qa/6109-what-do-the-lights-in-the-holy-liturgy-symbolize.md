✍🏾Question : What the incense and lights in the holy liturgy symbolises to?

✍🏾Answer : My beloved child,  Your insightful questions about the symbolism in our Holy Liturgy are truly a testament to your fervent desire to draw closer to the mysteries of our faith. We have spoken of the incense, and now you turn to the lights – indeed, they too are rich with spiritual meaning, illuminating our path and elevating our hearts.

Here is the answer about incense that you asked earlier.

✍🏾In the Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church, the use of light in the church is not decorative or optional. It is a sacred requirement rooted in Holy Scripture and deep spiritual symbolism. For this reason, the church is filled with light through candles, oil lamps, olive oil, and even modern lamps. These lights are used during regular prayers, the Divine Liturgy, the reading of the Holy Gospel, before holy icons, and on the pulpit.

Light holds profound spiritual meaning because it is a divine command. From the very beginning, God revealed Himself through light:

“And God said, ‘Let there be light,’ and there was light. And God saw that the light was good” (Genesis 1:3–4).

✍🏾The early Christians also prayed with light, as Scripture tells us:“There were many lamps in the upper room where they were gathered” (Acts 20:8).

✍🏾Above all, the church must be filled with light because it is the dwelling place of God, who is holy and luminous. Our Lord Jesus Christ said, “I am the light of the world” (John 8:12), and the Apostle John teaches, “God is light, and in Him is no darkness at all” (1 John 1:5). In the Book of Revelation, the Church itself is called a lampstand. Saint John saw Christ standing among the seven golden lampstands, which represent the seven churches (Revelation 1:20).

✍🏾The mystery of lamps lit with olive oil is especially deep. In the Old Testament, oil was used to anoint priests and kings, symbolizing the presence and power of the Holy Spirit. Scripture says, “Then Samuel took the horn of oil and anointed him in the midst of his brothers; and the Spirit of the Lord came mightily upon David from that day forward” (1 Samuel 16:13). For this reason, oil and light in the church remind us of our anointing by the Holy Spirit and His work within us.

✍🏾The candle and lamp burning in the church symbolize the mercy of the Holy Spirit working in our hearts and our continual remembrance of God. Their constant flame reminds us that God’s protection never leaves us. The light of the church also represents the righteous and the martyrs. Our Lord said, “You are the light of the world. Let your light so shine before men” (Matthew 5:14–16), and Scripture speaks of the righteous shining like stars (Daniel 12:3). They are described as lamps placed on a lampstand (Matthew 5:15).

✍🏾Therefore, lighting lamps before the icons of the saints and martyrs signifies that they were lights in their deeds and words. They burned with the fire of God’s love and became light to the world through their faith and sacrifice. This light reminds us of their suffering and their glory, as the Lord said, “The righteous will shine forth as the sun in the kingdom of their Father” (Matthew 13:43), and John the Baptist was called “a burning and shining lamp” (John 5:35).

✍🏾Our Lord also commands spiritual vigilance, saying, “Let your waist be girded and your lamps burning” (Luke 12:35). Thus, when we see the lamps burning in the church, we are reminded to remain spiritually awake and prepared for the coming of Christ.

✍🏾When a candle is lit during the reading of the Holy Gospel, it symbolizes the guiding power of God’s Word in our spiritual journey:

👉🏾“Your word is a lamp to my feet and a light to my path” (Psalm 119:105).
