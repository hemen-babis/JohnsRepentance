Answer to the last question regarding our Lady the virgin #Mary

My child, thank you for your thoughtful inquiry. It brings joy to my heart that you and your friend are wrestling with these important questions.  The Blessed Virgin Mary holds a special place in the hearts of Orthodox Christians, but it's important to understand it all comes from our deep reverence for God.

Regarding Intercession

You raise a great point about prayer. We do believe in approaching God directly through prayer. After all, He is closer to us than our breath (cf. St. Isaac the Syrian). However, the Bible also shows us the power of intercessory prayer. Abraham pleads for Sodom (Genesis 18:16-32), Moses intercedes for the Israelites (Exodus 32:11-14), and Paul asks for the prayers of the Thessalonians (1 Thessalonians 5:25).

Mary, Theotokos

The difference with Mary is her unique role as the Theotokos, the God-bearer.  She carried within her the very Son of God, conceived not by man but by the Holy Spirit (cf. Luke 1:35).  This special relationship allows her prayers to hold particular weight before God.

Thinking of Mary as a Bridge

Imagine Mary as a bridge. We pray directly to God, but we also ask for Mary's prayers to act as a bridge, carrying our petitions closer to Him. It's not that Mary is a separate mediator, but an amplifier of our own prayers.

Addressing Your Concerns

Mary's Uniqueness: The prophets interceded, yes, but Mary's role is singular. She is the bridge between humanity and divinity.

Jesus as Mediator: Absolutely, Jesus is the ultimate mediator (cf. 1 Timothy 2:5), but this doesn't exclude the prayers of the Saints, including Mary.

Mary's Sinlessness: The early Church debated this, but most agreed Mary was preserved from sin through God's grace in preparation for Christ's incarnation.

Mary's Knowledge: We don't claim Mary is God. The Holy Spirit guides her intercession, not omnipresence.

The Intercessory Symphony: A Chorus of Voices

You're right, the Bible speaks volumes about prayer. We are called to talk directly to the Almighty, our Father in Heaven. King David, the Psalmist, is a testament to this direct line of communication. Yet, the Scriptures also resonate with the power of intercession. Abraham pleads with God for Sodom, his pleas heavy with the weight of a righteous man (Genesis 18:16-32). Moses, the great lawgiver, wrestles with God on behalf of the Israelites, his unwavering faith a shield against their sin (Exodus 32:11-14). Even the Apostle Paul, the champion of God's grace, beseeches the Thessalonians to pray for him (1 Thessalonians 5:25).  See, my child, prayer is a symphony, a chorus of voices lifted to God. Our own prayers are the foundation, but they can be amplified by the righteous pleas of the Saints.
