About the appropriate length of a man's #Hair 

👉 Question፦ In 1 Corinthians 11:14 it says"14 Doth not even nature itself teach you, that, if a man have long hair, it is a shame unto him?"  Does this mean that it is a sin for men to have long hair?

Answer፦ Beloved child in Christ, peace be unto you. Your question is wise and sincere, and it shows a heart that longs to understand the will of God. Let us walk together in the light of the Scriptures.

The verse you asked about - “Doth not even nature itself teach you, that, if a man have long hair, it is a shame unto him?” (1 Corinthians 11:14) - is not a random comment by the Apostle Paul. It is part of a greater teaching about order, distinction, and honor in how we present ourselves before God and before others.

In Corinth, there was confusion among new believers - many of whom had just come out of pagan religions - about the proper appearance and behavior of men and women. Paul is not merely talking about fashion, but about preserving the God-given distinction between male and female, a distinction rooted in creation itself: “male and female created He them” (Genesis 1:27).

So, does this mean that it is always a sin for a man to have long hair? The answer depends on the reason. In general, yes, the Apostle is warning that men should not take on a feminine appearance, because doing so confuses the natural and spiritual order God has established. In most cases, growing long hair without cause reflects vanity, pride, or rebellion, and this is what the Apostle condemns.

Now, you may ask, what about the Nazarites - men like Samson, who never cut their hair? (Numbers 6:5). This was not a personal choice for fashion or identity. It was a special, divine calling under the Old Covenant. The long hair of a Nazarite was not about appearance - it was a symbol of separation from the world and total devotion to God. It was rare, specific, and done with the blessing of God.

Today, under the New Covenant and the fullness of life in the Church, such vows are no longer practiced in the same way. A man cannot simply declare himself a "Nazarite" to justify long hair. True spiritual devotion today is expressed not by growing hair, but by growing in repentance, obedience, and humility - under the care of the Church and the guidance of a spiritual father.

So, the teaching remains: a man should appear as a man, honoring the image God gave him. Let us not follow the confusion of the world, which seeks to erase the beauty of that difference. Let every part of our life - our dress, our behavior, our hair - glorify God and reflect who He made us to be.

“Let all things be done decently and in order” (1 Corinthians 14:40).

Now, my beloved, as you reflect on this truth, how will you begin today to bring your outward appearance into harmony with the inner life Christ is calling you to live?

May the name of the Holy God be praised forever and ever in every tongue that He has created, today and always.
