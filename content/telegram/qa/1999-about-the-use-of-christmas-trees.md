👉 About the use of #Christmas trees

👉🏾 Question፦ Greetings, our teachers from the Yohannes Nisiha, I pray by Gods Grace you all are well. I wanted to ask a private question about the Celebration of the birth of Our Lord and why we as Orthodox Christians do not put up Christmas Trees in our homes. Many of the faithful in America are starting to use these traditions of the West and I wanted to ask is this accepted by the faith or is this a pagan practice that is against the Celebration of the Incarnation of God. Also, what traditions are encouraged by our Fathers to glorify God in the season of His Holy Birth.

Answer፦ Greetings in the name of our Lord Jesus Christ,

Your query touches upon a significant aspect of our faith, especially in these times when cultures intertwine and traditions intermingle. The practice of setting up Christmas trees is indeed a Western tradition and not inherently part of the Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church or the broader Orthodox Christian tradition. It originated in Germany and gradually became popular in other parts of the world, including America.

In Orthodoxy, we focus on the spiritual essence of feasts, primarily through liturgy, fasting, prayer, and almsgiving. Our emphasis is on the incarnation of Christ, a mystery profoundly expressed in our liturgical celebrations. As St. John Chrysostom says, "The rich man is not one who has much, but one who gives much. For what he gives away remains his forever." This spirit of giving and self-denial is central to our commemoration of Christ's Nativity.

Regarding the Christmas tree, while it is not a traditional Orthodox practice, its use in itself is not considered a pagan or anti-Christian act, as long as it does not detract from the true meaning of the Nativity. However, as Orthodox Christians, we are called to discern and ensure that our practices align with the teachings and traditions of our Church.

Our Fathers encourage traditions that glorify God through increased prayer, participation in the Divine Liturgy, and acts of charity. These practices bring us closer to the mystery of the Incarnation. The Nativity Fast, which we observe, prepares us spiritually for the celebration of Christ's birth. We are also encouraged to read and contemplate the Scriptures, especially the Infancy narratives in the Gospels, and to engage in acts of love and kindness, reflecting the love that God showed by becoming man for our salvation.

In conclusion, while the Christmas tree is a cultural addition rather than an Orthodox tradition, what is most crucial is maintaining a focus on the Incarnation of Christ and living out the virtues that this great feast teaches us. How might you, in this blessed season, deepen your practice of the true spirit of Christ's Nativity in prayer, fasting, and acts of charity?

May the name of the Holy God be praised forever and ever in every tongue that He has created, today and always. 

May the blessings of The Virgin, and the cross be upon us. Amen.
