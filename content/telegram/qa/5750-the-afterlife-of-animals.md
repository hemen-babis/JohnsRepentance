👉🏾👉🏾👉🏾The #Afterlife of #Animals

Question :-Will animals go to heaven? My friemd was making reference to Romans 8:21-22 Any information on animal afterlife?

Answer :-My dear child, your question about animals in the afterlife, and your friend's reference to the powerful words of St. Paul in Romans 8:21-22, touches a chord in many hearts, for indeed, we witness the suffering of all creation and naturally yearn for its redemption. It is a compassionate and insightful question.

Let us turn our hearts and minds to the wisdom of the Holy Spirit, as revealed in Scripture and understood by the Holy Fathers.

You mentioned:
👉🏾Romans 8:21: "Because the creation itself also will be delivered from the bondage of corruption into the glorious liberty of the children of God."

👉🏾Romans 8:22: "For we know that the whole creation groans and labors with birth pangs together until now."

These verses are profoundly significant. St. Paul is speaking here of the cosmic redemption that awaits all creation, not just humanity. When Adam and Eve fell into sin, their sin had repercussions not only for humanity but for the entire created order, subjecting it to decay, suffering, and death what St. Paul calls "the bondage of corruption." The "groaning" of creation is its suffering under the curse of sin, awaiting its liberation.

Now, concerning animals specifically and their place in this redemption:

1.  The Restoration of Creation, Not Individual Animal Souls in the Human Sense:

The Orthodox understanding, drawing from the Fathers, emphasizes that while God loves and cares for all His creation, the "heaven" or eternal life promised to humanity is fundamentally different from what might be understood for animals.

✍🏾Human beings are created in the image and likeness of God, possessing an immortal, rational, spiritual soul capable of communion with God, of moral choice, repentance, and personal salvation unto deification (theosis). Our eternal destiny is a personal, conscious relationship with the Creator in His Kingdom.

✍🏾Animals possess a "soul" (psyche or nephesh* in Hebrew, meaning "life principle"), which gives them life, sensation, and instinct. However, this is generally understood not to be an immortal, rational, spiritual soul in the same way as human souls. Animals do not have moral responsibility, nor do they seek personal communion with God in the way humans do. Therefore, their "salvation" or "afterlife" is not understood in terms of individual, conscious, eternal existence in the same manner as for humans.

2.  The New Heavens and New Earth:

When St. Paul speaks of creation being "delivered from the bondage of corruption," he points to the New Creation—the "new heavens and a new earth" spoken of in Isaiah (65:17) and Revelation (21:1). This will be a transfigured, glorified creation, where the effects of the Fall are utterly removed. There will be no more suffering, decay, or death.

In this renewed creation, it is a pious and beautiful hope that animals, in some transfigured form, will indeed be present. The Prophet Isaiah gives us wonderful imagery of this harmonious new creation: "The wolf also shall dwell with the lamb, and the leopard shall lie down with the kid; and the calf and the young lion and the fatling together; and a little child shall lead them" (Isaiah 11:6, Septuagint). This suggests a world where the natural order, currently scarred by predation and suffering, is restored to its original peaceful state of harmony as God intended.
