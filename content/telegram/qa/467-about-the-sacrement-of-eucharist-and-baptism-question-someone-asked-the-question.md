---About the #sacrement of #Eucharist and #Baptism

👉Question: Someone asked the question can the Holy Body be given without the precious blood? Must Holy Communion happen after Holy Baptism?
 
✍️Answer #1: The first question is can the Holy Body be given without the precious blood? Our questioner; First of all, we must understand that we are commanded to receive His Holy Body and His Precious Blood. Our Lord our God Jesus Christ, crucified on the cross, broke his holy body, shed his precious blood, he told us that whoever ate my body and drank my blood has eternal life. He told us that this is my flesh and blood that will be shed for the welfare of the world. Therefore, the creator spoke about both his holy body and his precious blood.

Therefore, we cannot say that if the flesh is present, the blood is not needed, because that is not the teaching we have received from our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ. We do it God's way. Eat my flesh, we will eat his flesh; Drink my blood, we will drink his blood. We do what we are exactly told to do. When we look at John 6:55-56, "For my flesh is true food and my blood is true drink. He that eateth my flesh, and drinketh my blood, abideth in me, and I in him." He said. He did not say, "He who eats my flesh will live in me, and I will live in him." He who eats my flesh and drinks my blood means that we are commanded to take both. 
 
Therefore, we are commanded to receive his holy body and his precious blood. We are invited, given and commanded by God to have the flesh as food for the soul and the blood as drink for the soul. Therefore, it means that we receive his holy body and his precious blood. This command is God's, so when we accept it and carry it out, the benefit is for ourselves. 

Answer #2: To your second question, must Holy Communion happen after Holy Baptism, the answer is Yes. Our Lord Jesus Christ ordered his disciples: “Go therefore and make disciples of all the nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit.” (Mathew 28:19)  Baptism means immersion in a blessed water (in the name of the Holy Trinity). Baptism is the gate/door/Point of entry to Christianity. One cannot be a Christian without baptism. It’s the first sacrament to be performed for all Christians.

So, children, boys at 40 days and girls at 80 days, are baptized for the first time when they are baptized and after that they are sealed/confirmed/anointed with holy myron. The priest says, I anoint you in the name of the Father, I anoint you in the name of the Son, I anoint you in the name of the Holy Spirit, he anoints all the body parts and seals them with holy myron. Then they will be given a baptism name. After that, the person who brought the children to Christianity should take them to the church, and they will celebrate the day's Mass and receive Holy Communion.

So this is the baptism of childhood that we get once. But it does not mean that we are baptized regularly when we receive the holy flesh and the precious blood. Baptism is required for the first time. Only a baptized person can receive the holy flesh and the precious blood. Apart from that, an unbaptized person, an unbeliever will not be given his holy body and precious blood. Believing for the first time is mandatory to be baptized to receive His Holy Body and Precious Blood. Once we believe and are baptized, since infant baptism is once, we are not born again and again after being born once of the Trinity. It means that we are once born and baptized, and those who have fallen into sin, we rise in repentance and continue to receive His Holy Body and His Precious Blood. But for the first time, the foundation is baptism. The Church does not provide his holy body and precious blood to unbaptized people.
