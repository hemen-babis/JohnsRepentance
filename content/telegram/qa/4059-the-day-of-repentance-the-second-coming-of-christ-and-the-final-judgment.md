Part 2

Brethren beloved in Christ, let us set our hearts upon the immutable truth of the final judgment, a day appointed by the Almighty in which the righteous shall be glorified, and the wicked shall receive the due reward of their deeds. This is not a matter of speculation, nor a question for idle debate, but a certainty attested by the Holy Scriptures and the teachings of the Fathers. Let us, therefore, contemplate deeply upon the nature of this great and fearful day, and may our meditation bear fruit unto righteousness.

The Separation of the Righteous and the Wicked

The Gospel of Matthew records the words of our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ, who, as the Righteous Judge, shall divide all nations as a shepherd separates the sheep from the goats (Matt. 25:32). To those on His right, He shall say:

"Come, you who are blessed by My Father, inherit the kingdom prepared for you from the foundation of the world. For I was hungry, and you gave Me food; I was thirsty, and you gave Me drink; I was a stranger, and you took Me in; I was naked, and you clothed Me; I was sick, and you visited Me; I was in prison, and you came to Me."

And the righteous, in their humility, shall ask, "Lord, when did we do these things for You?" But the King shall answer, "Truly I say to you, as you did it to one of the least of these My brethren, you did it to Me."

Yet, to those on His left, He shall declare:

"Depart from Me, you cursed, into the eternal fire prepared for the devil and his angels. For I was hungry, and you gave Me no food; I was thirsty, and you gave Me no drink; I was a stranger, and you did not welcome Me; I was naked, and you did not clothe Me; I was sick and in prison, and you did not visit Me."

These, in their blindness, shall protest, "Lord, when did we see You hungry, thirsty, a stranger, naked, sick, or in prison and did not serve You?" And He shall answer, "Truly I say to you, as you did not do it to one of the least of these, you did not do it to Me."

Thus, the righteous shall enter into eternal life, and the wicked into everlasting punishment. This teaches us a most profound truth: faith without works is dead. It is not enough to profess Christ with our lips while our hearts remain far from Him. The works of mercy, charity, and compassion are the very proof of a living faith.

Life After the Resurrection

The mystery of the resurrection is not to be taken lightly. The Apostle Paul, in his inspired words, proclaims:

"We shall not all sleep, but we shall all be changed, in a moment, in the twinkling of an eye, at the last trumpet. For the trumpet will sound, and the dead will be raised incorruptible, and we shall be changed." (1 Cor. 15:52)

What does this mean? It signifies that at the appointed time, the bodies of all the dead shall rise—not in the frailty of flesh as we now know it, but in an incorruptible state. Those who are counted worthy of the resurrection unto life shall be as the angels of God, neither marrying nor being given in marriage, for they shall be children of the resurrection (Luke 20:35-36).

If there were no resurrection of the dead, then our faith would be in vain. If the dead do not rise, then Christ Himself has not risen, and if Christ has not risen, then our hope is empty, and we remain in our sins (1 Cor. 15:14-19). But we know that Christ has indeed risen, becoming the firstfruits of those who have fallen asleep, and therefore, we have full assurance that we, too, shall be raised in glory.

The Fate of Those Still Alive Before the End

The prophet Malachi foretold that before the great and dreadful day of the Lord, Elijah the prophet shall come to turn the hearts of the fathers to the children and the children to the fathers (Mal. 4:5-6). Our Lord affirmed this, saying, "Elijah indeed shall first come and restore all things." (Matt. 17:11)
