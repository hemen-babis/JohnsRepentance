👉👉👉 About #Reproduction

👉🏾 Question፦ Hello, I was wondering what the biblical purpose of having kids is? 

A couple of questions: 


1. Why do we bring kids to this world if the world is A) corrupted with sin and B) filled with suffering.

2) Should people give birth just because they want kids or because they want to continue their generation, so the kids can help them when they get old and other reasons related to fulfilling the parents. 

Or should it be because they want to show love to the kids and share the love of God with them? In other words, are there selfish reasons for having kids?  
Or is it a combination of all of these reasons? & What should the intention be when people think about having kids. 

Thank you.

Answer፦ Peace be upon you, my child, and may the grace of our Lord Jesus Christ be with you. Your questions delve into the heart of God's creation and His plan for us, His beloved children.  

Let us first acknowledge the reality you so keenly observe: our world, stained by the Fall, groans under the weight of sin and suffering. Yet, even in the face of this darkness, the light of God's love shines brightly through His greatest creation – humankind.

You ask about the purpose of bringing children into a world marred by such hardship.  Recall the words of the Psalmist,  "Behold, children are a heritage from the Lord, the fruit of the womb is a reward" (Psalm 127:3). Children are not burdens, but blessings, gifts from the Almighty. 

To address your questions directly:

1. Why bring children into a broken world? We bring children into this world not to suffer, but to know and love God, to become vessels of His grace, and to participate in His work of redemption. As St. John Chrysostom teaches, "The greatest gift that can be given to children is not money or other material things, but the opportunity to grow in virtue." Through their lives, they have the potential to spread God's love and bring light into the darkness.

2. What is the right intention for having children? It is natural and understandable to desire children for the joy they bring, the continuation of family, and even for support in later years. However, our primary motivation should always be rooted in love – love for the child and love for God. We are called to be stewards of these precious souls, to nurture them in the Orthodox faith, and to guide them towards the Kingdom of Heaven.  

Remember the words of our Lord, "Let the little children come to me, and do not hinder them, for the kingdom of heaven belongs to such as these" (Matthew 19:14).  

Therefore, my child, the decision to have children is a sacred one, not to be taken lightly. It requires prayerful discernment, seeking God’s will for your life and your family.  

Perhaps you might reflect on the lives of Saints Joachim and Anna, parents of the Theotokos. They were advanced in years and childless, yet they never lost faith in God's plan. Their unwavering devotion and persistent prayers were answered with the miraculous conception of the Virgin Mary, the very vessel of our salvation.

If you are considering parenthood, I urge you to seek guidance from your spiritual father. He can offer personalized counsel and prayers as you navigate this important decision.

May God guide you and grant you wisdom on your journey.

May the name of the Holy God be praised forever and ever in every tongue that He has created, today and always. 

May the blessings of God's mother, the virgin, and the cross be upon us. Amen.
