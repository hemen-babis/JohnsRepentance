About #fasting: how we should #fast

👉 Question: In the bible it says do not appear to be fasting. If people invite you over and ask you to eat but they aren’t christians, is it okay to say your fasting and ask for vegan meals or are we supposed to hide the fact we’re fasting. Also, if let’s say you want to fast untill 3:00 pm but family members want you to eat with them at 12:00, is saying you want to wait until 3:00 the wrong approach and should i just be eating with them at 12 or what’s the best approach. Another example is how my grandmother wants me to drink coffee with her around 12:00/1:00. Should i be saying okay and doing that or politely mention how i want to wait a little longer.

Answer፦ Thank you for your continued interest in deepening your spiritual practices. Regarding fasting, it is indeed important to maintain fidelity to the discipline of fasting while also considering its witness to those around us.

In situations where others invite you to eat during fasting periods, it can be both a testament to your faith and an opportunity for evangelism to kindly explain that you are fasting according to the traditions of the Orthodox Church. This approach aligns with the Scripture, "Sanctify Christ as Lord in your hearts, always being ready to make a defense to everyone who asks you to give an account for the hope that is in you, yet with gentleness and reverence" (1 Peter 3:15). It is an opportunity to witness about the practice and significance of fasting in our faith, always ensuring that it is done with the utmost humility, reflecting the gentle spirit of Christ.

If you are invited to eat or drink before your fasting period ends, such as the scenario with your grandmother wanting to share coffee, it is respectful to explain that you are still in your fasting period and would gladly join her later. This not only maintains your spiritual discipline but also honors the teachings of our Church Fathers on the importance of fasting.

These moments of fidelity to your fast are profound opportunities to live out your faith visibly and humbly. How might you prepare to share about your fasting practice in a way that enlightens and invites curiosity about Orthodoxy?
